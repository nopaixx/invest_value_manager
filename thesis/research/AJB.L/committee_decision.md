## Gate 2: Business Understanding -- PASS

AJ Bell operates the UK's largest listed investment platform. Revenue model: ad valorem fees (~0.25%) on GBP 108B AUA, plus transaction fees and interest income on client cash. Two channels: Advised (182K customers, 60% AUA, slower growth 6%) and D2C (462K customers, 40% AUA, faster growth 25%).

**Can I explain in 2 minutes:** Yes. AJ Bell clips ~32bp on assets people hold on their platform. Assets grow with markets + net inflows. Operating leverage drives margins higher as fixed costs (technology, compliance) spread over more AUA.

**Why is it cheap:** P/E 16.4x vs historical 20-25x. Market fears: (1) HL privatization removed sector benchmark, (2) fee compression from competitors, (3) BoE rate cuts reducing interest income, (4) UK macro weakness. Market implies only 5.7% growth vs 18-25% actual.

**Counter-thesis:** R2 (devil's advocate) identified that interest income is bundled into "ad valorem" revenue without separation, that the 24.7% historical CAGR includes a BoE rate tailwind now reversing, and that FCF is declining despite revenue growing. These are valid concerns partially addressed in R3 but with HARD GATES remaining.

**Value trap checklist: 0/10** -- However, R2 corrects: insider selling reclassified from POSITIVE to NEUTRAL-NEGATIVE (Andy Bell sold GBP 46M in 2024). This does not change the 0/10 score but changes the qualitative signal.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas sobre esta decision

- **Is IHP.L sufficient UK platform exposure?** If IHP.L thesis is correct (FV 390p, E[CAGR] 19.5%), we already have maximum exposure to the UK platform theme at 11.4%. AJB.L adds marginal diversification WITHIN the same narrow theme. The question is whether AJB.L's D2C growth story is sufficiently differentiated from IHP.L's adviser-focused model to justify a second position. My assessment: the differentiation exists but the correlated risks dominate.

- **Am I being too cautious?** The R3 entry of 375p requires a 17% decline from current 450p. In a Tier A business with genuine growth, waiting for a 17% pullback could mean the opportunity passes. However, with HL fee cuts going live NOW (March 2026) and H1 results due Apr/May, there IS a near-term catalyst for potential price weakness. Patience is justified.

- **FCF decline remains unexplained.** Three years of declining FCF (GBP 98M to 84M) while revenue grew 40% is unusual. If this is structural (growing business consuming more cash as regulatory capital scales), the OEY calculation is overstating value. If temporary (working capital timing), FCF should rebound in FY26. H1 FY2026 will answer this.

### Debilidades del analisis recibido

- **R1 thesis has a material error on insider activity.** Classified insider activity as POSITIVE based on 150 BAYE plan purchases of GBP 201 each while founder sold GBP 46M via placings. R3 corrected to NEUTRAL-NEGATIVE. The R1 analyst did not verify 10b5-1 status or distinguish automatic plan purchases from discretionary sales -- violating the Insider Analysis protocol (S202).

- **Interest income is still a black box.** None of the R1/R2/R3 analysts could decompose interest income from custody fee revenue within the GBP 232M "recurring ad valorem" line. This is the #1 risk and remains unquantified from primary data. Citi's 15% EPS headwind estimate is Level 2 (sell-side analysis) that has not been independently verified.

- **No independent QS calculation by committee.** I am relying on the R1 QS of 82adj and the tool output of 87. The tool output confirms Tier A. No adjustment needed from committee.

- **quality_scorer.py for financial services:** The tool produces 253% ROIC which is meaningless for asset-light platforms. A `--financial-services` flag that switches the primary metric from ROIC to ROE would produce more useful output and avoid the need for -3 manual adjustment every time.
