## Business Understanding

### What Diploma Does

Diploma is a specialty distributor of mission-critical technical products and services, organized into three segments:

**Controls (49% of revenue, 20% organic growth FY25):** Industrial automation, cable management, interconnect solutions, and fasteners for North American industrial markets. Beneficiary of reshoring, defense spending, data center buildout.

**Seals (34% of revenue, 2% organic growth FY25):** Aftermarket hydraulic and pneumatic seals for industrial, mobile, and process equipment. Highly recurring -- seals are consumables that must be replaced. Lower cyclicality as maintenance-driven.

**Life Sciences (17% of revenue, 6% organic growth FY25):** Clinical diagnostics consumables, instruments, and environmental testing products. Steady, non-cyclical demand.

### Business Model -- Why It Works

1. **Mission-critical niche products:** A missing seal can shut down a hydraulic system; a missing cable connector can halt a production line. These products are low-cost relative to the equipment they serve but critical to its function. Customers do not shop on price.

2. **Technical expertise as moat:** Diploma's businesses employ specialist engineers who advise customers on product selection, installation, and problem-solving. This creates switching costs -- the relationship is advisory, not transactional.

3. **Fragmented markets, consolidation play:** Each segment operates in highly fragmented markets. Diploma acquires small niche distributors (typically GBP 5-30M revenue), integrates them onto its platform, and drives margin improvement through procurement scale and operational best practices. 8 deals completed in the last two quarters for ~GBP 130M with ~GBP 20M annualized operating profit contribution.

4. **Aftermarket/consumables dominance:** Particularly in Seals, Diploma is positioned in the aftermarket -- maintenance-driven replacement of parts. This provides recurring demand regardless of new equipment sales cycles.

## Moat Assessment

**Moat Rating: Narrow-to-Wide**

**Sources of Moat:**
1. **Switching costs (PRIMARY):** Technical advisory relationships mean customers rely on Diploma's engineers for product selection. Switching to a generalist distributor means losing specialist knowledge. In Seals, the aftermarket model creates recurring relationships.

2. **Scale advantages in niche markets:** In each niche, Diploma has procurement scale that smaller competitors cannot match. A local seal distributor cannot match Diploma's product range, delivery speed, or engineering support.

3. **Intangible assets:** Specialist technical knowledge, supplier relationships (exclusive distribution agreements in some cases), and regulatory expertise in Life Sciences.

4. **Network effects (limited):** The more acquisitions Diploma makes in a vertical, the more attractive it becomes to the next acquisition target (platform effect).

**Moat durability:** HIGH. These niches are too small to attract large generalist competitors (like Amazon). The technical advisory component is not easily replicated digitally. The acquisition strategy deepens the moat over time by adding adjacent capabilities.

**GM premium vs sector:** +18.3pp above industrial sector median. This is strong evidence of moat -- customers are paying significantly more than commodity distribution rates because of the value-added service.
