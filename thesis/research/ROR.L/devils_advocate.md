## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis correctly identified Rotork as overvalued at 386p and set a distant entry of 270-290p. Since then, the stock has dropped 20.5% to 307p following FY2025 results (March 10) that confirmed growth deceleration and cautious 2026 guidance. The thesis was directionally correct on overvaluation. However, the thesis's Fair Value of 320p now requires downward revision: FY2025 FCF came in at GBP 106.8M (down 17% from GBP 128.8M in FY2024), and 2026 guidance signals continued headwinds in O&G upstream/midstream (~40% of O&G division). The thesis understated O&G cyclicality risk and overstated the probability of margin expansion. At 307p, the stock is closer to fair value but not yet at a compelling entry given remaining risks.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. FCF Margin Sustainability at 17%+

- **FA Assumption:** FCF margin 17-18%, using 16-17% base case (conservatively below current 17.1%)
- **Evidence Against:** FY2025 FCF was GBP 106.8M on GBP 777M revenue = **13.7% FCF margin** -- a SIGNIFICANT decline from FY2024's 17.1%. Operating cash conversion was 101% (down YoY). The 3-year average FCF margin is 13.4%, which the FA acknowledged but dismissed. The FY2024 figure (17.1%) now appears to have been an outlier high, not the new normal.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution:** DA recommends using 13-14% FCF margin (3yr average) as base case, not 16-17%. This directly lowers DCF fair value by approximately 15-20%.

### 2. Revenue Growth Recovery to 5-6%

- **FA Assumption:** Revenue growth 5-6% (years 1-3), recovering from soft patch, with order book support
- **Evidence Against:** FY2025 actual OCC revenue growth was 3.7%. Management 2026 guidance signals "mixed end markets" with O&G upstream/midstream "subdued." The order book at GBP 783M (+6% OCC) provides some support, but oil & gas orders grew only 0.6% OCC. Water & Power grew 6.1% but management flagged regulatory delays in Europe for 2026. CPI at +7% was the bright spot. Blended, 3-4% OCC growth for FY2026 seems more realistic than 5-6%.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Use 3.5-4% growth for years 1-2, 5-6% for years 3-5 (requires evidence of O&G recovery and energy transition acceleration).

### 3. Energy Transition Tailwind Materializing on Schedule

- **FA Assumption:** Energy transition (hydrogen, CCS) is a tailwind that drives TAM growth
- **Evidence Against:** Management explicitly stated in the FY2025 earnings call that "carbon capture and hydrogen underperformed expectations." This is a direct contradiction of the thesis's assumed tailwind. Energy transition spending is real but the timeline is MUCH slower than optimists expected. Hydrogen infrastructure is still in pilot/demonstration phase globally. CCS projects face financing challenges. The "energy transition tailwind" is a 5-10 year story, not a 1-3 year catalyst.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Remove energy transition from years 1-3 growth projections. Model as optionality only (bull case upside, not base case).

### 4. O&G Exposure Risk Underweighted

- **FA Assumption:** O&G at ~35-40% of revenue with diversified end markets providing resilience
- **Evidence Against:** O&G division grew only 0.6% OCC in FY2025 with specific project delays. Management flagged upstream and midstream (40% of O&G division = ~14-16% of group revenue) as "subdued" for 2026. In the Hormuz crisis context with oil at $90+, this creates a paradox: high oil prices SHOULD benefit O&G capex, but geopolitical instability actually DELAYS project decisions as companies wait for clarity. Additionally, the global energy transition pressure means O&G companies are reducing long-cycle capex commitments even when prices support it. Rotork's O&G revenue has been the weakest segment for 2 years running.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution:** Model O&G as flat-to-declining for FY2026-2027 until there is evidence of a recovery in midstream project commitments.

### 5. QS Adjustment from 73 to 78 (Tier B to Tier A)

- **FA Assumption:** +5 point QS adjustment for market position (#1-2 in valve actuators)
- **Evidence Against:** The market position claim is factually correct -- Rotork IS #1-2 in valve actuators. However, the adjustment is aggressive because: (a) 10% global share in a $8-10B fragmented market is leadership but not dominance; (b) Chinese competitors are advancing rapidly with "comparable R&D investment" and 15-20% cost advantages; (c) Emerson offers end-to-end automation integration that Rotork cannot match; (d) Flowserve offers valve+actuator integrated packages. The moat is NARROW, not "narrow-to-wide" as the FA suggested. At QS 73 (Tier B), the MoS requirement increases from ~10-15% to ~20-25%.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Accept QS 75-76 (borderline Tier A) rather than 78. The market position adjustment is valid but +5 is generous. +2-3 is more appropriate given competitive dynamics.
