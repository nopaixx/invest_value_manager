## 2. Resolution — Point by Point

### 2.1 FCF Margin: DA wins → 14%

The R1 used 16-17% FCF margin. FY2025 delivered 13.7% (GBP 107M on GBP 777M). The 3-year average is 13.4%.

FY2024's 17.1% now appears to be an outlier (working capital timing), not the new normal. The DA correctly identified this decline.

**Resolution:** Use 14% normalized FCF margin (slightly above 3yr avg 13.4%, giving credit for structural margin improvements from Access Solutions restructuring).

### 2.2 Revenue Growth: DA mostly wins → 4% near-term

- FY2025 actual: 3.7% OCC
- Management guidance: "mixed end markets," O&G upstream/midstream "subdued," Water & Power regulatory delays in EU
- Order book +6% OCC provides some buffer but O&G orders +0.6% is weak

**Resolution:** 4% growth years 1-2 (FY2026-27), 5% years 3-5 (requires O&G recovery or energy transition acceleration). DA's 3.5% was slightly too bearish given order book support.

### 2.3 Energy Transition: DA wins — remove from near-term

Management explicitly stated CCS and hydrogen "underperformed expectations." This is a direct contradiction of the R1's assumed tailwind. The energy transition is REAL but a 5-10 year story, not 1-3.

**Resolution:** Energy transition = bull case optionality only. Not in base or bear projections.

### 2.4 O&G Exposure: DA wins — more problematic than R1 weighted

O&G is ~35-40% of revenue. Division grew only 0.6% OCC in FY2025. Upstream/midstream (40% of O&G = ~15% of group) is "subdued" per guidance. The Hormuz ceasefire (2 weeks) doesn't resolve this — project decisions require sustained stability.

The paradox: high oil SHOULD benefit O&G capex, but geopolitical instability DELAYS decisions. And the energy transition means O&G companies are reducing long-cycle capex even when prices support it.

**Resolution:** O&G flat-to-declining FY2026-27. Recovery depends on geopolitical stabilization + project pipeline de-freezing.

### 2.5 QS Adjustment: Split → 75

R1: +5 (73→78). DA: +2-3 (73→75-76).

Rotork IS #1-2 in valve actuators — this is factual. But 10% share in a fragmented $8-10B market is leadership, not dominance. Chinese competition advancing. Emerson offers integrated automation. +5 is generous, +2 is conservative.

**Resolution:** +2 adjustment. QS 73→75. Tier A borderline. For MoS purposes, treat as **Tier A lower** (15-18% MoS, not 20%+ Tier B but not 10-12% full Tier A).

## 3. R3 Fair Value Calculation

### Method 1: EV/EBIT Normalized (60% weight)

**Bear case:**
- FY2026 EBIT: GBP 180M (revenue GBP 785M at +1%, margin 23% compressed)
- Multiple: 14x (low growth industrial, O&G headwind)
- EV = GBP 2,520M + net cash GBP 65M = GBP 2,585M / 823M shares = **314p**

Hmm — bear EV/EBIT gives 314p which is basically current price. Let me cross-check with FCF.

**Base case:**
- FY2026 EBIT: GBP 192M (revenue GBP 808M at +4%, margin 23.8%)
- Multiple: 16x (quality industrial with aftermarket growth)
- EV = GBP 3,072M + GBP 65M = GBP 3,137M / 823M = **381p**

**Anti-bullish-bias (60/40):** (314 × 0.60) + (381 × 0.40) = 188.4 + 152.4 = **341p**

### Method 2: FCF-Based DCF (40% weight)

Using updated FCF:
- FY2025 FCF: GBP 107M (actual)
- Growth: 4% years 1-2, 5% years 3-5
- WACC: 9.0% (tool), terminal growth 2.5%
- FCF year 5: GBP 130M
- Terminal value: GBP 130M / (9% - 2.5%) = GBP 2,000M
- PV of FCF stream + terminal: ~GBP 1,800M
- + net cash GBP 65M = GBP 1,865M / 823M = **227p** (bear anchor)

**Base FCF (using 14% margin on growing revenue):**
- FY2028 FCF: GBP 125M (GBP 890M rev × 14%)
- Terminal: GBP 125M / 6.5% = GBP 1,923M
- PV + cash: ~GBP 2,100M / 823M = **255p**

**Anti-bullish-bias:** (227 × 0.60) + (255 × 0.40) = 136.2 + 102.0 = **238p**

### Reconciliation

| Method | Anti-Bias FV | Weight | Weighted |
|--------|-------------|--------|----------|
| EV/EBIT | 341p | 60% | 204.6p |
| FCF DCF | 238p | 40% | 95.2p |
| **Weighted Average** | | **100%** | **299.8p** |

Two methods diverge significantly: EV/EBIT (341p) vs FCF DCF (238p). The gap reflects FCF margin compression — the EV/EBIT method uses EBIT margin (~24%) while DCF uses FCF margin (~14%). The 10pp gap between EBIT and FCF margins is driven by capex (Capex/Dep 1.5x) and working capital.

**R3 Fair Value: 300p** (rounded from 300p mechanical). Deliberately closer to the average than either extreme. The FCF DCF anchors the floor, the EV/EBIT provides the ceiling.

This is 6% below the R1's 320p and 4% above the DA's S202 288p. It properly reflects the FCF decline while maintaining the quality premium.

## 8. Verdict

**WATCHLIST at 245p — quality flow control, wrong cycle position.**

Rotork is a genuine quality industrial: #1-2 global valve actuators, ROIC 22%, net cash, expanding adjusted margins, 23% aftermarket revenue. The DA was MODERATE COUNTER — it correctly identified three material issues (FCF decline to 13.7%, O&G stagnation, energy transition delayed) that the R1 underweighted but didn't invalidate the business thesis.

At 319p, the stock is essentially at R3 FV (300p) with no MoS. The EV calculation confirms this: probability-weighted FV = 320p ≈ market price. The market is pricing Rotork correctly for the current reality.

At 245p, the stock prices in meaningful pessimism (O&G cuts, FCF compression) and provides 18% MoS. This requires a 23% drop — ambitious but not impossible given no positive catalyst until Aug and the soft O&G environment.

**Do not advance to R4. Resume if price reaches 260p or if H1 2026 shows FCF margin recovery >15%.**

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres
- The EV/EBIT vs FCF DCF divergence (341p vs 238p) is uncomfortably wide. It reflects the EBIT-to-FCF conversion gap (~10pp). If working capital normalizes (FY2025 was poor), FCF margin could recover toward 15-16% and the DCF improves significantly.
- Oil ceasefire (2 weeks) may unfreeze O&G project decisions. If upstream/midstream capex resumes, Rotork's order book could inflect positively by H2 2026.
- UK geographic concentration is real: adding ROR.L would be the 6th UK position (IHP.L, DNLM.L, ITRK.L, MEGP.L, ALFA.L). At 245p it's worth it for the quality, but flagged.

### Sugerencias
- ROR.L fits naturally in an industrial-technology basket alongside MEGP.L and potentially BEAN.SW. Flow control is mission-critical infrastructure — complementary to, not correlated with, self-service vending and HVAC actuators.
