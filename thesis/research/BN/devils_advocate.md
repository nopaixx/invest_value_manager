# Counter-Analysis: BN (Brookfield Corporation)

## Fecha: 2026-03-20

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis for BN identifies a legitimate opportunity in a complex conglomerate trading at a discount to its parts. However, the thesis has significant vulnerabilities: (1) the +31 QS adjustment is the 3rd largest ever and several components are debatable, (2) the bear case FV of $40 provides only 2.2% MoS at current $39.12 -- essentially zero downside protection, (3) the insurance book introduces embedded leverage and tail risk that the thesis acknowledges but underweights, (4) insider selling is notably one-sided with $15M+ in director sales (Howard Marks) against minimal purchases, and (5) private credit contagion risk is real and sector-wide (9.2% default rates, record levels). The thesis SURVIVES but the FV needs downward revision and the entry point of $35-36 is already correct -- buying at market ($39.12) would be imprudent.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. QS Adjustment of +31 Points (27 -> 58) Is Justified

- **FA claim:** BN's GAAP financials are structurally distorted; the tool's 27/100 is meaningless; adjusted 58 is appropriate for Tier B.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - This is the 3rd largest QS adjustment in our history (after KKR +38 and ERIE +20). Adjustments this large inherently carry high uncertainty.
  - The ROIC adjustment (+6 points) relies on segment-level ROE calculations that are management's supplemental disclosures, not audited GAAP. BN's consolidated ROIC of 3.3% vs 6.7% WACC = -3.4pp is the GAAP reality. The "blended ROIC on equity capital of 10-13%" is management's preferred framing.
  - The FCF adjustment (+7 points) substitutes Distributable Earnings for FCF. DE is a management-defined non-GAAP metric. Management has discretion over what to include/exclude. The GAAP reality: -$3.6B FCF in 2024, deteriorating from +$1.5B in 2022. This is a REAL cash outflow trend, not just accounting noise.
  - The leverage adjustment (+5 points) distinguishes corporate vs non-recourse debt. While technically correct that BN's corporate debt is ~$8-10B, the reality is that BN is responsible for the overall enterprise. If operating companies fail, BN's equity is destroyed. The $273B consolidated debt represents REAL obligations that flow through BN's economic reality.
  - **Cross-check:** KKR received +38 adjustment (to 62). But KKR's FRE margin is 69% vs BN's lower margin profile, KKR has simpler structure (2 entities vs 6), and KKR's insurance (Global Atlantic) is smaller relative to the whole. BN at 58 vs KKR at 62 is reasonable but I would argue BN's complexity justifies a WIDER gap -- 55 maximum.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Reduce QS adjustment to +28 (QS 55, still Tier B but at the floor). The FCF and leverage adjustments are each ~1 point too generous given the genuine cash flow deterioration and enormous consolidated leverage.

### 2. DE-Based Valuation at 20x Multiple Is Appropriate

- **FA claim:** BN trades at 16.3x DE; Blackstone at 25x; even at 20x (significant discount) BN is worth $48.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - The comparison to Blackstone is misleading. BX is a PURE asset manager with no operating businesses, no insurance, and far less structural complexity. BN is a conglomerate. Conglomerates trade at lower multiples for rational reasons: capital allocation opacity, related-party conflicts, complexity costs.
  - The thesis itself notes BN has 6 public tickers, incomprehensible GAAP financials, and governance concerns. A 20% discount to BX (20x vs 25x) is INSUFFICIENT for this level of complexity. A 35-40% discount (15-16x) is more appropriate -- which is exactly where the MARKET prices it.
  - Private credit default rates hit 9.2% in 2025 (record). BN has $363B in credit AUM. Even if BN's own book performs better, fee pressure and AUM outflows in the sector compress multiples for ALL alt managers.
  - Fundraising hit its weakest year since 2020 in 2025. Commitments to commingled funds fell ~24% YoY. This directly impacts BAM's fee-bearing capital growth assumption of 10-12%/yr.
  - The 15-16x DE multiple the market assigns may actually be RATIONAL given: conglomerate structure, insurance tail risk, real estate headwinds, fundraising slowdown, and private credit fears.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** The market at 16x DE may be more right than the thesis. A fair multiple range is 15-18x DE, not 20x. At 17x base: FV = $2.40 x 17 = $40.80. The thesis's $48 requires the market to give BN a HIGHER multiple than today -- i.e., the complexity discount must narrow. That is a bet, not a margin of safety.

### 3. Insurance Float Compounding Is "Berkshire-Like"

- **FA claim:** 2.25% spread on $143B float = structural earnings engine. Wealth Solutions DE $1.7B growing 24%.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Berkshire's insurance float has been PROVEN over 50+ years through multiple credit cycles, including 2008. BN's insurance book is ~2 years old post-AEL acquisition. Comparing to Berkshire is premature and potentially misleading.
  - The 2.25% spread assumes BN can invest insurance float in alternative assets at 5.7% while paying 3.5%. In a credit downturn, the investment return drops (defaults, markdowns) while the liability cost is fixed. This is embedded LEVERAGE -- the same mechanic that destroyed AIG in 2008.
  - AEL was a struggling annuity company before acquisition. Rating agencies had concerns (AM Best placed NERC ratings under review). The upgrade to "A" reflects Brookfield's backing but the underlying annuity book carries long-duration fixed obligations.
  - BN's office debt refinancing: $2.8B due in 2025 and $5.3B in 2026 -- $8.1B total. BN is not paying interest on 3% of its $33B real estate debt. If insurance float is being deployed into illiquid alternatives and real estate, a liquidity crunch during a credit event could be catastrophic.
  - Kill condition #2 (insurance credit losses >$3B/year) is set HIGH. At $143B invested assets, $3B = 2.1% loss rate. For comparison, during 2008, high-yield default rates exceeded 14%. Even investment-grade losses were material. $3B may be breached before it triggers.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** The insurance engine is real but UNTESTED in a downturn. The Berkshire comparison should be removed. The spread assumption should be stress-tested at 1.5% (compressed) and 0% (crisis). The kill condition threshold should be tightened to $2B.

### 4. Management's 30-Year Track Record Provides High Conviction

- **FA claim:** 19% CAGR over 30 years under Flatt is extraordinary. 17.4% insider ownership.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Bruce Flatt has stepped down as BAM CEO (replaced by 38-year-old Connor Teskey). He remains chairman of BAM and CEO of BN, but this is a significant leadership transition. The 30-year track record is Flatt's -- not the organization's going forward.
  - Insider SELLING is notable: Howard Marks (director) sold ~$15M in Nov 2025 alone. Brian Lawson sold ~$6.9M in Jan 2026. Total insider sells: 23 transactions. Total insider buys: 2 transactions ($3M total). The ratio is 7.7:1 sells-to-buys.
  - **10b5-1 check (mandatory S202):** The Lawson sells on Jan 5 (4 transactions same day, varying amounts) suggest a pre-planned disposition, not open-market discretionary selling. Mark's sells on Nov 14 and 17 could also be pre-planned. However, the VOLUME of sells vs buys is concerning regardless of scheduling.
  - The Feb 23 grant/conversion transactions for Flatt (4M shares converted, 400K granted) are compensation events, not open-market purchases. They should not be interpreted as bullish.
  - Flatt personally holds ~$3.5B -- but he's been selling BAM shares ($17.2M CAD sale May 2025). For a billionaire, these sales are portfolio management, not bearish signals per se. But the ABSENCE of significant open-market purchases at $39 is notable.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Track record is real but has succession risk. The insider selling pattern (23 sells vs 2 buys) is a yellow flag. Monitor Flatt's personal holdings and Teskey's early moves at BAM.

### 5. Real Estate Exposure Is "Actively Managed" and an Opportunity

- **FA claim:** BN is playing OFFENSE in RE, $60B deployed during trough, $24B capital generation expected.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - BN's office debt: $2.8B due 2025, $5.3B due 2026. BN is not paying interest on 3% of $33B debt.
  - "Deployed during trough" assumes the trough is past. With rates higher-for-longer and remote work structural, office trough may not be over.
  - The $24B "capital generation expected" is management's forecast. Real estate capital returns depend on transaction markets, which are frozen in the current rate environment. PE exit values are depressed -- same applies to RE.
  - GAAP FCF deteriorated from +$1.5B (2022) to -$3.6B (2024). While the thesis attributes this to consolidation of operating capex, some of this is genuine cash consumption from RE development that may not earn returns for years.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** RE exposure is a legitimate headwind. The "$60B deployed during trough" narrative is management framing -- verify actual returns before accepting.
