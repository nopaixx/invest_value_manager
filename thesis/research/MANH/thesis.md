## Business Understanding

### What Manhattan Associates Does

Manhattan Associates solves one of the most complex problems in enterprise software: **orchestrating the physical movement of goods through the supply chain**. Its software manages the warehousing, transportation, and fulfillment operations for major retailers, grocery chains, manufacturers, and logistics providers.

**Revenue breakdown (~$1.08B FY2025):**
- **Cloud subscriptions:** ~37% of total revenue (growing 20%+ YoY), now 96% of software revenue
- **Services (implementation/consulting):** ~38% of revenue
- **Maintenance/licensing (legacy):** ~25% of revenue, declining (legacy on-premise wind-down)

**Product portfolio:**
1. **Manhattan Active Warehouse Management (WM):** Core product. Cloud-native, microservices-based. Updates every 90 days (vs competitors every 5-6 years). 50% of revenue.
2. **Manhattan Active Order Management (OM):** Omnichannel order fulfillment. 25-30% of revenue.
3. **Manhattan Active Transportation Management (TM):** Route optimization, carrier management. 15-20% of revenue.
4. **New products (2025-2026):** Supply Chain Planning, Enterprise Promise & Fulfill, Point-of-Sale.

### Unit Economics

- **Subscription model:** Cloud subscription with multi-year contracts. RPO (Remaining Performance Obligations) = $2.1B, providing 2+ years revenue visibility.
- **LTV/CAC:** Not disclosed directly, but 95-100% customer retention rate + 3-4 year implementation cycles = extremely high switching costs. Once a customer deploys MANH WM across distribution centers, replacement is practically impossible.
- **Net revenue retention:** Not publicly broken out, but cross-sell (non-WMS = 50% of new bookings) and cloud migration (legacy to cloud at higher prices) suggest >110% NRR.
- **Customer base:** ~1,200 customers. Top customers include Walmart, Target, Hy-Vee, major retailers and 3PLs globally.

### Margin Structure (Expanding)

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Trend |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Gross Margin | 53.3% | 53.6% | 54.8% | 56.3% | EXPANDING +3pp in 4yr |
| Operating Margin | 19.9% | 22.6% | 25.1% | 26.1% | EXPANDING +6.2pp in 4yr |
| FCF Margin | 22.6% | 26.0% | 27.5% | 34.6% | EXPANDING +12pp in 4yr |

The margin expansion is driven by cloud transition: cloud subscriptions carry higher incremental margins than legacy license + maintenance. As legacy winds down and cloud scales, operating leverage compounds.

### Capital Requirements

- **Asset-light:** Capex/Revenue ratio low. Software business with no physical infrastructure.
- **Net cash:** $264M cash vs $23M debt. Pristine balance sheet.
- **Capital allocation:** Aggressive buybacks (~$200M/yr), no dividend. Share count declining steadily.
- **ROIC 397%:** Extraordinary, reflecting the asset-light nature of the business. Minimal invested capital required to generate substantial cash flows.

## Risk Assessment

### Risk 1: CEO Transition Execution (MEDIUM)
- Eddie Capel retired Feb 2025 after 25 years. Eric Clark (NTT Data) is new CEO.
- Clark's enterprise software experience (ServiceNow, Dell) is relevant but he lacks supply chain domain depth.
- Capel remains as Executive Vice-Chairman, mitigating knowledge loss.
- **Mitigation:** Product roadmap is engineering-driven, 90-day release cycles are institutionalized. Key question is commercial execution (sales, pricing, enterprise relationships).
- **Probability of material impact:** 20-25%

### Risk 2: Revenue Growth Deceleration (MEDIUM)
- FY2025 total revenue grew only 3.7% (vs 12.2% prior year, 21.1% year before).
- Cloud revenue still +20%, but legacy wind-down creates headline drag.
- 2026 guide: $1.133-1.153B implies 5-7% total growth.
- **Mitigation:** RPO $2.1B (+21% YoY) provides 2+ years visibility. Cloud mix shift will improve headlines as legacy base shrinks. SAP migration wave (3,000-5,000 customers) is multi-year tailwind.
- **Probability of sustained deceleration below 10%:** 30-35%

### Risk 3: SBC Dilution (LOW-MEDIUM)
- SBC/Revenue trending up: 7.7% (FY2022-23) to 8.9% (FY2024) to 10.3% (FY2025).
- This is above-average for software sector and approaching concerning levels.
- FCF margin 34.6% includes SBC as non-cash add-back. Adjusting for SBC: true economic FCF margin ~24-25%.
- **Mitigation:** Aggressive buybacks (~$200M/yr) partially offset dilution. Share count declining.
- **Impact on valuation:** Moderate. SBC-adjusted FCF should be primary metric for OEY calculation.

### Risk 4: Competition from SAP/Oracle (LOW)
- SAP migrating S/4 HANA WMS customers could recapture some. But MANH's 75% win rate against SAP in competitive deals suggests the opposite: SAP's WMS is inferior and migrations often flow TO MANH, not FROM.
- Oracle's cloud WMS is competitive in mid-market but not enterprise.
- Blue Yonder is "years behind" in cloud transition.
- **Probability of material share loss:** 10-15%

### Risk 5: Per-Seat/AI Pricing Disruption (LOW for MANH specifically)
- MANH is NOT per-seat SaaS. Pricing is based on distribution center throughput/volume, not headcount.
- AI enhances MANH's value proposition (warehouse robotics optimization, demand forecasting, autonomous supply chain).
- MANH launched "Agentic AI" platform capabilities -- positioned as enabler, not victim.
- **Probability of per-seat disruption:** <5% (structural misfit -- warehouses need WMS regardless of AI)

### Risk 6: Valuation Multiple Compression (MEDIUM)
- At P/E 40x, stock is valued for sustained growth. If revenue growth stays at 5-7%, multiple will compress toward 25-30x.
- EV/EBIT 30x is premium territory for a 5-7% revenue grower.
- **Mitigation:** FCF margin expansion (34.6%, guided higher) and cloud mix improvement may sustain premium multiples if earnings growth exceeds revenue growth through operating leverage.
