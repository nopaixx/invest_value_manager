## Key Assumptions Challenged

### 1. The 52% Price Crash Is Primarily Sentiment / Multiple Compression

- **FA Assumption (implicit):** The R1 set entry at 11,000-12,000p, implying this level would offer adequate MoS against FV of ~14,500p. The implication is that reaching this price would be a buying opportunity.
- **Counter-Evidence:**
  - The crash was NOT sentiment-driven. It was triggered by a March 23, 2026 trading update revealing:
    - (a) Loss of Sellafield contract worth GBP 45M+ despite "strong technical proposal"
    - (b) Loss of Easat radar tender worth EUR 18M for Estonian coastal installations
    - (c) Order book decline from GBP 365M to GBP 288M (-21% in 4 months)
    - (d) Middle East valve dispatch delays due to Hormuz crisis
    - (e) Board reviewing dividend policy downward (58% to 38% payout)
    - (f) Refractory division dampened by high precious metal prices
  - The stock fell 46% in a single day on this news. This is the market rationally repricing deteriorated fundamentals.
  - A dedicated GDWN bull (Cockney Rebel, 35% portfolio allocation) exited entirely, calling it his "worst trading day ever."
  - The insiders who sold GBP 25M+ at 250.19p in November 2025 were vindicated. They sold 52% higher than today's price.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution:** The R1's entry zone of 11,000-12,000p was set using pre-crash fundamentals. The zone MUST be recalibrated using post-crash inputs. At minimum, the FV should be revised to account for: GBP 288M order book (not GBP 365M), lost future revenue from Sellafield and Easat, and heightened geopolitical uncertainty on valve revenue.

### 2. FY2026 GBP 71M+ Guidance Remains Achievable

- **FA Assumption:** FY2026 pre-tax profit of GBP 71M+ is the foundation for valuation.
- **Counter-Evidence:**
  - The March 23 update states trading "remains in line with expectations." This suggests FY2026 (ending April 2026) is largely baked -- most of the year's revenue is already booked or in progress.
  - HOWEVER: The lost contracts (Sellafield + Easat) were NOT part of FY2026 guidance. They represent FY2027-FY2028 pipeline revenue that is now GONE.
  - Middle East dispatch delays could shift some FY2026 revenue into FY2027 if delays extend. The Hormuz crisis has WORSENED since March 23 -- as of April 7, the strait remains essentially closed with only limited southern route transits (3 vessels April 2-5). Iran is threatening to close Bab al-Mandeb as well. Trump's deadline is described as "final."
  - The question is not whether GBP 71M is hit THIS year, but what FY2027 looks like.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE** (FY2026 likely met; FY2027 is the concern)
- **Resolution:** FY2026 guidance appears achievable. The MATERIAL risk is that FY2027 earnings could be GBP 55-65M rather than the R1's "Blue Sky" GBP 90M. This represents a 25-40% earnings decline from peak that the market is now pricing in.

### 3. Defense/Nuclear Tailwinds Are Structural and Multi-Decade

- **FA Assumption:** AUKUS, nuclear waste containment, Type 26 frigates provide predictable multi-decade growth.
- **Counter-Evidence:**
  - **AUKUS under strain:** Virginia-class production runs at 1.1 boats/year vs 2.33 needed. The program faces political, industrial, and budget pressure. Australia paid AUD 310M for long-lead items (positive), but the overall program has "strategic drift."
  - **UK MoD budget shortfall of GBP 28B:** UK Chief of Defence Staff warned Starmer of a "multibillion-pound shortfall." The defense review has been delayed. New procurement contracts above GBP 50K require ministerial sign-off -- a procurement SLOWDOWN that directly affects Goodwin's pipeline.
  - **Sellafield loss is a direct challenge to the nuclear moat:** Goodwin lost this GBP 45M+ contract DESPITE strong technical proposal. This suggests either: (a) competitors underbid on price, implying margin pressure, or (b) political/procurement factors beyond Goodwin's control. Either way, the assumption of an unassailable nuclear position is weakened.
  - **The Easat radar loss (EUR 18M)** shows defense equipment faces real international competition.
  - Defense spending is GROWING but execution is slower and more competitive than the R1 implies.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Defense/nuclear tailwinds are real but not automatic. Contract wins are competitive, not guaranteed. The probability of AUKUS delays should be revised from 20% to 30-35%. The Sellafield loss is a data point that the nuclear moat is narrower than assumed.

### 4. The Refractory Division (80% Global Share) Is a Stable Moat

- **FA Assumption:** 80% global market share in investment casting materials provides stable recurring-ish revenue.
- **Counter-Evidence:**
  - High precious metal prices (gold up 46.7% YoY per macro_fragility) are dampening jewelry casting demand. Gold at USD 4,727/oz suppresses jewelry manufacturing globally.
  - The March update describes refractory conditions as "broadly unchanged" from DIFFICULT conditions. Not improving.
  - This is the division where the moat is strongest -- but the end-market (jewelry) is cyclically weak. Market share is meaningless if the market itself is shrinking.
  - The R1 notes refractory is 37% of segment profit. If this division underperforms, the blended growth story weakens.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** The refractory moat is real but the end-market headwind is genuine and could persist. Gold prices are structural (geopolitical uncertainty, central bank buying) not cyclical. Factor in 0-2% refractory growth for FY2027 rather than the historical 5-8%.

### 5. Tariff Risk Is Immaterial

- **FA Assumption:** The R1 does not discuss tariff risk.
- **Counter-Evidence:**
  - The US accounts for ~25% of Goodwin's revenue per company profile data. The 10% reciprocal tariff on UK exports to the US remains in force as of April 2026.
  - Additionally, 50% tariffs on steel, aluminum, and copper have been imposed. Goodwin manufactures steel castings and specialty valves -- these products may face tariff headwinds on US-bound exports.
  - Noreva GmbH (German subsidiary) manufactures valves with 75% export rate. Germany faces higher reciprocal tariffs than UK.
  - For perspective: JCB warned tariffs would cost "hundreds of millions of pounds." Goodwin is smaller but proportionally exposed.
  - The tariff impact could compress margins on US-destined products by 5-10pp if passed through, or lose price competitiveness if absorbed.
  - UK engineering sector output is expected to see zero growth in 2026, partly due to tariff headwinds.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Tariff exposure is a genuine risk not addressed in the R1. With 25% US revenue and steel/valve products subject to tariffs, this deserves monitoring. Potential FY2027 earnings impact: GBP 2-4M reduction if tariffs are not offset by pricing power.

## Independent Bear-Case Valuation

**Bear-case FY2027 EBIT: GBP 55M**

- No Duvelco contribution (still pre-revenue)
- Result: Revenue ~GBP 240M (vs ~280M FY2026E), EBIT margin 23% (vs 25% FY2026E peak), EBIT ~GBP 55M

**Multiple: 12-13x EV/EBIT**
- Below the R1's 15-18x range
- Justified by: earnings decline trajectory, contract losses signaling competitive pressure, illiquidity, lumpy project-based revenue, 64% family control = minority discount
- Comparable: UK mid-cap industrials with declining order books typically trade at 10-14x EBIT

- EV = GBP 55M x 12.5x = GBP 687M
- Net debt: ~GBP 8M (near zero)
