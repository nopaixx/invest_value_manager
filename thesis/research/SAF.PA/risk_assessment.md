## Top 3 Riesgos Criticos

### 1. LEAP Engine Durability Issues in Harsh Environments + De-Icing Flaw

- **Categoria:** Fundamental / Operativo
- **Descripcion:** Two distinct but related engine quality issues. First, premature wear of turbine components in LEAP engines operated in hot/sandy environments (Middle East, South Asia, Africa) causing higher-than-anticipated MRO workload. Second, a flaw in the LEAP-1B de-icing system that can cause the carbon-composite engine inlet to overheat in dry air, which has delayed Boeing 737 MAX 7 and MAX 10 certification into 2026.
- **Evidencia:** Safran CEO publicly acknowledged LEAP engines are "still suffering in harsh conditions." CFM has produced 1,450+ HPT hardware durability kits for LEAP-1A. Only ~50% of fleet has reverse bleed system fix. LEAP-1B de-icing fix has been in progress since Jan 2024 -- 2+ years without resolution. FAA issued temporary waivers for MAX 8/9 but requires permanent fix for MAX 7/10 certification.
- **Probabilidad:** Alta (issues are CURRENT and ongoing)
- **Impacto si materializa:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The durability issues increase MRO costs and could damage CFM's reputation vs competitors. The de-icing flaw delays MAX 7/10 revenue. However, the installed base is massive (1,800+ LEAP deliveries in 2025 alone) and switching costs for airlines are astronomical. Worst case: 5-10% margin pressure on engine services for 2-3 years, plus delayed revenue from MAX 7/10. Estimated impact: -5% to -10% on services margin until fleet retrofits complete.
- **Mitigante:** (1) HPT kits and reverse bleed fixes are being deployed at scale; (2) LEAP-1A fix is further advanced than LEAP-1B; (3) Competitor P&W GTF has had FAR worse durability issues (powder metal contamination, fleet-wide inspections), making LEAP relatively better positioned; (4) Airlines cannot switch engines mid-program -- the installed base is locked in.
- **Kill condition?:** YES -- if a safety-related grounding order (AD) is issued on LEAP engines similar to the GTF situation, this would be a kill condition.

### 2. COMAC CJ-1000A Engine Indigenization + ITAR/Export Control Risk

- **Categoria:** Competitivo / Geopolitico
- **Descripcion:** China is developing the CJ-1000A turbofan engine to replace the LEAP-1C on the C919 aircraft. Chinese certification and initial deliveries expected 2027-2028, mass production ~2030. Simultaneously, the US has demonstrated willingness to use export controls on engine sales to China (LEAP-1C sales to COMAC suspended May-Jul 2025). These two risks are correlated: US export restrictions ACCELERATE Chinese engine indigenization, permanently destroying a growing market for CFM.
- **Evidencia:** CJ-1000A flight testing began Mar 2023 on Y-20 test aircraft. Chinese certification likely 2027-2028 per OAG analyst. US suspended LEAP-1C sales to COMAC in May 2025 (lifted Jul 2025 but precedent is set). C919 fleet currently ~200 aircraft, with COMAC targeting 150/year production by 2030.
- **Probabilidad:** Media-Alta (CJ-1000A development is real; export control risk is elevated given Iran crisis and US-China tensions)
- **Impacto si materializa:** MEDIUM. China currently represents ~5-8% of narrowbody engine market but growing to 15-20% by 2035. LEAP-1C is only ~10% of CFM production. Loss of Chinese market would reduce total CFM addressable market by 10-15% over the next decade. Estimated revenue impact: -3% to -5% of Safran total revenue by 2030-2035.
- **Mitigante:** (1) CJ-1000A will need years to match LEAP reliability; Chinese airlines may prefer proven engines; (2) Aftermarket/services revenue from installed LEAP fleet persists even if new sales stop; (3) Global narrowbody market outside China is growing strongly (Airbus 75/mo target, Boeing ramp); (4) CFM RISE program for mid-2030s ensures next-gen competitiveness.
- **Kill condition?:** NO at current revenue levels. Would become a kill condition if US permanently bans LEAP sales to China AND CJ-1000A achieves mass production before 2030.

### 3. EUR/USD Hedging Concentration Risk (KO Barriers at 1.21-1.30)

- **Categoria:** Financiero
- **Descripcion:** Safran has a massive $56.4B FX options hedging book with net annual USD exposure of $14-17B. The company is fully hedged at EUR/USD 1.12 through 2028. However, management disclosed that all knock-out (KO) barriers are within the 1.21-1.30 range. If EUR/USD breaches these levels, Safran could lose a significant portion of its hedging volume, exposing it to the spot rate. With EUR/USD currently at ~1.16 and strengthening, this risk is NOT theoretical.
- **Evidencia:** Safran CFO explicitly warned during FY2025 earnings call: "all KO barriers are within 1.21-1.30, so if the euro dollar peaks again, there is a risk that we may lose part of our hedging volume." EUR/USD traded as high as 1.24 in late 2024. Current rate 1.16. Net USD exposure $14.3B in 2025, rising to $16-17B in 2026-2029.
- **Probabilidad:** Media (EUR/USD above 1.21 is plausible in a scenario where Iran war ends quickly, oil drops, and ECB holds while Fed cuts)
- **Impacto si materializa:** HIGH. If EUR/USD hits 1.25 and KO barriers are breached, Safran would be exposed at spot rate instead of hedged rate of 1.12. On $16B net exposure, the difference between 1.12 and 1.25 is ~EUR 1.7B in annual revenue reduction. This would reduce recurring operating income by ~25-30%. Estimated EPS impact: -25% to -30% in the year the KO is triggered.
- **Mitigante:** (1) Hedging is multi-year (2026-2028 fully hedged); not all KO barriers would be breached simultaneously; (2) Safran has been managing FX risk for decades; (3) If EUR strengthens, it's typically good for European purchasing power and can benefit costs; (4) The geopolitical environment currently supports USD strength (war, safe haven). EUR/USD going above 1.21 requires significant dovish Fed pivot.
- **Kill condition?:** YES -- if EUR/USD sustains above 1.25 for 6+ months AND Safran guidance explicitly warns of hedging losses, this should be a kill condition.

## Kill Conditions Sugeridas

1. **LEAP Grounding Order:** If FAA or EASA issues an airworthiness directive (AD) requiring fleet-wide grounding or mandatory inspection of LEAP engines for safety-related defects (similar to GTF powder metal AD). Probability: 5-10%.

2. **EUR/USD KO Barrier Breach:** If EUR/USD sustains above 1.25 for 2+ consecutive months AND Safran discloses material hedging volume losses. Probability: 15-20% over 3 years.

3. **Boeing 737 MAX Production Collapse:** If Boeing 737 MAX production drops below 30/month for 3+ consecutive months due to regulatory action, quality crisis, or demand destruction. This would materially reduce LEAP-1B volumes. Probability: 10-15%.

4. **US Permanent Export Ban on LEAP Engines to China:** If the US permanently bans LEAP-1C sales to COMAC (not temporary suspension) AND CJ-1000A achieves CAAC certification. Probability: 20-25% over 5 years.

5. **Receivables Quality Deterioration:** If receivables/revenue ratio exceeds 40% for 2+ consecutive quarters WITHOUT corresponding revenue acceleration, signaling customer payment stress or aggressive revenue recognition. Probability: 10-15%.

6. **Aftermarket Services Margin Collapse:** If LEAP aftermarket operating margin declines by >500bps for 2+ quarters due to durability-related warranty costs or competitive pressure. Probability: 10-15%.

  - Iran/Hormuz <-> Oil shock <-> Airline capex deferral <-> Boeing/Airbus production rates
  - COMAC CJ-1000A <-> ITAR export controls (correlated and mutually reinforcing)
  - LEAP durability <-> MRO costs <-> aftermarket margin
  - Boeing MAX production constraints <-> LEAP-1B de-icing flaw (compounding)
- Risk Score Final: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale for MEDIUM (not HIGH):** Despite 7 HIGH-scoring risks, the mitigants are substantial: (1) net cash EUR 1.5B balance sheet; (2) massive locked-in installed base with ~zero switching costs; (3) duopoly position in jet engines is structural; (4) FCF of EUR 3.9B growing to EUR 4.4-4.6B; (5) 17.4% insider ownership aligns management; (6) 5+ year backlog visibility. The key risks are real but manageable for a company of this quality. The risk level would escalate to HIGH if: (a) EUR/USD breaches 1.21 KO barriers, (b) LEAP safety grounding occurs, or (c) Boeing MAX production materially contracts.

## 🔄 META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- **Receivables growth of 36.4%:** This is the single data point I could NOT fully explain. The web search did not surface specific analyst concerns about it. It could be: (a) timing of advance payments from Airbus/Boeing for ramp-up, (b) Collins Aerospace acquisition consolidation effect, (c) longer payment terms extended to airlines. Without access to the 2025 annual report notes, I cannot determine which explanation is correct. This warrants PRIORITY investigation during fundamental analysis.
- **Zodiac impairment continuation:** EUR 89M in H1 2025 -- 7 years post-acquisition. Is this a trailing cleanup or a sign of persistent goodwill erosion? The total goodwill is only ~9.3% of assets (stable), so it appears manageable, but the persistence is notable.
- **OCF/Net Income ratio:** The -7.1x in 2024 is alarming on its face but driven by negative net income from Zodiac write-downs while operating cash flow remained strong (EUR 3.2B FCF). The 0.8x in 2025 is below 1.0x -- I would want to verify this is timing-related, not structural.

### Riesgos que Podrian Estar Subestimados
- **EUR/USD KO barrier risk:** I rate this MEDIUM probability, but given the Iran crisis resolution scenario (quick ceasefire -> oil drops -> Fed cuts -> EUR strengthens rapidly), the probability of EUR/USD hitting 1.21+ within 12 months could be 30-40%, not 15-20%. If the war ends quickly, this risk materializes fast.
- **Boeing dependency:** Boeing represents roughly half of CFM's narrowbody engine market. Boeing's persistent quality/production issues are structural, not cyclical. The thesis should model a scenario where Boeing remains at 38-42/month MAX production for 2+ years instead of ramping to 53/month.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
