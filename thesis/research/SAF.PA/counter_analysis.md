# Counter-Analysis: SAF.PA (Safran SA)

## Fecha: 2026-03-07

## Resumen Ejecutivo

Safran is a genuinely world-class business with one of the strongest competitive positions in any industry (jet engine duopoly). However, at EUR 317.70, the market already prices 20.9% FCF growth for 5 years -- 7.1pp above Safran's own historical FCF CAGR of 13.7%. The FA himself concludes the stock is overvalued (FV EUR 277, MoS -14.7%), and my independent bear case (EUR 215) reinforces that there is no margin of safety at current levels. Five HIGH-severity challenges -- LEAP durability issues, Rate 75 slippage, FX KO barrier risk, unfavorable asymmetry, and market-implied growth premium -- suggest the entry price should be EUR 230-240, not the FA's EUR 260.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. QS Adjustment from 73 (Tier B) to 79 (Tier A) -- the +6 Point IFRS Correction

- **FA claim:** IFRS EPS is distorted by Zodiac PPA amortization and hedging mark-to-market. Adjusted EPS CAGR is ~25% (2023-2025), justifying 10/10 for EPS growth and +6 QS points.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - The +6 adjustment crosses the Tier B/A boundary (73->79), which changes MoS requirements and deployment thresholds. This is a consequential classification change.
  - The FA cherry-picks the 2-year window (2023 EUR 4.88 to 2025 EUR 7.60 = 25% CAGR). But look at the most recent YoY: 2024 adjusted EUR 7.37 to 2025 adjusted EUR 7.60 = **+3.1% growth**. The explosive growth phase was post-COVID recovery, not sustainable run-rate.
  - Using 2022 adjusted EPS (EUR 2.76) as base inflates the CAGR because 2022 was still heavily depressed by aerospace recovery dynamics.
  - The adjustment mechanism is sound (IFRS genuinely distorts Safran), but 10/10 for EPS CAGR using a recovery-era base is aggressive.
  - Safran reports "adjusted data" as primary metric -- but this is the COMPANY'S preferred view, not independently verified. Companies naturally prefer the metric that shows them in the best light.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Accept EPS distortion argument but cap adjustment at +4 points (QS 77). The 2024->2025 deceleration to 3.1% does not support 10/10 for EPS CAGR forward-looking. QS 77 still = Tier A but with less headroom.

### 2. "10+ Year LEAP Aftermarket Tailwind" -- EUR 115-145B Lifetime Revenue

- **FA claim:** 11,600 LEAP engines in backlog x EUR 20-25M per engine over 25-30 year life = EUR 115-145B (Safran's 50% share) of future aftermarket revenue.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - **LEAP durability issues are CURRENT and material.** CFM has capped LEAP-1B HPT Stage 1 disk life at 10,000 cycles vs normal 17,500 cycles. Southwest Airlines is "concerned" about engine shortage. Ascend Airways (737 MAX operator) struggles with LEAP reliability, needing off-wing maintenance earlier than expected. Safran CEO publicly acknowledged engines "still suffering in harsh conditions."
  - **Warranty/goodwill costs suppress near-term margins.** CFM has produced 1,450+ HPT hardware kits and installed RBS on ~50% of LEAP-1A fleet -- these retrofits cost money. If RPFH contracts cap what Safran can charge, the margin on early-LEAP aftermarket work may be NEGATIVE or near-zero on retrofit/warranty items.
  - **Power-by-the-hour (RPFH) contracts cap upside.** Fixed-price agreements mean Safran bears durability risk. If shop visits are more frequent than modeled, Safran eats the cost. If they improve, revenue per engine-year drops.
  - **Airline pricing pushback is real.** IATA reports supply chain challenges cost airlines $11B in 2025, including $3.1B in additional maintenance costs. Airlines are actively lobbying for more aftermarket competition and reduced OEM dependency.
  - **The 11,600 backlog assumes delivery, not cancellation.** With Rate 75 now pushed to end-2027 at earliest, the backlog converts to installed base SLOWER than modeled.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Apply 15-20% haircut to lifetime aftermarket estimate. Use EUR 95-120B instead of EUR 115-145B. The LEAP aftermarket tailwind is real but the thesis overestimates the magnitude and underestimates near-term margin headwinds from durability fixes.

### 3. Airbus Rate 75 and LEAP Delivery Ramp

- **FA claim:** Airbus targeting Rate 75/month for A320neo by 2027, supporting 2,000+ LEAP engines in 2026 and 2,500 by 2028.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - **Rate 75 has been pushed back AGAIN.** Airbus now targets 70-75/month by "end of 2027" with stabilization "thereafter." This is a meaningful downgrade from original 2026 timing.
  - **Airbus "gliders" increased 250%.** Number of completed aircraft awaiting engines rose from 17 (Q1 2025) to 60 (Q2 2025). This is a DIRECT manifestation of CFM/Safran being a bottleneck.
  - **CFM and Airbus still "in discussions."** Safran CEO stated CFM has NO SURGE CAPACITY -- it is focused on 15% delivery growth to meet existing obligations.
  - **Airbus CEO said 2026 will be "another year of progressive ramp-up rather than a step change."** This contradicts the thesis's optimistic 2026 LEAP delivery target.
  - **Boeing MAX production remains at 38/month** despite targeting 53/month by end-2026. Boeing has persistently missed production ramp targets. 50% of CFM's narrowbody market depends on Boeing.
  - **Morocco production line for LEAP not yet operational.** New capacity targeting 2,500/year by 2028 requires successful ramp of a NEW facility in a NEW country.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Base case should model Rate 65-70 for end-2027, Rate 75 in 2029. Adjust 2028 ROI to EUR 6.0-6.5B (original pre-upgrade guidance) as the realistic base, not the raised EUR 7.0-7.5B.

### 4. EUR/USD FX Hedging KO Barrier Risk (1.21-1.30)

- **FA claim:** Thesis mentions EUR/USD in passing. The valuation scenarios do NOT model FX KO barrier breach.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - **Safran CFO explicitly warned:** "all KO barriers are within 1.21-1.30, so if the euro dollar peaks again, there is a risk that we may lose part of our hedging volume."
  - **A late-January 2026 event already triggered partial losses:** management disclosed KO breach reduced hedging volume by "under USD 1 billion."
  - **EUR/USD at 1.16 and has traded as high as 1.24 in late 2024.** The 1.21 threshold is only 5 cents away -- plausible in a scenario where Iran de-escalates, oil drops, and Fed cuts while ECB holds.
  - **The hedging book is massive: $56.4B FX options, $14-17B net annual USD exposure.** If KO barriers are breached, the impact on $16B exposure at 1.25 vs hedged 1.12 = EUR ~1.7B annual revenue reduction, or ~25-30% of recurring OI.
  - **This is NOT modeled in ANY of the FA's scenarios** (Bear EUR 210, Base EUR 285, Bull EUR 380). The bear case assumes "Airbus ramp delays + margin miss" but not FX tail risk.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** The bear case MUST include an FX scenario. If KO barriers are breached, 2028 ROI could fall to EUR 5.0-5.5B. This changes bear FV from EUR 210 to EUR 170-180.

### 5. Entry Price EUR 260-275: Will the Stock Ever Come Back?

- **FA claim:** Recommended entry EUR 260 (10.3% MoS vs EV of EUR 290), requiring ~18% decline from current EUR 317.70.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Safran's 52-week range is EUR 190.7 to EUR 350.8. EUR 260 represents the ~37th percentile of the 52-week range -- achievable but would require a significant correction.
  - **Analyst consensus PT is EUR 356** (mean), with lowest target EUR 257. The FA's EUR 260 entry sits BELOW the most bearish analyst on the street.
  - **Catalysts for pullback exist** (Q1 miss, FX move, Airbus glider crisis worsening) but timing is uncertain.
  - **The stock has been on a strong uptrend:** from EUR 190 (52wL, Mar 2025 vicinity) to EUR 317 now. The trend is against the entry thesis.
  - **The stock traded at EUR 260 as recently as ~Q4 2025.** It is not a fantasy price, but it requires a catalyst.
  - **Counter-argument from the FA's own analysis:** EUR 260 provides only 10.3% MoS vs an expected value (EUR 290) that is ITSELF optimistic given the challenges identified above. If the DA-adjusted expected value is closer to EUR 240-255, then EUR 260 provides NEGATIVE MoS.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Revise entry to EUR 230-240 to provide adequate MoS against a more conservative expected value. At EUR 240, MoS vs DA-adjusted FV (~EUR 255) = ~6%, and vs bear case (EUR 215) = buffer of only 11% -- still thin for a stock with HIGH FX and ramp-up risks.

### 6. "Defense & Rearmament" Basket Assignment

- **FA claim:** Safran fits the Defense & Rearmament basket through Equipment & Defense segment, military aircraft equipment, and defense revenue growth.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - **Safran is ~70%+ commercial aerospace.** Revenue breakdown: Propulsion (56%, overwhelmingly commercial), Equipment & Defense (33%, MIX of civil and defense), Interiors (11%, 100% commercial). Pure defense revenue is estimated at 15-20% of total.
  - **The thesis itself acknowledges this:** "Safran is PRIMARILY an aerospace company (~70%+ commercial), not a pure defense play."
  - **Within the Defense & Rearmament basket context:** BAH is a pure defense/intelligence play. If the basket thesis is about European rearmament spending, SAF.PA's defense exposure is diluted by its dominant commercial aero business. The basket risk profile is incoherent -- a war escalation that helps BAH would HURT Safran's commercial business (oil shock -> airline capex deferral).
  - **Negative correlation within basket:** In a defense crisis scenario, pure defense stocks rally while commercial aerospace underperforms. Putting SAF.PA in a "Defense" basket creates false thematic coherence.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Reclassify SAF.PA as "Aerospace" rather than "Defense." If it must be in a basket, create an "Aerospace Supply Chain" basket with different thematic drivers (air travel growth, fleet modernization) rather than defense spending.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- The 17.4% "insider ownership" cited by the FA appears to be the French state's stake (via GIMD and direct holdings), not management personal ownership. The insider tracker shows minimal individual director transaction data. State ownership provides stability but is NOT the same as CEO/CFO buying with personal capital. This distinction matters for alignment assessment.
- The EUR 115-145B aftermarket lifetime revenue estimate is difficult to independently verify. Industry sources cite ranges from $15M to $30M per engine depending on type, contract structure, and utilization. The uncertainty band is wider than the thesis acknowledges.
- I could not quantify exact warranty/goodwill costs of LEAP durability fixes. CFM producing 1,450+ HPT kits at significant cost -- this should show up in services margin but data granularity insufficient to isolate.
- Short interest data is sparse for SAF.PA (Paris-listed). SAFRY (US ADR) shows increasing short interest (+23.5% Jan, +51% Feb) but represents tiny fraction of float. Cannot determine if institutional shorts are building a position.

### Limitaciones de Este Analisis
- No access to Safran's 2025 Universal Registration Document (not yet published) for detailed receivables breakdown, hedging book KO barrier structure by maturity year, and segment-level working capital.
- Smart money data extremely limited for French stocks -- no 13F equivalent. Cannot determine who is long/short in size.
- Could not access detailed RPFH contract terms to model warranty/durability cost absorption.
- The FX KO barrier analysis relies on CFO's public warning -- the exact barrier levels per maturity year are not disclosed.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- For European companies with large USD revenue and FX hedging books (SAF.PA, ASML, Airbus), the DA should ALWAYS investigate the hedging structure. FX KO barriers are a non-obvious but material tail risk that is rarely covered in sell-side research.
- The 40% divergence between OEY and EV/EBIT methods for SAF.PA suggests the valuation framework needs a "growth-phase adjustment" for companies transitioning from recovery to steady-state growth. Consider blended approach with explicit growth-phase discounting.
- The quality_scorer.py EPS CAGR scoring is fundamentally broken for companies with large M&A accounting (Zodiac PPA, hedging mark-to-market). Consider adding an "adjusted EPS override" parameter.
