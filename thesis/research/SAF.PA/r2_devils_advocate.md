# R2 Devil's Advocate: SAF.PA (Safran SA)

## Fecha: 2026-03-08 | Session: S150

## Resumen Ejecutivo

Safran is a genuinely world-class business with one of the strongest competitive positions in any industry (jet engine duopoly). The FA correctly concludes the stock is overvalued at EUR 317.70 (FV EUR 290, MoS -9.6%). My independent research REINFORCES the overvaluation conclusion and identifies 5 HIGH-severity challenges -- LEAP durability issues imposing real warranty costs, Rate 75 slippage now confirmed by Airbus, EUR/USD KO barrier tail risk (CFO-acknowledged, already partially triggered in Jan 2026), the French corporate surtax extension risk (~EUR 500M/yr not in 2027-2028 guidance), and the misleading P/E optics. ## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. QS Adjustment from 73 (Tool, Tier B) to 79 (Adjusted, Tier A): Legitimate or Cherry-Picking?

- **FA claim:** IFRS EPS is distorted by Zodiac PPA amortization and hedging MTM. Adjusted EPS CAGR is ~25% (2023-2025), justifying 10/10 for EPS growth, yielding +6 QS points that cross the Tier B/A boundary.
- **Evidence against:**
  - The adjustment is CONSEQUENTIAL: it changes the Tier classification (B->A), which changes MoS requirements and deployment E[CAGR] thresholds.
  - The FA cherry-picks the 2-year window (2023 EUR 4.88 to 2025 EUR 7.60 = 25% CAGR). But the most recent YoY growth decelerated sharply: **2024 adjusted EUR 7.37 to 2025 adjusted EUR 7.60 = +3.1% growth**. The explosive growth phase was post-COVID recovery, not sustainable run-rate.
  - Using 2022 adjusted EPS (EUR 2.76) as base inflates the CAGR because 2022 was still a recovery year for aerospace (travel volumes not yet normalized).
  - Safran reports "adjusted data" as its preferred metric -- but this is the COMPANY'S chosen view, not independently audited. Companies naturally prefer the metric that flatters them. The "adjustments" strip out hedging losses that are REAL economic events (Safran's KO barriers CAN be breached -- see Challenge #4).
  - The EPS CAGR going forward is guided at mid-teens ROI growth with ~EUR 500M buyback annually -- EPS CAGR ~15-18%. That is strong, but the 25% CAGR used to justify 10/10 is backward-looking and decelerating.
  - **However:** the IFRS distortion IS real. Zodiac PPA amortization is non-cash and non-recurring by nature. The question is magnitude, not direction.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Accept QS adjustment but cap at +4 points (QS 77, still Tier A). The 2024->2025 deceleration to 3.1% adjusted EPS growth does NOT support 10/10 for EPS CAGR on a forward basis. Score 7/10 instead of 10/10. QS 77 provides thinner headroom above the 75 Tier A floor, which is honest.

### 2. FV EUR 290 with Entry EUR 260: Is the FV Itself Too Generous?

- **FA claim:** Expected Value EUR 290 (25% Bear EUR 210, 50% Base EUR 285, 25% Bull EUR 380). Two-method weighted average EUR 277.
- **Evidence against:**
  - **Raised 2028 guidance used as BASE case.** The FA uses EUR 7.0-7.5B ROI and EUR 21B cumulative FCF (the RAISED guidance from CMD Dec 2024) as the base. But the ORIGINAL guidance was EUR 6.0-6.5B ROI and EUR 15-17B cumulative FCF. Given Rate 75 slippage, FX risk, and LEAP durability costs, the original guidance should be the base case, with raised guidance as the bull case.
  - **OEY method produces EUR 238; EV/EBIT produces EUR 335.** The 40% divergence (EUR 238 vs EUR 335) signals high uncertainty. The FA weights 60/40 to get EUR 277, but the OEY method -- which discounts cash flows -- is more rigorous for a growth company. The EV/EBIT method's EUR 335 depends critically on the 25x multiple assumption, which is aggressive for any industrial.
  - **The 25x EV/EBIT multiple is generous.** FA justifies: sector median 18-22x + quality premium +3-4x + growth premium +2-3x = 23-27x (midpoint 25x). But aerospace/defense primes trade at 18-22x EV/EBIT historically. The quality and growth premiums are already reflected in consensus earnings estimates. Using 25x on ALREADY-ELEVATED earnings double-counts the quality.
  - **DCF tool gives EUR 158 at 8% growth / 10% WACC / 2% terminal.** Even at FA's own 13.7% historical FCF CAGR, the tool gives EUR 241. This suggests EUR 290 requires heroic assumptions.
  - **Analyst consensus PT: EUR 340-360.** Our FA's FV (EUR 290) is already well below consensus. This is normally a sign of rigor -- BUT if we're further below at EUR 255 (DA-adjusted), we need to ask: are 21 analysts wrong, or are we too bearish?
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Revise expected value to EUR 255. Use original guidance (pre-raise) as base case: ROI EUR 6.0-6.5B, cumulative FCF EUR 17-19B. Use 20x EV/EBIT (sector median, no quality premium in valuation to avoid double-counting vs earnings). Entry at EUR 230 provides 10% MoS vs EUR 255.

### 3. "12-15% Growth" Based on LEAP Aftermarket Secular Tailwind

- **FA claim:** LEAP installed base growing rapidly; aftermarket revenue tripling 2024-2028; each LEAP engine generates EUR 20-25M over 25-30 year life.
- **Evidence against:**
  - **LEAP durability issues are ACTIVE and COSTLY.** Three distinct problems identified:
    1. **HPT blade durability in harsh environments:** CFM produced 1,450+ hardware kits. Only ~50% of fleet retrofitted with RBS fix. Safran CEO publicly admitted engines "still suffering in harsh conditions."
    2. **HPT disk life limit:** CFM capped LEAP-1B Stage 1 HPT disk at 10,000 cycles vs normal 17,500. Southwest Airlines publicly "concerned" about engine shortage. Improved part not ready until H2 2026.
    3. **De-icing system flaw:** 2+ years without resolution. Delays MAX 7/10 certification into 2026+. FAA waivers are temporary.
  - **These durability issues SUPPRESS near-term aftermarket margins.** RPFH (Rate Per Flight Hour) contracts are FIXED-PRICE -- if shop visits are more frequent than modeled, Safran absorbs the cost. If warranty/goodwill retrofits are needed (1,450+ kits already), Safran pays.
  - **Airbus Rate 75 CONFIRMED DELAYED.** Airbus CEO Guillaume Faury: 2026 = "another year of progressive ramp-up rather than a step change." Rate 70-75 by "end of 2027" with stabilization "thereafter." This is a meaningful downgrade from original 2026 timing. Fewer new A320neo deliveries = slower LEAP installed base growth = delayed aftermarket inflection.
  - **"Glider" crisis at Airbus:** engineless airframes rose from 17 (Q1 2025) to 60 (Q2 2025) -- a 250% increase. CFM acknowledged no surge capacity. This is a DIRECT manifestation of LEAP supply constraints.
  - **Boeing MAX still at ~38/month** vs 53/month target. Boeing has persistently missed production ramp targets. 50% of CFM narrowbody market depends on Boeing execution.
  - **Supply chain tariff headwind:** Safran estimates EUR 100-150M tariff impact in 2025. Raw materials, forging, and casting constraints persist.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** Reduce expected growth from 12-15% to 10-12%. Model base case with Rate 65-70 at end-2027, Rate 75 in 2029. Apply 15% haircut to lifetime aftermarket estimates. The LEAP tailwind is REAL but the thesis overstates the near-term ramp pace and understates warranty/durability cost absorption.

### 4. EUR/USD KO Barrier Risk: The Unmodeled Tail Risk

- **FA claim:** EUR/USD KO barriers mentioned in kill conditions but NOT modeled in any valuation scenario (Bear EUR 210, Base EUR 285, Bull EUR 380 all assume hedging holds).
- **Evidence against:**
  - **Safran CFO EXPLICITLY warned:** "all KO barriers are within 1.21-1.30, so if the euro dollar peaks again, there is a risk that we may lose part of our hedging volume."
  - **Already triggered ONCE.** A late-January 2026 event reduced hedging volume by "under USD 1 billion" per management disclosure. This is NOT theoretical -- it has already happened.
  - **EUR/USD at 1.16, only 5 cents from the 1.21 lower KO boundary.** EUR/USD traded as high as 1.24 in late 2024. The trigger is plausible in scenarios where: Iran de-escalates quickly -> oil drops -> Fed cuts while ECB holds -> EUR strengthens.
  - **Massive exposure:** $56.4B FX options book, $14-17B net annual USD exposure. If KO barriers at 1.21-1.25 are breached on the majority of the book, and spot moves to 1.25, the impact on $16B net exposure = EUR ~1.7B annual revenue reduction vs hedged rate of 1.12. This is ~25-30% of recurring operating income.
  - **Currency impact already visible in 2025:** USD weakness cut reported revenue by 3.2% despite strong underlying growth.
  - **2026 hedging at 1.12 seems aggressive** given current spot at 1.16. If the KO barriers are breached, Safran falls from 1.12 hedged directly to 1.16-1.25 spot -- a painful step-function hit, not gradual.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** This is the single most material gap in the thesis. The bear case MUST include an FX scenario. Probability-weighted impact: ~20% chance of KO breach -> 25% of ROI reduction -> ~5% downward adjustment to expected FV. At minimum, widen the bear case to incorporate EUR/USD 1.25 scenario where ROI drops to EUR 5.0-5.5B. This changes bear FV from EUR 210 to EUR 170-180.

### 5. P/E Distortion: "P/E 18.5x" Is Misleading -- Adjusted P/E ~32x Forward Is the Reality

- **FA claim:** Acknowledges IFRS P/E is misleading but uses it for peer comparisons (vs sector 30-85x).
- **Evidence against:**
  - **Adjusted trailing P/E is ~42x** (EUR 317.70 / EUR 7.60 adjusted EPS). This is EXPENSIVE for any industrial company, let alone one with execution risks.
  - **Forward P/E on 2026E adjusted EPS (~EUR 9.50-10.00) is ~32-33x.** The FA acknowledges this but frames it as "reasonable for a duopolist growing 15% OI." However:
    - GE Aerospace trades at ~35x -- similar. But GE has 50% of the same CFM JV PLUS wide-body engines (GE9X). On a standalone basis, GE's portfolio is broader.
    - RTX (P&W parent) trades at ~28x -- CHEAPER despite also having defense exposure and engine business.
    - At 32x forward, the stock is priced for CONTINUED 15%+ earnings growth. Any deceleration (Rate 75 delay, FX headwind, durability costs) = multiple compression.
  - **Using P/E 18.5x (IFRS) in comparisons with defense stocks at 30-85x P/E is INVALID.** The defense stocks are priced on clean reported earnings, not IFRS-distorted ones. An apples-to-apples comparison requires using adjusted P/E for Safran vs adjusted P/E for peers.
  - **At 32x forward P/E for a company with:** (a) EUR/USD tail risk, (b) LEAP durability costs, (c) Rate 75 delay, (d) French surtax uncertainty -- the risk/reward is asymmetrically unfavorable.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** Committee must use adjusted P/E (~32x forward) for all comparisons. The IFRS P/E of 18.5x should NOT appear in any investment discussion without immediate clarification that it is an accounting artifact. This is material for framing -- if the committee sees "18.5x P/E," they might think it's cheap. It is NOT cheap on any relevant metric.

### 6. Defense Basket Assignment: SAF.PA Is 70-85% Civil Aerospace

- **FA claim:** Safran fits the Defense & Rearmament basket through Equipment & Defense segment and military contributions.
- **Evidence against:**
  - **Revenue decomposition:** Propulsion (56%, overwhelmingly civil -- LEAP/CFM56 are commercial engines), Equipment & Defense (33%, MIX of civil equipment + defense), Interiors (11%, 100% civil). Pure defense revenue is estimated at 15-20% of total.
  - **The thesis ITSELF acknowledges this:** "Safran is PRIMARILY an aerospace company (~70%+ commercial), not a pure defense play."
  - **Basket coherence problem:** In a defense spending surge scenario, pure defense stocks (BAH, RHM.DE) rally on contract awards. Safran benefits marginally through Equipment & Defense growth but is mainly driven by commercial aerospace cycle (air travel demand, fleet modernization, engine MRO).
  - **Negative correlation within basket:** A war escalation that benefits pure defense names would HURT Safran's dominant commercial business (oil shock -> airline capex deferral -> fewer new aircraft orders -> slower LEAP ramp). The basket's thematic coherence is diluted.
  - **However:** The FA explicitly positions SAF.PA as "diversification within the basket" with lower correlation to pure defense. This is intellectually honest but stretches the "Defense & Rearmament" theme.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Either (a) accept SAF.PA as diversifier within Defense basket but document clearly that it behaves as commercial aerospace, or (b) create an "Aerospace Supply Chain" basket with different thematic drivers. The committee should have a view.

### 7. French Corporate Surtax: EUR 500M/Year Headwind NOT in 2027-2028 Guidance

- **FA claim:** Not addressed in thesis.
- **Evidence against:**
  - **Safran management explicitly stated:** 2027-2028 guidance does NOT include potential continuation of the French corporate surtax, which could be "EUR 500 million for each year."
  - **2026 impact is concrete:** EUR 470M negative impact on FCF already included in 2026 guidance (EUR 4.4-4.6B FCF).
  - **2025 impact:** EUR 370-377M.
  - **If surtax continues through 2028:** Cumulative EUR 21B FCF target (2024-2028) becomes EUR ~20B. Annualized: EUR 4.0B/yr instead of EUR 4.2B/yr.
  - **French fiscal policy is unpredictable.** The surtax was introduced as "temporary" but has been extended. The French government faces persistent fiscal deficits.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Base case should assume 50% probability of surtax continuation in 2027-2028. Impact: reduce cumulative FCF by EUR 500M (50% * EUR 1B). Adjusted 2028 ROI should reflect EUR 250M/yr headwind probability-weighted.

State ownership provides stability and strategic alignment but is NOT equivalent to CEO/CFO buying with personal capital. This distinction matters for skin-in-the-game assessment. Could not find data on individual director holdings beyond ESOP.
- The EUR 115-145B lifetime aftermarket revenue estimate is extraordinarily large and difficult to independently verify. Industry sources cite ranges from $15M to $30M per engine depending on type, contract, and utilization. The uncertainty band is wider than the thesis acknowledges. The magnitude is plausible but treating it as a point estimate rather than a wide range is analytically dishonest.
- I could not precisely quantify LEAP durability warranty costs. The 1,450+ HPT kits, RBS retrofits, and disk replacements cost real money -- but CFM's disclosure is limited. The impact shows up in services margin but data granularity is insufficient to isolate. This is an important gap.

If contracts have escalation clauses for above-normal maintenance, the impact is smaller. Cannot determine which structure dominates.

- CJ-1000A "exceeding expectations in flight testing" (per SCMP) is Chinese state media -- inherently unreliable as a source. But the US export control precedent (May-Jul 2025 suspension) is independently verifiable and real.
