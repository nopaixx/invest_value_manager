## Business Understanding

### 1. What Safran Does

Safran is a French aerospace and defense company operating through three divisions:

**1. Propulsion (56% of revenue, ~22-24% operating margin target)**
- CFM International: 50/50 JV with GE Aerospace (extended to 2050). Produces CFM56 (legacy, ~23,000 installed base) and LEAP engines (next-gen, 11,600+ backlog)
- LEAP-1A: powers Airbus A320neo (~75% win rate vs PW GTF -- IMPROVED from ~60% due to GTF reliability issues)
- LEAP-1B: SOLE engine for Boeing 737 MAX (100% share -- P&W GTF physically cannot fit)
- LEAP-1C: SOLE engine for COMAC C919
- Helicopter engines via Safran Helicopter Engines (#1 global)
- **The Razor/Blade Model**: Engines sold at cost or small margin. Aftermarket (MRO, spare parts) is the profit driver -- 60-70% of lifetime engine revenue comes from aftermarket over 25-30 year engine life
- FY2025: 1,802 LEAP engines delivered (+28% YoY), civil spare parts revenue +17.6% in USD terms, service revenue for civil engines grew +30%

**2. Equipment & Defense (33% of revenue, mid-teens margin target by 2028)**
- Landing gear (#1 global -- Messier-Bugatti-Dowty)
- Nacelles (#1 global for A320neo family)
- Wiring systems, avionics, optronics
- Defense: navigation systems (AASM smart bombs), drones, soldier equipment, M88 engines for Rafale
- H1 2025: ROI margin 12.5%, organic revenue +8%

**3. Aircraft Interiors (11% of revenue, high single digits margin target by 2028)**
- Seats (former Zodiac Aerospace, acquired 2018)
- Cabin systems, galleys, lavatories
- Turnaround ongoing -- positive ROI EUR 27M in H1 2025 (vs EUR 10M H1 2024)
- Business class seat deliveries surging: 1,238 units in H1 2025 vs 750 in H1 2024

### 2. Revenue Model and Unit Economics

**Installed Base Annuity Model:**
- ~23,000 CFM56 engines in service (aging fleet, high-margin MRO, shop visit wave PEAKING 2025-2028)
- ~6,000+ LEAP engines in service and growing rapidly (2,000+ delivered in 2025)
- Each LEAP engine generates ~EUR 20-25M over its 25-30 year life in aftermarket revenue
- 11,600 LEAP engines in backlog = decades of captive aftermarket revenue
- 99.95% departure reliability rate for LEAP -- best in class

**Revenue Characteristics:**
- ~45% services/aftermarket (highly recurring, high margin, grows as fleet ages)
- ~55% OE (lower margin, drives future aftermarket -- the "razor")
- Contract lengths: 20-30 years for engine programs
- RPFH (rate-per-flight-hour) contracts converting lumpy MRO into smooth recurring revenue

**FCF Conversion:**
- FCF margin: 12.6% (2025), guided to improve
- Cumulative FCF 2024-2028: ~EUR 21B (RAISED from EUR 15-17B)
- EUR 5B share buyback program announced
- SBC/Revenue: 0.3% -- negligible dilution

### 3. The Jet Engine Duopoly -- Why This Moat is Nearly Impregnable

| Engine | Aircraft | Competitor | Safran/CFM Share |
|--------|----------|-----------|------------------|
| LEAP-1A | Airbus A320neo | PW GTF (RTX) | ~75% win rate (up from ~60% due to GTF issues) |
| LEAP-1B | Boeing 737 MAX | None -- EXCLUSIVE | 100% |
| LEAP-1C | COMAC C919 | None -- EXCLUSIVE | 100% |
| CFM56 | Legacy 737/A320 | V2500 (RTX) | ~75% installed base |

Why this is one of the strongest competitive positions in ANY industry:
- **Barriers to entry: insurmountable.** Developing a new jet engine takes 15-20 years and billions in R&D. The last new entrant to narrow-body engines was... never. P&W's GTF was a variant by an existing player.
- **Switching costs: infinite.** Once an aircraft is designed around an engine, there is no switching. A 737 MAX can ONLY use LEAP-1B engines for its entire 25-30 year life.
- **Captive aftermarket:** Airlines MUST use OEM-certified parts for safety-critical engine components. There are no "generic" alternatives for turbine blades or combustion chambers.
- **GTF competitive dynamics:** Pratt & Whitney's GTF has been plagued by powder metal contamination issues, driving airlines toward LEAP. LEAP's A320neo market share has increased from ~60% to ~75%.

### 4. EPS Volatility Explained (ACCOUNTING NOISE, NOT BUSINESS WEAKNESS)

Safran reports negative GAAP EPS in certain years (2022: -EUR 5.76, 2024: -EUR 1.60) despite consistently positive and growing FCF (EUR 2.7B -> 3.9B). The cause:
- IFRS requires marking FX hedging derivatives to fair value through P&L
- In 2024: EUR 4.67B of unrealized hedging losses hit the P&L
- These are PAPER losses on USD hedges that will settle over future years at the hedged rate
- Recurring operating income (the TRUE earnings measure) grew consistently: EUR 2.7B -> 3.6B -> 4.1B -> 5.2B

**NEVER use P/E for Safran. Use EV/ROI, EV/FCF, or FCF yield.**

### 5. Why It Looks "Cheap" at P/E 16.5x

The P/E 16.5x is an IFRS artifact. On adjusted metrics:

Premium but justified by duopoly moat with decades of captive aftermarket revenue.
