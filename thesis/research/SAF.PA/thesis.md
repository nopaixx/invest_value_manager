## Business Understanding

### 1. What Safran Does

Safran is a French aerospace and defense company operating through three divisions:

**1. Propulsion (56% of revenue, ~22-24% operating margin target)**
- CFM International: 50/50 JV with GE Aerospace. Produces CFM56 (legacy, ~22,000 installed base) and LEAP engines (next-gen, 11,600+ backlog)
- LEAP-1A: powers Airbus A320neo (~60% win rate vs PW GTF)
- LEAP-1B: SOLE engine for Boeing 737 MAX (100% share)
- LEAP-1C: SOLE engine for COMAC C919
- Helicopter engines via Safran Helicopter Engines (#1 global)
- **The Razor/Blade Model**: Engines sold at cost or small margin. Aftermarket (MRO, spare parts) is the profit driver -- 60-70% of lifetime engine revenue comes from aftermarket over 25-30 year engine life

**2. Equipment & Defense (33% of revenue, mid-teens margin target by 2028)**
- Landing gear (#1 global -- Messier-Bugatti-Dowty)
- Nacelles (#1 global for A320neo family)
- Wiring systems, avionics, optronics
- Defense: navigation systems (AASM smart bombs), drones, soldier equipment
- Benefits from Airbus/Boeing ramp-up AND defense spending surge

**3. Aircraft Interiors (11% of revenue, high single digits margin target by 2028)**
- Seats (former Zodiac Aerospace, acquired 2018)
- Cabin systems, galleys, lavatories
- Historically troubled (Zodiac integration issues), now improving
- Lowest margin division, turnaround ongoing

### 2. Revenue Model and Unit Economics

**Installed Base Annuity Model:**
- ~22,000 CFM56 engines in service (aging fleet, high-margin MRO)
- ~6,000+ LEAP engines in service and growing rapidly (2,000+ delivered in 2025)
- Each LEAP engine generates ~EUR 20-25M over its 25-30 year life in aftermarket revenue
- 11,600 LEAP engines in backlog = EUR 230-290B of future lifetime aftermarket revenue (Safran's 50% share = EUR 115-145B)

**Revenue Characteristics:**
- ~45% services/aftermarket (highly recurring, high margin)
- ~55% OE (original equipment, lower margin, drives future aftermarket)
- Contract lengths: 20-30 years for engine programs
- Revenue visibility: exceptional (aircraft fly for decades)

**FCF Conversion:**
- FCF margin: 12-13% trailing, guided to improve
- EBIT-to-FCF ratio: ~70% (2028 target per CMD'24)
- Cumulative FCF 2024-2028: ~EUR 21B (RAISED from EUR 15-17B)
- EUR 5B share buyback program announced

### 3. Competitive Position -- The Jet Engine Duopoly

The narrowbody jet engine market is effectively a DUOPOLY:

| Engine | Aircraft | Competitor | Safran/CFM Share |
|--------|----------|-----------|------------------|
| LEAP-1A | Airbus A320neo | PW GTF (RTX) | ~60% win rate |
| LEAP-1B | Boeing 737 MAX | None -- EXCLUSIVE | 100% |
| LEAP-1C | COMAC C919 | None -- EXCLUSIVE | 100% |
| CFM56 | Legacy 737/A320 | V2500 (RTX) | ~75% installed base |

This is one of the strongest competitive positions in ANY industry:
- Barriers to entry are essentially insurmountable (decades of R&D, certification, relationships)
- Switching costs are infinite once an aircraft is designed around an engine
- Aftermarket is captive -- airlines MUST use OEM-approved parts and maintenance
- The next narrowbody engine program (RISE/CFM) is 2035+ -- no new entrants possible

### 4. Why It Looks "Cheap" at P/E 18.5x

**Market narrative:** Safran is "aerospace, not pure defense" -- in the current defense frenzy (30-85x P/E for EU defense), aerospace names get less attention. Also:
- IFRS EPS distortion confuses screeners (negative EPS in 2022/2024)
- P/E 18.5x is based on IFRS EPS, not adjusted EPS
- On adjusted EPS (EUR 7.60), P/E = EUR 317.70 / EUR 7.60 = 41.8x -- still high vs history but reasonable for a duopolist growing ROI at 26% CAGR
- On 2026E adjusted EPS (~EUR 9.50-10.00 implied by ROI EUR 6.1-6.2B guidance), forward P/E = ~32-33x

**My contra-thesis:**
The "cheap" P/E 18.5x is an IFRS artifact. On adjusted metrics, Safran trades at ~42x trailing / ~32x forward -- PREMIUM but justified by:
1. Duopoly moat in the world's most essential transportation product
2. 26% ROI CAGR with management guiding continued mid-teens growth to 2028
3. 11,600 LEAP engine backlog = decades of captive aftermarket revenue
4. Net cash balance sheet (EUR 1.5B net cash)
5. 17.4% insider ownership (primarily French state via GIMD)

**Value Trap Checklist: 0/10** -- Safran has NONE of the value trap indicators.

### 5. Receivables Flag Investigation

Receivables growth 36.4% vs Revenue growth 12.5% (flagged by narrative_checker.py).

- Favorable working capital change of EUR 1,070M in FY2025 (advance payments from Rafale and OE contracts)
- This is typical for aerospace OEMs during production ramp periods
- **Monitor but NOT a concern** -- will normalize as Airbus reaches Rate 75

## Valuation

### Method 1: Owner Earnings Yield (Primary for Tier A, 60% weight)

```
FCF FY2025: EUR 3.92B
Depreciation: EUR 1.73B (estimated from capex/depreciation ratio)
Maintenance Capex: EUR 1.73B * 1.1 = EUR 1.90B
Owner Earnings: EUR 3.92B - EUR 1.90B + EUR 1.73B = EUR 3.75B

Market Cap: EUR 132.3B
OE Yield: EUR 3.75B / EUR 132.3B = 2.8%

Expected Growth (ROI CAGR): ~15% (mid-teens management guidance to 2028)
Sustainable long-term growth post-2028: ~8-10% (aftermarket compounds)

OE Yield + Growth = 2.8% + 15% = 17.8% (next 3 years)
OE Yield + Growth = 2.8% + 8% = 10.8% (long-term post-2028)
vs WACC: 9.3%

Near-term spread: +8.5pp (attractive)
Long-term spread: +1.5pp (modest but positive)
```

**OEY-derived Fair Value:**
Using a 10-year horizon blending near-term high growth with long-term moderation:
- FY2026E FCF: EUR 4.5B (midpoint guidance)
- FY2028E FCF: EUR 5.5-6.0B (implied by cumulative EUR 21B, 2024-2028)
- Terminal FCF (2028): EUR 5.75B at midpoint
- Terminal value at WACC 9.3%, g 2.5%: EUR 5.75B / (9.3% - 2.5%) = EUR 84.6B
- NPV of interim FCFs (2026-2028): ~EUR 13B
- Total EV: ~EUR 97.6B
- Plus net cash EUR 1.5B: Equity = EUR 99.1B
- Shares: ~417M
- **OEY Fair Value: EUR 238 per share**

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The OEY method produced EUR 238 while EV/EBIT produced EUR 335 -- significant divergence (40%). This reflects the fundamental uncertainty: the OEY/DCF approach discounts the high growth phase heavily (WACC 9.3%), while multiples capture peer comparisons better. The weighted average (EUR 277) is my best estimate but has wide uncertainty bands.
- The IFRS vs adjusted EPS gap is MASSIVE (~EUR 8-9 per share in 2022/2024). While the adjustment is well-justified, investors who rely on screener data (IFRS EPS) may not see the true earnings power -- this could be a source of edge OR a source of permanent discount.
- Receivables growth 36.4% vs revenue 12.5% -- I assessed this as ramp-related and structural, but it bears monitoring. If receivables continue growing 3x faster than revenue beyond 2027, it could signal collection issues or aggressive revenue recognition.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The quality_scorer.py should have a way to accept "adjusted EPS" as override when IFRS EPS is demonstrably distorted by PPA amortization. Safran is not the only company with this issue (any major acquirer will have it).
- The DCF tool's base case (EUR 169) is significantly below any reasonable estimate, suggesting the tool may not handle high-growth-transitioning-to-moderate-growth companies well. The sensitivity note (TV 74.5%, spread 75%) correctly flags unreliability.

### Anomalias Detectadas
- The tool reports EBIT margin as 33.4% in the reverse DCF -- this seems too high and may be conflating operating income with EBIT after adding back D&A. Safran's recurring OI margin is 16.6% for FY2025. The tool may be using a non-standard EBIT definition.
- OCF/Net Income ratio of -7.1x in 2024 and -1.4x in 2022 -- these extreme values are artifacts of the IFRS net income distortion, not cash flow problems. OCF itself is stable and growing.
