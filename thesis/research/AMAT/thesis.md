## TL;DR

Applied Materials is the world's largest semiconductor equipment company ($28.4B FY2025 revenue), supplying etch, deposition, and process control tools to every major chipmaker. The company has exceptional financial quality (ROIC 34%, net cash, 49% gross margin) but is trading at $357, a price that implies 31.6% FCF growth vs historical 3.2% revenue CAGR. The disconnect between the market's implied expectations and AMAT's actual growth profile makes this an OVERVALUED stock despite high quality. Management guides 20%+ semi systems growth in CY2026, but this is a cyclical uplift from the AI capex boom, not a permanent re-rating of growth. The Reverse DCF asymmetry ratio of 0.47x (unfavorable) confirms the risk-reward is poor at current prices.

## Business Understanding

### Model of Business

**Problem solved:** AMAT makes the machines that make semiconductor chips. Every new fab requires billions of dollars of AMAT equipment. As chips get more complex (more transistor layers, new materials, advanced packaging), AMAT's tools become more critical and higher-value.

**Revenue model:**

| Revenue Type | Description | % of Revenue | Characteristics |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Semiconductor Systems | Etch, deposition, CMP, implant, inspection tools | ~74% | Cyclical but trending up; tied to fab capex cycles |
| Applied Global Services | Spares, service contracts, upgrades | ~22% | Recurring, high-margin, growing as installed base expands |
| Display & Adjacent | Display manufacturing equipment | ~4% | Declining, being deprioritized |

**Unit economics:** Each new leading-edge fab requires $2-5B of AMAT equipment (out of $20-40B total fab cost). AMAT's tools are embedded in the manufacturing process -- switching costs are extreme (qualification takes 6-12 months, yield impact). The installed base of >50,000 tools generates recurring AGS revenue.

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Market Share (WFE) | Positioning |
|------------|-------------|-------------|
| Applied Materials | ~24% | #1 overall, broadest portfolio |
| ASML | ~22% | Monopoly in EUV lithography |
| Tokyo Electron (TEL) | ~16% | Strong in etch, deposition (Japan) |
| Lam Research (LRCX) | ~14% | #1 in etch, strong in deposition |
| KLA (KLAC) | ~8% | #1 in process control/inspection |

### Moat Assessment

**Type:** Switching Costs (tool qualification, yield optimization) + Oligopoly Market Structure (top 5 control 85%+ of WFE) + Technology IP (>16,000 patents) + Installed Base (50,000+ tools generating recurring revenue)

**Durability: WIDE** -- Semiconductor equipment is a natural oligopoly. The barriers to entry are enormous: $2-3B R&D/year, decades of process know-how, customer qualification cycles of 12-18 months. No new entrant has broken into the top 5 in 30+ years. China's efforts to build domestic equipment (AMEC, Naura) are limited to mature nodes and at least a decade behind on leading-edge. AMAT's moat is defended by R&D scale ($3.6B/year, +12.6% of revenue, increasing).

## Why It Is Cheap

1. **AI capex boom = multi-year supercycle for equipment:** Hyperscaler capex $660-690B in 2026 flows down to fabs which flows down to equipment orders.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- **Am I being too bearish on AMAT's growth re-rating?** Management guides 20%+ systems growth in CY2026 and the AI TAM may indeed be larger than historical semi cycles. If WFE grows from $130B to $200B+ over 5 years (not impossible given AI infrastructure needs), AMAT's normalized earnings would be much higher than my $8.5B estimate.
- **The DCF tool's default 5% growth is clearly wrong for AMAT forward-looking.** But my 12% estimate is also uncertain. The true normalized growth rate for AMAT in an AI-driven world is genuinely unknown.
- **FCF margin declined from 28-29% to 20% in FY2025.** This may be temporary (inventory build, capacity expansion) or structural (higher R&D + SBC). Need to monitor.
- **Deferred revenue declining -9.9% is a yellow flag** but could be driven by shift from advance payments to milestone-based billing. Not necessarily demand weakness.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- For cyclical equipment companies, the valuation framework should weight EV/EBIT at normalized (mid-cycle) earnings MORE heavily than DCF. DCF is particularly unreliable for cyclicals because the terminal value assumption (constant growth forever) does not match the reality of boom-bust cycles.
- The quality_scorer should have industry-specific GM benchmarks rather than broad "Technology" sector. AMAT scoring 0/10 on GM premium because it's compared to software companies is misleading.
