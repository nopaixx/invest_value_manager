## Sector Context: Online Gambling / iGaming B2B

**TAM:** The global online gambling market was USD 78.7B in 2024, projected to reach USD 153.6B by 2030 (11.9% CAGR). The online casino subsegment (most relevant to Evolution) was USD 19.1B in 2024, projected USD 38.0B by 2030 (12.2% CAGR). Live casino is the fastest-growing format within online casino, driven by demand for immersive real-dealer experiences.

**Competitive landscape:** Evolution holds ~45% of global live casino revenue, with up to 60-80% in Europe and ~90% in regulated US states. Playtech (~30% share) is the only credible competitor at scale, focusing on branded partnerships (MGM, bet365). Pragmatic Play (~25%) targets developing markets with lean studios and lower costs. Evolution launched 374 new titles in 2024 vs Playtech's 166 and Pragmatic's 140 -- a 2x content advantage. Smaller studios (OnAir, BetGames, Ezugi) serve niches but lack scale. The moat is built on: (1) network effects (operators need Evolution because players demand it), (2) scale economies in studio operations, (3) regulatory licenses across 20+ jurisdictions, (4) technology platform (API integration costs create switching friction), and (5) content library depth.

**Regulatory environment:** This is the sector's defining risk. Globally, regulators are tightening oversight and increasingly holding B2B suppliers (not just operators) responsible for unregulated activity. The UKGC's Section 116 review of Evolution (Dec 2024) is emblematic -- it investigates whether Evolution's games appeared on unlicensed UK-facing sites. Europe's "channelization" problem is structural: in countries with low channelization (50% in some markets), half the players gamble on unlicensed sites that Evolution now refuses to serve. The trend is toward more regulation globally, which long-term benefits compliant leaders like Evolution but creates short-term revenue headwinds as gray markets shrink. The US is the secular growth opportunity: only 8 states have legalized iGaming (NJ, PA, MI, CT, DE, WV, RI, ME), with Illinois likely next (2026). Evolution has ~90% share in regulated US states.

**ESG headwind:** Many institutional funds exclude gambling stocks from ESG mandates, creating a structural discount. This limits buyer base but is unlikely to change.

## Business Understanding

### Model

Evolution provides B2B live casino and RNG (random number generator) games to online gambling operators globally. The model: operators integrate Evolution's API and pay ~10% of Gross Gaming Revenue (GGR) as a revenue share. Evolution handles studios (physical locations with real dealers streaming games), technology, licensing, and game development. Live casino is 86% of revenue; RNG (NetEnt, Red Tiger brands from 2020 acquisition) is 14%.

**Why this is a great business model:**
- Revenue share = automatic alignment with operator success
- Capital-light relative to land-based casinos (studios, not buildings)
- High switching costs (deep API integration, operator reluctance to switch mid-platform)
- Network effects (players demand Evolution; operators must offer it)
- Regulatory moat (licenses in 20+ jurisdictions take years to obtain)

### Unit Economics

- **FCF margin 54.2%** (FY2025) -- extraordinary for any business
- **ROIC 30.9%** with WACC 8.4% = +22.5pp spread
- **Capex/Depreciation 0.8x** -- maintenance capex is minimal, growth capex is studio buildouts
- **OCF/Net Income 1.2x** -- earnings quality is excellent
- **Net cash SEK 727M** -- zero financial risk

The primary cost is personnel (dealers, studio staff). This creates operating leverage on the upside but margin compression when revenue stalls (as seen in FY2025: profit per table fell -19% YoY).

## Why Is It Cheap? (THE Critical Section)

**P/E 10.8x on a 31% ROIC, 54% FCF margin, net-cash, #1 market position business is DEEPLY anomalous.** The market is pricing in one or more of:

### 1. Revenue growth has permanently stalled (MOST IMPORTANT)

**Market narrative:** FY2025 was flat. Live casino revenue has plateaued at EUR 430-460M/quarter since early 2024. This is not a blip -- it is 8 consecutive quarters of stagnation. The business has hit structural saturation.

**My contra-thesis:** The stagnation is driven by two identifiable, partially transient headwinds:
- **Europe ring-fencing (Q1-Q4 2025):** Evolution voluntarily exited unregulated European markets, implementing geolocation blocking. This was a one-time compliance adjustment that reduced Europe revenue by ~8% in Q4. The adjustment is largely complete -- future quarters compare against an already-reduced base.
- **Asia cybercrime (2025):** Continuous cyberattacks disrupted Asian operations. Q4 showed modest recovery. Management says resolution uncertain but trend improving.

Meanwhile, Americas growth hit record highs. US has only 8 states with legal iGaming. Each new state (Illinois likely 2026, NY/MA under consideration) adds meaningful revenue. The US runway is 5-10 years.

**Probability market is right:** 30%. Revenue may not return to 15%+ growth, but sustained 0% seems overly bearish given US/LatAm expansion.

### 2. Operating margin is structurally declining

**Market narrative:** Op margin fell from 63.5% (2023) to 58.3% (2025). Profit per table -19% YoY. The studio-heavy model has negative operating leverage when revenue stalls.

**My contra-thesis:** The margin decline is predominantly driven by (a) investment in new studios for US/LatAm expansion (structurally higher cost per table in regulated markets requiring local presence), and (b) compliance costs from ring-fencing implementation. Management guides 66% EBITDA margin for 2026 (vs 66.1% in 2025). The margin floor appears to be ~58-60% operating, which is still extraordinary.

**Probability market is right:** 25%. Margins may not return to 63%+, but 58-60% on a growing revenue base is still elite.

### 3. UKGC investigation could revoke UK license

**Market narrative:** Section 116 review since Dec 2024. If UK license revoked, it signals to other jurisdictions. Domino risk.

**My contra-thesis:** UK is only 3% of revenue. UKGC investigations typically result in fines and conditions, not license revocation. Evolution has cooperated fully and already implemented ring-fencing. The investigation is about historical gray market exposure, which Evolution has already addressed. Even Redburn Atlantic (bearish report) acknowledged revocation is unlikely.

**Probability market is right:** 10% for revocation, 40% for fine + conditions (manageable).

### 4. ESG exclusion limits buyer base

**Market narrative:** Many ESG-mandated funds cannot hold gambling stocks. This permanently caps demand.

**My contra-thesis:** This is real and structural but already reflected in the P/E. It creates a permanent discount but does not impair business value.

### 5. Gray market exposure / reputational damage

**Market narrative:** Short sellers have alleged Evolution profits from unregulated markets. Only 47% of player activity in Q4 2025 was in regulated markets. The business model depends on gray markets.

**My contra-thesis:** Evolution is proactively ring-fencing. The 47% figure reflects a global market where many jurisdictions simply lack regulation (not that Evolution is breaking rules). As more markets regulate, Evolution benefits as the compliant leader. The transition pain is the current headwind.
