## Section 1: Business Understanding

### The Two-Minute Test

Halozyme owns a proprietary enzyme (rHuPH20) that enables subcutaneous (under-the-skin) delivery of drugs that traditionally required intravenous infusion. They license this technology (branded ENHANZE) to pharma companies who incorporate it into their drugs. When those partner drugs sell, Halozyme collects mid-single-digit royalties -- forever, as long as the drug sells. This creates a recurring, high-margin revenue stream that scales without proportional cost increases. It is essentially a "toll booth" on blockbuster biologic drugs.

### Unit Economics

This is the critical insight: royalties have NEAR-ZERO marginal cost.

- **Royalty revenue cost:** Essentially zero incremental cost per dollar of royalty
- **FCF Margin:** 46.1% (FY2024), guided >70% of EBITDA going forward
- **R&D/Revenue:** 7.8% (declining as royalty mix grows)

- **LTV/CAC:** Not applicable in traditional sense. Each partner deal = upfront milestone ($30-80M) + decades of royalty streams worth $500M-$2B+ each.

## Section 2: Moat Assessment

### Moat Type: WIDE -- Platform Monopoly + Patent Estate + Switching Costs

**Confidence: 80%**

**1. Patent Estate (130+ patents globally)**
- Core rHuPH20 enzyme composition-of-matter: through 2027-2029
- MDASE patents (anti-Merck): through 2030s
- Hypercon IP: through mid-2040s
- Surf Bio IP: through mid-2040s
- European patent (ENHANZE): validated in 37 EU countries, expires 2029

**2. Switching Costs (EXTREME)**
- Once a pharma partner formulates their drug with rHuPH20, switching requires complete reformulation + new clinical trials + new FDA/EMA approval = 5-8 years and $500M+
- DARZALEX SC, Phesgo, VYVGART Hytrulo are ALL already approved and commercialized. These royalties are locked in for the product lifecycle.
- The 97% US market share for DARZALEX SC means patients and physicians have switched AWAY from IV. They are not going back.

**3. Network Effects (Moderate)**
- Each successful partnership validates the platform, attracting more partners
- Roche, J&J, Pfizer, AbbVie, Lilly, BMS = the "who's who" of pharma = social proof for new partners (Takeda, Skye Bio, Merus signed in 2025)
- 15 programs in development by end of 2026 = expanding pipeline

**4. Regulatory Moat**
- rHuPH20 is the ONLY approved recombinant hyaluronidase for SC drug delivery
- Any competitor must demonstrate bioequivalence or develop novel enzymes (Merck/Alteogen trying this)
- FDA approval timelines for biologics = 10+ years

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas

1. **Merck litigation outcome is genuinely binary.** I cannot assess the probability of PTAB invalidation with high confidence. Patent law outcomes are notoriously unpredictable. The 35% loss probability I assigned is based on the general observation that PTAB invalids patents ~30-40% of the time, not on deep legal analysis.

2. **ENHANZE contract terms opacity.** I could not determine from public sources whether partner royalty contracts terminate on patent expiry or on product commercial lifecycle. Management claims royalties survive patent expiry, but I have not seen the specific contract language. This is CRITICAL -- if royalties terminate on patent expiry (2027-2029), the thesis fundamentally changes.

3. **Surf Bio/Hypercon acquisition costs unknown.** The specific deal terms were not disclosed in search results. If these acquisitions cost $500M-$1B+, the balance sheet impact matters more than the current 2.3x ND/EBITDA suggests.

4. **Revenue and FCF figures from quality_scorer.py are based on FY2024 data** ($1.015B revenue), but the FY2025 results ($1.397B) and 2026 guidance ($1.71-1.81B) represent a dramatically different picture. My valuation uses the more current data from earnings releases, which I validated against multiple sources.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema

1. **quality_scorer.py should have a mechanism to input forward-year data** when earnings have been recently reported. The lag between yfinance data and actual reported results creates significant distortion for fast-growing companies.

2. **The DCF tool's default scenario produces unreliable results for high-growth companies** transitioning from one revenue level to a much higher one. The $52 base case is misleading when 2026 guided FCF is ~$850M vs the trailing $468M used as input.

3. **Patent risk analysis tool needed.** For companies where patents are central to the thesis (pharma, tech IP), a structured patent risk assessment framework would be valuable.
