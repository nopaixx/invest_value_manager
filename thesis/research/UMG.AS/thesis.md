## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

Universal Music Group is the world's largest music company. It operates across three segments:

1. **Recorded Music (75.6% of revenue, EUR 9,456M):** Owns and licenses music recordings. Revenue comes from streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, TikTok), physical sales, downloads, and synchronization licensing (TV/film/ads). This segment owns the MASTER RECORDINGS -- the actual sound recordings. The catalog includes Beatles, Rolling Stones, Taylor Swift, Drake, Billie Eilish, Bad Bunny, and thousands more. These are irreplaceable, infinite-duration assets.

2. **Music Publishing (18.1%, EUR 2,260M):** Owns and administers song compositions (lyrics + melody). Revenue from mechanical royalties, performance royalties, synchronization fees, and digital licensing. Margin: 24.3% EBITDA. The publishing catalog includes songs by Adele, Elton John, Post Malone, and others. Publishing rights are SEPARATE from master recordings and generate revenue independently.

3. **Merchandising & Other (6.5%, EUR 811M):** Artist merchandise, brand licensing, live events. Low margin (2.0% EBITDA) and declining (-3.7% YoY). This is the weakest segment.

**Revenue Model:** Highly recurring. Streaming is ~70%+ of recorded music revenue. Once a song is on Spotify, it generates royalties every time it's played, with near-zero marginal cost. The catalog is a compounding royalty stream.

**The Oligopoly:** UMG (~30%), Sony Music (~25%), Warner Music (~15%) collectively control ~70% of global recorded music. This is a natural oligopoly because:
- The cost of acquiring/developing superstar artists is enormous
- Catalog accumulation over 100+ years creates an insurmountable library
- Distribution relationships with 100+ platforms globally require massive scale
- No new entrant has broken into the top 3 in decades

### Unit Economics
- Revenue per subscriber (Spotify model): Streaming platforms pay labels ~70% of subscription revenue
- CAC for new artists: High and uncertain (A&R spending EUR ~1.5B+/yr)
- LTV of a hit artist: Enormous -- catalog generates revenue for decades (Beatles still generating hundreds of millions annually, 60+ years after recording)
- Capital intensity: Low for catalog exploitation; High for new artist development (A&R risk)

### Margin Structure
```
Gross Margin:     44.4% (2022) -> 44.1% (2023) -> 43.0% (2024) -> 42.5% (2025) -- DECLINING
Operating Margin: 15.6% (2022) -> 12.7% (2023) -> 15.0% (2024) -> 16.0% (2025) -- IMPROVING
EBITDA Margin:    22.5% (FY2025, flat YoY)
FCF Margin:       12.4% (2022) -> 14.3% (2023) -> 11.0% (2024) -> 9.6% (2025) -- DECLINING
```

The gross margin decline (2pp over 3 years) is concerning and reflects: (a) rising artist royalty rates as streaming matures and artists gain leverage, (b) merchandising drag (negative EBITDA contribution), (c) higher content/A&R costs. Operating margin improvement is driven by cost savings program (EUR 40-50M incremental savings in 2026).

## Projections

### Revenue Projection

**TAM:** Global recorded music market ~$30B (2025), growing ~7-9% annually. Music publishing ~$8B, growing ~8-10%.

**Market Share:** UMG holds ~30%. Stable oligopoly -- no meaningful share shifts in years.

**Pricing Power:** MODERATE. Streaming platforms are raising prices (Spotify +$1-2/month in 2024-2025). Labels capture ~70% of subscription revenue. But ARTIST negotiating power is increasing (higher royalty splits compress label margins).

**Revenue Growth Derivation:**
- TAM growth: +7% (streaming subscriber growth + ARPU increases + emerging markets)
- Market share change: 0% (stable oligopoly)
- Pricing/mix: +1% (net of ARPU increases minus artist share compression)
- = CC revenue growth: ~8% (consistent with FY2025 actual 8.7% CC)
- FX headwind: -3 to -5% (2026 guidance)
- = Reported growth: ~3-5% (2026), normalizing to 6-8% from 2027+

### Margin Projection

- Gross margin: Expect continued gradual compression to ~41-42% by 2027 (artist leverage increasing)
- Operating margin: Improvement to 17-18% by 2027 driven by cost savings (EUR 75-95M cumulative 2026-2027) and operating leverage on fixed costs
- EBITDA margin: Stable to slight improvement from 22.5% to 23-24% by 2027
- FCF margin: Recovery from 9.6% (depressed by catalog acquisitions) toward 10-11% normalized

### WACC Derivation
- Risk-free rate: 2.8% (10Y EUR government bond)
- Equity risk premium: 5.0%
- Beta: 1.05 (media sector, moderate cyclicality)
- Ke = 2.8% + 1.05 x 5.0% = 8.05%
- Kd after tax: ~3.5% (investment grade, 0.9x leverage)
- Capital structure: 92% equity / 8% debt (low leverage)
- **WACC = 0.92 x 8.05% + 0.08 x 3.5% = 7.7%** -- round to 8.0% for conservatism

### Terminal Growth: 2.5% (music industry has clear secular tailwind from streaming penetration in emerging markets, but capped at GDP-level long term)

## Kill Conditions

1. **Gross margin drops below 40%** -- would signal structural loss of pricing power vs artists/platforms
2. **Subscription revenue declines in constant currency for 2 consecutive quarters** -- would break secular growth thesis
3. **AI-generated music exceeds 5% of actual streaming listens** (currently <0.5%) -- would signal real demand cannibalization
4. **Major artist defection wave** (top 10 artists leave UMG for independent distribution) -- would impair catalog growth
5. **Net debt exceeds 3x EBITDA** -- excessive leverage for a business with cyclical components (merch, live)
6. **Bollore/major insider sells >5% stake within 12 months** -- conviction signal from controlling shareholder
7. **Antitrust action forces catalog divestiture or licensing unbundling** -- would destroy oligopoly economics
8. **Dividend cut >20%** -- would signal FCF stress and management loss of confidence
