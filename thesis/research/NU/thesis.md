## Business Understanding

### What Nu Does

Nu Holdings operates the world's largest digital banking platform, serving 131 million customers across Brazil (114M), Mexico (14M), and Colombia (3.4M). Founded in 2013 by David Velez (Stanford, ex-Sequoia Capital partner, ex-Goldman Sachs) with the mission of fighting complexity in Latin American financial services.

**Problem solved:** Latin American banking was dominated by 5 oligopolistic incumbents (Itau, Bradesco, Banco do Brasil, Santander BR, Caixa) charging excessive fees, providing poor service, and excluding ~200M underbanked adults. The average cost-to-serve at Itau is $218.60/customer/year. Nu does it for $9.60/customer/year.

**Revenue model:** Multi-product platform generating:
1. **Net Interest Income (NII):** $2.8B Q4'25 (primary -- lending, credit cards, personal loans)
2. **Fee & Commission Income:** Interchange, account fees, marketplace commissions
3. **Insurance:** Growing segment, cross-sold to existing base

### Why It Looks Expensive (and Why It Might Not Be)

**Market narrative:** "NU is a bank trading at 7x book -- banks trade at 1x book. This is insanity."

**My counter-thesis:**

1. **P/B reflects ROE.** Justified P/B = (ROE - g) / (Ke - g). At 33% ROE, 12% growth, 10.6% Ke: P/B justified = (33% - 12%) / (10.6% - 12%) -- this formula breaks because growth > Ke, meaning at sustained 33% ROE and 12% growth, virtually any P/B is justified. Even at normalized 25% ROE, 8% growth: P/B = (25% - 8%) / (10.6% - 8%) = 6.5x. Current P/B of 7.1x implies the market expects ROE to stay at 27-30%, which is plausible.

2. **Efficiency ratio of 20% is structural, not cyclical.** Traditional banks at 45-50% efficiency ratio have branch networks (Itau: 42,900 branches/ATMs). Nu has zero branches. This cost advantage is PERMANENT. Even as Nu invests in expansion, efficiency ratio unlikely to exceed 30%.

3. **Customer acquisition is essentially free.** Nu adds ~4M customers/quarter through word-of-mouth and organic referrals, at near-zero CAC. Traditional bank CAC in Brazil is $50-100+ per customer.

4. **ARPAC growth runway is massive.** At $15/month, Nu monetizes a fraction of what traditional banks earn per customer. Cross-selling insurance, investments, secured credit, payroll loans, SMB services expands ARPAC toward $25-30 over 3-5 years.

**BUT -- honest concerns:**

1. **Credit quality is the key risk.** 90+ NPL at 6.6% is elevated vs traditional banks (~3-4%). Nu lends to a younger, lower-income demographic. A recession in Brazil (Selic 13%+, possible oil-driven stagflation) would hit this cohort hardest.

2. **Berkshire Hathaway fully exited in Q1 2025.** Buffett's team sold the entire position. This is not automatically bearish (could be portfolio rebalancing) but removes a smart money anchor.

3. **FX risk is real.** BRL depreciation compresses USD-reported earnings. ~85% of revenue is BRL-denominated.

4. **2026 is an "investment year."** Management guided that efficiency ratio may temporarily worsen as they invest in US expansion, AI, and return-to-office. Near-term margin pressure.

## Valuation

### Method 1 (Primary, 60%): P/B vs ROE (Justified P/B)

For banks, the fundamental relationship is:

**Justified P/B = (ROE - g) / (Ke - g)**

Where:
- **ROE sustained:** 25% (conservative -- below current 33%, assumes normalization as book grows)
- **g (sustainable growth):** 10% (ROE x retention = 25% x 100% = 25% max; constrained by addressable market to ~10% long-term)
- **Ke:** 11.2%

**Bear case (ROE normalizes to 20%):**
P/B = (20% - 8%) / (11.2% - 8%) = 12% / 3.2% = 3.75x
Book value ~$10.1B (Q4'25 equity). BV/share ~$2.09.
FV = 3.75 x $2.09 = **$7.84**

Wait -- this gives an unreasonably low number because current book value is small relative to earnings power. Let me use forward book value.

**Revised approach -- Forward P/B on 2027E book:**
2025 equity: ~$10.1B. Adding 2 years of retained earnings (~$3.95B + $5.25B = $9.2B). 2027E equity ~$19.3B. BV/share ~$4.00.

**Bear (ROE 20%, g 8%):** P/B = (20-8)/(11.2-8) = 3.75x. FV = 3.75 x $4.00 = **$15.00** (discounted 2 years at 11.2% = $12.14)

**Base (ROE 25%, g 10%):** P/B = (25-10)/(11.2-10) = 12.5x. FV = 12.5 x $4.00 = **$50.00** (discounted = $40.47)

The P/B formula produces extreme results when g approaches Ke, which it does for hyper-growth banks. This method is **unreliable as a point estimate** for NU. Instead, I will use it as directional: at sustained 20% ROE, NU is roughly fair-valued today; at sustained 25%+, it is deeply undervalued.
