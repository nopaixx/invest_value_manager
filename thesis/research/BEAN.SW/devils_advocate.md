## Key Assumptions Challenged

### 1. "Data Center Cooling Is a Structural Shift, Not Cyclical"

**R1 Claim:** Data center cooling grew from 10% to ~18% of sales in 18 months, representing a "structural shift."

**Counter-Evidence:**
- Data center capex IS accelerating: hyperscaler capex projected >$600B in 2026 (+36% YoY), with Moody's forecasting $820B in 2027. Near-term demand is SUPPORTED, not threatened.
- However, there is a significant ROI gap: only ~$25B in AI services revenue vs $450B in AI capex spending in 2025. Only 25% of AI initiatives have delivered expected ROI. This gap cannot persist indefinitely without triggering a spending pullback.
- Belimo management confirmed data centers accounted for "slightly less than half" of total group sales growth in 2025. If data center demand normalizes, Belimo's growth drops from 23% back to ~10-12%.
- The data center liquid cooling valves market is projected to grow from USD 0.27B (2025) to USD 1.80B by 2032 at 30.8% CAGR. This is a REAL market -- but Belimo faces real competition: Danfoss, Siemens, Schneider Electric, Emerson are all identified as leading players. Belimo is NOT the monopolist in data center cooling that it is in traditional HVAC actuators.
- Physical constraints (electricity availability, construction time) may actually constrain data center buildout through 2027, creating a natural deceleration -- S&P Global flagged "less certain winning odds" for data center demand in 2026.
- Historical precedent: cloud/crypto capex cycles showed 2-3 year bursts followed by digestion periods. Current cycle started ~2023; by 2026-2027, a normalization is plausible.

**Severity: MODERATE.** The near-term demand is real and well-supported by evidence. The risk is medium-term (2027-2028) if hyperscaler ROI disappointment triggers capex pullback. The R1 already identified this risk correctly. The competitive landscape in data center cooling specifically (vs traditional HVAC) deserves more attention.

**Resolution:** Data center contribution should be modeled with a decay function: 18-20% growth contribution for 2 years, tapering to 8-10% by year 4-5. This is roughly what R1 base case assumes. But the competition angle means Belimo's SHARE of this growth is not guaranteed.

### 2. "FY2025 Confirmed Exceptional Growth" -- But the DETAILS Tell a Different Story

**R1 Claim:** FY2025 showed 23.3% revenue growth, margin expansion, exceptional year.

**Counter-Evidence (NEW data from actual FY2025 annual report, not available at R1):**

| Metric | R1 Estimate | FY2025 Actual | Delta |
|--------|-------------|---------------|-------|
| Revenue | CHF 1,121M | CHF 1,120.8M | Accurate |
| EBIT | CHF 225-240M | CHF 232.9M | Within range |
| EBIT margin | ~21% | 20.8% | Slightly below |
| Net Income | CHF 180-190M | CHF 182M | Accurate |
| EPS | ~CHF 14.6 | CHF 14.77 | Accurate |
| FCF | CHF 165-175M | CHF 99M | **-40% MISS** |
| Capex | ~CHF 60M | CHF 87M | **+45% above estimate** |
| Gross Margin | 61.6% (FY2024) | 60.7% (FY2025) | **Declined** |
| ROCE | 28.9% (ROIC) | 36.3% (ROCE) | Improved (different metric) |

**Critical findings:**

1. **EPS MISSED consensus by 3%:** CHF 14.77 actual vs CHF 15.17 expected. Dividend also missed (CHF 10.00 vs CHF 10.34 consensus). This is the FIRST signal that the market's expectations were running ahead of delivery.

2. **Gross margin DECLINED:** 61.6% in FY2024 to 60.7% in FY2025. The R1 narrative of "4 consecutive years of expansion" is now broken. At peak demand with record revenue, margin SHOULD be expanding from operating leverage. The compression suggests either: (a) product mix shift toward lower-margin data center products, (b) raw material cost increases, or (c) competitive pricing pressure. Any of these is concerning for the premium valuation thesis.

3. **FCF DROPPED 26%:** From CHF 132M (FY2024) to CHF 99M (FY2025) despite 18.7% revenue growth. FCF margin collapsed from 14.0% to 8.8%. This is driven by:
   - Capex surged to CHF 87M (+38% YoY, from CHF 63M) -- capacity expansion for data center demand
   - Operating cash flow declined to CHF 185M (from CHF 195M in FY2024) despite higher EBIT, signaling working capital absorption
   - Capex/Depreciation ratio surged to approximately 2.8x (from 2.1x in FY2024)
   - Company confirms capex will remain elevated in 2026

4. **Receivables grew ~22% vs revenue ~19%** -- the gap widened from R1's observation (14.3% vs 9.9% in FY2024). Two consecutive years of receivables outgrowing revenue is a yellow flag for collection quality or channel extension.

5. **Inventory build** appears elevated relative to revenue growth, possibly reflecting pre-tariff stocking or demand uncertainty.

**Severity: HIGH.** The FCF deterioration fundamentally changes the valuation math. The R1 used CHF 132M as the FCF base for FY2024 and estimated CHF 165-175M for FY2025. The actual FY2025 FCF of CHF 99M is 40% below the R1 estimate. This mechanically reduces all FCF-based fair values. The gross margin decline is a separate, independent concern that challenges the "expanding moat" thesis.

**Resolution:** All valuation methods must be recalculated using actual FY2025 data. FCF-based valuations need to distinguish between (a) current FCF (CHF 99M, depressed by investment cycle) and (b) normalized FCF (capex reverts to 1.5x D&A, ~CHF 50M, giving FCF ~CHF 135-140M). The truth is likely between these bounds for the next 2-3 years.

### 3. "US Tariffs Are a Major Headwind" (NEW -- Not in R1)

**R1 Claim:** Not addressed (R1 predates tariff announcements).

**New Evidence:**
- Trump imposed 39% tariff on Swiss exports -- well above the initially announced rates
- Belimo generates 50% of revenue from Americas. Most products manufactured in Switzerland and shipped to the US.
- Company implemented 7.9% average price increase effective January 2026, but 39% tariff far exceeds this pass-through
- Management explicitly acknowledged "new U.S. tariffs ranging from 10% to 39%" as a headwind for 2026
- Q1 2026 margins will absorb deferred tariff costs shifted through supply chain timing
- Company guided mid-teens growth for 2026 (deceleration from 23%) with EBIT margin "above 20%" -- but this guidance may not fully incorporate the tariff impact
- Shares fell 7.7% on the day of FY2025 results announcement, with tariff concerns cited as a factor

**Impact Assessment:**
- If 50% of revenue faces an effective 20-30% cost increase from tariffs that cannot be fully passed through, EBIT margin could compress 200-400bps in 2026
- Belimo's pricing power is real (products are <1% of total HVAC project cost), but a 39% tariff is not a normal input cost increase -- it is punitive and may trigger customer resistance or sourcing of alternatives
- Honeywell and Johnson Controls have US manufacturing facilities -- tariffs give them a COST ADVANTAGE vs Swiss-manufactured Belimo for the first time in decades
- Belimo is expanding US production capacity but this takes 2-3 years to materially shift the manufacturing mix
- Even with full price pass-through, demand elasticity at 39% cost increase is unknown. Belimo has never faced this magnitude of cost shock.

**Severity: HIGH.** This is a material risk that the R1 could not have anticipated. A 39% tariff on a company with 50% Americas revenue and predominately Swiss manufacturing is structurally negative. The mitigation (price increases + US capacity expansion) takes time. Near-term (2026-2027) margin compression is likely.

**Resolution:** Committee must factor tariff headwind into FV. Base case should assume 150-300bps EBIT margin compression in 2026-2027. Monitor (a) whether tariffs are negotiated down (Swiss-US trade talks), (b) Q1 2026 margin data, and (c) Belimo's US capacity ramp timeline.

### 4. "P/E 62-65x Is Egregious" (R1 claim -- now 44.5x at CHF 658)

**R1 Claim:** At CHF 903, P/E was 62-65x on trailing earnings, wildly overvalued.

**Updated Reality:**
- At CHF 658, P/E is 44.5x on FY2025 EPS of CHF 14.77
- EV/EBIT is 35.8x on FY2025 EBIT of CHF 233M
- The 27% price drop has compressed multiples by ~30%
- If FY2026 EPS guidance of ~CHF 18-19 materializes (mid-teens revenue growth + stable margins), forward P/E drops to ~35-37x

**Is 44.5x reasonable?**
- For a Swiss industrial growing 7-9% historically: NO. Traditional peer multiples: Spirax-Sarco 25x EBIT, Atlas Copco 30x
- For a Swiss industrial growing 15%+ with data center tailwind: ARGUABLE, but still at the extreme end. Halma (similar quality, 8% growth) trades at 35x EBIT.
- R1 base FV of CHF 470 implies P/E ~32x on FY2025 EPS -- still premium but reflects a company growing 10-12% not 23%
- R1 bull FV of CHF 680 implies P/E ~46x -- the stock at CHF 658 is now BELOW the bull FV

**Key point: At 44.5x P/E, the market is pricing BETWEEN the R1 base and bull scenarios, skewed heavily toward the bull.** If data center growth sustains at mid-teens and tariff impact is manageable, 35-40x forward P/E is justifiable. If growth normalizes to 8-10%, the stock needs to de-rate to 25-30x, implying CHF 450-570.

**Severity: MODERATE.** The extreme overvaluation has partially corrected. At 44.5x P/E, Belimo is expensive but within the range seen for exceptional quality industrials during growth inflection points. The risk is asymmetric -- more downside scenarios than upside scenarios from here.

## META-REFLECTION

### Doubts/Uncertainties
- **The FY2025 FCF drop may be largely TEMPORARY.** Capex at CHF 87M (vs CHF 63M in FY2024) is driven by capacity expansion -- new facilities in Switzerland and the US. If capex normalizes to CHF 50-55M by 2027 and revenue grows at mid-teens, FCF could rebound to CHF 160-180M. The question is whether to value on trough FCF or normalized FCF. My bear case uses both (CHF 99M actual and CHF 138M normalized), but the truth for 2026-2027 is probably closer to CHF 100-120M given continued elevated investment.
- **The 39% US tariff situation is highly fluid.** Tariffs could be negotiated down (Swiss-US bilateral talks), Belimo could accelerate US manufacturing (they are already expanding), or the tariff structure could change entirely under political pressure. The range of tariff outcomes is wider than for most other risks.
- **Gross margin decline from 61.6% to 60.7%** could reflect temporary product mix effects (more data center products at slightly lower margin) rather than structural moat erosion. One year of margin compression does not invalidate a decade of 60%+ margins. However, at 44.5x P/E, the market is pricing for IMPROVEMENT, not stability.
- **ROCE of 36.3% in FY2025** (vs ROIC 28.9% in FY2024 -- slightly different metrics) is exceptionally strong and confirms the quality franchise. The business is GOOD. The question is entirely about PRICE.
