## Business Understanding

### The Business Model

Booz Allen Hamilton is the largest pure-play US government IT services and consulting firm. Founded in 1914, public since 2010, the company provides management consulting, analytics, digital solutions, AI/ML, engineering, and cybersecurity services almost exclusively (98% of revenue) to the US federal government.

**Problem solved:** The US government needs external expertise for technology modernization, cybersecurity, AI implementation, data analytics, and mission-critical operations. Federal agencies lack internal technical talent -- especially in areas requiring security clearances (TS/SCI takes 2+ years to obtain). BAH fills this structural gap with 22,000+ cleared employees out of ~35,000 total workforce.

**Revenue model:** Project-based contracts (cost-reimbursable ~50%, time-and-materials ~30%, fixed-price ~20%). Contracts run 3-7 years with 80-90% recompete win rates, creating quasi-recurring revenue. Not SaaS-style subscription, but the incumbency advantage and switching costs make revenue sticky.

**Combined defense + intelligence = ~65% of revenue and GROWING. Civil = ~33% and DECLINING. This is the core thesis: the 65% structural part is overwhelmed in the narrative by the 33% cyclical headwind.**

### Unit Economics

- **Gross Margin:** 54.8% (FY2025), expanding from 53.4% (FY2022). Very high for a services company -- reflects the premium nature of cleared-workforce consulting and the shift toward higher-value digital/AI work.
- **Operating Margin:** 11.4% (FY2025), volatile (4.8% in FY2023, 8.2% in FY2022). Trending up as AI/digital mix grows.
- **FCF Margin:** Normalized 5-7%. FY2025 was 7.6% ($911M) but FY2024 was only 1.8% ($192M). The 3-year average FCF margin is 5.0%.
- **Capital intensity:** Very low. Capex ~$50-60M/year on $12B revenue (<0.5%). Asset-light, people-based business.
- **SBC/Revenue:** 0.8-0.9% -- minimal dilution.
- **Receivables vs Revenue growth:** Receivables growth (11.6%) roughly tracks revenue growth (12.4%) -- no red flag.
- **Goodwill/Total Assets:** 32.9% (FY2025), declining from 35.7% (FY2024). Not a serial acquirer; manageable.

### Competitive Moat -- WIDE (7/10)

Three reinforcing moat sources:

1. **Security Clearance Workforce (PRIMARY -- irreplaceable).** 22,000+ of ~35,000 employees hold security clearances. TS/SCI clearances take 2+ years to obtain and cannot be fast-tracked by competitors throwing money at the problem. The supply of cleared workers is fundamentally constrained. This is the single most defensible asset in US government IT services -- no competitor can replicate it quickly.

2. **Incumbency Advantage.** Government contracts have 80-90% recompete win rates. Once BAH is embedded in a client mission (legacy systems, institutional knowledge, cleared staff in place), switching is extremely costly and risky for the government. $38B backlog provides 3-4 years of visibility.

3. **AI/Digital Technology Leadership.** BAH's AI business topped $800M+ growing 30%+ YoY. The a16z Technology Acceleration Partnership (first-ever for governments) positions BAH as the bridge between Silicon Valley AI innovation and classified government environments. This is NOT commodity consulting -- it's proprietary technology deployment in environments where competitors cannot operate.

**Moat threats:**
- DOGE could structurally reduce the number of government contracts (not just delay them). If agencies permanently reduce outsourcing to consultants, the TAM shrinks. However, the historical precedent (2013 sequestration) shows government outsourcing recovers.
- AI could automate some traditional consulting tasks, reducing headcount needs. But BAH is positioning as an AI provider, not a labor arbitrage play.

### Informational Advantage

- **Horizon temporal:** Market prices DOGE disruption as permanent. Government contracting has ALWAYS been cyclical. BAH survived 2013 sequestration (-7% revenue, recovered within 2 years), 2018-19 shutdown, and multiple budget crises.
- **Overreaction:** -38% decline for a company with $38B backlog, 22K cleared staff, and $800M AI business growing 30%+ is likely excessive. The market is pricing 0.4% FCF growth (reverse DCF) when historical FCF CAGR is 11.5%.
- **Iran crisis catalyst:** The market has not yet priced in the Iran war's positive implications for defense IT/AI spending. Supplemental defense funding is being discussed. BAH's CEO was on CNBC discussing defense technology. The $1.5T FY2027 defense budget proposal + Iran supplemental = unprecedented demand for defense AI/cyber capabilities -- BAH's core competency.
- **Quantitative gap:** P/E 12.1x is near BAH's lowest ever as a public company (IPO 2010 range: 12-25x). EV/EBIT 9.3x implies deep skepticism. Book-to-bill 1.41x TTM (6-year high) contradicts the "demand destruction" narrative.
