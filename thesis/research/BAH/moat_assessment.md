# Moat Assessment: BAH (Booz Allen Hamilton)

## Fecha: 2026-03-10
## Clasificacion: WIDE (structural, multi-layered -- defense/intel core is impregnable)

---

## Moat Sources Identified

### 1. Security Clearance Barrier (PRIMARY -- VERY WIDE)

**Type:** Intangible Asset / Regulatory Barrier
**Durability:** PERMANENT (as long as US classification system exists)

BAH employs 22,000+ personnel with active security clearances (TS/SCI and above), representing the largest cleared workforce among IT services firms. This is the core moat:

- **Time barrier:** TS/SCI clearance takes 18-24 months to obtain. A competitor cannot "hire up" a cleared workforce quickly.
- **Cost barrier:** Each clearance costs $50K-$100K in investigation/processing.
- **Skill barrier:** Cleared personnel must ALSO have technical skills (AI, cyber, data science). The intersection of "security clearance + technical expertise" is an extremely narrow talent pool.
- **Sponsorship requirement:** Only existing contract holders can sponsor clearance investigations. This creates a chicken-and-egg problem for new entrants.
- **Attrition protection:** When BAH loses a cleared employee, the replacement takes 18-24 months. This creates involuntary switching costs for agencies.

**Evidence:** BAH's 80-90% recompete win rate on contracts. Defense/intelligence book-to-bill of 1.42x. $38B backlog record.

**Quantification:** Gross margin premium of +26.8pp vs sector median (54.8% vs 28.0% for Industrials/Consulting). This premium is primarily driven by the cleared workforce's irreplaceability.

### 2. Incumbency and Switching Costs (WIDE)

**Type:** Switching Cost / Relationship
**Durability:** HIGH (5-10 year contract cycles)

Government IT contracts are deeply embedded in agency operations:

- **Institutional knowledge:** BAH teams spend years understanding classified systems, agency processes, and mission requirements.
- **Integration depth:** BAH personnel sit in government facilities, use government networks, are integrated into daily operations.
- **Recompete advantage:** 80-90% win rate on recompetes reflects this entrenchment.
- **Contract duration:** 3-7 year base periods with option years extending to 10+.
- **Security review:** Any new contractor must undergo extensive security review before accessing classified systems -- adding 6-12 months to any switch.

### 3. Scale, AI/Cyber Leadership (MODERATE-WIDE)

**Type:** Cost Advantage / Knowledge Capital
**Durability:** MODERATE-HIGH

- **$800M+ AI business**, recognized as largest AI defense contractor for the West
- **8,000+ cyber professionals**, leading provider of cybersecurity to federal government (FY2021-2023)
- **a16z partnership:** First-ever Technology Acceleration Partner for Governments (Jan 2026)
- **$1.58B DIA WAEDS contract** for WMD intelligence analysis -- validates deep tech capability
- **Cross-selling:** Works across 20+ federal agencies; talent redeployment across segments

### 4. Regulatory/Structural Barrier (MODERATE)

**Type:** Regulatory Barrier
**Durability:** MODERATE (subject to political change -- see DOGE)

- ITAR/NIST compliance requirements
- FedRAMP authorization (years of compliance work)
- US citizenship requirements for many contracts

## Moat Width: WIDE

The cleared workforce moat is permanent and self-reinforcing. DOGE threatens UTILIZATION in the civil segment but does NOT threaten the moat itself. Defense/intel moat (65% of revenue) is strengthening under wartime conditions. ROIC of 23.6% with +18pp spread over WACC confirms the moat generates economic value.

**Key distinction:** NARROW in civil segment (DOGE can break contract relationships there), VERY WIDE in defense/intel (classified programs are untouchable by political efficiency drives). Blended assessment: WIDE.
