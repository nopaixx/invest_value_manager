# Risk Assessment: BAH (Booz Allen Hamilton)

## Fecha: 2026-03-10
## Risk Score: HIGH (concentrated customer + political risk, offset by structural moat)

---

## Risk Categories

### 1. DOGE / Political Risk -- HIGH (PRIMARY RISK)

**Description:** DOGE has already cancelled $200M+ in BAH contracts, primarily in the civil segment (~35% of revenue). The risk is that DOGE expands to defense/intelligence contracts.

**Quantification:**
- Civil segment revenue declining "low double digits" in FY2026 (-12-15%)
- Treasury cancelled all 31 BAH contracts ($21M total, $4.8M annual)
- 5 major civil IT program run-rates reduced
- VA contract non-renewal adds -300bps headwind
- Total corporate growth headwind from civil: ~-600bps (6%)
- 2,500 layoffs announced (7% of workforce, concentrated in civil)

**Mitigating factors:**
- DOGE has NOT touched defense or intelligence contracts
- Iran war makes defense IT cuts politically impossible
- CEO publicly "welcomes DOGE scrutiny" (positioning as aligned, not adversarial)
- FY2027 pipeline $53B (+12%) indicates demand is ACCELERATING in non-civil segments
- Defense/intel book-to-bill 1.42x (healthy demand signal)

**Kill Condition trigger:** DOGE expands to defense/intelligence contracts with specific BAH targeting (KC #8)

### 2. Customer Concentration -- HIGH

**Description:** 98% of revenue from US federal government. This is extreme single-customer risk.

**Quantification:**
- Revenue breakdown: ~47% Defense, ~18% Intelligence, ~35% Civil
- Within each segment, multiple agencies and programs provide some diversification
- Backlog of $38B across hundreds of contracts reduces single-contract risk

**Mitigating factors:**
- The "customer" (US government) is not going to disappear
- Within the government, BAH serves 20+ agencies with different budgets and priorities
- Defense/intel spending is constitutionally mandated and growing
- Contract types (cost-plus, T&M, fixed-price) provide structural revenue predictability

**Kill Condition trigger:** Defense/intel revenue declines >5% for 2 consecutive quarters (KC #1)

### 3. FCF Volatility -- MODERATE-HIGH

**Description:** FCF has ranged from $192M (FY2024) to $911M (FY2025). This makes valuation uncertain.

**Analysis of volatility sources:**
- FY2024 trough ($192M, 1.8% margin): Driven by acquisition-related payments + working capital build + government shutdown timing
- FY2025 recovery ($911M, 7.6% margin): Normalization + strong collections
- FY2023 ($527M, 5.7%): Below average
- 3-year average: $543M (5.0% margin)

**The question:** Is FY2025's $911M the new normal or an outlier?
- Management did NOT guide to $911M+ for FY2026 (FCF guidance $750-$850M est.)
- Conservative assumption: normalized FCF margin 6-7%, producing $680-$800M on $11.3-11.4B revenue

**Kill Condition trigger:** FCF negative for 2 consecutive quarters (KC #5)

### 4. Leverage -- MODERATE

**Description:** Net Debt/EBITDA of 2.6x is manageable but not conservative.

**Details:**
- Total debt: $4.1B
- Cash: $882M
- Net debt: $3.25B
- Interest coverage: 7.4x (comfortable)
- Debt used primarily for acquisitions (EverWatch 2022, others)

**Mitigating factors:**
- Strong and growing EBITDA trajectory
- Interest coverage 7.4x provides wide cushion
- No near-term debt maturities forcing refinancing at high rates
- Payout ratio 32.6% leaves room for debt reduction

### 5. Management / Governance -- LOW-MODERATE

**Description:** CFO resignation in 2025, Littlejohn breach scandal (Treasury contract loss), low insider ownership.

**Details:**
- CFO Matt Calderone resigned Oct 2025; interim CFO Komal Monga (CTO) in place
- Charles Littlejohn breach (former employee stole tax records 2018-2020) led to Treasury contract loss
- Insider ownership only 1.1% (Carlyle fully exited years ago)
- BUT: CEO bought $2M shares at ~$85 (Feb 2026)

**Mitigating factors:**
- CEO purchasing shares is a strong positive signal
- Breach was an insider threat, not a systemic security failure
- Littlejohn was prosecuted and convicted -- not an ongoing issue
- CFO transitions are operationally manageable for a firm of BAH's maturity

### 6. Iran War De-escalation (UPSIDE RISK) -- MODERATE

**Description:** If Iran war ends quickly, the defense spending tailwind weakens.

**Analysis:** This is actually a risk to the BULL case, not the base case. BAH's base case doesn't require war premium -- the structural spending upcycle (NATO 5% GDP, US defense modernization, AI/cyber secular trends) exists independently of the Iran crisis. A ceasefire would reduce the urgency premium but not the structural demand.

### 7. AI Disruption -- LOW

**Description:** Could commercial AI tools reduce need for BAH's custom solutions?

**Analysis:** BAH is the LEADER in government AI, not a victim. The a16z partnership positions BAH as the bridge between Silicon Valley AI innovation and classified government environments. Commercial AI tools INCREASE demand for integration services (someone has to deploy them in classified settings). BAH's $800M+ AI business is GROWING, not threatened.

### 8. Competitive Risk -- LOW

**Description:** Leidos ($250M+ DOGE-cancelled contracts), SAIC, CACI could gain share.

**Analysis:** BAH's cleared workforce moat makes market share shifts slow and difficult. The 80-90% recompete win rate is the evidence. CACI at P/E 25x validates that the market values quality govcon IT -- BAH at 11.5x is the outlier.

## Overall Risk Assessment

**Risk is REAL but PRICED.** The market has already discounted BAH by 41% from its 52wH. The key question is not "is BAH risky?" (it is) but "is the risk PRICED AT $77.52?" My assessment: YES, and then some. The market is pricing permanent DOGE impairment across ALL segments when the actual impact is concentrated in civil (35% of revenue) and quantifiable at ~6-7% total revenue impact.

**The asymmetry is favorable:** Downside to bear case ($69) is 11%. Upside to base case ($105) is 35%. Upside to bull case ($160) is 106%. The risk/reward ratio is 3.5:1.
