## Business Understanding

### Model of Business

**Problem solved:** Valmet provides mission-critical process technology, automation, and services for the pulp, paper, and energy industries. Customers (pulp/paper mills, energy producers) need Valmet's equipment to produce paper, board, tissue, pulp, and energy from biomass. These are essential goods -- packaging demand is structural (e-commerce, food safety), tissue is non-discretionary, and biomass energy is a key decarbonization pathway.

**Revenue model:**

| Revenue Type | Description | % of Revenue | Characteristics |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Services | Maintenance, spare parts, process optimization, automation | ~40% | Recurring, higher margin (PPS segment 19.6% EBITA margin), installed base driven |
| Process Technology | New pulp mills, paper/board machines, tissue lines | ~45% | Project-based, lumpy, long cycle (12-24mo), order backlog provides visibility |
| Automation | DCS, QCS, analyzers, Industrial Internet | ~15% | Mix of project + recurring, high margin, switching costs |

**Revenue split by segment (2025):**
- Process Performance Solutions (services + automation): Higher margin, ~19.6% EBITA margin
- Biomaterial Solutions & Services (process tech + some services): ~10.3% EBITA margin

**Unit economics:**
- Revenue per employee: ~EUR 340K (EUR 5.2B / ~15,000 employees)
- Order backlog: EUR 4.3B = ~10 months of revenue visibility
- Book-to-bill: ~1.0x in 2025 (orders EUR 5.2B vs sales EUR 5.2B)
- Capital intensity: Medium (Capex/Depreciation 0.9x in 2025, asset-medium business)

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | Market Share | Positioning |
|------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Valmet** | #1: 50% board/paper, 45% automation, 35% pulp | Broadest portfolio, lifecycle services, automation leader |
| **Andritz AG** | #2 overall, strong in pulp | Austrian, diversified (hydro, metals), less automation focus |
| **Voith Group** | #3 in paper/board | German, private, narrower product range |
| **Kadant** | Niche in flow control | US, smaller, complementary products |
| **Toscotec** | Tissue specialty | Italian, smaller, tissue-focused |

**Key insight:** This is a tight oligopoly. Valmet + Andritz + Voith control 80%+ of the global market. Customer relationships span decades. Switching costs are enormous -- changing a paper machine supplier means retraining staff, re-engineering processes, losing optimization data. The installed base creates a services annuity stream.

### Moat Assessment

**Type: Switching Costs + Scale + Intangible Assets (Narrow-to-Wide)**

1. **Switching costs (primary):** Paper/pulp mills are designed around specific equipment platforms. Valmet's automation systems (DNA, IQ) are deeply integrated into customer operations. Switching means 12-24 months of disruption + retraining + lost optimization. Services contracts lock customers into Valmet's ecosystem for 15-25 year equipment lifecycles.

2. **Scale:** Only 3 companies globally can deliver turnkey pulp mills (EUR 1B+ projects). This requires global engineering capabilities, reference installations, and financial strength. Barriers to entry are structural.

3. **Intangible assets:** 150+ years of process know-how. 260+ fluidized bed boiler references. Proprietary automation platform. R&D at 2.4% of revenue and growing.

**Durability:** Narrow moat with potential to widen. ROIC has been inconsistent (below WACC recently), which prevents a "wide" classification despite dominant market position. If the operating model renewal sustainably lifts ROIC above WACC, moat strengthens to wide.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- **ROIC trajectory uncertainty:** The +14 QS adjustment is largely predicated on ROIC improving above WACC. If the EUR 80M savings target is not fully achieved, ROIC could stay below WACC, making this a perennial value trap despite strong market positions. The 2022 ROIC of 10.8% included some Neles synergy tailwinds -- unclear if structural or temporary.

- **Dividend payout ratio 89%:** High payout limits reinvestment capacity. If earnings don't grow, the dividend constrains capital allocation. Sustainable only if FCF continues at current levels.
