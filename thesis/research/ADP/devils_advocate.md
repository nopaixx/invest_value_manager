# Counter-Analysis: ADP (Automatic Data Processing, Inc.)

## Fecha: 2026-03-20

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis survives scrutiny as a high-quality business at a fair price, but it offers NO margin of safety at $210.66 and the "Triple Squeeze" risks are correlated, real, and already materializing. The FA's FV of $210 is well-calibrated (within $1 of current price), which itself is the problem: there is no upside at market. My independent bear-case FV of $163 suggests 22% downside if the correlated bear scenario plays out. The thesis correctly identifies ADP as an exceptional business, but the entry at $175 still carries meaningful risk given the structural headwinds. The Morningstar moat downgrade from WIDE to NARROW deserves more weight than the thesis grants it.

---

## Calibration (Phase 0.5)

**Market anchor:** ADP at $210.66 implies 7.8% FCF growth for 5 years. Historical revenue CAGR is 7.6%. The market is pricing ADP EXACTLY for its historical trajectory -- no discount, no premium. The FA must prove the market is wrong to justify any upside.

**DA historical stats:** 25 prior corrections, avg -15.7%, median -13.0%. All negative. No outcome data yet to confirm whether corrections were sufficient or excessive.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "Revenue grows 6-6.5% sustainably"

- **Evidencia en contra:** Revenue growth of 6% is ENTIRELY driven by pricing power (+2-3%) and new client wins (+1-2%). Organic volume (PPC) is FLAT. This is a fundamentally different quality of growth than volume-driven expansion. Pricing-only growth has a ceiling: clients eventually push back or switch to cheaper alternatives. ADP's Q2 FY2026 showed PPC "rounded to 1%" but guided flat for full year. PEO worksite employee growth downgraded to 2% (from prior ~5%). Feb 2026 ADP Employment Report: only +63K private jobs. Professional services shed 30K jobs. PEO margins already compressing 70bp in Q2.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Stress-test at 4% revenue growth (pricing only, zero volume). The resulting FV ($165-175) barely supports the $175 entry.

### 2. "Float income is protected by laddered portfolio for 2-3 years"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis correctly notes float income of $1.31B but UNDERWEIGHTS its contribution to profitability. Float income is approximately 24% of operating profit ($1.31B near-100% margin vs $5.41B EBIT), NOT "6% of EPS" as the prior R1 framed it. The current thesis acknowledges this (incorporating prior DA correction), but the valuation still does not fully model the decline scenario. If rates normalize to 2-2.5% (market consensus for late 2026-2027), float income drops to ~$750-900M. That is a $400-550M hit to operating profit, representing 7-10% operating profit decline. The laddered portfolio smooths the impact but does NOT prevent it. Current guidance uses 3.8% blended rate for new purchases -- this drops with each Fed cut.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Model float income at 2.5% yield ($900M) in base case and 1.5% yield ($550M) in bear case. Recalculate operating margins -- the "26% and expanding" story becomes "26% at risk of compressing to 24-25%."

### 3. "AI is a tailwind for ADP, not a headwind"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis argues AI augments rather than replaces payroll. This is partially correct for the payroll FUNCTION but misses the second-order effect: AI reduces the NUMBER of employees being paid. Evidence is accelerating:
  - 100,000+ employees impacted by AI-driven layoffs in 2025; 30,000+ already in early 2026
  - Block (Jack Dorsey) announced cutting from 10,000 to <6,000 employees citing AI
  - EY report: nearly 20% of firms actively reducing headcount due to AI adoption
  - 54% of companies cutting employee compensation to fund AI investments
  - CNBC survey: 89% of HR leaders say AI will impact jobs in 2026

  Each eliminated employee = one fewer paycheck processed = less ADP per-employee revenue. The thesis estimates 7-9% revenue impact in a severe 10% headcount scenario including second-order float effects. But if AI-driven headcount reductions are structural (not cyclical), they compound over time, creating permanent drag on PPC growth. ADP's own PPC metric is ALREADY flat -- could be the early signal.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE (3-5 year horizon risk, not immediate)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Monitor closely. Add kill condition: "US employment declines >3% from peak sustained 2+ quarters AND ADP has not announced alternative pricing model."

### 4. "Morningstar moat downgrade is overstated / ADP moat is WIDE"

- **Evidencia en contra:** Morningstar downgraded from WIDE to NARROW as part of a systematic AI-driven moat reassessment across enterprise software/HCM. Their reasoning: AI could simplify compliance automation enough to lower barriers to entry. Supporting evidence for narrowing moat:
  - Rippling at $570M ARR growing 30%+ with G2 rating 4.8/5.0 vs ADP's 4.2/5.0
  - Rippling explicitly markets against ADP's "75-year-old punch-card architecture"
  - Deel at $1B run rate, 75% growth, expanding from global into domestic payroll
  - HCM market grew 11.7% YoY (to $58.7B) but ADP grew only 6-7% -- underperforming the market it dominates
  - ADP's gross margin of 46% is BELOW sector median of 55%

  Our moat-assessor rated WIDE based on historical evidence (retention, ROIC, scale). But moat assessments should be FORWARD-looking. Morningstar's NARROW reflects a forward view where AI progressively reduces the compliance expertise barrier. Also, ADP's Morningstar FV was cut from $297 to $231 -- a 22% reduction, the largest I have seen Morningstar apply to a former wide-moat stock.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Weight Morningstar NARROW view at 40% probability. Apply higher MoS for borderline moat quality.

### 5. "At $175, risk-reward is compelling"

- **Evidencia en contra:** At $175 entry:
  - P/E: 16.7x -- historically cheap, but this is the P/E a 4-5% grower DESERVES
  - MoS vs base FV ($210): 17% -- adequate for Tier A, but requires PPC to NOT go negative
  - MoS vs DA bear FV ($163): NEGATIVE (-7%) -- the stock would be ABOVE my bear case even at $175
  - E[CAGR] at $175: thesis claims ~10.5%, but this includes 7% "expected growth" which assumes PPC recovery + margin expansion + buybacks simultaneously
  - Bear scenario at $175: FV $147 implies -16% downside even from the "discounted" entry

  The thesis requires a 17% pullback from $210.66 to reach $175. What catalyst causes this? The stock already fell 36% from peak. Another 17% requires a new negative catalyst not yet priced in. The thesis identifies "recession" as the path to $175, which is the same scenario that makes the bear case ($147) more likely.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Consider $160-165 as entry, providing MoS of 22-24% vs base and marginal cushion above DA bear.

## DA Bear Case Valuation (Independent, Phase 3B)

**Method: EV/EBIT Normalized (different from FA's primary OEY method)**

Bear assumptions:
- EBIT: $5.00B (current $5.41B less $0.41B float income decline from rates normalizing to 2.5%)
- EV/EBIT multiple: 14.5x (below historical ~22x, below current 15.1x, reflecting utility-like pricing for a structurally decelerating mid-single-digit grower)
  - Rationale: If growth settles at 4% (pricing only, zero volume), ADP deserves a utility-like multiple. PAYX at ~18x has similar growth but higher margins. Structurally challenged ADP at 14-15x.
- Terminal growth: 2.0% (below nominal GDP -- structural headcount decline offsets)

```
Bear EBIT (float decline): $5.41B - $0.41B = $5.00B
EV = $5.00B x 14.5x = $72.5B
Net debt: $2.05B
Equity = $70.5B
Shares: 403M
FV/share = $175

More aggressive bear (full Triple Squeeze: -15% OP from PPC negative + float + retention):
EBIT = $4.60B
EV = $4.60B x 14.5x = $66.7B
Equity = $64.7B
FV/share = $161

Midpoint DA bear: ($175 + $161) / 2 = $163 (after rounding)
```

Cross-check: DCF tool bear output = $144. My EV/EBIT bear midpoint ($163) is more generous than the DCF bear. Using average of both methods: ($163 + $144) / 2 = $154. I use $163 as it is the less extreme of the two.
