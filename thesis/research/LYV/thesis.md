## Business Understanding

### Business Model

Live Nation solves the coordination problem in live entertainment: matching artists with venues, fans with tickets, and brands with audiences. It operates a vertically integrated monopoly across three segments:

**1. Concerts ($20.9B, 83% of revenue):** Promotes ~55,000 shows for 159M fans globally. Low-margin (3.3% AOI margin) but massive scale creates flywheel: more shows -> more data -> better pricing -> more artists -> more fans. International now >50% of attendance for first time.

**2. Ticketmaster ($3.1B, 12% of revenue):** Primary ticketing platform with 80%+ market share in major venues. HIGH margin business (estimated 25-30% EBIT margin). Service fees of 25-30% on face value. Dynamic pricing captures consumer surplus. This is the monopoly profit engine.

**3. Sponsorship & Advertising ($1.3B, 5% of revenue):** Highest margin segment (AOI $845M, ~65% margin). Brands pay to reach captive live audiences. Growing 11% YoY. 70%+ already booked for 2026. Extremely sticky, recurring.

**Revenue mix:** ~60% recurring/predictable (sponsorship contracts, deferred ticket revenue $4.0B up 21% YoY). Concerts revenue is technically variable but has shown remarkable consistency post-COVID.

### Unit Economics

- **LTV/CAC:** Not traditionally applicable. LYV's moat IS the platform — artists and fans have no real alternative at scale. Customer acquisition cost is essentially zero for Ticketmaster given monopoly position.
- **Ticket take rate:** Ticketmaster captures ~12-15% of GTV (gross transactional value) through service fees and platform fees.
- **FCF Margin (adjusted):** 5.2% ($1.3B adjusted FCF on $25.2B revenue). GAAP FCF margin depressed to 1.2% by $1.1B capex (venue buildout).
- **Capital intensity:** MEDIUM-HIGH currently (capex/rev 4.4%) due to venue expansion. Management guides $1.1-1.2B capex for 2026 with $250M offset from JV partners. Maintenance capex estimated at $300-350M (1.2-1.4% of revenue) — the rest is growth.

## Moat Assessment

**Moat: WIDE — Vertically Integrated Monopoly**

1. **Network effects:** More artists -> more fans -> more venues -> more sponsors -> more data -> better pricing -> more artists. Self-reinforcing flywheel that no competitor can replicate at scale.

2. **Scale advantage:** 55,000 shows, 159M fans. The next competitor (AEG) does maybe 20% of this volume. This scale gives negotiating power with artists, venues, and sponsors that cannot be matched.

3. **Switching costs:** Venues are locked into multi-year Ticketmaster contracts. Artists need LYV's promotion + venue network. Sponsors need the audience data. Each participant faces high switching costs because no alternative offers comparable reach.

4. **Regulatory moat (paradoxically):** The DOJ settlement actually STRENGTHENS the moat by RESOLVING the breakup uncertainty while imposing manageable behavioral remedies. The state litigation is weaker without federal backing.

5. **Venue ownership:** LYV is investing $800-850M/year building and acquiring venues. By 2028+, this creates a physical asset moat that is impossible to replicate.

**Moat risk:** The state antitrust trial (36 states + DC) could theoretically force structural remedies beyond the DOJ settlement. However, without federal support, the states' bargaining position is weaker. The most likely outcome is additional behavioral remedies (more fee transparency, more third-party ticket access), not structural breakup.
