# Devil's Advocate: LYV (Live Nation Entertainment)

## Fecha: 2026-03-21

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The LYV thesis identifies a genuine monopoly in live entertainment but prices it generously given material unresolved risks. The FA appropriately set a WATCHLIST verdict at current prices ($148.85 vs $130 FV), which limits the damage of the identified issues. However, the thesis contains several assumptions that deserve scrutiny: (1) the QS adjustment of +10 points inflates the quality tier from C to B, which loosens MoS requirements; (2) the "adjusted FCF" of $1.3B relies on management's capex classification as growth rather than maintenance; (3) the state antitrust trial (36 states, active as of March 2026) is more threatening than the thesis acknowledges; and (4) negative GAAP EPS despite record revenue is a genuinely concerning signal. The thesis survives because it was conservative on entry price, but if the stock reaches the proposed $115-120 entry, these issues will need resolution.

---

## Calibration

**Market anchor:** LYV at $148.85 implies >50% FCF growth (from reverse DCF on GAAP FCF of $305M). The DCF tool produces FV of $4-15 using trailing FCF -- essentially saying the market is pricing in massive FCF normalization. The FA acknowledges this and abandons DCF in favor of EV/AOI and normalized FCF yield. This methodological choice is itself a thesis assumption.

**DA historical stats:** 25 corrections, avg -15.7%, median -13.0%. DA has never increased an FV. Corrections have ranged from -1.0% (ADBE) to -36.1% (EEFT). No outcomes measured yet (first review due Aug 2026).

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. QS Adjustment of +10 Points (48 to 58, C to B)

- **The thesis claim:** Tool gives 48 (Tier C). FA adjusts to 58 (Tier B) by adding +8 for market position and +2 for FCF/GM premium.
- **Evidence against:**
  - The market position adjustment (+8 points, from 0 to maximum 8/8) is the single largest component. LYV is unquestionably #1 in live entertainment, but the tool scored 0 because it cannot assess this automatically -- the same is true for MANY companies. The question is whether LYV deserves the MAXIMUM score (8/8).
  - The FCF margin adjustment (+2 points) acknowledges that even adjusted FCF margin (5.2%) only merits 2/10. This is a modest adjustment but it partially depends on accepting management's $1.3B adjusted FCF figure.
  - The net effect of moving from Tier C to Tier B is significant: it changes the MoS framework from "30-40% required" to "20-25% required" (per CLAUDE.md QS table). This is the most consequential single adjustment in the thesis.
  - Comparable monopolies that get maximum market position scores (Visa, Mastercard) have FCF margins of 40-50%, not 5%. LYV's monopoly generates a lot of revenue but thin margins -- the monopoly quality is different from a capital-light payments monopoly.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** The market position score is defensible at 6-7/8 rather than 8/8. LYV is #1 but competes with AEG (Coachella, Goldenvoice) and faces regulatory constraints that true monopolies like Visa do not. A score of 6/8 would give QS 54-55, still Tier C, which would require higher MoS. The net impact on the WATCHLIST verdict is minimal since FA already requires $115-120 entry, but if an SO is formalized, Tier C treatment would shift entry lower to ~$100-105.

### 2. "Adjusted FCF" of $1.3B vs GAAP FCF of $305M

- **The thesis claim:** GAAP FCF ($305M) is depressed by $1.1B growth capex (venue buildout). "Adjusted FCF" of $1.3B is the real earnings power. Maintenance capex is only ~$300M.
- **Evidence against:**
  - The gap between GAAP FCF ($305M) and "adjusted" FCF ($1.3B) is a 4.3x multiple. This is an unusually large adjustment. The thesis relies entirely on management's classification of capex as "growth" vs "maintenance."
  - Capex/Depreciation ratio surged from 1.6x (2022) to 2.9x (2025) -- the FA flagged this as a yellow flag in META-REFLECTION. When capex is 3x depreciation, it suggests either aggressive growth OR that maintenance costs are being under-reported.
  - LYV's venue strategy requires ONGOING capital to maintain competitive venues. Once venues are built, they require renovation, technology upgrades, and operational investment. The line between "growth" and "maintenance" capex is blurry for venue-based businesses.
  - Management guides $1.1-1.2B capex for 2026. There is no clear end date for the capex cycle -- "post-2027" is aspirational, not guided.
  - If true maintenance capex is $500-600M (rather than $300M), normalized FCF drops to $800-900M, and the normalized FCF yield at current price drops to 2.3-2.6%. This would undermine the valuation significantly.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** The investment committee should stress-test the thesis with maintenance capex at $500M (splitting the difference between FA's $300M and full capex of $1.1B). This would produce normalized FCF of ~$900M and meaningfully lower the FV.

### 3. State Antitrust Trial Probability-Weighted Too Lightly

- **The thesis claim:** DOJ settlement removes federal breakup risk. States are weaker without federal support. Most likely outcome: behavioral remedies, not structural.
- **Evidence against:**
  - As of March 2026, the state trial is ACTIVELY underway with 36 states and DC. The states explicitly rejected the DOJ settlement as insufficient and resumed trial on March 16, 2026.
  - The states hired Jeffrey Kessler, a high-profile antitrust attorney. This signals serious intent, not pro-forma litigation.
  - CEO Rapino testified on March 19, 2026, with his compensation reportedly linked to resolving antitrust issues. This suggests the company itself views this as material.
  - Harvard antitrust scholars called the DOJ deal "just a Band-Aid." The states are "essentially fed up" after 16 years of battles.
  - The thesis assigns 25% probability to "states force meaningful structural remedies." Given that 36 states are actively pursuing structural remedies with dedicated counsel, 30-35% is more realistic.
  - Even behavioral remedies (15% fee cap, competing ticketing platforms allowed) could erode Ticketmaster's pricing power. The thesis treats the DOJ settlement terms as "manageable" but hasn't quantified the revenue/margin impact of a 15% fee cap at ALL venues (not just owned ones) or true third-party ticketing access.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** Increase bear case probability from 25% to 30-35%. Quantify the margin impact of settlement terms (15% fee cap, third-party platforms) being extended to ALL venues via state remedies, not just LYV-owned ones. If states succeed in expanding the fee cap and platform access, Ticketmaster's monopoly pricing power erodes significantly.

### 4. Negative GAAP EPS Despite Record Revenue

- **The thesis claim:** EPS went negative ($-0.24) due to non-cash items. AOI grew 10% to $2.4B. "Real earnings power masked by accounting."
- **Evidence against:**
  - The FA's own META-REFLECTION flagged this as a red flag requiring investigation. Yet the thesis dismisses it as "non-cash items" without fully decomposing the gap.
  - LYV reported net income of $496M but EPS was negative -- this likely reflects preferred shares, minority interest, or dilution that the AOI metric strips out. These are real economic costs to common shareholders.
  - Interest expense was ~$350M in 2025 (guided ~$280M for 2026 after refinancing). On $10.4B gross debt, this is a real ongoing cash drain.
  - When a company has $25.2B revenue, $2.4B AOI, but NEGATIVE EPS, the distance between management's preferred metric and GAAP reality is a warning sign. Management is incentivized to use AOI because it makes them look better.
  - AOI is a non-GAAP metric that excludes: depreciation, amortization, stock-based compensation, loss on operations, and other items. At LYV's scale, these "excluded" items can be $1B+.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Decompose the path from $2.4B AOI to negative EPS. What specifically is being excluded? D&A (~$650M), SBC, interest expense ($350M), minority interests, and what else? If the "excluded" items total $2.5B+ on $2.4B AOI, the real earnings power is minimal at the common equity level.

### 5. Consumer Discretionary Vulnerability in Current Macro

- **The thesis claim:** Experience economy resilience. Concerts are "last discretionary cut." 2026 ticket sales up double-digits.
- **Evidence against:**
  - Concert ticket prices rose 32% over the past 5 years. Average ticket cost $130.36 in 2024. Dynamic pricing creates significant consumer backlash (Lady Gaga $600+ nosebleed seats).
  - Music festivals face structural challenges: production costs up, post-pandemic debts, generational shifts. Some festivals (When We Were Young) are pausing in 2026.
  - The FTC is suing Ticketmaster for deceptive pricing practices -- separate from the DOJ/state antitrust cases. This adds a THIRD regulatory front.
  - Oil at $95-100 creates indirect consumer spending pressure. If recession materializes in 2H 2026, attendance could decline -- the thesis itself acknowledges "MODERATE-HIGH" recession sensitivity.
  - Gen Z and Millennials are "starting to rethink their entertainment dollars" per industry research.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** This risk is somewhat mitigated by the WATCHLIST verdict (not buying now). However, if the entry trigger ($115-120) is reached via recession-driven selloff, the recession risk is highest precisely when the stock is cheapest. The thesis should explicitly address: would you buy at $115 during a recession, given attendance may decline 10-15%?

## Independent Bear-Case Valuation

**Method: EV/AOI at bear multiple with higher maintenance capex**

Assumptions:
- AOI 2026E: $2.5B (conservative, below thesis's $2.7B)
- Multiple: 11x EV/AOI (bear multiple reflecting ongoing litigation, macro risk, thin margins)
- Net debt: $3.3B
- Shares: 235M

Calculation:
- EV = $2.5B x 11 = $27.5B
- Equity = $27.5B - $3.3B = $24.2B
- FV/share = $24.2B / 235M = **$103**

Alternative: Normalized maintenance FCF with stress assumptions:
- OCF: $1.4B
- Maintenance capex: $500M (conservative)
- Normalized FCF: $900M
- At 4.5% FCF yield: Market cap $20B / 235M = **$85**
- At 3.5% FCF yield: Market cap $25.7B / 235M = **$109**

**DA bear FV: $100 (weighted average of above methods)**

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- The DCF tool is completely broken for LYV ($4-15 FV vs $148 market price). This means the standard quantitative anchor is missing. The entire valuation rests on non-standard methods (EV/AOI, normalized FCF yield), which are more subjective.
- I could not verify the FA's $1.3B adjusted FCF figure independently. Management's adjusted FCF definition may include add-backs beyond just growth capex that I cannot identify without reading the 10-K.
- The state antitrust trial outcome is genuinely binary and hard to probability-weight. Legal outcomes are among the most uncertain risks to quantify.

### Limitaciones de Este Analisis
- No access to LYV's 10-K for capex decomposition (growth vs maintenance by management's own filing breakdown).
- No moat_assessment.md or risk_assessment.md from specialist agents to cross-reference.
- Concert attendance data relies on management's reported figures, not independent verification.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- For companies with heavy capex cycles (LYV, infrastructure companies), the DCF tool should accept "normalized FCF" as an override input rather than only trailing GAAP FCF.
- Consider adding a "capex quality" check to the business analysis framework that explicitly flags when capex/D&A > 2x.
