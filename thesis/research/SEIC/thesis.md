## TL;DR

SEI Investments is a high-quality financial technology and asset management platform ($1.9T in administered/managed assets) with exceptional ROIC (29%), net cash balance sheet, 16.8% insider ownership, and expanding margins. The stock trades at 14.6x P/E -- historically cheap for a Tier A compounder -- but the low valuation reflects slow revenue growth (~5% CAGR) and the market's skepticism about whether the Stratos acquisition ($441M) and platform investments will accelerate growth. At $81.44, the market implies only 4.5% FCF growth -- essentially baking in no acceleration. If SEIC can deliver 6-7% top-line growth through Stratos integration and alternative asset manager wins, meaningful upside exists.

## Business Understanding

### What SEI Does

SEI Investments provides technology-driven investment processing, operations, and asset management solutions to financial institutions, advisors, and wealth managers. Think of it as the "operating system" for wealth management firms -- they outsource their back-office, middle-office, and investment management operations to SEI's platform.

### Revenue Model (FY2025, $2.3B total)

| Segment | Revenue | % Total | Op Margin | Description |
|---------|---------|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Investment Managers | $220.8M (Q4) | ~36% | 41% | Platform for alternative + traditional asset managers |
| Investment Advisors | $156.2M (Q4) | ~26% | 46% | Wealth platform for RIAs and advisors |
| Private Banks | $149.8M (Q4) | ~25% | 19% | Technology for private banks and trust companies |
| Institutional Investors | $72.8M (Q4) | ~12% | 47% | OCIO solutions for pensions, endowments |
| New Businesses | $8.3M (Q4) | ~1% | Negative | Stratos, new ventures |

**Revenue mix**: ~60% asset-based fees (tied to AUM/AUA), ~40% platform/processing fees. This creates moderate market sensitivity but with high switching costs.

### Unit Economics

- **Client retention**: Very high (multi-year contracts, deep integration). Once a bank or asset manager is on SEI's platform, switching costs are measured in years and millions of dollars
- **FCF Margin**: 24.2% (FY2025) -- excellent for the sector
- **Capital intensity**: Asset-light. Capex/Depreciation at 1.7x suggests modest growth investment. SBC/Revenue at 2.3% is well-controlled
- **LTV/CAC**: Not directly disclosed but implied by 95%+ retention and 20+ year client relationships. Very favorable

### Margin Structure

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Trend |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| Gross Margin | 52.5% | 51.3% | 53.1% | 53.5% | Expanding |
| Operating Margin | 23.9% | 22.1% | 26.0% | 27.3% | Expanding |
| FCF Margin | 24.7% | 20.2% | 26.6% | 24.2% | Stable-volatile |
| Net Margin | N/A | N/A | N/A | 31.1% | Strong |

Operating margin expansion from 22.1% to 27.3% over 3 years is noteworthy -- driven by operating leverage on the platform business and strong cost discipline.

### Competitive Position

SEIC operates in a fragmented but sticky market:
- **Wealth Platform**: Competes with FNZ, Tegra118/Broadridge, InvestCloud, Envestnet. SEI's platform serves ~$1.9T in assets. Not dominant #1 but deeply entrenched with existing clients.
- **Investment Operations Outsourcing**: Competes with State Street, BNY Mellon, Northern Trust for fund admin. SEIC is smaller but more technology-focused.
- **Asset Management**: $554.6B AUM across proprietary strategies. Mid-tier scale.

SEIC is best described as a **niche platform champion** -- not the largest in any segment, but deeply integrated with clients and extremely difficult to displace.

### Stratos Acquisition ($441M)

Completed initial close Q4 2025 for 57.5% of Stratos Wealth Holdings:
- 350 advisors, $38.4B client assets across 29 states
- Expands SEIC into advice distribution (previously only technology/operations)
- Strategic logic: combine SEIC's platform + operations with Stratos's advisor network
- Risk: integration execution, goodwill increase (goodwill/total assets rose from 6.3% to 10.9%)
- Uses balance sheet cash -- no debt needed (net cash position maintained)

## Why Is It Cheap?

### Market Narrative

The market sees SEIC as a **slow grower** in a world that pays premium multiples for growth:

1. **Revenue CAGR only ~5%**: In a sector where SPGI grows 8-10% and TW grows 15%+, SEIC's 5% organic growth looks pedestrian
2. **AUM/Fee compression risk**: Passive investing, fee compression, and mutual fund outflows continue to pressure revenue per dollar of AUM
3. **Stratos uncertainty**: $441M acquisition of an advisor network is a strategic pivot -- market unsure if it will work or dilute returns
4. **Market sensitivity**: 60% asset-based revenue means a bear market hits SEIC's revenue directly
5. **No clear growth catalyst**: Unlike MORN (CRSP), SPGI (ratings cycle), or MSCI (ETF licensing), SEIC lacks an obvious growth accelerant

## Moat Assessment

### Type: Switching Costs + Scale Economies (Narrow-Wide)

**Switching Costs (Primary)**: SEI's platform is deeply embedded in clients' operations. A bank or asset manager running on SEI's investment processing platform would need 12-24 months and millions of dollars to migrate to a competitor. This is evidenced by:
- 20+ year client relationships
- 95%+ estimated retention rate
- Record net sales events in FY2025 ($149.9M)

**Scale Economies**: SEIC's fixed costs are spread across $1.9T in administered assets. Each incremental dollar of AUA/AUM has minimal marginal cost, driving operating leverage (margin expanding from 22% to 27%).

**NOT a wide moat because**:
- No #1 market position in any single segment
- No regulatory moat (unlike SPGI/MCO in ratings)
- No network effects (unlike exchanges)
- Platform is replaceable in theory, even if switching costs are high in practice

**Moat Score: 18/25**

### Durability

- Switching costs are STRUCTURAL and increasing as platforms become more complex
- Alternative asset manager outsourcing trend is secular (two-thirds of Q4 Investment Manager wins from US alternative managers)
- Risk: AI-driven operations could eventually reduce switching costs if platforms become more interoperable (5-10 year timeframe)

## Valuation

### Method 1: Owner Earnings Yield (Primary, 50% weight)

```
FCF (FY2025):         $555M
Depreciation:         ~$65M (estimated)
Maintenance Capex:    ~$72M (Dep * 1.1)
Owner Earnings:       $555M - $72M + $65M = $548M
Market Cap:           $10.0B
OEY:                  5.5%

OEY + Expected Growth = 5.5% + 7.0% = 12.5%
vs WACC 9.4% = spread of +3.1pp (favorable)
```

At current prices, SEIC offers a 12.5% total expected return (OEY + growth) vs 9.4% cost of capital. Attractive.

For fair value, using a target OEY of ~4.5% (consistent with Tier A compounders at this growth rate):
```
FV = $548M / 4.5% = $12.2B market cap
FV/share = $12.2B / 122.5M shares = ~$99
```

### Method 2: DCF (30% weight)

From tool output:
- **Bear**: $66.69 (2% growth, 10.5% WACC)
- **Base**: $83.02 (5% growth, 9% WACC)
- **Bull**: $106.15 (8% growth, 8% WACC)

**Sensitivity Assessment**: FV Spread 74%, TV 74.5% of EV. HIGH sensitivity. The DCF is unreliable as a point estimate -- use as range.

The base case at $83 assumes only 5% growth -- consistent with historical but NOT with my thesis of 6-7% growth from Stratos + alternatives momentum. Using 6.5% growth (my base projection): implied FV ~$88-93.
