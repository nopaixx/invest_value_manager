# Counter-Analysis: NDAQ (Nasdaq, Inc.)

## Date: 2026-03-20

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis is directionally sound -- NDAQ is an excellent business priced for near-perfect execution. The thesis already identifies the key vulnerability (19% overvalued at market) and sets a reasonable entry at $62. However, three challenges require attention: (1) the ROIC spread is dangerously thin at 0.3pp above WACC, making value creation marginal at best; (2) the Adenza acquisition created $15.2B in goodwill (46% of assets) at 31x EBITDA -- an aggressive price that requires years of flawless execution to justify; and (3) the reverse DCF confirms the market is pricing in 17.4% FCF growth vs 8.6% historical, with an unfavorable asymmetry ratio of 0.68x. The thesis survives scrutiny but the entry at $62 may still be too generous for a Tier B name with this leverage profile.

## Assumptions Challenged

### 1. ROIC Recovery Trajectory (CRITICAL)

**FA assumption:** ROIC is "recovering" from 5.5% (2023) to 9.3% (2025) and will continue upward as Adenza synergies flow through. ROIC above WACC (9.3% vs 9.0%) signals value creation.

**Counter-evidence:**
- ROIC 9.3% vs WACC 9.0% = spread of 0.3pp. This is NEGLIGIBLE. Within margin of error. A company earning 0.3pp above its cost of capital is barely creating value -- it is destroying value on a risk-adjusted basis once you account for estimation uncertainty in WACC.
- Pre-acquisition (2022) ROIC was 10.5% -- only 1.5pp above WACC even without Adenza debt. This was never a high-ROIC business relative to basket peers.
- Basket comparison: TW (Tradeweb) ROIC ~17%, WKL.AS ROIC ~12-14% (pre-AI scare), MORN ROIC ~20%+. NDAQ at 9.3% is the LOWEST quality member by ROIC in the Data & Analytics Monopolies basket.
- Adenza paid 31x EBITDA. At that price, even with full synergy realization, the ROIC on the incremental $10.5B invested may never exceed the cost of capital. The goodwill ($15.2B, 46% of total assets) makes the denominator permanently inflated.
- The non-GAAP operating margin (56%) is flattering but strips out ~$400M/yr in acquisition amortization. This amortization represents REAL economic cost -- the premium paid for Adenza. Ignoring it overstates profitability.

**Severity:** HIGH
**Resolution:** The committee should require ROIC to exceed 11% (2pp above WACC) before considering NDAQ as basket-quality. At current trajectory, this requires 2-3 more years of execution. The $72 FV should be stress-tested against a scenario where ROIC plateaus at 9-10% permanently.

### 2. Adenza Integration Creates Durable Value (MODERATE-HIGH)

**FA assumption:** Adenza integration is "ahead of schedule" with 42 cross-sells completed, $100M AI efficiency target, and FinTech revenue growing 13% organically.

**Counter-evidence:**
- Cross-sell count (42) is a vanity metric without revenue attribution. How much incremental ARR do these 42 cross-sells represent? The thesis does not quantify this. A cross-sell could be $50K or $5M -- the number alone is meaningless.
- Sell-side research flags potential 10% reduction in FY26 EPS due to slower capital markets activity and underperformance in the Financial Technology segment (Adenza). This is the segment that MUST deliver for the bull case to work.
- The $10.5B price at 31x EBITDA was the HIGHEST multiple Thoma Bravo extracted for a portfolio company. Nasdaq competed against no other buyer. This was a seller's market deal.
- Goodwill trajectory is concerning: 38.8% (2022) to 46.3% (2025) of total assets. If Adenza revenue growth decelerates to low single digits, impairment testing becomes a real risk. A 20% goodwill impairment ($3B) would wipe out 1.5 years of FCF.
- Deferred revenue growth is decelerating: +66% (2023, Adenza added), +20% (2024), +10% (2025). This suggests the initial pipeline conversion surge is normalizing.

**Severity:** MODERATE-HIGH
**Resolution:** Monitor FinTech segment organic ARR growth (not just revenue). If ARR growth decelerates below 10% for 2 quarters, the synergy narrative is stalling. The thesis KC2 (Solutions <7%) may be too lenient -- FinTech specifically should be tracked.

### 3. Market Correctly Prices Exchange/Data Moat Premium (MODERATE)

**FA assumption:** The market is still anchoring to "Nasdaq = exchange" when 77% is Solutions. Implies the market is wrong and a re-rating will occur.

**Counter-evidence:**
- The market knows about the transformation. Analyst consensus PT is $102-$112. TD Cowen upgraded to Buy (Feb 26, 2026). Morgan Stanley maintains Overweight. 86% of analysts rate Buy or Strong Buy. The transformation is consensus, not contrarian.
- If our FV ($72) is 30-35% below consensus PT ($102-$112), WE are the outliers, not the market. The burden of proof is on us to explain why 14 analysts are all wrong.
- NDAQ trades at 28x P/E, which is actually BELOW peers: SPGI ~32x, MSCI ~35x, ICE ~25x (but ICE has less recurring revenue). At 28x with 77% recurring revenue and 9% growth, the valuation is in the fair-to-slightly-expensive range, not wildly overvalued.
- The anti-bullish-bias methodology (60% bear + 40% base) produced FV $72, which is conservative by design. This is appropriate for Tier B, but it means our "edge" may simply be a methodology artifact rather than genuine informational advantage.

**Severity:** MODERATE
**Resolution:** The committee should explicitly articulate what informational edge we have over 14 sell-side analysts who all see $100+. If the answer is "we use more conservative assumptions," that is a methodology choice, not an edge. True edge would require knowing something the analysts do not (e.g., a specific integration failure, a regulatory risk, a competitive threat they have not modeled).

### 4. Index Licensing Revenue Is a Pure Toll-Booth (LOW-MODERATE)

**FA assumption:** Index licensing ($827M, +17% YoY) is the highest-quality revenue stream with toll-booth economics. $882B AUM growing with market.

**Counter-evidence:**
- 70% of index revenue is AUM-linked. AUM = market price x units. In a sustained bear market (-30%), AUM drops mechanically by 30%, and index revenue drops by ~$175M (21% of index revenue). This is NOT recurring in the traditional sense -- it is market-dependent.
- The +17% YoY growth is primarily driven by market appreciation and ETF inflows, not organic pricing power. If markets are flat for 3 years, index growth reverts to 3-5% (unit growth only).
- Competition from self-indexing is real. Vanguard, BlackRock, and large asset managers are increasingly creating proprietary indices to avoid licensing fees. While this has not materially impacted Nasdaq-100 licensing yet, it represents a long-term secular risk.
- QQQ (Invesco) concentration: A significant portion of Nasdaq index AUM is in a single ETF. If Invesco faces outflows or decides to launch a competing index product, the revenue concentration becomes apparent.

**Severity:** LOW-MODERATE
**Resolution:** The thesis correctly identifies this risk under KC7. No adjustment needed, but the bull case scenario ($882B growing to $1T+) should acknowledge that this requires continued equity market appreciation, which is not guaranteed in the current geopolitical environment.

### 5. Leverage Is Being Actively Managed (LOW-MODERATE)

**FA assumption:** Net debt/EBITDA 2.9x is being actively reduced ($826M repaid in 2025) and will reach 2.0x by end-2027.

**Counter-evidence:**
- Debt-to-equity of 0.82 is worse than 70% of capital markets industry peers (industry median 0.29). This is not "average" leverage -- it is high relative to the sector.
- Interest expense at ~$370M/yr reduces FCF conversion. The 7.1x interest coverage is adequate but leaves limited margin for EBITDA softness.
- Deleveraging assumes consistent FCF generation AND no additional acquisitions. Management's stated strategy is "technology-led transformation" which historically implies more M&A. If another acquisition occurs before reaching 2.0x target, the leverage thesis breaks.
- In a rising rate environment (current), refinancing the existing $8.6B debt stack becomes more expensive. If rates stay elevated, the $826M annual debt repayment may slow as management weighs prepayment costs.

**Severity:** LOW-MODERATE
**Resolution:** KC1 (leverage below 2.5x by end-2026) is appropriate. No adjustment needed. The risk is more about WHAT IF they stop deleveraging to pursue M&A rather than inability to delever.

### Interpretation

The thesis is fundamentally sound in its conclusion (WATCHLIST at $62 entry). The business quality is real -- exchange monopoly, index licensing, regulatory tech, and anti-financial-crime all have durable competitive positions. However, the thesis understates three issues:

1. **The ROIC problem is structural, not transitional.** Even pre-Adenza, ROIC was only 10.5%. The $10.5B acquisition at 31x EBITDA permanently inflated the capital base. ROIC may never exceed 12% unless Adenza delivers exceptional organic growth.

2. **No identified informational edge.** Our FV of $72 is a methodology artifact (60/40 bear/base weighting), not a differentiated view. We do not know something the market does not know. We simply use more conservative assumptions. This is prudent but not alpha-generating.

3. **The entry at $62 requires a 28% decline from current.** Without a specific catalyst (earnings miss, leverage covenant concern, acquisition gone wrong), this entry may never be reached. The stock has strong downside support from buyback activity and institutional demand.

## Recommendation to Investment Committee

1. **MAINTAIN WATCHLIST status.** Both FA and DA agree the stock is overvalued at $86.

2. **Question the $62 entry.** At $62, MoS vs DA bear FV ($56) is only 10%. For a Tier B name with 2.9x leverage and 0.3pp ROIC spread, the committee should consider whether $55-58 would be more appropriate to provide adequate downside protection.

3. **Add a ROIC-specific KC.** Current KCs do not include an explicit ROIC threshold. Suggest adding: "KC8: ROIC fails to exceed 11% by end-2027." This ensures the Adenza acquisition is creating value, not just growing revenue.

4. **Quantify cross-sell revenue.** Before advancing to R3, request management disclosure (from earnings calls or investor day) on the revenue impact of the 42 cross-sells. Without this data, the "ahead of schedule" narrative is unverifiable.

5. **Articulate informational edge before deploying capital.** If the committee cannot identify what we know that 14 Buy-rated analysts do not, the position should remain low priority within the basket pipeline. Capital is better deployed into SPGI (Tier A, cleaner balance sheet) or existing basket positions with identified edge.

6. **Reduce DCF weight in reconciliation.** Given FV Spread 103% and TV 74.5% of EV, the DCF is unreliable for NDAQ. The EV/EBIT method is more appropriate for a company in post-acquisition integration. Consider 40% DCF / 60% EV/EBIT (inverting current weights).

## META-REFLECTION

### Doubts/Uncertainties
- The ROIC calculation depends heavily on how goodwill and intangibles are treated. If we strip Adenza goodwill and measure ROIC on organic capital, the picture may look different (possibly 15%+). The thesis and my challenge both use total-capital ROIC, which may penalize acquisition-driven growth strategies unfairly. However, goodwill represents real capital deployed and should be included.
- The "no informational edge" finding is concerning but honest. If our only advantage is conservative methodology, we are not discovering mispriced securities -- we are applying a haircut to everything and hoping the market comes to us. This may be a broader system issue, not NDAQ-specific.

### Limitations of This Analysis
- No access to Adenza-specific financial breakdowns (pre-acquisition standalone financials). The cross-sell revenue impact is impossible to verify without management disclosure.
- Smart money graph does not contain NDAQ (not enrolled). Institutional holder analysis was limited to insider_tracker.py output.
- I could not find specific analyst bear cases with quantified downside scenarios -- most coverage is bullish, making adversarial research harder.

### Suggestions for the System

- Consider enrolling NDAQ in the smart money graph for institutional holder tracking, especially given Investor AB's significant purchase.
