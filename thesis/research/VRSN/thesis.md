## Business Understanding

### What VeriSign Does

VeriSign operates the authoritative registry for .com and .net domain names. Every time anyone in the world registers, renews, or queries a .com or .net domain, VeriSign's infrastructure processes that transaction. The company does NOT sell domains directly to consumers -- it operates the backend registry that all ~2,500+ registrars (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Google Domains, etc.) must connect to.

**The analogy:** VeriSign is the toll booth on the internet highway. Every .com domain pays VeriSign ~$10.26/year, regardless of which registrar the customer uses. With 173.5 million .com/.net domains, that is $1.66B in annual revenue from a recurring, non-discretionary fee.

### Revenue Model

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Revenue source | Wholesale registry fees per domain per year |
| Current price | $10.26/domain/year (.com), ~$9.02 (.net) |
| Domain base | 173.5M (.com + .net), growing 2.6% YoY |
| Revenue split | ~95% registry services, ~5% security services |
| Recurrence | ~75% renewal rate, highly predictable |
| Customer concentration | No single registrar >15% of revenue |

### Unit Economics

This is perhaps the most capital-efficient business in existence:
- **Revenue per domain:** ~$10.26/year
- **Cost per domain:** ~$1.21/year (88.2% gross margin)
- **Capex per domain:** ~$0.03/year ($55-65M capex / 173.5M domains)
- **FCF per domain:** ~$6.17/year (64.5% FCF margin on $10.26)
- **LTV/CAC:** Effectively infinite -- VeriSign does not acquire customers. Registrars do.
- **Incremental margin on new domain:** ~95%+ (virtually zero incremental cost)

### Capital Requirements: ULTRA-LIGHT

- **Capex:** $55-65M guided for 2026 (elevated for equipment refresh + AI demand), typical run-rate ~$40M
- **Capex/Revenue:** ~3.5% (vs 10%+ for typical tech)
- **Working capital:** Negative -- deferred revenue ($1.1B) means customers pay in advance
- **Maintenance capex:** ~$30M (servers, DNS infrastructure)
- **Growth capex:** Essentially zero -- more domains run on the same infrastructure

The .com registry contract is the core asset. Key terms:

## 🔄 META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- **ROIC distortion:** The 275% ROIC is mathematically meaningless due to negative tangible equity. The QS tool handles this correctly (gives 15/15 for spread, which IS correct -- the economics ARE exceptional). But the ROIC Persistence score (7/7) may be inflated if historical ROIC was also calculated on distorted equity. This doesn't change the Tier A classification but is worth noting.
- **Receivables anomaly:** Narrative checker shows receivables growth 37.5% vs revenue growth 6.4%. This is a large divergence. Could be timing (domain renewals concentrated at quarter-end) or a genuine collection issue. Needs investigation before R4.
- **FY2025 FCF margin jump:** 64.5% vs trailing 3yr average of 58.3%. The +620bps jump is large. Could be one-time (lower capex year, working capital benefit) or genuine operating leverage from price increases. I used 62% for FY2026E which is conservative, but need to understand the driver.
- **Negative equity:** Standard valuation frameworks break with negative equity. P/B is meaningless. EV/EBIT and OEY are the right methods, but this is an unusual profile.

- **quality_scorer.py Market Position:** Should auto-detect monopoly positions (e.g., if market share >80% in defined industry, auto-score 8/8). Currently defaults to 0 requiring manual adjustment.
