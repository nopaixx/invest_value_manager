## Key Assumptions Challenged

### 1. Serial Acquirer QS Adjustment: +18 (60 -> 78, Tier B -> Tier A)

**R1 Claim:** The QS tool's raw score of 60 is "severely distorted" by IHS Markit goodwill. Tangible ROIC is 63%+. Adjustment: +18 points to reach 78 (Tier A).

**Counter-Evidence:**
- The +18 adjustment is at the UPPER BOUND of precedent (+20 max, MORN at +18). This is not a moderate adjustment -- it is aggressive.
- The adjustment adds +12 points just for ROIC, which assumes tangible ROIC is the "real" ROIC. BUT: SPGI PAID $44B in real economic value for IHS Markit. That capital IS deployed. The fact that it shows as goodwill does not mean it was not a real capital allocation decision. Stripping it entirely overstates the return on ACTUAL capital deployed.
- **Partial-goodwill ROIC is the correct framework** (established in decisions_log for HLNE, MORN, FDS). The precedent is to use a BLENDED approach: partial strip of goodwill for organic business + recognize acquisition capital as real investment. Pure tangible ROIC of 63% dramatically overstates the economic return on total invested capital.
- Using partial-goodwill (strip 50% of goodwill): Invested Capital = ~$8B tangible + $17.75B (50% goodwill) = $25.75B. NOPAT ~$5.1B / $25.75B = **19.8% ROIC**. This is 19.8% vs WACC 9.6% = +10.2pp spread. That earns **12/15 on ROIC** (same as thesis), but the reasoning is MORE HONEST.
- The +8 for Market Position is justified (undisputed #1-2 positions).
- **Net assessment: QS adjustment should be +14 to +16, not +18.** This places QS at 74-76. At 74, SPGI is Tier B (high-end). At 76, it is Tier A (very low-end). This matters because Tier B requires ~20-25% MoS (precedent), not 10-15%.

**Severity: MODERATE**
**Resolution:** Adjust QS to 74-76 range. If 74 (Tier B), the MoS requirement increases to ~17-20%, which makes the $420 SO more appropriate but the $442 market price less attractive.

### 3. Revenue Growth 7% Assumed Sustainable -- But Deceleration is the Trend

**R1 Claim:** Revenue growth 7% CAGR sustainable, with margin expansion of 50-75bps/yr.

**Counter-Evidence:**
- Revenue growth trajectory: 11.8% (2023) -> 13.7% (2024) -> 7.9% (2025). The direction is DOWN.
- Management guides 6-8% organic CC growth for 2026. The midpoint is 7%, but the LOW END is 6%.
- The 2024 revenue growth of 13.7% was inflated by a refinancing boom in Ratings. Management explicitly guided "low to mid-single digit" billed issuance growth for 2026 -- this is a DECELERATION from +12% in 2025.
- Market Intelligence organic CC growth was only 5% in 2025 -- the WEAKEST segment. This is 36% of revenue growing at 5%.
- If MI grows 5%, Ratings normalizes to 5-6% (management guide), and only Indices maintains 10%+, the blended growth rate is closer to 6-6.5%, not 7-8%.
- The thesis uses management's GUIDED range as the base case. Management has a history of guiding conservatively and beating, BUT: (a) the Q4 2025 EPS missed consensus ($4.30 vs $4.32), which is unusual for SPGI, and (b) the 2026 guidance miss that triggered the -24% drop suggests management is NOT being overly conservative this time -- they are flagging real headwinds.

**Severity: MODERATE**
**Resolution:** Use 6.5% as base case (low-end of management guide + MI deceleration), not 7%. This reduces the FV by approximately 5-8%.

### 4. FCF Decline in 2025 Dismissed -- Margin Expansion Projected Despite Evidence

**R1 Claim:** FCF margin expanding from 35.6% to 37.5% by 2028. IHS Markit synergies continue.

**Counter-Evidence:**
- FCF DECLINED from $5.57B (2024) to $5.46B (2025) -- a 2% drop while revenue grew 7.9%. This is a NEGATIVE sign.
- FCF margin: 39.2% (2024) -> 35.6% (2025). This is a 360bps CONTRACTION, not expansion.
- The thesis attributes this to "merger integration costs" but integration costs should be DECLINING 3 years post-merger. If FCF margin is falling despite integration cost reductions, something else is consuming cash.
- Capex/Depreciation rose from 0.8x (2022) to 1.8x (2025) -- investment intensity is INCREASING, not normalizing.
- OCF/Net Income dropped from 1.5x (2024) to 1.3x (2025) -- cash conversion is DETERIORATING.
- The thesis projects FCF margin expanding to 36.5-37.5% without addressing WHY 2025 saw contraction. This is projecting the future as better than the present without explaining the present deterioration.

**Severity: HIGH**
**Resolution:** Use trailing 3-year average FCF margin (34.4%) as base case, not projected 36.5%+. FCF expansion is an upside scenario, not a base case, until the 2025 contraction is explained.

### 5. Receivables Growth (+20% vs +8% Revenue) -- Unresolved Red Flag

**R1 Claim:** "Flagged as anomaly, may be Q4 timing."

**Counter-Evidence:**
- Receivables growing 2.5x faster than revenue is a classic earnings quality deterioration signal.
- The thesis speculates this could be "Q4 issuance surge billed late" but provides NO EVIDENCE for this claim. This is hope, not analysis.
- Possible benign explanations: Q4 issuance timing, large enterprise deals with extended payment terms, Ratings upfront billing for annual subscriptions.
- Possible malign explanations: Channel stuffing, deterioration in customer credit quality, recognition of revenue on deals where collection is uncertain, MI customers delaying payments (competitive pressure).
- The thesis has an existing kill condition (#8) for receivables growth exceeding revenue growth by >10pp for 2+ quarters. At 20% vs 8% (12pp gap), this is ALREADY EXCEEDING the kill condition threshold in its first observation.
- Without the detailed 10-K breakdown of receivables by segment, we cannot resolve this. But the thesis should NOT dismiss it as "probably timing."

**Severity: HIGH**
**Resolution:** This should be a HARD GATE for deployment. Do not set standing order until FY2025 10-K receivables detail can be verified AND Q1 2026 shows normalization.

### 6. Mobility Spinoff Creates Valuation Uncertainty

**R1 Claim:** "Mobility spinoff (Form 10 in Q2 2026) -- could unlock value."

**Counter-Evidence:**
- The spinoff creates NEAR-TERM UNCERTAINTY, not immediate value:
  - Mobility contributes ~$1.8B revenue (12% of total) and ~35% adj OP margin (~$630M EBIT).
  - Post-spinoff RemainCo loses this revenue and EBIT, but the proportional reduction in overhead/debt allocation is uncertain.
  - If Mobility trades at 12-15x EBITDA ($960M EBITDA -> $11.5-14.4B EV), the standalone Mobility could be worth $38-$48/share to SPGI holders. But the market may apply a discount to spinoff companies.
  - RemainCo (ex-Mobility) would have higher margins (~53%+ adj OP margin vs 50.4% blended) but lower growth and revenue diversification.
  - The R1 FV of $511 is calculated on COMBINED SPGI (including Mobility). Post-spinoff, the sum-of-parts may or may not exceed $511. The thesis does not model this.
- **Execution risk:** Loss of shared services synergies, one-time separation costs, debt reallocation, management distraction.
- The stock dropped 10% after the spinoff announcement -- the market is NOT treating this as pure value-unlocking.
- SPGI shares slid 10% after the Mobility unit rebrand ahead of the spinoff, suggesting market skepticism about the separation.

**Severity: MODERATE**
**Resolution:** The FV should either be modeled pre-spinoff (current) OR post-spinoff (two entities). Claiming value-unlocking without modeling both scenarios is incomplete analysis.

### 7. WIDE Moat Largely Correct -- But Market Intelligence is NARROW and AI Risk is Real

**R1 Claim:** WIDE moat across all segments. AI "enhances data toll-booths."

**Counter-Evidence:**
- I AGREE that Ratings and Indices moats are near-impenetrable (regulatory + brand + network).
- I AGREE that Commodity Insights (Platts) has a strong benchmark monopoly.
- However, Market Intelligence (~36% of revenue) has a NARROW moat:
  - Bloomberg is the dominant competitor and is actively developing AI-native tools.
  - Capital IQ faces competition from AI-powered alternatives that can aggregate financial data at lower cost.
  - Seeking Alpha analysis notes that "Moody's regulatory-embedded models offer greater resilience than S&P Global's Market Intelligence" against AI disruption.
  - MI organic growth of only 5% (2025) in a sector growing 8-10% suggests MARKET SHARE PRESSURE, not just cyclicality.
  - The "With Intelligence" acquisition mentioned in the thesis is a SMALL acquisition that adds niche alternative data -- it does not fundamentally change the competitive position vs Bloomberg.
- KBRA and other alternative rating agencies have barely dented the Big Three's >90% combined market share, confirming the Ratings moat. This is POSITIVE evidence for the R1 thesis.
- **Net assessment:** The moat is WIDE for 62% of revenue (Ratings + Indices + Commodity Insights) and NARROW for 38% (MI + Mobility). The blended moat rating should be WIDE with a segment qualification, as the R1 states. This is an area where the R1 is largely correct.

**Severity: LOW**
**Resolution:** The moat assessment is acceptable. The risk is not moat erosion but rather margin compression in MI from competitive investment, which affects the margin expansion assumption (see Challenge #4).
