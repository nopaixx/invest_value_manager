## DA Challenges Resolution

### 1. Microsoft Defender Partnership Loss (HIGH) — **ACCEPTED**

DA is correct. The May 2024 termination of Qualys as default VM provider in Microsoft Defender for Cloud is a MATERIAL event omitted from R1. It validates hyperscaler competition as PROVEN, not hypothetical. Microsoft didn't just threaten — it REPLACED Qualys with MDVM. This weakens the switching costs component of the moat assessment.

**Impact on thesis:** Moat assessment should be downgraded from "moderate switching costs" to "moderate switching costs for direct customers, but OEM/hyperscaler channel is ZERO switching cost." Revenue impact unclear but the precedent is established. AWS Inspector and GCP Security Command Center are the next potential disintermediation vectors.

**R1 process failure:** This should have been caught. Adding "material events in past 24 months" to R1 checklist in meta-reflection.

### 2. Platformization Severity (HIGH) — **PARTIALLY ACCEPTED**

DA presents compelling evidence: TENB -50%, RPD -80%, all 12 mid-cap cyber stocks declined in 2025. The platformization trend is real and accelerating.

**Where DA is right:** The severity is underweighted in R1. "Manageable" is too dismissive when the #1 VM vendor (Tenable by IDC) is being destroyed.

**Where DA overshoots:** Qualys is NOT Tenable. Qualys has 45% FCF margins (TENB has losses), 70%+ ROIC, net cash, and a compliance-driven customer base that is stickier than vulnerability scanning alone. The CHKP parallel is instructive — Check Point was declared "legacy" for 15 years and still generates $2.9B revenue with 30%+ margins. Qualys could survive as a profitable niche even if it doesn't win the platform war.

**Resolution:** Platformization is the PRIMARY risk (upgraded from secondary). BUT the bear case is "slow margin erosion over 5-10 years" not "collapse like RPD." Apply a -$10 discount to FV for platformization risk premium.

### 3. Hyperscaler Native VM Proven (HIGH) — **ACCEPTED as subset of #1**

This is a corollary of the MSFT Defender loss, not a separate finding. The precedent is set. The key question is whether direct customer relationships (which represent >90% of revenue) are more defensible than OEM/channel relationships. I believe they are — but the ceiling on TAM expansion is lower.

### 4. NRR Declining + Billings Deceleration (MODERATE) — **ACCEPTED**

DA correctly identifies the layered deceleration: billings 5.6% < deferred rev 8% < revenue 10% < ARR 9.5%. This is a classic SaaS deterioration signal. The FA's argument that NRR is "less relevant" for asset-based pricing has merit but doesn't negate the directional trend.

**Resolution:** Add billings growth as primary leading indicator to kill conditions. If Q1 2026 billings <5%, thesis review mandatory.

### 5. FV $128 Too High (MODERATE) — **PARTIALLY ACCEPTED**

DA's conservative anchor of $105-110 is reasonable. The weighted $128 relies on high-sensitivity DCF (TV 74.5%) which I agree is unreliable. However, DA's bear case of $102 is also below market price of $93 — meaning even the DA's bear case says the stock is cheap.

**Resolution:** Post-DA FV = $110 (weighted: 50% conservative DCF $108 + 50% DA-adjusted $112). This is a -14% revision from R1's $128.

### 6. FTNT vs QLYS Capital Allocation (MODERATE) — **ACCEPTED**

FTNT is already in portfolio at $79.12. Both are cybersecurity. FTNT is the platform winner, QLYS is the niche specialist. If platformization is the dominant force (which we now agree it is), capital should flow to the platform (FTNT), not the point solution (QLYS).

**Resolution:** QLYS remains WATCHLIST with SO, but deployment priority is SECONDARY to FTNT. Max 2 cybersec positions per sector view protocol.
