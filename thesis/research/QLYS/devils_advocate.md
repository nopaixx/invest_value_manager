## Calibration (Fase 0.5)

**Market Anchor:** Market at $96.44 implies -7.7% FCF decline for 5 years. Historical FCF CAGR: 20.3%. Gap: 27.9pp. Asymmetry ratio: 11.96x (HIGHLY FAVORABLE -- bull +298% vs bear -25%).

**DA Historical Stats:** 25 corrections, avg -15.7%, median -13.0%. Previous QLYS DA: -14.1% ($128 to $110). All corrections negative.

**Anchor Principle:** The market at $96.44 prices in -7.7% FCF decline -- dramatically pessimistic for a company guiding 7-8% revenue growth with 42%+ FCF margins. Even my bear case should not assume FCF decline of this magnitude. The question is: does the market know something about platformization that we do not?

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis and R3 resolution correctly identify QLYS as a genuinely high-quality business (ROIC 70%+, FCF margin 45%, QS 81 Tier A) at a depressed valuation. The previous DA (Feb 27) raised 3 HIGH findings (MSFT Defender loss omission, platformization severity, hyperscaler native VM) that led to FV reduction from $128 to $110 and a GATED SO at $82. One month later, the stock has RISEN from $93.68 to $96.44 (+2.9%), moving AWAY from the entry target.

**What has changed since the prior DA:** (1) Insider selling continues unabated -- CFO Kim sold another 6,799 shares ($873K) in March 2026 under 10b5-1 plan, bringing total insider sells to 52 with ZERO buys in the past year. (2) Saham Capital published analysis questioning whether QLYS's earnings beat offsets concerns around NRR and insider activity. (3) Q1 2026 earnings date is approaching (expected April) -- the billings gate is the most critical near-term catalyst. (4) Cybersecurity platformization continues to accelerate, with Tenable remaining near 52-week lows.

**Net verdict:** The thesis remains fundamentally sound. QLYS is a genuinely cheap high-quality business -- the reverse DCF asymmetry of 11.96x is the most favorable in the entire pipeline. The R3 FV of $110 is reasonable but should be trimmed to $105 to account for continued insider selling and the narrowing path between "temporary SaaSpocalypse discount" and "structural VM specialist decline." The SO at $82 (GATED on Q1 billings) remains the correct approach. At current $96.44, MoS vs $105 is only 8.9% -- insufficient for a stock with platformization as its primary structural risk.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "Platformization is the primary risk but Qualys can survive as a profitable niche"

- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Tenable (TENB) remains near 52-week lows at $19.38 (down 50% from 52wH). This is the #1 VM vendor by IDC. If the market leader is being destroyed, the #2-3 player (Qualys) faces the same structural forces.
  - Rapid7 (RPD) at $6.39 is down 80% from 52wH. The third VM specialist is being effectively wiped out.
  - CrowdStrike Falcon Exposure Management now integrates with 180+ tools, offering unified RBVM, CAASM, and ITAM from a single console. This directly competes with Qualys's core offering.
  - The "Platform Paradox" analysis (Jan 2026) explicitly states: "The cybersecurity market is in the most aggressive consolidation phase since the early 2010s." Mid-cap specialists ($1.1B-$14.9B) are in a "valuation no-man's land."
  - Google acquired Wiz for $32B, PANW acquired CyberArk for $25B. Platform vendors are spending billions to build what Qualys is trying to achieve organically with TotalCloud at 5% of bookings.
  - The CHKP parallel cited in R3 (Check Point survived as "legacy" for 15 years) is instructive but has limits: CHKP had firewall hardware lock-in that VM scanning does not have. VM is MORE commoditizable than network security.
- **Evidencia a favor (balance):**
  - Qualys has 45% FCF margins vs Tenable's losses. Qualys can survive a prolonged platform war because it generates cash, while competitors burn it.
  - ROIC 70%+ means Qualys creates enormous value from its installed base even if growth slows.
  - Net cash position ($394M) provides buffer. No debt service pressure.
  - Compliance-driven demand (NIS2, DORA, SEC disclosure, FedRAMP) provides regulatory floor for VM spending.
- **Severidad:** HIGH (unchanged from prior DA)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Maintain as primary risk. The Q1 billings gate is the right mechanism. If billings <5% AND NRR <103%, platformization is accelerating. If billings >8%, the threat is manageable for now.

### 2. "Insider selling is routine 10b5-1 activity"

- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - **52 insider sells, ZERO buys in the past year.** This is a stark asymmetry that goes beyond normal 10b5-1 diversification.
  - CFO Kim Joo Mi: sold 4,753 shares (Jan 5, 2026), 3,414 shares (Jan 29), 6,799 shares (Feb 4 / Mar 11). Total: ~15,000 shares in 2.5 months, reducing direct holdings from ~95K to ~88K shares.
  - CEO Thakar: sold ~5K shares ($669K) in Jan 2026. Ownership is only ~0.7% -- very low for a CEO.
  - Chief Legal Officer Posey: sold 65 shares (Mar 13). Even the legal officer is selling.
  - Saham Capital analysis (Mar 18, 2026) explicitly questions: "Does Qualys' Earnings Beat Offset Questions Around Net Dollar Retention And Insider Activity?"
  - The R1 thesis correctly notes the 10b5-1 plans but does NOT verify whether these are pre-scheduled or discretionary-timing plans. Per the S202 Insider Analysis Protocol, we must check: are sells on the same day as awards? Are they monthly same-amount sells? The CFO's sells appear to be on irregular schedules and varying amounts ($873K one time, $438K another), which suggests MODIFIED 10b5-1 plans rather than simple automatic monthly selling.
  - **Counterpoint:** The Courtot Family Trust (10.7%) has NOT sold since 2021. This provides a founder-estate anchor. But the trust is passive -- it does not indicate current management's view.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE (upgraded from LOW in prior DA)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Track insider sell/buy ratio quarterly. If any officer makes a DISCRETIONARY open-market purchase, that would be a strong positive signal. Current 52:0 sell:buy ratio is concerning but not disqualifying given 10b5-1 context.

### 3. "NRR at 103% is stabilizing and less relevant for asset-based pricing"

- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - NRR declined from 104% to 103% in Q4 2025. Management guided for "stable NRR around 103%" in 2026 -- they are NOT projecting improvement.
  - Billings growth in Q4 2025 was 5.6% -- the weakest leading indicator. Average billings growth over the last 4 quarters was 8.3%, itself underwhelming.
  - Deferred revenue growth decelerated from 13.9% (2022) to 11.5% (2024) to 8% (2025). This is a consistent downward trend.
  - The layered deceleration pattern: billings 5.6% < deferred rev 8% < revenue 10% < ARR 9.5%. Each layer is slower than the one behind it. This is a classic SaaS deterioration signal.
  - The FA's argument that NRR is "less relevant for asset-based pricing" has partial merit -- but NRR measures whether EXISTING customers are expanding spend. If they are not (103%), it means cross-sell into ETM/TotalCloud is failing. This directly undermines the platform pivot thesis.
  - Gross retention "above 90%" is vague. Management's reluctance to provide a specific number is itself a yellow flag.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE (unchanged)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** If Q1 2026 NRR drops below 103%, this becomes HIGH severity. If billings growth drops below 5%, it becomes a kill condition.

### 4. "FV of $110 (R3 post-DA) provides adequate upside"

- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - At current price $96.44, MoS vs $110 FV is only 14.1%. While the reverse DCF asymmetry is highly favorable (11.96x), the absolute MoS is below typical Tier A entry precedents (ADBE 31%, NVO 38%, even LULU 15% was the minimum).
  - The DCF tool base gives $160.84 (67% upside!) and even the DCF bear gives $130.15 (35% upside). But the tool uses default 5% growth + current WACC/margins. The thesis correctly noted high DCF sensitivity (TV 74.5%). The tool's $160 is unreliable as a point estimate.
  - Consensus PT at $138.53 is 28% above our $110. We are significantly MORE bearish than the street. This is unusual for a Tier A stock -- normally the DA makes us more conservative, but here we started more conservative than consensus.
  - Our FV of $110 implies forward P/E of ~20x on FY2026E earnings. For a company growing 7-8% with declining NRR, 20x is FAIR, not cheap. The "deep value" narrative requires either (a) growth re-acceleration, or (b) the market being irrationally pessimistic. The reverse DCF suggests (b) -- market prices -7.7% FCF decline, which IS irrational for this business.
  - **However:** The Fundsmith holding (647,806 shares per smart money graph) provides quality validation. Fundsmith is among the most disciplined quality investors globally. Their presence confirms this is not a value trap.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Reduce FV marginally to $105 (from $110) to account for continued insider selling and billings deceleration trend. At $105, SO at $82 provides MoS of 22% -- consistent with Tier A precedents.

### 5. "Microsoft Defender partnership loss was a one-time event"

- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - The previous DA correctly flagged this as a CRITICAL omission from R1 and a HIGH severity finding. The R3 resolution accepted this and incorporated it.
  - No NEW hyperscaler partnership losses have been reported. The Qualys-Microsoft Copilot integration continues.
  - However, the PRECEDENT remains: Microsoft CHOSE to replace Qualys with its own MDVM in Defender for Cloud. AWS Inspector and GCP Security Command Center exist as potential future disintermediation vectors.
  - The risk is not another sudden loss -- it is the GRADUAL erosion of the OEM/embedded channel as hyperscalers build native capabilities.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE (downgraded from HIGH -- the event is now priced in and acknowledged)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Maintain KC#7 from R3 (billings growth <5% for 2 consecutive quarters). The MSFT loss is now adequately reflected in the thesis.

- The platformization risk is the hardest to calibrate. TENB -50% and RPD -80% are concrete evidence, but Qualys's FCF profile is fundamentally different from these peers. Qualys could survive as a profitable niche for a decade (like CHKP) while TENB and RPD collapse. The question is whether "profitable niche" deserves EV/FCF 12x or 8x.
- The insider selling pattern is concerning but may be explained by 10b5-1 plans adopted when the stock was at $150+. Officers may simply be diversifying a concentrated position that has already declined 38% from highs. The ABSENCE of buys at $85-95 levels is the more telling signal.
- I could not find specific data on QLYS customer churn to hyperscaler native tools. The MSFT Defender loss is the only concrete example. If this was isolated (OEM channel, not direct customers), the impact is contained.
