## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis on IHG is competent and already conservative -- the analyst correctly identifies that IHG is overvalued at $133 and sets an entry at $108. However, the thesis has three material weaknesses: (1) it underestimates the severity of the current US RevPAR environment (first non-recessionary decline EVER in 2025, with 2026 forecast at only +0.6%), (2) the $120 FV still implies growth above what the macro environment supports given oil at $96+ and Hormuz crisis, and (3) the missing sector view is not just a procedural gap but reflects genuine analytical blindness about lodging industry dynamics. The thesis survives scrutiny but the FV should be reduced to $105 and the entry tightened to $95.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. US RevPAR Will Recover to +1-2% (Thesis Base Case)

- **Thesis assumes:** RevPAR +1-2% globally, with US as the recovery engine
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - US RevPAR declined -0.3% in 2025 -- the **first non-recessionary RevPAR decline in recorded history** (CoStar/STR data)
  - Full-year occupancy fell 1.2% YoY to 62.3% in 2025
  - 2026 forecast: only +0.6% RevPAR growth (STR/Tourism Economics), with Q1 2026 expected at -0.2%
  - Midweek occupancy (Mon-Wed, historically driven by business travel) has NOT recovered to pre-pandemic levels
  - Business travelers are staying for shorter periods, increasing booking volatility
  - Oil at $96+ is pushing airline fares up ~11%, which directly reduces leisure travel demand to distant destinations
  - Government cutbacks (DOGE) reducing federal travel spending
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution sugerida:** Base case RevPAR should be 0-1%, not 1-2%. This reduces fee revenue growth from 7% to 5-6%, which compounds significantly over 5 years.

### 2. Fee Revenue Growth of 7% is Sustainable

- **Thesis assumes:** System growth 4-5% + RevPAR 1-2% + ancillary 1-2% = 7%
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - If RevPAR is 0-0.5% (current trajectory), fee revenue growth drops to 5-6%
  - Net system growth of 4.7% in FY2025 was strong, but pipeline conversion depends on hotel owners securing financing -- higher rates impede this
  - Rising construction costs and higher-for-longer interest rates make new hotel development less economically attractive for franchisees
  - Franchisee profit margins are being squeezed by brand mandates (technology fees, renovation requirements, loyalty program costs)
  - The thesis counts ancillary fees growing 2-3%, but these are partially extracted from the same franchisee base under economic pressure
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution sugerida:** Fee revenue growth should be 5-6% base case (not 7%). The thesis already models 7% as "management guidance consistent" -- but management guides the bull case, not the base.

### 3. IHG's ROIC of 42% Reflects Genuine Economic Returns

- **Thesis acknowledges but minimizes:** ROIC is "partly inflated by negative equity"
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - IHG has negative book equity due to aggressive share buybacks -- the denominator in ROIC is artificially small
  - Invested Capital for an asset-light franchisor with negative equity is essentially meaningless as a ROIC denominator
  - The ROIC-WACC spread of +32.8pp is mathematically correct but economically misleading -- it suggests IHG generates extraordinary returns per dollar invested, when really the company has stripped out its equity base through financial engineering
  - Compare: Hilton has similar ROIC inflation for the same structural reason
  - The QS tool's +15pp ROIC spread score (max 15 points) is correctly earned, but the headline "42% ROIC" creates a false impression of competitive advantage vs peers who are structurally identical
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution sugerida:** ROIC should be contextualized, not headlined. The actual competitive metric is fee margin (64.8%) and FCF conversion (~100%), not ROIC on a negative-equity base. The +5 QS adjustment partially compensated for this, but the thesis should not rely on ROIC as a differentiator.

### 4. The QS Adjustment from 72 to 77 (Crossing Tier A Boundary) is Justified

- **Thesis argues:** +5 for market position (0/8 in tool, should be 5/8 as #3 globally)
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - The tool scored 0/8 because Market Position is a manual field -- the analyst filled it. The adjustment corrects a data entry issue, not a tool limitation. This is valid.
  - However: crossing the Tier A boundary (75) changes MoS requirements and valuation methodology. A 72 QS (Tier B) requires ~20-25% MoS; a 77 QS (Tier A) requires ~10-15%. This is a meaningful economic difference.
  - IHG is a cyclical business with HIGH recession sensitivity. Tier A typically implies "lower risk of permanent capital loss" -- but hotels are among the most cyclical consumer discretionary businesses. In 2008-09, hotel REITs fell 50-70% and franchise fees dropped 15-20%.
  - The moat score of 11/25 is LOW. A Tier A company should have stronger moat evidence.
  - Insider ownership at 0.0-0.2% is extremely low for a "quality compounder" designation.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution sugerida:** Treat IHG as high-end Tier B (QS 72-74), not borderline Tier A. This means MoS requirement should be 15-20%, not 10%. Entry at $108 (10% MoS) is insufficient; $100 (17% MoS) is more appropriate.

### 5. Greater China is a Growth Opportunity, Not a Risk

- **Thesis frames:** China as 15% of fee revenue with potential recovery tailwind
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - Greater China RevPAR declined -3.2% in H1 2025, -1.6% for full year
  - While Q4 2025 turned positive (+1.1%), recovery is "U-shaped" per CFO Glover
  - IHG has 880+ hotels in China with 550+ in pipeline -- this is massive exposure (largest international operator pipeline in China)
  - Chinese government policies affecting group travel remain a headwind
  - Geopolitical tensions (US-China trade war, tariffs) could escalate and impact international hotel brands
  - The thesis assigns "LOW (25%)" probability to China recovery -- but China downside (further RevPAR declines, regulatory risk, geopolitical escalation) has higher probability than acknowledged
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution sugerida:** China should be modeled as a risk factor (potential -2 to -3% drag on group RevPAR), not a potential tailwind. The 550-property pipeline commitment in China is a concentration risk.
