## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

IHG operates the world's third-largest hotel company by room count. The business model is predominantly **asset-light franchising**: over 80% of rooms are franchised, with the remainder under management contracts and a small number owned/leased. IHG earns fees from hotel owners -- franchise fees (based on room revenue), management fees (base + incentive), and ancillary fees (loyalty program, technology, procurement). The company does NOT own the physical hotels in most cases.

**Revenue structure (FY2025):**
- Total revenue: $5.2B (includes reimbursable costs pass-through)
- **Fee revenue: $1.9B** (the true economic revenue -- this is what IHG earns)
- Fee margin: 64.8% (up 3.6pp YoY) -- exceptionally high and expanding
- Non-fee revenue: pass-through costs (procurement, loyalty program) that are cost-neutral

**Brand portfolio (21 brands):**
- Luxury: Six Senses, Regent, InterContinental, Kimpton
- Premium: Crowne Plaza, voco, Hotel Indigo, EVEN Hotels
- Essentials: Holiday Inn, Holiday Inn Express, Staybridge, Candlewood
- New: Noted Collection (upscale/upper-upscale, launched 2026)

**Geographic mix:** Americas ~55% of fee revenue, EMEAA ~30%, Greater China ~15%

### Unit Economics

The asset-light model means IHG's "unit economics" are fundamentally different from asset-heavy operators:
- **Capital deployed per room added:** Near zero (hotel owners invest $150-300K/room; IHG invests brand + systems)
- **Fee revenue per room:** ~$1,860/year (growing with RevPAR + ancillary fees)
- **Incremental margin on new rooms:** Very high (systems already built, fee scales with no capex)
- **FCF margin:** 15.8% of total revenue, but **~43% of fee revenue** -- exceptional conversion
- **Capex/Depreciation:** 0.5x -- capital-light confirmed
- **FCF conversion:** ~100% of adjusted earnings (management target, achieved in FY2025)
- **SBC/Revenue:** 1.4% -- low and stable (not diluting shareholders)

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The gross margin in the QS tool (31.3%) is misleading because it includes pass-through costs that net to zero. The TRUE margin metric for IHG is fee margin (64.8%), which is exceptionally high. This structural distortion likely understates the moat score.
- IHG's ROIC of 42% is partly inflated by negative equity (aggressive buybacks create negative book value). The ROIC-WACC spread of +32.8pp is real economically but the absolute ROIC number is somewhat artificial.
- The DCF is highly unreliable for IHG (TV 74.5% of EV, FV spread 101%) because the fee-based model has low starting FCF relative to its growth potential. EV/EBIT multiples are more appropriate.
- FY2025 revenue data from yfinance ($5.2B) appears to be total revenue including pass-throughs, not fee revenue ($1.9B). This affects any margin/growth metric calculated from total revenue.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The quality_scorer.py should have sector-specific adjustments for companies with significant pass-through revenue (hotels, travel agents, insurance). The gross margin comparison vs "Consumer Cyclical" sector median is meaningless for franchise hotel companies.
- Consider creating a "franchise/fee-based business" valuation template that focuses on fee revenue, fee margin, and system growth rather than total revenue.
