## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis rests on three pillars: (1) the commercial monopoly is worth more than the current market cap alone, (2) Homes.com investment is being rationalized and creates optionality, and (3) smart money (Akre, Polen, insider buys) confirms conviction. I find the commercial core pillar credible but overvalued in the thesis. The Homes.com pillar is significantly weaker than presented -- D.E. Shaw and Third Point have produced detailed evidence of missed targets, obscured reporting, and a breakeven timeline that keeps moving out. The smart money pillar is mixed: Third Point and D.E. Shaw are also "smart money" and they are in open revolt. Short interest surged 57% in one month. The thesis survives but at a substantially lower FV.

## Assumptions Challenged

### 1. QS Adjustment of +33 Points (35 to 68) -- The Largest Ever Applied

- **Evidence against:** The tool score of 35 reflects ACTUAL trailing financials: ROIC negative, FCF margin 1.3%, operating margin -2.2%, EPS near zero. The thesis argues these are "distorted by Homes.com investment." But Homes.com IS the company's strategic direction -- you cannot simply back it out and pretend the core business operates in isolation. The consolidated entity is what shareholders own.
- **Specific challenge:** The "commercial segment 47% EBIT margin" used to justify the ROIC adjustment comes from a single quarter's data (Q3 2025), cited from a Motley Fool article (T2/T3 source). CoStar does NOT report segment EBITDA in its 10-K with this granularity. And as of March 2026, D.E. Shaw has specifically criticized CoStar for REDUCING segment disclosure -- making this number harder to verify, not easier.
- **The adjustment methodology is circular:** "The QS tool is wrong because it doesn't capture the hidden quality of the commercial core" --> "The commercial core quality justifies a +33 adjustment" --> "Therefore CSGP is Tier B quality." But the tool is measuring what the consolidated company actually delivers to shareholders TODAY. A Tier B company should deliver Tier B financials.
- **Precedent concern:** No other company in the portfolio has received a +33 adjustment. The next largest is likely +8-10 points. This sets a dangerous precedent where any company with a "hidden quality" narrative can bypass QS discipline.
- **Severity: HIGH**
- **Resolution:** Accept QS 35-50 range (Tier C). The investment phase is real but so is the execution risk. A Tier C classification demands higher MoS -- which is fine, given the stock is at $44.

### 2. Homes.com Valued at $0 in Bear Case (But Treated as Optionality in Base)

- **Evidence against:**
  - D.E. Shaw documented that CoStar guided >$700M revenue and >15% EBITDA margins for Homes.com by 2027. Those targets were "subsequently abandoned when it became clear they were completely unachievable."
  - Homes.com annual revenue is ~$80M (some sources say $100M ARR). Against $3B+ cumulative investment, that is a 2.7% return on invested capital.
  - Homes.com pricing was CUT by 30%+ in 2025, indicating product-market fit challenges.
  - Breakeven has been pushed from 2027 (original) to 2029/2030 (current guidance) -- a 3-year delay.
  - Zillow has 44% of search traffic vs Homes.com's 3.3%. Even claiming #2 by traffic, this is achieved by aggregating ALL CoStar property sites, not Homes.com alone.
  - CoStar needs 25x revenue growth just to reach Zillow's revenue level ($2.1B vs ~$80M).
  - CoStar is REDUCING Homes.com disclosure (new segment reporting criticized by D.E. Shaw), making it harder to track performance -- a classic move when metrics are deteriorating.
- **The thesis assigns $0 to Homes.com in bear, $2.5B in base.** But $0 is not actually the bear case for Homes.com -- the bear case is that Homes.com CONTINUES to burn cash for 3-4 more years before being written off, destroying additional value. The option value is negative if the company keeps spending.
- **Severity: HIGH**
- **Resolution:** Homes.com should be valued at NEGATIVE $1-2B in the bear case (present value of continued cash burn until 2029 breakeven or abandonment), $0 in a "quick kill" scenario, and $1-2B max in base case given the missed targets and 30% pricing cuts.

### 3. SOTP Valuation: Commercial Platform at $18.5-21B (Bear-Base)

- **Evidence against:**
  - The thesis uses 22-25x EBITDA for the commercial platform, citing Verisk (28x), MSCI (30x), and SPGI (25x) as comps.
  - But Verisk, MSCI, and SPGI all have: (a) positive and growing GAAP EPS, (b) no $3B loss-making ventures attached, (c) no activist campaigns, (d) no antitrust investigations, (e) significantly higher margins (50-60% vs CoStar's -2.2% blended).
  - The commercial segment's "47% margin" is an estimated, unaudited single-quarter figure. The actual CONSOLIDATED operating margin is -2.2%.
  - The STR antitrust investigation (CMA launched March 2026, plus US class action) directly threatens the data-sharing business model. If STR's data-sharing is found anticompetitive, it creates precedent risk for the core CoStar data platform.
  - Office vacancy at 14.1% and projected to stay above 13.5% through end of decade -- structurally higher than any previous cycle. This depresses transaction volumes and potentially CRE data demand growth.
- **My independent SOTP (bear assumptions):**
  - Commercial platform: 18x $700M estimated EBITDA (haircut from thesis's $840M, using lower margin assumption without unaudited Q3 data) = $12.6B
  - Apartments.com: 15x $300M EBITDA (lower multiple for competitive marketplace) = $4.5B
  - Homes.com: -$1.5B (PV of cash burn to 2029)
  - Matterport + Domain: $1.5B (below acquisition cost, integration risk)
  - Corporate overhead: -$2.0B
  - Net cash: +$0.5B
  - **DA Bear SOTP = $15.6B / 416M shares = $37.50/share**
- **Severity: MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Use more conservative multiples and acknowledge that the "commercial standalone" narrative is theoretical -- shareholders own the consolidated entity including Homes.com losses.

### 4. Management Credibility and Governance

- **Evidence against:**
  - CEO Florance received $130M in compensation over 5 years while stock declined 27% (vs S&P +94%). His 2024 pay was $37M -- top decile of S&P 500 CEOs.
  - Say-on-pay vote saw "near 50% revolt" -- exceptional for a large-cap company.
  - Original Homes.com targets ($700M revenue, 15% margins by 2027) were "subsequently abandoned as completely unachievable."
  - Two major activist funds (Third Point, D.E. Shaw) are in open proxy fight -- Third Point seeking board overhaul.
  - D.E. Shaw specifically accuses CoStar of REDUCING segment transparency to hide Homes.com underperformance.
  - Layoffs confirmed Feb 2026 following investor criticism -- reactive, not proactive.
  - Stock awards of 124K shares to Florance on March 10, 2026 (even while cutting costs elsewhere).
- **The thesis treats insider buying ($2.5M Florance, $1M Glaser) as conviction signal.** But $2.5M is 3.5% of Florance's annual compensation. Against $70M in holdings and $130M in 5-year pay, this is a trivial amount. Meanwhile, other insiders (Saint, Cann, Hill) were selling in 2025 at $65-80 prices.
- **Severity: HIGH**
- **Resolution:** Management credibility deserves a discount, not a premium. The insider buying signal is weak relative to the compensation/selling context.

### 5. Short Interest Surge (+57% MoM)

- **Evidence against:**
  - Short interest jumped from 16.6M to 26.2M shares in one month (Feb 2026 report). Short % of float now 7.4%.
  - This is NOT legacy short positioning -- it is ACCELERATING while the stock is near 7-year lows.
  - Smart money is not unanimously bullish: Akre and Polen are long, but Third Point (also in the SM graph) is an activist seeking board overhaul, and D.E. Shaw is openly hostile.
  - The thesis presents "4-fund convergence + insider cluster = STRONG BUY signal" but Third Point's position is not a conviction long -- it is an activist position designed to force strategic change.
- **Severity: MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** The smart money signal is mixed, not uniformly bullish. Third Point's presence should be classified as ACTIVIST_PRESSURE, not as convergence buy signal.

### Interpretation

The thesis survives on the strength of the commercial monopoly core, which is genuinely valuable and defensible. However:

1. The $80 FV is built on a SOTP methodology that uses unaudited segment margins, premium comps inappropriate for a company with negative operating margins and active proxy fights, and a Homes.com valuation that understates downside risk.
2. The +33 QS adjustment is unprecedented and insufficiently justified -- it effectively allows the analyst to ignore the company's actual financial performance.
3. Management credibility is poor: targets missed, compensation excessive, transparency reduced under activist pressure.
4. The smart money signal is MIXED, not uniformly bullish -- two of the four named funds are activists demanding strategic overhaul.
5. Short interest surging +57% MoM is a warning signal that contradicts the "everyone agrees this is undervalued" narrative.

**Post-DA suggested FV: $50-55** (midpoint between FA $80 and DA $38, but closer to DA given the weight of evidence on governance, missed targets, and overstated SOTP).

- What is Homes.com actually worth? Not $0 (bear) or $2.5B (base) -- more likely -$1B to +$1B range.
- Should the proxy fight be viewed as catalyst (positive) or risk (negative)?

## META-REFLECTION

### Doubts/Uncertainties
- I cannot independently verify the "47% commercial segment margin" that anchors the SOTP. CoStar's reduced disclosure makes this harder. This is my most significant data gap.
- The activist campaign could be resolved in management's favor (board maintained, Homes.com rationalized on schedule). In that scenario, the stock could re-rate significantly upward. My analysis may underweight this tail risk to the UPSIDE.
- I am uncertain whether the antitrust risk (STR/CMA) is material or a nuisance suit. The RealPage settlement provides a road map for resolution, but CMA investigations can be prolonged and unpredictable.

### Limitations of This Analysis
- No access to CoStar's 10-K to verify segment margins, segment revenue attribution, or SOTP component values independently.
- Traffic claims (108M unique visitors, #2 portal) could not be independently verified against SimilarWeb data within this analysis.
- The DA bear EV/EBITDA approach ($26-32) may be excessively punitive because it applies a blended multiple to a company where the high-margin core subsidizes the loss-making venture.

### Suggestions for the System
- The QS tool should flag when an adjustment exceeds +15 points and require a second-opinion review (e.g., automatic R2 trigger).
- For companies in investment phases (negative FCF by choice), the system needs a forward-looking QS variant that uses guided financials. The current binary (tool score vs. manual adjustment) creates too much subjectivity.
