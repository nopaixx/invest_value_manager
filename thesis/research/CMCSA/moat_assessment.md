# Moat Assessment: CMCSA (Comcast Corporation)

## Fecha: 2026-02-27

## Clasificacion: NARROW

**Moat Score: 3.0/5**

Comcast possesses a narrow moat built on a combination of efficient scale in broadband (local infrastructure monopoly/duopoly), moderate switching costs, and intangible assets (NBCU content, theme parks). However, the moat is ERODING: the broadband local monopoly -- historically the strongest moat source -- is under structural attack from 5G fixed wireless access (T-Mobile, Verizon) and fiber overbuilders (AT&T, regional players). The moat is narrower than it was 5 years ago and will continue narrowing over the next decade.

## Detailed Moat Source Analysis

### 1. Efficient Scale (PRIMARY MOAT -- Strength: 3.5/5, ERODING)

**The Case FOR:**
- Comcast has a functional monopoly in broadband for ~47 million households where no comparable alternative exists.
- Infrastructure capex to overbuild is $500-1,000+ per home passed -- prohibitive for most entrants.
- The cable plant passes ~60M homes in 39 states. This physical infrastructure took decades and tens of billions to build.
- In monopoly markets, Comcast charges premium prices ($70/month for 400Mbps) vs competitive markets ($30/month for same speed).

**The Case AGAINST (and why this is ERODING):**
- Fiber overlap increasing from ~34% to projected ~55% of Comcast's footprint. AT&T, Verizon FiOS, and regional fiber overbuilders (US Internet, Sonic, municipal networks) are aggressively expanding.
- 5G Fixed Wireless Access (FWA) is the most disruptive threat: T-Mobile added ~450K FWA subs in Q2 2025 alone. Verizon has 4.8M FWA subs aiming for 8-9M by 2028. FWA requires ZERO last-mile infrastructure investment per household -- it uses existing cell towers. This fundamentally undermines the "capex barrier to entry" argument.
- T-Mobile + Verizon + AT&T collectively added 932K 5G Home Internet subs in Q2 2025 vs Comcast + Charter losing ~500K combined.
- FCC broadband mapping and BEAD subsidies ($42.5B) will fund fiber to underserved areas, further reducing Comcast's monopoly pockets.

**Durability: 10-15 years.** The physical infrastructure advantage persists in rural and suburban markets where FWA coverage is spotty and fiber economics don't work. But in urban/suburban markets (Comcast's highest-value territories), the monopoly is eroding year by year.

### 2. Intangible Assets (Strength: 3.0/5, MIXED trajectory)

**NBC Broadcast License:**
- One of only 4 major US broadcast networks. Oligopoly with regulatory barrier.
- Carries NFL Sunday Night Football, Notre Dame football, Premier League, Olympics (through 2032).
- However: broadcast TV audiences declining secularly. Cord-cutting reduces distribution reach.
- Revenue contribution: Media segment ~$28B FY2025 revenue.
- Post-Versant spinoff: Comcast RETAINED NBC, Telemundo, Peacock, and the sports rights. Spun off the declining cable networks (USA, CNBC, MSNBC, etc.).

**Universal Theme Parks:**
- Only 2 major US theme park operators (Disney, Universal). Efficient scale + brand.
- Epic Universe (opened May 2025): Q4 2025 theme parks revenue +22% YoY to $2.9B, EBITDA +24%.
- Theme parks delivered $9.84B revenue FY2025, $3.08B EBITDA.
- Epic Universe driving higher per-cap spending, longer stays, less cannibalization than expected.
- This is the MOST DURABLE moat source: theme park IP (Harry Potter, Nintendo, Jurassic World) creates pricing power. Epic Universe is a multi-decade asset.
- **Durability: 20+ years.** Theme parks are nearly impossible to replicate (capital, IP, permitting, brand).

**Peacock Streaming:**
- 44M paid subscribers, growing 22% YoY. Revenue $5.4B FY2025.
- Still unprofitable: EBITDA losses of $552M in Q4 (worsened by $2.7B/yr NBA deal).
- In a crowded streaming market (Netflix, Disney+, Amazon, Apple), Peacock lacks the scale advantages of Netflix.
- NOT a moat source on its own. It is a distribution asset that leverages NBCU content.

### 3. Switching Costs (Strength: 2.5/5, DECLINING)

**What Creates Switching Costs:**
- Bundling: Internet + TV + Mobile (9.3M wireless lines, 15% penetration of broadband base). The convergence strategy creates multi-product stickiness.
- Equipment: Cable modem/router rental, in-home wiring, email migration.
- 5-year price lock guarantee (new 2026 initiative) aims to create contractual switching costs.
- Business Services: $2.59B quarterly revenue, 5.8% YoY growth. Business customers have higher switching costs (dedicated circuits, SLAs, multi-site contracts).

**What Is Reducing Switching Costs:**
- FWA: No installation required, no contract, self-install. T-Mobile FWA is literally "plug in and go."
- Fiber competitors: Many offer free installation and contract buyouts.
- Mobile number portability: Easy to switch wireless.
- Price transparency: Comparison tools make it easy to evaluate alternatives.
- Comcast's own admission: "In this intensely competitive environment, we are not winning in the marketplace" -- Comcast president Michael Cavanaugh, Q1 2025 earnings call.

**Quantitative Evidence:**
- Broadband losses: -199K (Q1 2025), -226K (Q2 2025), stabilizing to -181K (Q4 2025).
- Annual broadband losses: ~425K+ in 2025. This is NOT consistent with high switching costs.
- Industry broadband churn: 20-50% annually (varies by provider).
- Comcast's churn is lower than industry average (likely ~15-18%) but INCREASING.

### 4. Cost Advantage (Strength: 2.0/5, STABLE but LIMITED)

- Scale: Largest US cable operator by subscribers (32.3M broadband). Purchasing power for content, equipment, and technology.
- Capex efficiency: Capex/Depreciation declining from 1.8x to 1.5x (2023-2025) -- the network is maturing.
- DOCSIS 4.0 upgrade path extends the useful life of the HFC (hybrid fiber-coax) plant at lower cost than full fiber replacement.
- But this is infrastructure leverage, not a sustainable process advantage. Once a fiber competitor overbuilds, Comcast's per-subscriber cost advantage disappears in that market.
- The cost advantage exists because they ALREADY built the network. But the moat is the network itself (efficient scale), not a replicable cost process.

## Escenarios de Erosion

### 1. Most Probable: Gradual Broadband Erosion (Probability: 70%)
FWA and fiber overbuild steadily erode Comcast's broadband subscriber base by 1-3% annually for the next 5 years. ARPU stagnates or declines as competitive pricing becomes necessary. Comcast partially offsets through wireless growth, business services, and theme parks, but the core broadband cash machine slowly diminishes. ROIC stays above WACC but spread narrows from +3.9pp to +1-2pp. Moat transitions from Narrow to borderline None over 10 years.

### 2. Tail Risk: FWA Technology Leap (Probability: 15%)
5G/6G fixed wireless achieves reliable 1Gbps+ speeds at <$30/month with low latency, making cable broadband redundant for most consumers. Comcast loses 20-30% of broadband subs within 5 years. The cable plant becomes a stranded asset. Moat evaporates within 5-7 years. Offset: NBCU theme parks + content provide floor valuation.

### 3. Bull Case: Convergence Moat Strengthens (Probability: 15%)
Comcast successfully bundles broadband + mobile + streaming + smart home into a sticky "super-bundle" that FWA can't match on quality/reliability. DOCSIS 4.0 delivers multi-gig speeds cheaply, maintaining speed advantage over FWA. Wireless penetration reaches 30-40% of broadband base, creating true switching cost moat via bundling. Broadband losses stabilize and ARPU recovers.

## Post-Spinoff Moat Assessment

The Versant spinoff (completed January 2, 2026) is a NET POSITIVE for Comcast's moat assessment:

**Removed:** Declining cable TV networks (USA, CNBC, MSNBC, Syfy, etc.) that generated ~$7B revenue but were in secular decline. These were moat-NEGATIVE assets -- they had negative trajectory and dragged on valuation multiple.

**Retained:** The higher-moat assets:
- Xfinity broadband/connectivity (efficient scale)
- NBC broadcast + sports rights (intangible/regulatory moat)
- Universal theme parks (intangible + efficient scale -- strongest moat source)
- Peacock streaming (distribution leverage, uncertain standalone value)
- Xfinity Mobile (convergence strategy)

**Net effect:** RemainCo (Comcast post-spinoff) has a cleaner moat profile but the core broadband erosion issue is unchanged.

## 🔄 META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- **FWA capacity constraints**: The biggest uncertainty is whether T-Mobile/Verizon FWA can scale to tens of millions of subscribers without degrading network quality. FWA uses shared wireless spectrum -- at scale, throughput degrades. If FWA hits capacity limits at 15-20M total subs, the threat is contained. If technology improvements allow unlimited scaling, the threat is existential for cable broadband. I could not definitively resolve this.
- **DOCSIS 4.0 timeline and effectiveness**: Comcast is upgrading to DOCSIS 4.0 (multi-gig speeds over existing coax). If deployed broadly by 2027-2028, it could maintain speed parity with fiber at lower cost. Execution risk is real.
- **Peacock economics**: The $2.7B/yr NBA deal is a 10-year commitment. If Peacock fails to reach profitability, that is $27B of value destruction. I classified this as moderate probability but the streaming market is brutal.
- **Business Services growth**: This segment is growing 5.8% and has higher switching costs, but I have limited data on its margin profile and competitive position relative to dedicated enterprise players.

### Discrepancias con Thesis
- If the fundamental-analyst thesis values CMCSA primarily as a broadband play, I would flag that the moat on broadband is NARROWER than historical consensus assumed. The "local monopoly" story is being structurally dismantled by FWA.
- If the thesis assigns Wide moat, I DISAGREE. The broadband erosion is real, ROIC persistence is not 10/10, and the post-spinoff company has multiple segments with different moat characteristics (broadband narrowing, theme parks widening).
- The most defensible moat source is actually Universal theme parks, not broadband. This is a potential thesis differentiation point.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- Cable/broadband companies should have a specific moat sub-assessment that separates "infrastructure efficient scale" from "FWA-vulnerable markets" -- the moat is geographically heterogeneous.
- For conglomerates like CMCSA, the moat assessment should consider sum-of-parts moat evaluation rather than a single classification.
