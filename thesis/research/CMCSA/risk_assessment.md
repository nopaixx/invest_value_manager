## Top 3 Riesgos Criticos

### 1. Broadband Business in Structural Decline -- Cord-Cutting 2.0

- **Categoria:** Fundamental
- **Descripcion:** Comcast lost ~710,000 internet subscribers in FY2025 (199K in Q1, 226K in Q2, 104K in Q3, ~181K in Q4). This is NOT a cyclical dip -- it is a structural shift. Fixed wireless access (FWA) from T-Mobile, Verizon, and AT&T now serves 12% of US internet households (vs 0% in 2020, 8% in 2023). FWA has the highest Net Promoter Score (36) of any connection type. Fiber overbuilders (AT&T, Google Fiber) are expanding aggressively into Comcast's footprint. Analysts at New Street do not expect cable operators to return to broadband subscriber gains until AFTER 2030.
- **Evidencia:**
  - Comcast + Spectrum collectively lost 1.11M internet customers in FY2025 and are tracking to lose 1M+ again in 2026
  - FWA capacity has expanded: "Big 3" now have room for 32M FWA customers
  - Goldman Sachs downgraded CMCSA to Neutral, cut PT from $39 to $30, citing "broadband pricing reset" as key risk
  - BNP Paribas downgraded CMCSA to Underperform (PT $27), calling it "most exposed" to fiber headwinds
  - Comcast has announced NO broadband rate increase in early 2026 -- deliberately sacrificing ARPU to reduce churn, but ARPU growth expected to turn NEGATIVE in Q1-Q2 2026
- **Probabilidad:** Alta -- this is already happening, not hypothetical
- **Impacto si materializa (further acceleration):** If broadband losses accelerate to 300K+/quarter, Connectivity EBITDA could decline 5-8% annually. Broadband generates ~63% of revenue and nearly all of Connectivity's high-margin EBITDA. A 10% decline in Connectivity EBITDA = ~$2B EBITDA loss = $5-8/share impact on fair value. Estimated portfolio impact: -25% to -35% from current price.
- **Mitigante:** DOCSIS 4.0 upgrade (multi-gig speeds over existing coax), wireless bundling (9M+ lines), 5-year price guarantee to lock in customers. Management claims losses are "stabilizing" -- Q3 loss of 104K was lowest of the year.
- **Kill condition?:** YES -- broadband losses >300K/quarter for 3+ consecutive quarters (already in thesis KC#1). I suggest TIGHTENING to >250K/quarter for 2+ quarters, given the ARPU headwind compounding the volume decline.

### 2. FY2025 FCF of $19.2B is Non-Recurring -- Real Normalized FCF is ~$14-15B

- **Categoria:** Financiero
- **Descripcion:** The thesis and the "cheap" valuation case both hinge on Comcast's enormous FCF. But the FY2025 figure of $19.2B (or $21.9B per another measurement) is materially inflated by one-time items. This is the single most dangerous analytical trap in this thesis.
- **Evidencia:**
  - **$2.0B one-time cash tax benefit** from internal corporate reorganization (Q4 2025). Management explicitly stated this will NOT recur in 2026
  - **Lower capex**: Total capital spending declined 5% to $14.4B, including a 17% decline in Content & Experiences capex as Epic Universe construction completed. This is a one-time capex trough -- future parks maintenance + DOCSIS 4.0 rollout will require higher investment
  - **Favorable working capital**: Lower studio production spend created a WC tailwind that will reverse as content pipeline normalizes
  - **Management guidance**: "We saw an outsized benefit in 2025 and expect the benefit to be significantly lower in 2026"
  - Adjusting for $2B tax benefit alone: Normalized FCF = ~$17B. Further adjusting for capex normalization (~$1-2B higher) and WC reversion: **Sustainable FCF is ~$14-15B**
  - At $14.5B normalized FCF: P/FCF = 7.8x (not the 5.9x implied by headline $19.2B). Still cheap, but 32% less cheap than the headline suggests.
- **Probabilidad:** Alta -- this is a mathematical certainty, not a forecast
- **Impacto si materializa:** If the market anchors to $19.2B FCF and then 2026 FCF comes in at $14-15B (a 22-27% decline), the stock could sell off 10-15% on "FCF deterioration" headlines even though the decline was entirely predictable. The thesis bear case FV of $25.04 was partly calibrated to a higher FCF base.
- **Mitigante:** Even at $14.5B normalized FCF, CMCSA is cheap on a P/FCF basis (7.8x). Buybacks are being funded from real cash flow. Dividend is well-covered (18.4% payout ratio). The risk is more about narrative whipsaw than fundamental destruction.
- **Kill condition?:** YES -- already in thesis as KC#5 (normalized FCF drops below $10B). This is appropriate. However, I suggest adding a MONITORING condition: If FY2026 FCF < $13B, re-evaluate the entire valuation framework as it would suggest structural FCF deterioration beyond one-time normalization.

### 3. Sports Rights Cost Inflation + Peacock Cash Burn = Content Cost Spiral

- **Categoria:** Fundamental
- **Descripcion:** NBC/Comcast will spend $8B+ on sports rights in 2026 alone -- more than double the spend from 4 years ago. The NBA deal alone costs $2.5B/year for 11 years ($27B total). The 2026 Milan Olympics cost $1.026B (record for Winter Games). Olympics extended through 2036 for an additional $3B. Peacock, which is supposed to monetize this content, posted $552M in losses in Q4 2025 alone (wider than $372M in Q4 2024). Total cumulative Peacock losses exceed $10B.
- **Evidencia:**
  - Q4 2025 Peacock: $1.6B revenue, $552M loss. Loss WIDENED YoY due to NBA rights payments kicking in
  - Sports rights commitments: NBA ($27B/11yr), Olympics ($7.75B through 2036), plus NFL, MLB, WWE
  - Peacock has 44M subscribers but has never been profitable
  - Management says "meaningful improvement" in 2026 but provides no specific breakeven timeline
  - If Peacock losses persist at $2B+/year AND broadband declines, the combined effect on free cash flow is devastating
  - Netflix and Amazon are also bidding up sports rights, creating a prisoner's dilemma where opting out means losing relevance but opting in means paying irrational prices
- **Probabilidad:** Alta for continued cash burn in 2026-2027; Media for permanent unprofitability
- **Impacto si materializa (Peacock never profits):** $2B+/year drag on FCF indefinitely. At 7x capitalization rate, that is $14B in destroyed value = ~$3.85/share. Combined with broadband decline, would push fair value into the low $20s.
- **Mitigante:** Peacock has reached "meaningful scale" at 44M subs. Ad revenue growing. February 2026 "Legendary February" sports programming may demonstrate monetization potential. Management has track record of eventually rationalizing content spend.
- **Kill condition?:** YES -- already in thesis as KC#2 (Peacock annual losses widen beyond $2B). I suggest this is correctly calibrated. Add supplementary KC: If total sports rights commitments exceed $10B/year, reassess the entire content economics.

## Kill Conditions Sugeridas

1. **TIGHTEN KC#1:** Broadband losses >250K/quarter for 2+ consecutive quarters (currently 300K threshold is too generous given ARPU headwind). RATIONALE: At 250K/quarter with negative ARPU growth, Connectivity EBITDA declines faster than the thesis models.

2. **NEW KC: ARPU + Volume Double Decline.** If broadband ARPU declines >2% YoY AND net subscriber losses >200K in same quarter, trigger full thesis re-evaluation. RATIONALE: The thesis implicitly assumes ARPU growth offsets subscriber losses. If both go negative simultaneously, the economics fundamentally change.

3. **NEW KC: FY2026 FCF < $13B.** If normalized FCF (excluding one-time items) drops below $13B, the "cheap on cash flow" argument materially weakens. RATIONALE: At $13B FCF, P/FCF = 8.7x, which is no longer notably cheap for a structurally declining business.

4. **NEW KC: Total content/sports commitments exceed $10B/year.** RATIONALE: At that level, content spend consumes >65% of normalized FCF, leaving insufficient capital for debt reduction, buybacks, and broadband investment.

5. **KEEP KC#4 (Net leverage >3.5x)** -- this remains correctly calibrated given the debt load.

## Riesgo Agregado

- **Numero de riesgos HIGH + CRITICAL: 6** (3 CRITICAL + 3 HIGH)
- **Riesgos correlacionados?:** YES -- risks #1 (broadband decline), #2 (ARPU decline), and #4 (FWA/fiber competition) are HIGHLY CORRELATED. They are essentially three manifestations of the same structural threat: cable broadband losing its monopoly position. If one worsens, all three worsen simultaneously. This correlation means the tail risk is fatter than the individual probabilities suggest.
- **Additional correlation:** Risk #3 (FCF sustainability) is partially correlated with #5 (Peacock losses) and #6 (sports costs). If content spending rises while broadband EBITDA declines, FCF gets squeezed from both sides.
- **Risk Score Final: HIGH**

### Justification for HIGH (not VERY HIGH):

Despite 3 CRITICAL risks, the mitigants are substantial:
- Broadband is declining but not collapsing (104K loss in Q3 was the best quarter)
- Even normalized FCF of $14-15B supports the stock at P/FCF 7-8x
- Theme parks ($4.5B EBITDA, growing with Epic Universe) are a genuinely valuable hidden asset
- IG credit rating, well-laddered debt maturities, active refinancing
- 4 quality value funds hold (Markel, Tweedy Browne, Dodge & Cox, Capital World Investors)
- Management is making rational decisions (Versant spin-off, no bad M&A, buybacks at 7x)

The case does not merit VERY HIGH because there is no single existential risk -- rather, there is a slow structural erosion that could be managed if broadband stabilizes and theme parks deliver. The question is whether the stock is cheap ENOUGH to compensate for these risks at the current $26 entry (SO price), and the answer is: barely.

## 🔄 META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- **DOCSIS 4.0 effectiveness:** I could not find reliable data on DOCSIS 4.0 deployment timelines or whether it genuinely slows FWA churn. If DOCSIS 4.0 delivers multi-gig speeds by late 2026 and FWA remains at ~100-300 Mbps, the broadband decline COULD stabilize faster than bears expect. This is the biggest uncertainty in the assessment.
- **Theme park valuation:** The thesis assigns $50-60B to theme parks. This is plausible (14x $4.5B EBITDA = $63B) but highly sensitive to the multiple. In a recession, parks EBITDA could fall 30-40% and the multiple compresses -- the "hidden asset" could be worth $25-30B instead of $60B.
- **Peacock profitability path:** Management says "meaningful improvement" in 2026 but gives no breakeven date. I cannot determine with confidence whether Peacock ever reaches profitability as a standalone unit or whether it becomes a permanent cost of doing business (like Amazon Prime).

### Riesgos que Podrian Estar Subestimados
- **Dual-class governance discount:** The market typically applies 10-15% discount to dual-class structures. Roberts's 33% voting power with <1% economic interest creates a classic agency problem. The thesis does not apply any governance discount to fair value. This alone could explain 10-15% of the "cheapness."
- **ARPU + volume double decline in 2026:** The thesis was written when ARPU was growing +1.7%. Guidance now points to NEGATIVE ARPU in H1 2026. Combined with continued sub losses, Connectivity revenue could decline 3-5% in 2026 -- far worse than the thesis's -2% assumption. This makes the bear case more likely than the thesis's 25% probability weighting.

### Discrepancias con Thesis
1. **FCF figure:** The thesis references "normalized FCF ~$14-15B" in some places but also uses FY2025 headline $19.2B to calculate P/FCF of 7.5x. The true normalized P/FCF is closer to 7.8-8.0x. Still cheap, but less cheap.
2. **ARPU trajectory:** Thesis says "ARPU +1.7%, growing" -- this is now stale. Company guidance says ARPU will be FLAT TO NEGATIVE in 2026. This materially changes the revenue model.
3. **Sports cost escalation:** Thesis mentions the $2.5B/yr NBA deal but does not account for the aggregate $8B/yr sports spend (doubled in 4 years). This content cost inflation is not modeled in the projections.
4. **Value trap indicators:** The thesis flags 3/10 value trap indicators as YES. My assessment is 4/10 (adding ARPU decline as evidence of pricing power erosion). A 4/10 score with 3 correlated structural risks warrants more caution than the thesis provides.
5. **Analyst consensus shift:** The thesis was written pre-downgrade wave. Goldman (Neutral, $30), BNP (Underperform, $27), Rosenblatt ($30 PT) -- the consensus is now significantly more bearish than when the thesis was written. The mean analyst target of $33 is virtually equal to the thesis's expected FV of $33.13, which means the thesis has NO informational edge vs consensus.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- **FCF normalization should be a mandatory step in any thesis involving companies with >$1B in one-time items.** The quality_scorer.py shows FY2025 FCF of $19.2B without flagging the one-time tax benefit. Analysts (and the thesis) can anchor to this inflated figure.
- **ARPU tracking should be added to kill conditions for any subscription-based business.** Volume declines offset by ARPU growth is a common bull narrative -- when ARPU also turns negative, the thesis breaks down.
- **Governance discount for dual-class structures should be systematized.** A 10-15% discount to FV for companies where insiders have >25% voting power with <5% economic interest.
