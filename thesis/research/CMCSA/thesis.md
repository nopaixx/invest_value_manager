### Segment Deep Dive

**1. Broadband: Cash Cow Under Siege**
- 31.3M subscribers at $86/month ARPU = ~$32B/yr broadband revenue
- ARPU grew +1.7% in FY2025, but management froze prices for early 2026 -- ARPU expected to turn NEGATIVE in H1 2026
- Lost ~710K net broadband subs in FY2025 (Q1: -199K, Q2: -226K, Q3: -104K, Q4: -181K)
- FWA threat: T-Mobile/Verizon collectively have capacity for 32M FWA customers. Already at 12% US broadband penetration (from 0% in 2020)
- Management response: DOCSIS 4.0 (multi-gig over coax), 4-tier national pricing, 5-year price guarantee, free wireless lines to bundlers
- Q3 loss of 104K was best of year, suggesting some stabilization. But Q4 worsened to 181K.
- **Assessment:** Volume declines 1.5-3%/yr offset partially by wireless bundling and business services growth. Revenue flat to down modestly. Cash generation remains strong ($18-20B EBITDA from Connectivity) but the trend is negative.

**2. Theme Parks: The Crown Jewel**
- FY2025: $9.84B revenue (+14.2%), $3.08B EBITDA (+4.5%). Margins: 31.3%
- Q4 2025: Revenue $2.89B (+22% YoY), EBITDA $1.035B (+24%) -- crossed $1B quarterly for first time
- Epic Universe opened May 2025, still NOT at full capacity. Full ops expected late 2026
- Driving higher per-cap spending, longer stays, attendance across ALL Orlando parks
- Universal Kids Resort opening 2026 (additional capacity)
- **Only 2 US theme park operators of this scale (Disney + Universal).** Near-impossible to replicate: IP (Harry Potter, Nintendo, Jurassic World, DreamWorks), physical infrastructure, permits, brand.
- **Assessment:** Parks EBITDA reaches $3.8-4.0B in 2026 and $4.3-4.6B in 2027-2028 as Epic ramps fully. This is the strongest moat source (20+ year durability) and the primary upside driver.

**3. NBC/Media & Peacock: Revenue with Losses**
- NBC: One of 4 US broadcast networks (oligopoly). Carries NFL, NBA ($2.7B/yr, 11 years), Olympics (through 2036)
- Peacock: 44M paid subscribers (+22% YoY). Revenue $5.4B (+10%)
- Peacock Q4 loss: -$552M (WIDER than Q4 2024's -$372M). NBA rights payments started Q4
- Total sports rights spend: ~$8B/yr in 2026 (DOUBLED from 4 years ago)
- Cumulative Peacock losses: $10B+. No management breakeven guidance.
- **Assessment:** Peacock is binary. Either reaches breakeven 2028+ (adding $2-3B value) or becomes permanent cost of doing business (Amazon Prime model). The $2.7B/yr NBA deal is a 10-year anchor. I assign 30% probability to breakeven by 2028.

**4. Wireless: The Convergence Play**
- 9.3M lines (+1.5M net adds in FY2025, record year)
- MVNO on Verizon with own small cells. Revenue ~$4.5B, growing ~30%/yr
- 15% penetration of broadband base -- significant runway to 25-30%
- Strategic value: bundled customers churn ~50% less on broadband. Wireless is a churn-reduction tool as much as a revenue source.

**5. Business Services: Stable Growth**
- ~$10.3B quarterly run rate, +5.8% growth
- Higher switching costs (dedicated circuits, SLAs, multi-site contracts)
- Less impacted by FWA/consumer competition

### FCF Normalization (CRITICAL ANALYSIS)

FY2025 reported FCF of $19.2B is inflated by one-time items. This is the most important analytical question for CMCSA:

| Item | Amount | Recurring? |
|------|--------|------------|
| Reported FCF | $19.2B | -- |
| less: One-time tax benefit | -$2.0B | NO (internal corporate reorg, mgmt explicitly said non-recurring) |
| less: Capex trough (Epic done, 2026 is "largest broadband investment year") | -$1 to -2B | NO (capex rises 2026) |
| less: Favorable working capital timing | -$0.5 to -1B | NO (normalizes) |
| **Normalized Sustainable FCF** | **$14.5-16.0B** | YES |
| Midpoint | **$15.25B** | |

**FCF Normalization Rule:** FY2025 FCF margin 15.5% vs trailing 3yr avg 10.4% = +510bps gap. Management explicitly guided lower 2026 FCF. Without one-timers, FCF margin is ~11.5-12.5%, within range of trailing average. I use $15B as base case normalized FCF.

At $15B normalized FCF:
- P/FCF: 7.5x (vs headline 5.9x)
- FCF Yield: 13.3% (vs headline 17.0%)
- Still cheap for IG-rated company, but NOT as screaming cheap as headlines suggest

## Governance Note

Roberts family holds ~33% voting power on <1% economic stake. This creates structural governance discount:
- Can block hostile takeovers (eliminates M&A premium)
- Can force value-destructive acquisitions (though recent track record is rational)
- Market typically applies 10-15% governance discount for dual-class

I apply a 10% conglomerate/governance discount at the SoP level. This partially captures the governance risk. The EV/EBITDA and FCF methods do not separately adjust for this, which is conservative (the discount is implicit in the lower multiples the market assigns).
