## META-REFLECTION

### Committee Doubts

- The thin edge is the primary concern. Post-R3 FV of $33 being below consensus mean of $34.90 means we are not seeing something the market is not seeing. Our thesis relies on analytical rigor (FCF normalization, SoP discipline) rather than proprietary information. This is a valid but not compelling basis for investment.
- The SO conflict between R1 ($28) and R3 ($26) was resolved in favor of $28 given the stock's decline from $30.93 to $29.02 since R3. However, the R3 reasoning for $26 (thin edge, 3 CRITICAL risks) remains valid. If the committee had stronger conviction, $26 would be the more prudent entry.

### System Improvement Suggestions

- **Conglomerate SoP tool:** CMCSA-type analyses require extensive manual SoP work. A tool that accepts segment EBITDA + multiples and outputs weighted equity value would reduce error risk and save time.
- **R1/R3 SO reconciliation protocol:** When R3 tightens an SO but R1 is later updated with a different SO, the system has no clear rule for which prevails. Suggest: R3 SO is canonical; any subsequent loosening requires explicit justification referencing what changed.
- **Pre-earnings gate protocol:** For stocks where earnings are the primary catalyst and occur within 30 days, the committee should have a standard protocol: smaller position pre-earnings, full position post-confirmation.

### Dissent Record

- **MoS at $28 (17.9%) vs Tier B requirement (20-25%):** This is borderline. The E[CAGR] of 17.3% exceeds the 15% Tier B threshold, which per the Expected Return framework (P14) can justify a lower MoS. However, the DA's thin-edge finding (Error #49) argues for MORE MoS, not less. Resolution: Accept $28 as a reasonable compromise -- the 4.3% dividend yield provides current income that reduces effective risk while waiting for re-rating.
- **Pre-Q1 execution risk:** Some committee members might argue for HARD-GATING on Q1 earnings (no execution before late April). Counter-argument: the stock could reach $26-27 in the pre-earnings window if FCF normalization concerns drive selling, and that entry would be excellent. Reducing size (3% instead of 4%) is the compromise.
