### 1. FCF Sustainability -- Is $15B Normalized FCF Too Generous?

**R1 assumption:** Normalized sustainable FCF is $14.5-16B (midpoint $15.25B).

- 2026 is described by management as "the largest broadband investment year in company history." Capex guidance is flat at ~$14.4B, but the MIX shifts heavily to broadband (away from completed Epic Universe), suggesting lower FCF yield from the investment.
- ARPU is turning NEGATIVE in H1 2026 -- the R1 thesis notes "+1.7% ARPU growth" but this is the TRAILING number. Forward ARPU is negative due to price freezes and free wireless lines offered to bundlers.
- Peacock losses WIDENED to $552M in Q4 2025 (from $372M in Q4 2024) as NBA rights payments kicked in. The $2.7B/yr NBA deal frontloads costs. Peacock losses in 2026 could reach $1.5-2.0B.
- Risk assessment independently estimated sustainable FCF at $14-15B. But this range assumes ARPU only turns "slightly negative." If broadband revenue declines 3-5% (volume -2.5% AND ARPU -1 to -2%), Connectivity EBITDA could be $1-2B lower than modeled.
- Comcast CFO Jason Armstrong said Peacock reached "meaningful scale" but loss improvement will be gradual -- there is NO breakeven guidance.

**My assessment of sustainable FCF:**

| Scenario | FCF | Probability |
|----------|-----|-------------|
| Optimistic | $15-16B | 25% |
| Base (DA) | $13.5-14.5B | 50% |
| Bear | $12-13B | 25% |

The R1 midpoint of $15.25B is in my optimistic range, not my base case. My base case is $14.0B, which is $1.25B lower. At $14B FCF: P/FCF = 8.1x. Still cheap, but NOT the "screaming value" that a $15B+ figure suggests.

**Severidad: HIGH**

The entire valuation framework depends on FCF. A $1B shift in sustainable FCF moves FV by ~$2-3/share. The R1 thesis uses $15B as "conservative" -- it is actually the upper half of the realistic range.

**Resolucion sugerida:** Investment committee should use $14B as the base-case FCF, not $15B. Recalculate P/FCF and all FCF-dependent valuations accordingly.

### 2. Theme Parks at 14x EBITDA -- Unjustified Premium

**R1 assumption:** Universal theme parks valued at 14x 2026E EBITDA ($3.8B) = $53.2B, representing 22% of gross SoP EV.

**Evidence in contra:**

- **Disney parks trade at 12-14x EBITDA.** Disney is the DOMINANT global theme park operator with 6 resort destinations, 12 parks, far stronger IP ecosystem (Marvel, Star Wars, Disney Animation, Pixar), and 50+ years of brand heritage. Universal has 3 resort destinations and significantly lower brand recognition outside the US.
- **Industry benchmarks:** Amusement park EBITDA multiples range from 4x to 8x for standard operators. Premium operators (Cedar Fair, Six Flags pre-merger) averaged 8.7x and 12x respectively. Assigning 14x places Universal at the TOP of the peer set -- above pure-play operators with longer track records.
- **Epic Universe backtracking:** In February 2026, NBCUniversal quietly REMOVED the word "expansion" from a press release about Epic Universe, backtracking on expansion claims. The park is STILL NOT at full capacity 8+ months after opening. Full capacity now expected "toward the end of 2026." This suggests the ramp is slower than initially projected.
- **Recession sensitivity is severe:** In the 2008-2009 recession, Universal Orlando's attendance slumped nearly 20%, and operating profit fell 72% in one quarter. Disney Parks operating profit fell 50%. In a recession scenario, parks EBITDA could drop from $3.8B to $2.3-2.7B (-30-40%). At a recession-compressed 10x multiple: parks value = $23-27B vs R1's $53.2B -- a $26-30B swing ($7-8/share).
- **Theme park forward earnings multiples (Feb 2026):** Comcast (entire company) at 7x, United Parks at 10x, Disney at 15x. The embedded park multiple should not exceed the comparable standalone multiples.
- **Epic Universe is a one-time step-up, not recurring growth.** Once full capacity is reached late 2026 and Universal Kids opens, the parks growth rate normalizes to 3-5% from 15-22%. The market may already be pricing in the Epic ramp.

**My bear-case parks valuation:**

| Scenario | Parks EBITDA | Multiple | Value |
|----------|-------------|----------|-------|
| R1 Base | $3.8B | 14x | $53.2B |
| DA Base | $3.5B (slower ramp) | 11x (peer-appropriate) | $38.5B |
| DA Bear | $2.5B (recession) | 9x (compressed) | $22.5B |

The difference between R1 and DA base is $14.7B = $4.10/share. This is the single largest driver of valuation disagreement.

**Severidad: HIGH**

The 14x multiple is the most aggressive assumption in the SoP. It embeds a premium above Disney (the dominant operator) and above industry benchmarks. Even an 11-12x multiple more consistent with peers produces significantly lower FV.

**Resolucion sugerida:** Use 11-12x as base case for parks EBITDA. The R1 used 14x with "Epic Universe premium" rationale, but Epic's slower-than-expected ramp and capacity backtracking undermine this premium.

### 3. Broadband Trajectory -- Worse Than R1 Models

**R1 assumption:** Broadband revenue declines -2%/yr (2026E), -1.5%/yr (2027E), -1%/yr (2028E). Stabilization thesis.

**Evidence in contra:**

- **New Street Research (Feb 2026):** "Cable broadband will continue its decline" and cable operators "unlikely to grow subscribers this decade." No return to positive net adds before 2030. This is the industry's most cited independent research firm on broadband.
- **FWA capacity DOUBLED:** The big 3 wireless operators now have capacity for 32M FWA subscribers, up from 16M just one year ago. With additional C-Band spectrum, capacity could reach 36M. Current FWA penetration is ~13M -- the headroom for further gains against cable is enormous.
- **Q4 2025 subscriber loss WORSENED:** Q4 lost 181K, up from Q3's 104K. The R1 thesis highlighted Q3's 104K as evidence of "stabilization" -- but Q4 refuted this narrative. The trend is NOT consistently improving.
- **ARPU turning negative H1 2026:** Management froze broadband prices and is offering free wireless lines. Combined with sub losses, broadband revenue could decline 3-5% in 2026, not the R1's -2%.
- **BNP Paribas (Feb 24, 2026):** Downgraded CMCSA to Underperform ($27 PT), specifically citing Comcast as "most exposed" to incremental fiber headwinds. Fiber overlap growing from 34% to 55%+ of Comcast's footprint.
- **Comcast's own admission (Q1 2025 earnings):** "In this intensely competitive environment, we are not winning in the marketplace" -- Comcast President Michael Cavanagh.

**Impact on SoP:** If broadband EBITDA is $15B (not $16B) and declines faster, a 5.0x multiple (not 5.5x) is more appropriate. Broadband value: $75B vs R1's $88B -- a $13B difference ($3.60/share).

**Severidad: HIGH**

The broadband trajectory is the core bear case. Q4 2025 data refutes the stabilization narrative that partially underpins the R1 thesis. New Street's "no growth until after 2030" is the consensus view among independent analysts.

**Resolucion sugerida:** Model broadband revenue declining -3% in 2026, -2% in 2027-2028. Use 5.0x EBITDA multiple for broadband in SoP, not 5.5x.

### 4. Governance Discount -- 10% Is Insufficient

**R1 assumption:** 10% conglomerate/governance discount in SoP.

**Evidence in contra:**

- Roberts family holds 33.3% voting power with <1% economic interest. This is one of the most extreme dual-class structures among US large-caps.
- Roberts's control is NON-DILUTABLE -- fixed at 33.3% regardless of future share issuances. This permanently blocks hostile takeovers and shareholder activism.
- A 2019 shareholder proposal for an independent Board Chairman won 26% support DESPITE Roberts voting against it -- showing significant minority shareholder frustration.
- Academic research on dual-class discounts (DeAngelo & DeAngelo, Masulis et al.) consistently finds 15-25% valuation discounts for companies with entrenched dual-class governance.
- Comcast previously discussed acquiring Warner Bros. Discovery (CNBC, Dec 2025) -- Cavanagh outlined an "unsuccessful WBD offer." This demonstrates ongoing M&A appetite that shareholders cannot veto.
- Peacock losses of $10B+ cumulative represent value destruction that shareholders cannot challenge through governance mechanisms.
- The appointment of Cavanagh as co-CEO (first non-family member) is a positive signal, but Roberts RETAINS all Class B supervoting shares.
- The conglomerate discount and governance discount are ADDITIVE, not the same thing. A conglomerate structure warrants 10% discount. Dual-class governance warrants an ADDITIONAL 5-10% discount.

**Severidad: MODERATE**

A 10% discount vs 15-20% discount affects FV by $3-5/share. Not thesis-killing, but material in a thesis with only 12% edge over consensus.

**Resolucion sugerida:** Apply 15% combined conglomerate + governance discount in SoP, not 10%. This is consistent with academic evidence for extreme dual-class structures.

### 6. Peacock Economics -- Permanent Value Destruction Risk

**R1 assumption:** Peacock valued at 1.5x revenue ($5.4B) = $8.1B.

**Evidence in contra:**

- Peacock has $10B+ in cumulative losses. Q4 2025 loss WIDENED to $552M (from $372M Q4 2024) -- the trajectory worsened, not improved.
- The NBA deal costs $2.7B/yr for 11 years ($27B total commitment). NBA rights payments just started in Q4 2025 -- this is the BEGINNING of the cost increase, not the end.
- Management says "meaningful improvement" in Peacock losses in 2026, but provides NO breakeven date. This is concerning given the NBA cost step-up.
- Streaming industry economics: Netflix (the dominant player) took 12+ years to become consistently profitable. Peacock at 44M subs is NOT at Netflix scale (260M+). The scale economics that make Netflix work may never apply to Peacock.
- Reports indicate Comcast explored a merger/partnership for Peacock and discussed a WBD acquisition. "They know they have to buy or sell" -- former executive. If Peacock requires a merger to survive, it may have ZERO standalone value.
- If Peacock NEVER breaks even: $2B/yr permanent FCF drag, capitalized at 7x = $14B in destroyed value = $3.90/share.
- If Peacock is sold or merged at distressed terms, the value could be $0 to the parent company.

**Bear-case Peacock valuation:** $0 (if merged/sold at cost) to -$14B (if permanent cash drain). R1's $8.1B assumes 1.5x revenue -- this is generous for a money-losing streamer that management itself may abandon.

**Severidad: MODERATE**

Peacock is binary, and the R1 correctly notes this. However, assigning $8.1B of positive value to a unit with $10B cumulative losses and widening quarterly losses is optimistic. A ZERO value is more defensible as a base case.

**Resolucion sugerida:** Value Peacock at $0 in base case. Include $8.1B only in bull case scenario.

### 8. Value Fund Validation -- Challenge the Signal

**R1 argument:** 4 value funds (Dodge & Cox, First Eagle, Markel, Tweedy Browne) converging = bullish signal.

**Evidence in contra:**

- Dodge & Cox listed CMCSA as a DETRACTOR in their 2025 semi-annual review, alongside Charter Communications. They hold it because they believe broadband losses have limits -- this is the CONSENSUS bullish view, not a differentiated insight.
- Value funds holding declining businesses is the DEFINITION of the value trap pattern. The question is not "do smart people hold it?" but "are they right?" Value funds also held: Eastman Kodak, Sears, General Electric, Baidu -- many of which were value traps.
- Dodge & Cox's own words: "Charter and Comcast trade at mid-single-digit P/E ratios with durable earnings." This is literally the consensus bull case -- nothing differentiated.
- Tweedy Browne holds only 31K shares -- this is a de minimis position, not a conviction bet.
- Fund convergence requires a BASE RATE to be informative. How often does 4-fund convergence on a declining business predict outperformance vs the S&P 500? Without this data, the signal is anecdotal.

**Severidad: LOW**

Fund holdings are Level 4 information (consensus) per the critical-thinking framework. They confirm sentiment, not value. The R1 correctly notes this as "confirmation, not proof" -- so the thesis does not over-rely on this signal.

My $0 base case may be too harsh if strategic options exist. But "buy or sell" from a former executive is not a guaranteed positive outcome.

- I do not have access to Comcast's Q1 2026 data (earnings late April). The broadband trajectory (improving or worsening) is the single most important variable and I can only extrapolate from Q4 2025.
- I could not verify DOCSIS 4.0 deployment timelines from primary sources.
- My post-Versant EBITDA estimates are derived from available data, not from Comcast's clean financials (which don't exist yet).
- Theme park recession sensitivity is based on 2008-09 precedent, which may not be directly applicable to 2026 conditions.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- **FCF normalization should be a MANDATORY step** in any thesis where FCF margin deviates >200bps from 3yr average. The quality_scorer.py and DCF tool both use headline FCF, which was dangerously misleading for CMCSA ($19.2B headline vs ~$14B sustainable).
- **Conglomerate SoP threshold:** When a SoP produces FV >20% above consolidated EV/EBITDA, flag it as a potential "breakup premium" that may never be realized. In CMCSA's case, SoP = $39.25 vs consolidated EV/EBITDA base = $31.68 -- a 24% gap that represents value the market may never unlock due to governance constraints.
- **Governance discount protocol:** For dual-class structures with >25% voting power on <5% economic interest, apply minimum 15% FV discount. Currently applied ad hoc.
