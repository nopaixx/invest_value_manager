## TL;DR

Exelixis is a focused oncology biotech transforming from a single-drug company (cabozantinib/Cabometyx, $2.9B global franchise) into a multi-franchise oncology platform. The market is pricing in NEGATIVE growth (-2.9% FCF CAGR) despite 13% revenue CAGR and 37% ROIC, creating a disconnect. Zanzalintinib (PDUFA Dec 3, 2026 for mCRC) and patent protection through 2030-2031 provide both defensive moat and growth optionality. QS 79/100, Tier A.

## Business Understanding

### Modelo de Negocio

**Problem solved:** Exelixis develops and commercializes oncology drugs targeting solid tumors, primarily through inhibition of tyrosine kinase receptors (VEGF, MET, AXL) that drive tumor growth and angiogenesis.

**Revenue model:** Primarily pharmaceutical product sales (Cabometyx/Cometriq), supplemented by collaboration revenues (royalties from ex-US partners Ipsen and Takeda).

**Revenue breakdown (FY2025):**
- Net product revenues (US): $2,123M (91.5% of total)
- Collaboration/other: $197M (8.5% of total — royalties from Ipsen/Takeda ex-US sales)
- Total revenues: $2,320M

**Unit economics:**
- Gross margin: 96.4% (biotech/pharma standard for IP-protected drugs)
- R&D intensity: 35.6% of revenue ($825M FY2025, declining from 57% in FY2023 as pipeline matures)
- SG&A: 22.4% of revenue ($519M)
- Operating margin: 38.5% (expanded from 9.3% in 2023 — massive operating leverage)
- FCF margin: 36.4% ($844M)

**Capital intensity:** Asset-light. Capex negligible (<1% revenue). Cash generation machine.

### Structure of Growth

Cabometyx volume grew 16% in FY2025, driven by:
1. **RCC (renal cell carcinoma):** #1 TKI monotherapy, most prescribed TKI+IO combination
2. **Neuroendocrine tumors (NET):** Growing adoption since approval
3. **3% price increase** effective January 2026
4. **Global franchise:** $2.9B total (including partner revenues from Ipsen/Takeda)

### Por Que Esta "Barata" (P/E 15.7x for a 38% margin, 37% ROIC biotech)

**Narrativa del mercado:**
1. **Single-drug dependency:** ~80% US revenue from cabozantinib. Market treats it as a "one-trick pony" that will face patent cliff
2. **Patent cliff 2030-2031:** Teva and Cipla licensed to sell generics from Jan 2031. MSN earliest entry Jan 2030. Market already discounting post-patent revenue collapse
3. **Heavy R&D spend:** $825M in R&D (35.6% of revenue) for a pipeline that hasn't yet delivered a second franchise drug
4. **Insider selling:** $12.5M in insider sales last quarter — raises questions about management conviction

**Mi contra-tesis:**
1. Patent cliff is 4-5 years away and the market is pricing NEGATIVE growth TODAY (-2.9% implied FCF growth). Even with cliff, 5 years of 10%+ growth before it hits
2. Zanzalintinib is NOT pipeline speculation — it has positive Phase 3 data (STELLAR-303 OS benefit, HR 0.80, p=0.0045) and FDA accepted NDA with PDUFA Dec 3, 2026
3. CRC (colorectal cancer) is a $15B+ global market. Peak zanzalintinib estimates range $875M-$5B depending on indications
4. R&D/revenue ratio is DECLINING (57% -> 35.6%), meaning operating leverage is kicking in as cabozantinib revenue grows and pipeline spending normalizes
5. $2.16B in buybacks since 2023 (76.7M shares retired) shows capital allocation discipline
6. Insider selling is via 10b5-1 plans (pre-scheduled), common in biotech where comp is stock-heavy. Not necessarily a negative signal

### Ventaja Informacional

My edge is recognizing that the market prices EXEL as if cabozantinib revenue will DECLINE, while:
1. Volume is still growing 16% and a 3% price increase was just taken
2. A second franchise drug (zanzalintinib) has positive Phase 3 OS data and NDA accepted
3. Patent protection extends to 2030-2031, providing 4-5 years of protected revenue growth
4. Operating leverage is creating accelerating margin expansion

The market seems anchored to "biotech with patent cliff" narrative while ignoring the transformation into a multi-franchise oncology company.

## Moat Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW (15/25 moat evidence)**

**Strengths:**
- **Regulatory IP moat:** Patent protection through 2030 (MSN) and 2031 (Teva, Cipla settlements). EP0 ruled in favor of formulation patent. Strong IP defense track record
- **Market position in RCC:** #1 TKI monotherapy and most prescribed TKI+IO combination. Clinical evidence accumulated over years creates physician preference and prescribing inertia
- **High switching costs in oncology:** Once a patient starts a treatment regimen, switching is uncommon. Clinical data from combo studies (cabo + nivo) creates entrenched position
- **Gross margin 96.4%:** 41pp above sector median — classic moat indicator

**Weaknesses:**
- **Single molecule dependency:** Cabozantinib IS the franchise. Pipeline success (zanzalintinib) is necessary to transform moat from NARROW to WIDE
- **Commodity risk post-patent:** Once generics enter (2030-2031), pricing power collapses. Biotech moats are inherently time-limited by patent expiry
- **No platform switching costs:** Unlike SaaS or data companies, pharma moats don't compound. Each new drug requires new clinical data, new FDA approval
- **Competition intensifying:** Merck's Keytruda combos, BMS's Opdivo combos, and next-gen TKIs all compete in overlapping indications

**Moat duration:** NARROW for ~4-5 years (until generic entry). If zanzalintinib succeeds across CRC + RCC + NET, moat could WIDEN as multi-franchise creates portfolio effect and oncology expertise moat deepens.

## Risk Assessment

### Critical Risks

1. **Single-drug concentration (HIGH):** ~80% of US revenue from cabozantinib. Any safety signal, competitive displacement, or unexpected generic entry would be devastating. Mitigant: zanzalintinib diversification if approved.

2. **Patent cliff 2030-2031 (HIGH, but 4-5yr horizon):** Generic entry from MSN (Jan 2030), Teva/Cipla (Jan 2031). Revenue from cabozantinib could decline 60-80% over 2-3 years post-generic entry. Mitigant: zanzalintinib peak sales could partially offset.

3. **Zanzalintinib binary event (MEDIUM-HIGH):** PDUFA Dec 3, 2026. If FDA rejects or requires additional studies, stock could drop 15-25%. STELLAR-303 OS benefit was statistically significant (HR 0.80, p=0.0045) but modest (10.9 vs 9.4 months median OS). FDA could question clinical meaningfulness.

### Material Risks

4. **R&D execution risk (MEDIUM):** $875-925M R&D guidance for 2026. Multiple STELLAR trials running. Failure in STELLAR-304 (non-clear cell RCC) or STELLAR-311 (NET) would limit zanzalintinib's franchise potential.

5. **IRA drug pricing (MEDIUM):** Cabometyx could be subject to Medicare price negotiations under the Inflation Reduction Act. If selected, could reduce pricing by 25-60% for Medicare patients (estimated 30-40% of volume).

6. **Insider selling pattern (LOW-MEDIUM):** $12.5M sold in last quarter via 10b5-1 plans. Pre-scheduled, but pattern of consistent selling raises mild concern about management's private view of terminal value.

7. **Competition in RCC (MEDIUM):** Merck's belzutifan (Welireg) approved in RCC. Lenvatinib + pembrolizumab combinations competing. Market share erosion possible even before generic entry.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The biggest uncertainty is how to value a pharma company with a time-limited moat. The patent cliff is REAL and KNOWN, but 4-5 years of protected revenue growth is also real. The market seems to be pricing the cliff NOW when it's years away
- Zanzalintinib's commercial potential is highly uncertain. The Phase 3 OS benefit (HR 0.80, 1.5 month median OS improvement) is statistically significant but clinically modest. FDA approval is likely (75%+) but peak sales estimates range 3x ($875M to $2B+)
- Beta of 0.42 seems too low for a biotech with binary pipeline events. I adjusted to 0.65 but this is subjective. WACC could reasonably range 7-10%

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The pharma-healthcare.md sector view should add an "Oncology/Biotech" subsector. EXEL is fundamentally different from Big Pharma (patent cliff dynamics, single-drug risk, pipeline binaries)
- The quality_scorer.py should have a "patent cliff" penalty mechanism for pharma companies approaching loss of exclusivity. Current framework doesn't penalize time-limited moats

### Preguntas para Orchestrator
1. Should EXEL be added to the quality universe given QS 75 borderline and E[CAGR] below threshold?
2. Is the zanzalintinib PDUFA date (Dec 3, 2026) worth tracking in the calendar as a pipeline catalyst even if we don't take a position?

### Anomalias Detectadas
- Reverse DCF implies NEGATIVE growth (-2.9%) when the company just guided for +11% revenue growth. This is a significant disconnect that suggests the market is pricing the patent cliff far too aggressively in the near term
- ROIC trajectory (7% -> 6% -> 23% -> 38%) shows massive inflection. This is unusual and reflects the company crossing from R&D-heavy to commercially mature. The 38% may not be sustainable as zanzalintinib launch requires new investment
- Insider selling is consistent and systematic but through 10b5-1 plans. Could be compensation-related selling (standard for biotech execs) rather than conviction-based
