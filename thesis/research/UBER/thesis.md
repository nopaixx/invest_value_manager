## Business Understanding

### What Problem Does Uber Solve?

Uber operates a three-sided marketplace connecting riders/consumers with drivers/couriers and merchants (restaurants, grocery, retail). The platform solves:

1. **Mobility:** On-demand transportation without car ownership. 76% US ride-hailing market share. Present in 70+ countries.
2. **Delivery:** Food, grocery, alcohol, retail delivery via Uber Eats. #2 globally, #2 US (23% share behind DoorDash at 56%).
3. **Freight:** Digital freight brokerage connecting shippers and carriers. Smaller segment but addressable market is massive.

**Revenue model:** Take rate on Gross Bookings. Uber takes ~28-30% of each ride/delivery as revenue. FY2025: $52B revenue on $193B gross bookings = ~27% take rate.

### Revenue Mix (FY2025)
- Mobility: ~60% of revenue (higher margin)
- Delivery: ~30% of revenue (margin improving)
- Freight: ~10% of revenue (breakeven/slight loss)

### Unit Economics
- **Uber One members:** 46 million (up from 35M in 2024), accounting for 40% of all bookings
- **MAPCs (Monthly Active Platform Consumers):** 202 million (+18% YoY)
- **Trips:** 11.1 billion in FY2025 (+18% YoY)
- **Revenue per MAPC:** ~$257/year (growing with engagement)
- **Uber One LTV/CAC:** Not disclosed but 40% of bookings from members = powerful retention and cross-sell

### Margin Structure and Trajectory

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Trend |
|--------|------|------|------|------|-------|
| Gross Margin | 38.3% | 39.8% | 39.4% | 39.8% | Stable ~40% |
| Operating Margin | -5.7% | 3.0% | 6.4% | 10.7% | Rapid expansion |
| FCF Margin | 1.2% | 9.0% | 15.7% | 18.8% | Rapid expansion |
| SBC/Revenue | 5.6% | 5.2% | 4.1% | 3.5% | Declining |
| R&D/Revenue | 8.8% | 8.5% | 7.1% | 6.5% | Operating leverage |

The margin expansion story is the core investment thesis. Operating leverage is powerful: fixed costs (R&D, G&A) are declining as % of revenue while take rates remain stable. FCF margins have gone from 1% to 19% in 3 years.

### Capital Intensity
- Asset-light marketplace model. Capex/Revenue ~1% ($0.4x capex/depreciation ratio)
- Working capital is a SOURCE of cash (negative working capital cycle from prepaid rides)
- NOTE: Robotaxi pivot introduces asset risk. Rivian deal = up to $1.25B investment for 10K vehicles. This could shift capital intensity upward, but remains small relative to $10B FCF.

## Autonomous Vehicle Risk — Deep Dive

This is the #1 risk and deserves thorough analysis.

**Current State (March 2026):**
- Waymo: Operating commercially in SF, Phoenix, Austin, LA. ~150K trips/week. Expanding to 15+ cities.
- Tesla: FSD improving but no commercial robotaxi service yet.
- Uber: NOT building AV tech. Strategy = platform partnerships:
  - Nvidia: 100K robotaxi deployment across 28 cities by 2028
  - Waymo: Partnership fraying (SF, Dallas, London) but still active
  - Waabi: $1B deal for 25K robotaxis
  - Rivian: $1.25B investment for 10K autonomous R2 vehicles
  - Nuro: Lucid-based robotaxis in SF by late 2026

**Bear Case on AV:** Waymo goes direct-to-consumer, bypasses Uber entirely. Tesla achieves L4 and deploys massive fleet via Tesla Network. Uber's driver network becomes obsolete. Result: Uber becomes a commodity dispatch app, margins compress to near zero.

**Bull Case on AV:** AVs are hard to scale (insurance, regulation, edge cases, weather, cities). Uber becomes the "Android" of autonomous mobility — the platform layer that all AV operators deploy through because Uber has the demand side (202M users). Uber's take rate on AV rides could be HIGHER than human rides (no driver incentives). FCF margins could expand to 25%+.

**My Assessment:** The truth is between these. AV deployment will be gradual (city-by-city, weather-limited, regulatory-gated). Uber has 5+ years of dominance in most markets. During this transition, Uber is actively building AV partnerships AND investing in its own fleet (Rivian, Nvidia). The risk is real but manageable and priced in. At 16x P/E, the market already discounts significant AV disruption.

6. **KC#6: Competitive Convergence.** DoorDash launches ride-hailing at scale, OR Lyft gains >5pp share, OR new entrant disrupts with significantly lower pricing.
