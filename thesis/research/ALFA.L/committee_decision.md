## Gate 2: Business Understanding

**Can I explain this business in 2 minutes?**

YES. Alfa Financial Software makes mission-critical software (Alfa Systems) for the asset finance industry -- companies that do auto leasing, equipment finance, wholesale lending. Their platform manages the entire lifecycle from loan origination to collections and end-of-term. Clients include Bank of America, Mercedes-Benz Financial Services, Barclays, John Deere Financial. Once installed (12-48 month implementation), clients never leave (96% retention, NRR 109-112%). Revenue is transitioning from perpetual licenses + services to subscription SaaS (ARR growing 16%). The company has net cash, ROIC 71%, and 56.7% insider ownership. It is at a 52-week low because the founder's holding company (CHP, 55% stake) periodically sells shares creating supply overhang, plus UK small-cap tech de-rating from SaaSpocalypse.

**Why is it cheap?**
1. CHP serial share disposals (structural overhang, 3+ years)
2. UK small-cap tech de-rating (SaaSpocalypse contagion)
3. Low free float (~43%) amplifying any selling
4. EQT bid withdrawal stigma (2023)

**Counter-thesis:** CHP selling is NOT transient -- it is structural. Page tried to sell the whole company at 208p (EQT bid 2023), failed, now selling incrementally. Growth may decelerate from 15% to 6-8% as SE revenue declines. Solifi (TA Associates-backed) is executing a buy-and-build strategy targeting ALFA's TAM. Bear case at 169p (DA-calibrated) is tight.

**Value Trap Score: 0.5/10** (only partial mark for CHP disposals). This is NOT a value trap.

**Edge:** Patience edge + UK small-cap inefficiency. Market focuses on CHP selling and illiquidity; I focus on 109% NRR, 71% ROIC, net cash, and 96% retention in mission-critical software. FV 215p vs 173p market = ~25% undervaluation. Our edge is the willingness to own an illiquid UK small-cap that institutions avoid.
