## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

Interactive Brokers provides automated electronic brokerage services to professional traders, institutional investors, hedge funds, financial advisors, and increasingly retail investors worldwide. The company operates a single unified platform across 170+ markets in multiple countries and currencies. Founded in 1978 by Thomas Peterffy, IBKR pioneered electronic market-making and transitioned to electronic brokerage.

**Revenue Mix (FY2025, $6.205B total net revenue):**

| Stream | Amount | % of Total | YoY Growth | Driver |
|--------|--------|-----------|------------|--------|
| Commissions | $2.149B | 35% | +26.6% | Trading volume (DARTs), account growth |
| Net Interest Income | $3.563B | 57% | +13.2% | Client cash balances, margin loans, rates |
| Other Fees & Services | $291M | 5% | +3.9% | Market data, PFOF, FDIC sweep |
| Other Income | $202M | 3% | +237% | Investment returns |

**Problem Solved:** Professional-grade execution at lowest cost globally. IBKR's commissions are ~60% below competitors. Margin loan rates are the lowest in the industry (benchmark - 0.5%). The platform pays interest on cash (0.5% below interbank rate) while most competitors pay near-zero. This creates a self-reinforcing flywheel: lowest cost attracts sophisticated clients with larger balances, larger balances generate more NII, NII funds further cost reductions.

### Unit Economics

- Revenue per account (FY2025): ~$1,410 (declining from ~$1,557 in 2024)
- Customer accounts: 4.40M (+32% YoY), Peterffy expects similar pace in 2026
- Customer equity: $779.9B (+37% YoY)
- Customer margin loans: $90.2B (+40% YoY)
- DARTs: 4.04M (+30% YoY)
- Customer acquisition: primarily organic (word-of-mouth, awards, lowest-cost reputation)
- Pre-tax margin: 79% (Q4 2025), ~77% FY2025
- Non-interest expenses: $1.434B -- grew only +3.7% while revenue grew +19.7%

**Operating Leverage is Exceptional:** The automation-first model means incremental accounts add minimal cost. FY2025 opex $1.434B on $6.2B revenue = 23% cost ratio. This is among the best in financial services globally. The 574 employees in tech and support handle 4.4M accounts -- that is 7,665 accounts per employee.

### Capital Intensity: Asset-Light Platform

- Capex is negligible relative to revenue (<1%)
- Working capital is dominated by client money flows (distorts standard FCF analysis)
- Real capital needs: regulatory capital requirements (broker-dealer), which scale modestly with AUM
- Dividend payout ratio: 13.6% -- retains most earnings for growth

## Por Que Esta Cara (Why It's Expensive)

**Market narrative:** IBKR is a best-of-breed secular growth story in financial infrastructure. 32% account growth, 79% margins, founder-controlled, benefiting from global retail trading boom + rate environment. Recent S&P 500 inclusion (2025) drove index buying. Hormuz crisis + elevated volatility = DARTs tailwind.

**Why it's trading at 31x P/E:**
1. Account growth (32%) is among the highest of any brokerage globally
2. Pre-tax margins (79%) are the highest in the industry by wide margin
3. Founder-controlled with extreme alignment (Peterffy's $75B+ at stake)
4. Global platform with no direct comparable -- Schwab is domestic, Robinhood is retail-only
5. S&P 500 inclusion created forced buying
6. Hormuz crisis/volatility is a direct revenue tailwind (commissions + margin activity)

**My contra-thesis (why it may not be expensive ENOUGH or why it IS too expensive):**

The market is pricing 15-20% forward EPS growth sustainably. BUT:

1. **NII is 57% of revenue and rate-sensitive.** If Fed cuts 100-150bp over 18 months, NII could decline $200-300M annually. This would turn 15% EPS growth into 5-8%.
2. **Revenue per account is declining** as new accounts are smaller retail (average account size shrinking). 32% account growth translates to only ~20% revenue growth.
3. **The 32% account growth is not sustainable at this base.** Growing from 1M to 1.32M accounts is different from growing 4.4M to 5.8M. International expansion provides runway but each marginal account is less valuable.
4. **Competitor response:** Schwab (post-Ameritrade integration), Robinhood (launching futures/options), and crypto-native platforms are all investing in professional tools.

**Value Trap Assessment:** 0/10 -- This is NOT a value trap. It's a growth premium stock trading above fair value. The risk is overpaying for growth, not fundamental deterioration.

### Ventaja Informacional

My edge is recognizing the NII risk that the market may be underweighting. The market sees 32% account growth and extrapolates. I see 57% NII dependency and ask: what happens in a rate-cutting cycle? Additionally, the revenue-per-account dilution is not well-discussed in sell-side research.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The ownership structure creates complexity. Net income to common ($984M) vs total net income ($4.357B) -- the P/E of 31x on Class A EPS is the right metric for Class A investors, but the total entity trades at 31x total earnings too ($135B implied / $4.357B). Both approaches yield the same P/E, which is reassuring.
- FCF distortion from client money flows makes the DCF tool useless. I had to rely entirely on earnings-based methods. This is correct for a financial services company but limits my toolkit.
- The 40.4% receivables growth vs 9.7% revenue growth is a yellow flag. Could be settlement timing (normal for a broker during high-volume periods) or could signal credit risk in margin lending. Needs deeper investigation in R2/DA.
- Peterffy's age (80) and succession planning is the elephant in the room. Galik has been CEO since 2019, but the founder premium is real for this company.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The DCF tool and quality scorer need financial services exemptions. FCF margins >100% flagged correctly but the DCF still produced nonsensical $800 FV. For brokerages/banks, the tool should automatically switch to P/E or earnings-based valuation.
- The quality scorer's "insider ownership" at 2.8% dramatically understates Peterffy's 75% economic interest. For dual-class/LLC structures, the scorer should flag this for manual override.
- Consider creating a "financial-services-brokerage" sector view or expanding financial-data-analytics to include electronic brokerage.
