# Moat Assessment: SPG (Simon Property Group)

## Fecha: 2026-03-17

## Clasificacion: WIDE

Simon Property Group possesses a Wide moat built on at least 3 distinct and reinforcing sources of competitive advantage: irreplaceable location quality (efficient scale), cost advantages from unmatched scale, and network effects between premium tenants and foot traffic. The moat has been tested by the e-commerce narrative for over a decade and has emerged stronger in A-class properties, though B/C mall exposure (limited for SPG) remains structurally challenged.

## Escenarios de Erosion

### 1. Structural Decline of Physical Retail (MOST PROBABLE erosion path)
- **Trigger:** E-commerce share exceeds 40-50% of total retail (currently ~16-20%), combined with autonomous delivery and VR shopping making physical visits unnecessary.
- **Probability:** LOW for A-class properties within 10 years. E-commerce penetration has plateaued at ~20% after rapid COVID gains. Physical retail serving different need (experiential, immediate, social). Outlet malls serve price-discovery function e-commerce cannot replicate well.
- **Timeline:** If it happens, 15-20+ years. Mall traffic is currently RISING, not falling.
- **Impact:** Would erode both network effects and efficient scale advantages.

### 2. Secular Shift in Consumer Behavior Away from Mall-Based Shopping (TAIL RISK)
- **Trigger:** Gen Z/Alpha generations fundamentally reject mall-format shopping in favor of direct-to-consumer, social commerce, or entirely new retail formats.
- **Probability:** LOW-MEDIUM. Current data shows younger demographics still visit malls for social/experiential purposes. However, behavioral shifts are hard to predict over 20+ year horizons.
- **Timeline:** 10-20 years.
- **Impact:** Would gradually erode tenant demand, occupancy, and rent growth.

### 3. Credit/Interest Rate Crisis (NEAR-TERM RISK)
- **Trigger:** Prolonged high interest rates (10Y > 6%) combined with recession forces refinancing at much higher rates. $29.2B debt becomes a structural burden.
- **Probability:** LOW-MEDIUM. A credit rating provides significant buffer. Debt maturity is laddered. But 6.0x ND/EBITDA is not conservative.
- **Timeline:** 1-5 years.
- **Impact:** Would compress FFO through higher interest expense, not destroy moat itself. Temporary, not permanent.

## Moat Classification Justification

**WIDE MOAT** based on:

1. **3+ sources of moat identified:** Cost advantage (scale), network effects (tenant-traffic flywheel), efficient scale (irreplaceable locations), intangible assets (brand, credit rating). Switching costs are moderate but additive.

2. **ROIC > WACC consistently for 10+ years:** ROIC averaged ~12.1% over 10 years vs WACC ~9-10%. Current spread is +8.4pp and WIDENING. This is the strongest quantitative evidence of a durable moat.

3. **Gross margin premium of +21.9pp vs sector median:** Not a marginal advantage. SPG operates at 81.9% gross margin vs 60% sector median. This is pricing power plus operational scale.

4. **Durability >20 years for core advantages:** You cannot build new Class A malls. Land is gone, zoning prohibits it, community opposition blocks it, and economics don't support it. This creates a permanent scarcity value for existing premier locations.

5. **Moat is STRENGTHENING, not weakening:** Occupancy at cycle highs (96%+), foot traffic rising, leasing spreads at double digits, no new supply entering the market. The e-commerce threat has been fully stress-tested (COVID forced 100% e-commerce for months) and A-class malls recovered fully.

**Why not NARROW:** The combination of irreplaceable physical assets + scale + network effects is genuinely rare. MAC (ROIC 0.8%, -9.6pp spread) and SKT (ROIC 5.6%, -2.9pp spread) operate in the same industry but cannot replicate SPG's economics. That differential IS the moat, and it has persisted for decades.

**Caveat:** The Wide moat classification applies to SPG's A-class properties (which represent the majority of NOI). Lower-tier properties within the portfolio have narrower moats and face more structural risk. The blended assessment is Wide because the portfolio skews heavily toward premier assets.

## 🔄 META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- **REIT leverage distortion in QS:** The quality_scorer.py penalizes SPG for 6.0x ND/EBITDA, but REITs structurally carry more debt. The 64 QS may mislead the orchestrator into treating SPG as Tier B when its moat evidence is clearly Tier A quality. I recommend the orchestrator consider a REIT-adjusted QS around 72-76.
- **10-year ROIC data granularity:** I relied on external source showing 10yr avg ROIC of ~12.1% (from DiscoverCI). The quality_scorer.py only provides 4 years of data (2022-2025). I could not independently verify each year from 2015-2021 via T1 tools. However, the 4 visible years all show ROIC > WACC, and the trend is strongly upward.
- **Tenant sales PSF:** The $640-800/sqft figures come from company disclosures and T2/T3 sources. I could not independently verify via T1 tools. These are plausible given the 81.9% gross margin and premium rent levels, but should be cross-checked against SPG's 10-K when available.

### Sugerencias de Mejora
- **REIT QS adjustment:** The quality_scorer.py should consider sector-specific leverage benchmarks. For REITs, 6.0x ND/EBITDA is moderate (MAC is 8.7x). Penalizing equally as an industrial company distorts the profile.
- **Market Position manual input:** The 0/8 for market position is a systematic undercount when quality_scorer.py cannot auto-detect dominance. For obvious #1 players, the orchestrator should manually adjust.

### Anomalias Detectadas
- **Saks Global $100M investment loss:** SPG's recent loss on Saks Global (bankruptcy March 2026) is a yellow flag on capital allocation discipline. This is not a moat issue but a management judgment issue worth monitoring. If SPG continues making large retail equity investments that go bankrupt, it erodes the capital allocation quality.
- **EPS CAGR of 29.5%:** This is unusually high and likely includes one-time items or accounting changes (REIT EPS includes gains on asset sales). FFO/AFFO growth is the correct metric for REITs and is likely in the 4-7% range. The EPS figure should not be used for growth projections.
