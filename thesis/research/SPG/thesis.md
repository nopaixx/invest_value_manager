**Adjustment: +8 points. Justification (REIT structural distortion):**

1. **Leverage (0/10 -> 6/10, +6 pts):** The tool scores Net Debt/EBITDA 6.0x as 0/10 using generic corporate thresholds (<1x = 10, <2x = 8, etc.). For REITs, 5-7x ND/EBITDA is NORMAL and reflects the asset-backed, mortgage-financed business model. SPG has A/A2 credit rating, 6.5x interest coverage, and $9.1B liquidity. Scoring 6/10 reflects "adequate leverage for a REIT" rather than the 0/10 the generic formula assigns.

2. **Market Position (+2 pts correction):** Tool scored 0/8 (manual default). SPG is the undisputed #1 retail REIT globally ($62B market cap vs next largest ~$15B). Correcting to 8/8, but attributing only +2 pts net to avoid double-counting with existing moat score.

**Offsetting factor:** EPS CAGR of 29.5% is distorted by a 2025 accounting item (EPS $7.26 -> $14.17, nearly doubled, while Real Estate FFO grew only 4.0%). The +8 leverage/position adjustment roughly offsets this EPS inflation in the Growth sub-score.

## Business Understanding

### The Business

Simon Property Group owns, develops, and manages premier shopping, dining, entertainment, and mixed-use destinations:

- **114 traditional malls** (Class A regional malls in high-income, dense metros)
- **108 Premium Outlets** (SPG's crown jewel — unmatched global brand)
- **14 Mills centers** (value-oriented, 99.2% occupied)
- **6 lifestyle centers + 12 other properties**
- **International:** Europe (McArthurGlen JV), Asia (Jakarta Premium Outlets opened Mar 2025, Italian outlet acquisitions Jan 2025)

**Problem solved:** Premium physical retail space in irreplaceable locations. Class A malls and premium outlets serve functions online cannot replicate: experiential shopping, luxury brand experience, tourism destination, and outlet bargain-hunting.

**Revenue model:**
- Base minimum rent (contractual, escalating — $60.97/sqft, +4.7% YoY)
- Percentage rent (tied to tenant sales — retailer sales $799/sqft, +8.1% YoY)
- Tenant reimbursements (CAM, taxes, insurance)
- Ancillary income (parking, advertising, events)

~90%+ recurring via long-term leases. Average lease term 5-7 years with built-in escalators.

### Unit Economics

- **Occupancy:** 96.4% (malls/outlets), 99.2% (Mills) — near-full
- **Leasing spreads:** Positive, 17M sqft signed in 2025
- **NOI growth:** 4.4% domestic, 4.7% total portfolio (2025)
- **Base rent/sqft growth:** +4.7% YoY (compound escalation)
- **Retailer sales/sqft:** $799, up 8.1% — tenants are thriving
- **FCF Margin:** 50.3% (declining from 58.9% in 2022 due to growth capex, not margin erosion)
- **Capital intensity:** Asset-heavy (REIT by nature), but maintenance capex covered by depreciation; growth capex ($2B acquisitions 2025) is discretionary

## Moat Assessment

### Moat Type: WIDE

**1. Irreplaceable Assets (Primary)**
SPG owns the highest-quality retail real estate portfolio in the world. Class A malls in dense, high-income metros cannot be replicated — there are no new greenfield mall developments in the US. Zoning restrictions, NIMBYism, and the capital required create permanent barriers. Premium Outlets is a globally recognized brand with no equivalent competitor at scale.

**2. Scale Advantages**
At $62B market cap, SPG is 4x the size of the next retail REIT. This provides: lowest cost of capital in the sector (A/A2 rating), bargaining power with national tenants (one lease negotiation covers 100+ locations), ability to invest in redevelopment ($2B acquisitions, $4B pipeline), and data/analytics across 200+ properties.

**3. Switching Costs for Tenants**
Premium retailers NEED to be in the best malls. Nike, Louis Vuitton, Apple cannot afford to NOT be in Simon properties. The occupancy cost ratio is manageable, making leaving economically irrational.

**4. Network Effects (Mild)**
The best tenants attract the best consumers who attract more tenants. SPG's $799/sqft retailer sales (vs industry median ~$400) demonstrates this virtuous cycle.

### Moat Durability Risks
- E-commerce continues to grow share (but Class A malls are experiential, not transactional — proven resilience)
- Interest rate sensitivity (higher rates compress REIT valuations and increase refinancing costs)
- Tariff-driven tenant stress (David Simon explicitly flagged small/mid retailer pressure for 2026)

## Valuation (REIT-Specific Multi-Method)

### Method 1: P/FFO Valuation (50% weight — primary REIT metric)

**2025 Real Estate FFO:** $12.73/share (record)
**2026 Guided FFO midpoint:** $13.13/share

Historical P/FFO range (5yr): 12x-18x. Current: 15.0x TTM, 14.5x forward.

| Scenario | FFO | Multiple | FV |

| Bear | $12.73 (flat) | 13x | $169 |
| Base | $13.13 (guided) | 16x | $210 |

Bear P/FFO multiple (13x) assumes: rate shock, no FFO growth, sentiment collapse. Base (16x) is slightly above current but appropriate for best-in-class. Bull (17.5x) requires rate cuts and investor re-rating.

### Method 2: NAV-Based Valuation (25% weight)

Estimated stabilized NOI: ~$3.8B
Cap rates per Green Street: top 100 malls ~4%, next 200 ~5.4%. SPG portfolio is predominantly Class A with Premium Outlets.

| Scenario | NOI | Cap Rate | Property Value | - Net Debt | NAV/share |
|----------|-----|----------|---------------|------------|-----------|
| Bear | $3.6B | 5.75% | $62.6B | $28.4B | $104 |
| Base | $3.8B | 5.0% | $76.0B | $28.4B | $147 |
| Bull | $4.0B | 4.5% | $88.9B | $28.4B | $170 |

**Bear NAV includes 10% conglomerate/portfolio discount:** $104 * 0.90 = ~$121 adjusted.

Caveat: NAV is highly cap-rate-sensitive. 100bp change = ~$60/share. Market implies ~4.2% cap rate (EV $90B / NOI $3.8B), suggesting SPG trades at a premium to private market NAV — justified by management quality, scale, and development optionality.

### DCF Tool Context

The dcf_calculator.py reverse analysis implies 16.6%/yr FCF growth at current price vs 0.9% historical. The DCF tool output is disregarded for this analysis.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- AFFO per share was not available from public sources — SPG reports Real Estate FFO but not AFFO in press releases. Tenant improvement allowances and leasing commissions (FFO-to-AFFO bridge items) are buried in the 10-K supplemental. This introduces ~10-15% uncertainty in per-share earnings power.
- Cap rate assumptions drive NAV enormously: 100bp difference = ~$60/share. NAV method is inherently imprecise for a diversified portfolio.
- The DCF tool is fundamentally broken for REITs (uses FCF distorted by mandatory distributions and lumpy development capex). The 16.6% implied growth from reverse DCF is nonsensical.
- EPS jumped from $7.26 to $14.17 in 2025 (vs FFO growth of only 4%) — likely a one-time gain or accounting item. OCF/NI ratio dropped from 1.6x to 0.9x simultaneously. Worth investigating in the 10-K for R2.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- **REIT valuation tool:** A REIT-specific calculator taking FFO, AFFO, NOI, cap rate would be more useful than dcf_calculator.py for this asset class.
- **Quality scorer `--reit` mode:** Net Debt/EBITDA systematically penalizes REITs (0/10 on leverage for a company with A/A2 rating). A flag using Debt/Total Assets and interest coverage would be more accurate.
- **60/40 anti-bullish protocol calibration:** Consider tiering: 60/40 for growth stocks, 55/45 for stable businesses, 50/50 for defensive/income (REITs, utilities). The current one-size-fits-all approach is excessively conservative for predictable cash flow businesses.

### Anomalias Detectadas
- EPS CAGR 29.5% inflates Growth sub-score (10/10) when actual FFO growth is ~4%. quality_scorer.py should ideally use FFO for REITs, not EPS.
- OCF/Net Income dropped from 1.6x to 0.9x in 2025 — anomalous. The same accounting event that doubled EPS likely affected this ratio. Needs 10-K investigation.
