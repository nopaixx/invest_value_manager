## Phase 2: Independent Research Findings

### Finding #1: FY2025 Confirms Deceleration, Not Re-Acceleration
**Severity: HIGH**

FY2025 revenue: EUR 3,132M, +3% cFX. The thesis header uses 6% growth. Actual delivered: 3%. The bull argument rests on Q4 +7% showing "re-acceleration," but:

- Q4 is Moncler's STRONGEST quarter (winter = down jacket season). Seasonal strength is not trend reversal.
- Q2 2025: Moncler brand revenue DECLINED 2% YoY. EMEA down 8%. Asia flat.
- FY2025 net income: EUR 627M, DOWN 2% YoY despite revenue growing 3%. This means NEGATIVE operating leverage.
- Moncler guided "mid-single-digit" price increases for 2026, which means volume growth is approximately zero.
- EBIT margin: 29.2% vs 29.5% prior year. Slight compression despite price increases = volume weakness.

**The pattern 27% -> 15% -> 4% -> 3% is a textbook maturation curve, not a cyclical dip.** At 270+ stores globally, significant markets are approaching saturation. The thesis needs to prove WHY growth re-accelerates from here.

**Counter-evidence to my finding:** HSBC upgraded to Buy with EUR 72 target, expecting Moncler brand to grow ~10% (fastest in sector). Q4 2025 showed Asia +11%, Americas strong. Stone Island Q4 +16% was genuinely impressive.

**Resolution:** Growth of 6% remains unproven. FY2025 delivered 3%. Even with Q4 momentum, Q2 weakness suggests inconsistency. I use **4% as DA base case** (splitting between FY2025 actual 3% and thesis 6%).

---

### Finding #2: CEO Transition Is Now Imminent -- Not Theoretical
**Severity: HIGH**

When the R2 DA was written in February, the CEO transition was "upcoming." As of March 18, 2026, it is **14 days away** (April 1, 2026):

- Bartolomeo "Leo" Rongone replaces Ruffini as CEO. Ruffini becomes Executive Chairman.
- Roberto Eggs (Chief Business & Global Market Officer) already stepped down March 1. That is a key commercial executive GONE.
- Rongone comes from Bottega Veneta (Kering) -- a very different luxury house with different brand dynamics.

**Luxury sector CEO transition precedents:**
- Burberry: CEO change led to multi-year brand confusion, stock -40%
- Hugo Boss: CEO transition created strategic drift, took 3+ years to stabilize
- Gucci/Kering: Creative director departure led to significant brand heat loss

**The timing risk is acute.** Q1 2026 results (due ~April) will be the FIRST quarter under the new leadership structure. Any misstep in messaging, strategy continuity, or guidance will be magnified.

**Counter-evidence:** Ruffini stays as Executive Chairman with creative direction. His 18.2% stake (via Double R, backed by LVMH at 22%) ensures alignment. Rongone is experienced (Fendi, YSL, Bottega Veneta). The transition is planned, not crisis-driven.

**Resolution:** The risk is not that Rongone is incompetent. The risk is DISTRACTION and MISEXECUTION during a period of already-weak demand. A planned transition is better than a crisis one, but luxury brands are built on vision consistency. Weight: MEDIUM-HIGH probability of some disruption, HIGH impact if it occurs.

### Finding #3: Market Is NOT Mispricing Moncler -- No Informational Edge
**Severity: MODERATE**

This is the most fundamental challenge to the thesis:

- **Reverse DCF:** Market implies 5.7% FCF growth. The thesis assumes 6% revenue growth. These are effectively convergent. The market is already pricing the bull case.
- **Consensus PT:** Mean EUR 59.18 (range EUR 44-70). Our thesis FV of EUR 58 is BELOW consensus. We are not finding an edge -- we are finding the same number everyone else finds.
- **HSBC just upgraded to EUR 72.** Our FV of EUR 52 (R3) is 28% below the most bullish analyst. Either HSBC is wildly wrong, or our conservatism is too extreme, or both. Either way, we have no differentiated view.
- **Error #49 applies:** If FV = consensus, we have no informational edge. The thesis explicitly acknowledged this in R3: "Quality-at-discount thesis, not contrarian."

**The thesis is honest about this.** The R3 resolution explicitly states: "If EUR 42 never comes, we never buy. That is acceptable." This is price discipline, not insight. The DA agrees with this framing but notes that at EUR 54.24, the stock is 23% above the EUR 44 SO trigger. The probability of reaching EUR 44 without a market-wide selloff is low.

---

### Finding #4: Tariff Headwinds Are Real and Worsening
**Severity: MODERATE**

Since the R2 DA was written, tariff developments have WORSENED:

- Moncler raised prices "mid-single-digit" for H2 2025 and H1 2026 specifically to offset tariffs.
- The company stated pricing would be "more conservative" -- signaling concern about consumer pushback.
- Store expansion plans for 2026 (~12 new stores) may be POSTPONED if conditions worsen (CNBC, July 2025).
- Q2 2025 EMEA decline of -8% was partially driven by weakened tourist flows (Chinese/Korean tourists spending less in Europe).
- FX headwind: EUR strength expected to impact 2026 top line by -4%, with -6% in Q1.

**Moncler is NOT Ferrari.** Ferrari proved full tariff pass-through. Moncler is "aspirational luxury" -- its customers are more price-sensitive than ultra-luxury buyers. The tariff pass-through assumption is unproven for Moncler specifically.

---

### Finding #5: Stone Island Q1 2025 Decline + Wholesale Collapse
**Severity: MODERATE**

Stone Island delivered mixed results:
- FY2025: EUR 411M, +4% cFX. Respectable.
- But **Q1 2025: -5% decline**, driven by -19% wholesale collapse.
- DTC growth is offsetting wholesale decline, but the transition is consuming management attention.
- The EUR 1.15B acquisition (2020) at ~2.8x revenue is still not generating growth commensurate with the price paid.

The thesis uses Stone Island 8-10% growth in the blended 6% assumption. FY2025 delivered 4%. Even with Q4 +16% acceleration, the wholesale model is structurally declining and DTC transition is incomplete.

---

### Finding #6: Gross Margin Near Theoretical Ceiling
**Severity: LOW**

Gross margin at 78.1% is exceptional. However:
- Moncler management stated the theoretical cap is ~80%.
- At 78.1%, there is only ~2pp of expansion potential. This means margin expansion CANNOT be a growth driver going forward.
- The moat assessment noted Moncler's 78% GM exceeding Hermes (~70%) may be partially an accounting artifact (IFRS 16, product mix). If so, the quality signal is slightly inflated.

This is LOW severity because the current level is not at risk -- it is about future expansion potential being limited.

### 3B. Independent Bear-Case Valuation

**Method: EV/EBIT on bear-case EBIT, cross-checked with DCF**

**Bear assumptions:**
- Revenue growth: 3% (FY2025 actual, not thesis 6%)
- EBIT margin: 28% (slight compression from 29.2% due to tariffs + FX + A&P)
- Bear multiple: 14x EV/EBIT (current luxury sector stressed multiple; Kering at 15x, sector trough ~12-14x)
- Terminal growth: 2.0% (below thesis 2.5%)
- WACC: 10.0% (thesis 9.0% + 50bps CEO transition + 50bps China risk)

**Bear EBIT calculation:**
- FY2025 revenue: EUR 3,132M x 1.03 = EUR 3,226M (FY2026E)
- Bear EBIT: EUR 3,226M x 28% = EUR 903M
- Bear EV: EUR 903M x 14 = EUR 12,642M
- Net cash: EUR 1,458M (FY2025 actual)
- Bear equity: EUR 14,100M / 271.6M shares = **EUR 51.9**

Wait -- that is close to market price. Let me use the deeper bear case:

**Stress bear (recession + China decline):**
- Revenue: EUR 3,132M x 0.97 = EUR 3,038M (China -15% = -EUR 94M drag)
- EBIT margin: 25% (margin compression under volume decline)
- Stress EBIT: EUR 760M
- Multiple: 13x (recession luxury trough)
- Stress EV: EUR 9,880M
- Equity: EUR 11,338M / 271.6M = **EUR 41.7**

**DCF bear cross-check:** Growth 3%, WACC 10.0%, Terminal 2.0% = **EUR 40.18** (from tool output)

**DA Bear FV: EUR 40** (convergence of stress EV/EBIT EUR 42 and DCF bear EUR 40)
