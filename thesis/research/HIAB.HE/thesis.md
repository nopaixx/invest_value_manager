## Business Understanding

### Model of Business

**Problem solved:** Hiab enables efficient on-road loading and unloading of goods from trucks without requiring separate infrastructure (forklifts at warehouses, fixed cranes at construction sites). This is mission-critical for last-mile delivery, construction logistics, forestry, recycling, and general freight.

**Revenue model:**

| Revenue Type | Description | % of Revenue | Characteristics |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Equipment | New loader cranes, truck-mounted forklifts, forestry cranes, tail lifts, hooklifts | 71% (EUR 1,088M) | Cyclical, order-driven, 12.9% op margin |
| Services | Spare parts, maintenance, ProCare contracts, digital services, refurbished equipment | 29% (EUR 469M) | Recurring, growing, 23.2% op margin |

**Unit economics:**
- Equipment is a capital good with long lifecycle (10-15 years), creating a large installed base for aftermarket services
- ProCare contracts: 25,000+ contracts (+25% growth YoY), providing predictable recurring revenue
- 3,000 sales/service locations in 100+ countries -- extensive distribution moat
- Services margin (23.2%) is nearly 2x equipment margin (12.9%) -- mix shift toward services drives structural margin expansion
- R&D/Revenue: 2.4% -- moderate, focused on electrification (first all-electric lineup) and digital (HiVision, HiConnect)

**Capital intensity:** Asset-medium. Capex/Depreciation ~0.3x (2025), suggesting current capex is well below depreciation -- either under-investing or depreciating legacy Cargotec assets. Revenue per employee data not available but the global service network (3,000 locations) suggests moderate capital requirements.

### Competitive Landscape

| Hiab | #1 truck-mounted forklifts, #2 loader cranes | EUR 1.6B revenue. Broader product range + services |

### Moat Assessment

**Type:** Scale + Switching Costs + Installed Base

1. **Installed base moat (STRONG):** 100,000+ MOFFETT forklifts sold. Each unit creates 10-15 years of aftermarket revenue. The installed base grows monotonically -- every new unit sold adds to the Services TAM.

2. **Distribution network (STRONG):** 3,000 sales/service locations in 100+ countries. This is capital-intensive to replicate and creates proximity-based switching costs (customers use the nearest service point).

3. **Brand portfolio (MODERATE):** HIAB (loader cranes), MOFFETT (truck-mounted forklifts -- globally #1), LOGLIFT (forestry), MULTILIFT (hooklifts). Multiple specialized brands serving different niches.

4. **Switching costs (MODERATE):** Once a fleet standardizes on HIAB or MOFFETT equipment, switching involves retraining, new spare parts inventory, and new service contracts. ProCare contracts increase lock-in.

5. **Scale (MODERATE):** EUR 1.6B revenue with 6,000+ employees provides procurement and R&D scale vs smaller competitors. But Palfinger is similar size.

**Durability:** NARROW to MODERATE. This is not a wide moat -- the equipment is somewhat commoditized, and Chinese competitors are growing. But the Services layer (29% of revenue, growing) creates a recurring, higher-margin stream that is harder to compete with than equipment alone. The strategy of growing Services to 35-40% of revenue over time would widen the moat.

**The issue:** The EV/EBIT method at 11x gives EUR 39, which is BELOW current price of EUR 41.26. The DCF gives EUR 55-59. The divergence (34%) is significant and demands investigation.

**Why they diverge:**
1. The EV/EBIT uses current-year EBIT (EUR 208-210M) which is at trough revenue. If revenue recovers 5-10%, EBIT would rise to EUR 230-250M, making EV/EBIT FV much higher.
2. The DCF projects FUTURE growth, which captures the cyclical recovery. The EV/EBIT is a snapshot.
3. For a cyclical at trough, DCF is MORE appropriate because it captures the normalization. EV/EBIT at trough understates value.

**Revised approach:** For a cyclical at trough, weight DCF more heavily.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- **Spin-off data distortion is significant.** Revenue CAGR of -27.5% is meaningless -- it compares consolidated Cargotec (EUR 4.1B) with standalone Hiab (EUR 1.6B). The tool's QS of 66 may understate true quality by 5-8 points, but I cannot quantify precisely without clean standalone historical data going back 5+ years. I kept QS at 66 per tool-first rule.
- **Normalized FCF is uncertain.** The 3-year average of 19% FCF margin includes pre-spin-off Cargotec working capital dynamics. True standalone Hiab normalized FCF margin is probably 11-14%, but I have only 1 year of clean standalone data.
- **US tariff impact is genuinely uncertain.** Hiab manufactures in Finland, US, Ireland, Netherlands, and India. If Trump imposes tariffs on European goods, this directly hits ~50% of production capacity for US sales.
- **The EV/EBIT and DCF methods diverge significantly (34%).** I weighted DCF more for a cyclical at trough, which is methodologically sound, but it introduces sensitivity to growth assumptions.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The quality_scorer.py needs a flag for spin-off situations where revenue CAGR is distorted. Perhaps `--spin-off-year 2024` could adjust the lookback window.
- For Helsinki-listed stocks, short interest data is not available through standard EU regulatory databases (FCA/AMF). Consider adding Euronext/OMX disclosure tracking.

### Preguntas para Orchestrator
1. Should this go into the industrial-technology sector view or does load handling equipment warrant its own sector view? It overlaps with both industrials and industrial-technology but is distinct from both.
2. The EUR 48 FV is conservative due to S202 anti-bullish-bias methodology. The raw weighted average is EUR 50-52. Is the WATCHLIST at EUR 35-38 too demanding, or should I consider entry closer to current price given the spin-off catalyst?

### Anomalias Detectadas
- **Revenue data inconsistency:** yfinance shows EUR 4.1B revenue for 2022, which was consolidated Cargotec. This corrupts all growth metrics. The standalone Hiab 2025 revenue of EUR 1,556M is the first clean data point. 2024 standalone was EUR 1,647M (pro-forma), so organic decline was -5.5%.
- **ROE trajectory (1.6% -> 19.8% -> 104.0% -> 16.2%):** The 104% in 2024 is anomalous -- likely due to equity restructuring during the spin-off (low equity base temporarily). Not indicative of true profitability.
- **Capex/Depreciation of 0.3x:** Unusually low. Either Hiab is under-investing in maintenance capex, or depreciation is still running on legacy Cargotec assets that are now with Kalmar. This could mean: (a) FCF is overstated because maintenance capex is deferred, or (b) the asset base is genuinely light. Needs monitoring.
