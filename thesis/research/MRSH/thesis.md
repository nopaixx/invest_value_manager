## Business Understanding

### What Marsh Does

Marsh is the world's largest professional services firm in risk, strategy, and people. Four segments:

**1. Marsh Risk (53% of revenue, ~$14.4B)** -- Insurance Broking
- Places commercial/personal insurance on behalf of clients with carriers
- Earns commissions (% of premiums) or fees. Revenue is RECURRING: 90%+ annual retention
- Clients: corporations, governments, institutions in 130+ countries

**2. Guy Carpenter (9%, ~$2.5B)** -- Reinsurance Broking
- Places reinsurance for insurance companies with reinsurers
- Highly specialized, only 3-4 meaningful global competitors
- Complex catastrophe modeling creates high barriers

**3. Mercer (24%, ~$6.5B)** -- HR Consulting
- Health/benefits consulting, retirement, investment management ($350B+ AUM)
- Recurring advisory relationships with large corporate HR departments

**4. Marsh Management Consulting (fka Oliver Wyman, 14%, ~$3.6B)** -- Strategy Consulting
- Financial services, healthcare, energy sector focus
- Project-based (less recurring) but high margins. 8% organic growth Q4 2025

### Why This Model is Exceptional

**No underwriting risk.** Unlike insurers, Marsh NEVER takes balance sheet risk on claims. Pure intermediary earning commissions on premiums placed. When catastrophes hit, insurers suffer; brokers see INCREASED demand.

**Asset-light.** Primary assets are people and relationships. Capex <2% of revenue. >85% of operating income converts to FCF.

**Recurring revenue.** Insurance is mandatory for most businesses. ~85%+ of revenue is recurring or quasi-recurring. Revenue has grown every single year for 30+ years except 2008-09.

**Bidirectional pricing power.** Hard market = higher premiums = higher commissions. Soft market = more advisory demand = fee revenue holds. Brokers benefit in BOTH insurance cycle directions.

## Greensill Litigation: Detailed Risk Assessment

### Background
Greensill Capital collapsed in March 2021. Marsh had arranged trade credit insurance policies (via Tokio Marine/IAG) for Greensill-backed financial products. When Greensill collapsed, the insurance policies allegedly failed to respond, leaving investors (Credit Suisse funds, White Oak, others) with losses.

### Current Status (Mar 2026)

| Claim | Amount | Status | Notes |
|-------|--------|--------|-------|
| White Oak (London, US) | $143M | **SETTLED** (May 2025) | Confidential terms. Removed from risk |
| Australian Federal Court | A$7B total | **Trial Aug 2026** (5 months) | Multiple plaintiffs vs multiple defendants (Marsh, IAG, Tokio Marine) |
| Anti-anti-suit ruling | N/A | **Lost** (Oct 2025) | Marsh cannot block Australian proceedings from London |

### Key Risk Assessment

**The A$7B is NOT Marsh's sole liability.** This is the TOTAL damages sought across ALL defendants including Insurance Australia Group (IAG) and Tokio Marine. Marsh's share would depend on apportionment.

**Potential liability scenarios:**

| Scenario | Marsh Liability | Probability | Reasoning |
|----------|----------------|-------------|-----------|
| Full settlement pre-trial | $300M-$800M | 35% | Confidential like White Oak. Cheaper than 5-month trial |
| Partial adverse judgment | $500M-$1.5B | 30% | Court apportions liability among defendants |
| Full adverse judgment | $2B-$4B | 10% | Marsh found primarily at fault |
| Marsh prevails | $0 (+ legal costs ~$50M) | 25% | Claims dismissed or minimal liability |

**Expected Greensill cost = ~$650M** (probability-weighted)

**Impact on valuation:**
- $650M expected cost / 484.5M shares = ~$1.34/share
- Vs FV $195: reduces to $193.66 -- immaterial at expected value
- Worst case ($4B): $8.26/share reduction. Still survivable with $5B annual FCF.

**What I don't know:**
- Whether Marsh has reserved for this (10-K language is typically "cannot estimate" for pending litigation)
- Settlement discussions underway (White Oak settled -- pattern could repeat)
- Internal documents surfaced showing Marsh executives called it "frightening absence of corporate governance" and "fraud" -- this is BAD for Marsh's defense

### Greensill Sizing Constraint (from R3 resolution)
**Original R3 rule:** Half position at initial entry, full position AFTER Greensill resolution (Aug 2026+). This remains prudent. The tail risk is real even if expected value impact is modest.
