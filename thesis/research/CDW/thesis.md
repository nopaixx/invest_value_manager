## Business Understanding

### What CDW Does

CDW is the largest IT solutions provider and value-added reseller (VAR) in the United States. The company serves as a critical intermediary between 1,000+ technology vendors (Dell, Cisco, Microsoft, HP, Lenovo, etc.) and 250,000+ customers ranging from small businesses to enterprises, government agencies, healthcare systems, and educational institutions.

**Problem it solves:** Most organizations (especially SMBs, schools, hospitals) lack the internal IT expertise to evaluate, procure, integrate, and manage technology solutions. CDW provides a single point of contact for the full IT stack -- hardware, software, cloud, security, networking -- with 5,400+ technical specialists who help customers architect and deploy solutions.

**Revenue model:** Product resale (~78% of revenue) + services/cloud (~22%, growing). Revenue is largely transactional but highly recurring in practice -- CDW's customer retention rate is extremely high because switching costs include losing the relationship with technical advisors who understand the customer's IT environment.

### Unit Economics

- **CAC:** Very low. CDW's sales force is inbound-focused; customers come to CDW because vendors refer them and because CDW's scale provides breadth/availability competitors cannot match. The long tail of 250K customers is difficult for large tech vendors to reach directly, making CDW an essential channel partner.
- **LTV:** High. Multi-year relationships, expanding wallet share. A typical mid-market customer starts with hardware, then adds software, then cloud, then security, then managed services.
- **LTV/CAC:** Very favorable (estimated >10x) due to low acquisition cost and high retention.
- **Capital intensity:** Asset-light. Capex/revenue ~1%. The business is fundamentally logistics + expertise.

### Why It's Cheap

**Narrative the market believes:**
1. **IT spending downcycle hangover:** Revenue declined from $23.7B (2022) to $21.0B (2024) -- two consecutive years of decline. Market fears this is structural, not cyclical.
2. **AI disintermediation risk:** If enterprises buy directly from cloud hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) or AI vendors, CDW's intermediary role could erode.
3. **Low margins = commodity business:** 21.7% gross margin looks thin vs "tech" peers at 55%+.
4. **Leverage concern:** Net debt/EBITDA 2.8x is moderate but not conservative.
5. **Macro uncertainty:** Tariffs, oil shock, and geopolitical risks threaten IT budgets.

**My contra-thesis:**

1. **Cyclical, not structural.** FY2025 revenue grew +6.8%, confirming the recovery. Small Business (+13.3%) and Healthcare (+13.3%) are inflecting strongly. Gartner forecasts global IT spending at $6.15T in 2026 (+10.8%). CDW guides 200-300bps outperformance.

2. **AI is a tailwind, not a threat.** CDW is not being disintermediated -- it is ENABLING AI adoption. Most of CDW's 250K customers are not hyperscaler-native; they need help evaluating AI solutions, integrating them with existing infrastructure, and managing security/compliance. CDW reported AI is "pervasive" across product and services revenue. AI increases complexity for customers, which increases CDW's value as an advisor. The Q4 2025 earnings call highlighted production-stage AI deployments across customer sizes.

3. **Margins are best-in-class for the business model.** CDW's EBIT margin of 7.4% is approximately 2x the peer average (~3-4% for SHI, Insight). This is the HIGHEST margin VAR in the industry, reflecting scale advantages, vendor rebate leverage, and services mix. Comparing to software company margins is a category error.

4. **Leverage is manageable.** Net debt/EBITDA 2.8x with interest coverage 7.3x. CDW generates >$1B FCF annually, providing ample capacity to service debt. The company has reduced total debt from peak levels and maintained investment-grade credit.

5. **Tariff impact is limited.** CDW does not manufacture. Tariffs on hardware (if imposed) would be passed through to customers. Software/cloud (growing share) has zero tariff exposure.
