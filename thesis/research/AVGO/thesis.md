## Business Understanding

### What Broadcom Does

Broadcom is a semiconductor and infrastructure software conglomerate operating in two segments:

**1. Semiconductor Solutions (~65% of revenue, ~$12.5B Q1 FY26)**
- **AI Custom ASICs (XPUs):** Designs custom AI accelerators for hyperscalers. Google TPUs (largest customer), Meta MTIA, OpenAI accelerators. Revenue: $8.4B Q1 FY26 (+106% YoY).
- **AI Networking:** Ethernet switching silicon (Tomahawk 5, Jericho3-AI), optical transceivers, PCIe switches for AI cluster connectivity.
- **Legacy/Non-AI:** Broadband (cable modems, PON), wireless (Apple WiFi/Bluetooth), server storage. ~$4.1B Q1 FY26, roughly flat YoY.

**2. Infrastructure Software (~35% of revenue, ~$6.8B Q1 FY26)**
- **VMware Cloud Foundation (VCF):** Enterprise virtualization platform, transitioning to subscription model. ARR growing 19%.
- **CA Technologies + Symantec enterprise:** Mainframe, security, DevOps tools. Stable, high-margin cash cows.
- Software gross margin: 93%. Software operating margin: 78%.

### Unit Economics
- **Revenue model:** Mix of product sales (semis) + recurring subscription (software). Software ARR $9.2B and growing 19%.
- **Gross margin:** 67.8% blended (93% software, ~60% semis).
- **FCF margin:** 42.1% (FY2025), best-in-class for this scale.
- **Capital intensity:** Low-moderate. Fabless semiconductor model (TSMC manufactures chips). Capex/Rev ~5%.
- **Switching costs:** Extremely high. Custom ASIC designs require 2-3 year co-development cycles. VMware is deeply embedded in enterprise infrastructure.

### Why the Market Loves It (And Why It's Expensive)
The market is pricing Broadcom as the picks-and-shovels winner of AI. With:
- AI revenue growing 106% YoY (Q1) and guided 140% YoY (Q2)
- $73B AI backlog over 18 months
- CEO line of sight to $100B AI chip revenue by 2027
- VMware integration boosting margins
- $10B buyback

The narrative is: "Broadcom is the next Nvidia-tier platform company."

### Por Que Podria Estar Cara (The Bear Case)
1. **At 62x P/E and 59x EV/EBIT, the market prices 35% annual FCF growth for 5 years.** Historical FCF CAGR was 18.2%. Even with AI acceleration, sustaining 35% for 5 years is extremely aggressive.
2. **AI capex cyclicality risk.** Hyperscaler capex ($660-690B 2026E) could peak if AI ROI disappoints. If capex growth slows from +80% to +10%, Broadcom's AI revenue growth collapses.
3. **Custom ASIC customer concentration.** Google alone is estimated at ~58% of ASIC shipments. Loss of any one hyperscaler contract would be devastating.
4. **SBC at 11.8% of revenue and rising** (was 4.6% in 2022). $25B unrecognized SBC cost. This is real dilution partially masked by buybacks.
5. **Non-AI business is stagnant.** Legacy semis flat YoY. If AI slows, there's no growth offset.
6. **Receivables growing 61.8% vs revenue 23.9%.** This divergence warrants monitoring -- could indicate channel stuffing or extended payment terms to large customers.
7. **Tariff risk:** 30% tariff threat on semiconductor equipment/chips could slow customer capex decisions.

### Contra-Tesis (Why Bears Could Be Wrong)
- AI capex is NOT discretionary anymore -- it's competitive survival for hyperscalers. Google/Meta/Microsoft MUST invest or lose to competitors. This is more structural than cyclical.
- Custom ASICs are CHEAPER than Nvidia GPUs for inference workloads. As AI shifts from training to inference, Broadcom's TAM expands.
- VMware provides a floor: $30B+ in stable, 93% gross margin software revenue regardless of AI cycle.
- Hock Tan is arguably the best capital allocator in tech. Track record of value creation through disciplined M&A spanning 2 decades.

### Value Trap Checklist: 0/10
Not applicable -- this is not a value trap situation. The concern is overpayment for quality, not quality deterioration.


- $28.5B OE / 12% = $237B market cap = $50/share
- This shows the valuation is heavily dependent on SUSTAINED growth.
