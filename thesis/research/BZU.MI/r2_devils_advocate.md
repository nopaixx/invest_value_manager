# Counter-Analysis: BZU.MI (Buzzi SpA)

## Fecha: 2026-03-06

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis is fundamentally sound in its assessment of Buzzi as a high-quality cyclical business with exceptional family alignment and best-in-class margins. However, the R1's verdict of WATCHLIST at EUR 49.54 was correct -- and the stock has since fallen 12% to EUR 43.62, vindicating the cyclical peak concern. The through-cycle EBIT normalization of EUR 850-900M is the thesis's most critical and debatable assumption. FY2025 data shows US revenue declined 7%, margins compressing, and cement demand in the US declining for a second consecutive year. The thesis survives the counter-analysis, but with important adjustments to fair value and risk assessment.

---

## Calibration Anchor

**Before reading thesis:**
- Market at EUR 43.62 implies 1.2% FCF growth. Historical FCF CAGR: 8.7%. Gap: -7.4pp.
- DA historical median correction: -13.0%. Average correction: -15.7%. All 25 prior DAs reduced FV (never increased).
- Current EV/EBIT: 6.3x (market price). FA thesis: 10.5x fair multiple.
- The market is skeptical. The FA must PROVE the market is pricing in too little growth, not assume it.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. Through-Cycle EBIT of EUR 850-900M

- **FA assumption:** Structural margin improvements justify normalized EBIT of EUR 850-900M (~20% operating margin), above the 2021-2024 average of EUR 783M.
- **Evidence against:**
  - FY2025 preliminary EBITDA: ~EUR 1,230M (down from EUR 1,276M in 2024), with **US revenue declining 7%** (EUR 1,606M vs EUR 1,727M). The "crown jewel" is weakening.
  - H1 2025 EBITDA fell 16.1% (EUR 235.1M vs EUR 280.2M) in the first reporting period, driven by weak demand AND rising production costs.
  - US cement consumption declined >5% in 2024, AND another decline in 2025. That's TWO consecutive years of volume decline -- not a one-off.
  - Cement prices in the US, which had remained stable, **declined slightly in the final months of 2025**. This is the first evidence of pricing power erosion.
  - If the "structural improvement" is really +3-4pp margin, and trough was 15% (2021-2022), the structural normalized margin is 18-19%, not 20%. On EUR 4,500M revenue, that yields EBIT of EUR 810-855M -- the LOW END of the FA's range.
  - Germany at ~15% EBIT margin weighs on the consolidated average. German construction has been in deep recession since 2022.
- **Severidad:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution suggested:** Use EUR 800-850M as through-cycle EBIT (vs FA's EUR 850-900M). The 2025 data showing simultaneous US volume decline AND price softening undermines the structural improvement thesis. The FA assumed "60% structural, 40% cyclical" for the margin expansion -- I think 50/50 is more realistic given the 2025 evidence.

### 2. The Italian Discount is Excessive (vs Partially Justified)

- **FA assumption:** Italian discount should be ~10-15%, not ~40% vs US peers. Kerrisdale calls it "prejudice."
- **Evidence against:**
  - The discount is NOT just about Italy. CRH relisted on NYSE specifically because the US listing alone added 20-30% to its valuation. Holcim spun off its North America business (Amrize) in June 2025, which listed at ~$30B on NYSE. These structural moves confirm that the "Italian/European listing discount" is RATIONAL -- it reflects liquidity, index inclusion, analyst coverage, and institutional access.
  - Buzzi has NO English-language investor days, NO bold capital markets campaigns. This is not "prejudice" -- this is a communication deficit that reduces the potential investor base by perhaps 70%.
  - Buzzi's board has ONLY 1 independent minority shareholder representative (Marcella Logli). With 53% family control and Italian governance norms, minority shareholders have limited voice.
  - The family has NEVER considered a US listing or dual listing. If the discount were truly "undeserved," rational management with EUR 1B+ net cash would pursue a listing change. Their inaction suggests either (a) they don't care about the share price (bad for minorities) or (b) they believe the discount is structural (confirming it's deserved).
  - Italy ranks below UK, Switzerland, Germany in corporate governance indices for minority protection.
- **Severidad:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution suggested:** The FA's 10.5x multiple already includes a -1.5x Italian discount. This seems approximately right. But the committee should note that this discount is unlikely to close without a structural catalyst (US listing, dual listing, spin-off) that the family has shown no interest in pursuing.

### 3. US Infrastructure Spending Will Drive Cement Demand

- **FA assumption:** IIJA spending ramping, AI data center demand, reshoring = positive US tailwind for cement volumes.
- **Evidence against:**
  - **US cement consumption declined >5% in 2024 AND declined again in 2025.** Two consecutive years of decline despite IIJA being in effect since 2021. Only 40% of IIJA money has been spent as of September 2025.
  - Inflation has eroded the PURCHASING POWER of IIJA spending. The dollar amount is large, but cement volumes haven't followed.
  - The US Supreme Court struck down most Trump tariffs in 2026, meaning the temporary cement import restriction (which would have helped domestic producers like Buzzi) is largely removed.
  - US cement market outlook for 2026 is "shaky" per Argus Media. "Mild recovery" expected, not a boom.
  - AI data center cement demand: ~1 million metric tons by 2028. Buzzi's US capacity is ~10Mt/year. AI adds ~2-3% to national demand at best -- not a game-changer.
  - Housing starts remain weak. Residential construction (the largest single consumer of cement) is in a multi-year soft patch.
  - Buzzi's US revenue fell 7% in FY2025. This is not "infrastructure tailwinds."
- **Severidad:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution suggested:** The infrastructure narrative is overhyped for cement in the short-to-medium term. IIJA spending is being diluted by inflation. The FA should downgrade the US infrastructure catalyst from 70% probability / +5-10% EPS impact to 50% probability / +2-5% EPS impact. The AI data center catalyst should be downgraded from 50% probability to "marginal" (it's real but immaterial at 2-3% of US demand).

### 4. Kerrisdale Capital EUR 85 Target is a Credible Bull Case

- **FA assumption:** Kerrisdale thesis is "well-researched" and used as bull case reference at EUR 85.
- **Evidence against:**
  - **Kerrisdale's public track record on LONG positions is poor.** According to TipRanks data, copying Kerrisdale's trades since 2011 and holding for one year would produce a NEGATIVE average return of -28.5%, with only 39.13% of transactions generating a profit. Their expertise is in SHORT selling, not long investing.
  - Kerrisdale's EUR 85 target uses **peak 2024 EBITDA** (EUR 664M US EBITDA) and applies US peer multiples (12x EBITDA). This is double-counting the best assumptions: peak earnings x peak multiples x no discount.
  - Since Kerrisdale published, the stock has fallen from ~EUR 49 to EUR 43.62 -- down ~11%. The market has not responded to the thesis.
  - Kerrisdale is primarily known as an activist short seller (C3.ai, etc.). Their long thesis track record is not their core competency.
  - The EUR 85 target implies 14-15x EV/EBITDA on peak earnings. No Italian industrial has EVER traded at this multiple sustainably.
- **Severidad:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution suggested:** Remove Kerrisdale from any weight in fair value calculation. The FA already gives 0% weight, which is correct. But the committee should note that citing Kerrisdale as a credibility indicator for the thesis is misleading given their poor long-side track record.

### 5. Capital Allocation: "Why Aren't They Doing Buybacks?"

- **FA assumption:** The thesis OMITS any discussion of buybacks, implying capital allocation is limited to dividends.
- **Evidence FOR Buzzi (thesis gap, not challenge):**
  - **Buzzi IS doing buybacks.** They executed EUR 148.9M in share repurchases in 2024 (4.1M shares = 2.1% of capital). They authorized a NEW EUR 200M buyback plan starting February 2026 for up to 20% of share capital.
  - Total shareholder returns in 2024: EUR 111M dividends + EUR 147M buybacks = EUR 258M, or ~3.3% of current market cap.
  - This is a POSITIVE that the R1 thesis missed. It strengthens the capital allocation score.
- **Severidad:** **LOW** (this is a thesis omission, not a challenge -- it's actually POSITIVE)
- **Resolution suggested:** The committee should update the thesis to include buyback data. This modestly improves the investment case.

## Independent Bear-Case Valuation

### Method: EV/EBITDA on Through-Trough Normalized Earnings

I use a DIFFERENT method than the FA's primary (EV/EBIT). I use EV/EBITDA with BEAR assumptions.

**Bear assumptions:**
- Through-cycle EBITDA: EUR 1,050M (below FA's EUR 1,150M, reflecting 2021-2022 average ~EUR 840M + structural improvement of +EUR 200M, weighted more toward historical norms)
- Multiple: 7.5x EV/EBITDA (below FA's implied ~9.0x, reflecting Italian listing discount, cyclical trough sentiment, and comparable Heidelberg/CEMEX territory)
- Terminal growth: 2.0% (cement = GDP-level at best)
- Net cash: EUR 1,000M (conservative, between 2024 EUR 755M and 2025 EUR 1,131M)

**Calculation:**
- EV: EUR 1,050M x 7.5x = EUR 7,875M
- Equity: EUR 7,875M + EUR 1,000M = EUR 8,875M
- Per share: EUR 8,875M / 181.5M shares = **EUR 48.90**

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- The through-cycle EBIT normalization is inherently uncertain with only 4 years of yfinance data. Pre-2021 data would help enormously -- the 2015-2019 period would show margins before COVID and energy crisis distortions. Without this data, the "structural improvement" thesis remains partially unverifiable.
- The convertible bond (EUR 150M, May 2025) terms are unknown to me -- the conversion price and dilution impact could be material but I couldn't find the specific terms.
- The FY2025 preliminary results show recurring EBITDA ~EUR 1,230M but no EBIT or EPS breakdown. The full annual results (expected March 2026) would provide critical data on margin trajectory.
- US cement pricing softening in late 2025 could be seasonal or could be the start of a cyclical price decline. Insufficient data to distinguish.

### Limitaciones de Este Analisis
- I could not access the Argus Media article on US cement 2026 outlook (paywall/timeout). This would have provided the most granular industry data.
- European cement demand data for Italy and Germany in 2025 was limited in my research.
- I could not find specific terms of the May 2025 convertible bond issue (EUR 150M).
- Buzzi's annual report 2024 (PDF) was available but not parsed in detail for IFRS accounting treatment of margins.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The R1 thesis missed the EUR 148.9M buyback in 2024 and the EUR 200M buyback authorization. Future R1s for capital-intensive industrials should explicitly search for buyback programs as part of capital allocation analysis.
- The quality_scorer.py gross margin premium (+40pp) for Buzzi is likely an accounting artifact (cost classification differences in cement). A flag or note for materials/industrials companies where GM comparison is unreliable would be valuable.
