## Conflict Resolution

### CONFLICT 1: Through-Cycle EBIT — EUR 850-900M (R1) vs EUR 800-850M (R2)

**R1 position:** EUR 850-900M (~20% operating margin on EUR 4,500M), structural margin improvement is 60% structural / 40% cyclical. The +8pp EBITDA margin expansion (22% to 30%) includes ~+4-5pp permanent improvement from plant efficiency, pricing discipline, and geographic optimization.

**R2 counter:** EUR 800-850M. FY2025 US revenue -7%. H1 2025 EBITDA -16%. US cement demand declining 2 consecutive years. Prices softened in late 2025. Structural split should be 50/50, not 60/40.

**Resolution: EUR 825-875M (midpoint EUR 850M).**

Reasoning:
1. The R2 evidence on US demand decline is compelling. Two consecutive years of volume decline AND price softening in late 2025 is the first sign of pricing power erosion. R1 assumed US volumes would grow; they are contracting. This is a material miss.
2. However, the R2 overweights H1 2025 data. The H1 2025 EBITDA decline of -16% includes one-time effects (weather in Texas, timing of IIJA disbursements). Full-year FY2025 EBITDA of EUR 1,230M is only -3.6% from EUR 1,276M -- margin compression is real but modest, not a collapse.
3. The structural vs cyclical split: I split the difference at 55/45 structural/cyclical. The efficiency gains and pricing discipline ARE real (Buzzi's US margins are BEST in industry, not average). But 2025 proves the cyclical component is larger than R1 assumed. The normalized EBITDA margin is 25-27% (not 26-28% per R1, not 23-25% per full R2 bear).
4. On EUR 4,400-4,600M normalized revenue, 25-27% EBITDA margin gives EUR 1,100-1,240M EBITDA. Subtracting ~EUR 250-280M D&A gives EBIT of EUR 820-960M. Through-cycle midpoint: EUR 850M.
5. This is the LOW END of R1 and the HIGH END of R2. I believe this is honest -- I am NOT splitting the difference mechanically. The FY2025 full-year data showing only -3.6% EBITDA decline validates that margins are compressing gradually, not crashing.

**Impact on FV:** Reduces through-cycle EBIT from EUR 875M (R1 midpoint) to EUR 850M. At 10.5x multiple: EV drops from EUR 9,188M to EUR 8,925M. Per-share impact: approximately EUR -1.50.

### CONFLICT 2: US Cement Demand — Growth Assumed (R1) vs 2-Year Decline (R2)

**R1 position:** IIJA spending ramping (70% probability, +5-10% EPS impact), AI data center demand (50% probability, +3-5% volumes), reshoring = positive US tailwind.

**R2 counter:** US cement consumption -5% in 2024 AND declined again in 2025 despite IIJA being in effect since 2021. Only 40% of IIJA money spent by Sep 2025. Inflation eroded purchasing power. AI data center demand is ~1Mt by 2028 (~2-3% of national demand = immaterial). Housing starts weak.

**Resolution: R2 is largely correct. Downgrade US growth assumptions.**

1. IIJA catalyst: Downgrade from 70%/+5-10% to 50%/+2-5% EPS impact. R2 is right that despite IIJA being law since 2021, cement volumes have DECLINED. The spending is being diluted by construction cost inflation. The catalyst is real but slower and weaker than R1 assumed.
2. AI data center demand: Downgrade from "catalyst" to "marginal positive." 1Mt by 2028 against a 100Mt+ US market is noise, not signal. Remove from catalysts table.
3. US volumes: Model as FLAT over the cycle (0% volume growth), not +0-2% as R1 assumed. Price gains (+3-5% annual) remain the primary US revenue driver.
4. Net effect: US organic revenue growth of +2-4% (price-led), not +3-7% (price + volume) as implicitly assumed in R1.

**Impact on FV:** Already captured in EBIT adjustment (Conflict 1). No additional FV haircut needed here, but reduces confidence in bull case. Bull EBIT trimmed from EUR 1,050M to EUR 1,000M.

### CONFLICT 3: Structural Margin Improvement — 60/40 (R1) vs 50/50 (R2)

**R1 position:** 60% structural (plant efficiency, geographic optimization, pricing discipline), 40% cyclical (energy cost gap, construction demand).

**R2 counter:** 50/50. FY2025 shows simultaneous US volume decline AND price softening, undermining the "structural pricing discipline" thesis.

**Resolution: 55/45 structural/cyclical.**

1. Buzzi's US EBITDA margin of 38.4% is THE BEST in the US cement industry. This is not an accident of the cycle -- it reflects genuine operational excellence (plant locations, logistics network, Texas RMX integration). Some of this premium is structural.
2. However, R2 is correct that the late-2025 price softening is the first evidence that pricing power has limits. In a true cyclical downturn, even best-in-class operators see margin compression.
3. The German operations at ~15% EBIT margin weigh on the consolidated figure and are in a multi-year construction recession. This drags the structural component lower than US alone.
4. Net: normalized EBITDA margin of 25-27% (vs pre-2023 average of 22-23% and peak of 30%). This embeds ~+3pp structural improvement, which on a +8pp total expansion = approximately 37% structural. I round up to 45% cyclical / 55% structural because Buzzi's US cost advantage IS durable, even if the overall margin is not.

**Impact on FV:** Already captured in EBIT normalization (Conflict 1).

### CONFLICT 4: Kerrisdale EUR 85 Target Credibility

**R1 position:** "Well-researched" thesis, used as bull case reference at 0% weight.

**R2 counter:** Kerrisdale long track record is poor (39% win rate, -28.5% average return per TipRanks). Uses peak EBITDA x peak multiples. Stock down 11% since thesis published. EUR 85 implies 14-15x EV/EBITDA on peak earnings -- no Italian industrial has traded at this sustainably.

**Resolution: R2 is correct. Remove Kerrisdale as a credibility anchor.**

1. Kerrisdale is a SHORT specialist. Their long thesis track record is weak. Citing them as validation of the bull case is misleading.
2. The SOTP logic (value US operations alone at EUR 8B using US peer multiples of 12x EBITDA on peak EUR 664M) is intellectually interesting but practically irrelevant -- Buzzi is an Italian-listed company with no plans for US listing or spin-off.
3. Action: Remove any reference to Kerrisdale as supporting evidence. Their EUR 85 target should be treated as an outlier, not a credible bull case.
4. Adjusted bull case: EUR 70 (down from EUR 76 in R1), using EUR 1,000M EBIT at 11.5x (modest re-rating, not Kerrisdale's 14x).

**Impact on FV:** Reduces expected value via lower bull case. Bull FV EUR 70 (vs EUR 76 R1).

### CONFLICT 5: Buyback Program — R1 OMISSION (Positive)

**R2 finding:** R1 missed that Buzzi executed EUR 148.9M in buybacks in 2024 (4.1M shares, 2.1% of capital) and authorized EUR 200M new buyback starting Feb 2026 (up to 20% of share capital).

**Resolution: Incorporate as positive. Modestly improves capital allocation assessment and provides price floor.**

1. Total shareholder returns 2024: EUR 111M dividends + EUR 147M buybacks = EUR 258M (~3.3% of market cap at the time). This is excellent capital allocation for a net-cash company.
2. The EUR 200M buyback authorization for 2026 (Feb-Aug 2026) means Buzzi management is ACTIVELY buying shares in the EUR 40-50 range. This provides a price floor and signals management conviction.
3. At current EUR 43.62 price and EUR 200M authorization: Buzzi could retire ~4.6M shares (~2.5% of outstanding) in 2026 alone. Combined with ~1.4% dividend yield, total shareholder return is ~3.9%.
4. This strengthens the E[CAGR] calculation: sustainable growth component should include buyback-driven EPS accretion of ~2%/year.

**Impact on FV:** Adds ~EUR 1 to FV via higher sustainable shareholder yield. Also increases dividend yield component of E[CAGR] from 1.4% to ~3.5% (dividends + buybacks as combined yield).

### CONFLICT 6: EUR 150M Convertible Bond Dilution — R1 OMISSION (Negative)

**R2 finding:** EUR 150M convertible bond issued May 2025, terms not fully known. Potential equity dilution.

**Resolution: Acknowledge but assess as LOW severity.**

1. EUR 150M convertible on a company with EUR 8B+ market cap = ~1.8% potential dilution. This is immaterial.
2. The convertible was issued WHILE the company had net cash of EUR 755M+. This suggests it was done for financial optimization (low coupon), not out of desperation.
3. If converted at a typical 30-40% premium to issue-date price (~EUR 50 at time), conversion would occur at ~EUR 65-70 -- well above current price and above my FV estimate. Conversion at these levels would be value-neutral.
4. Net: dilution risk is REAL but IMMATERIAL at ~1.8% of equity. No FV adjustment warranted. Monitor terms if they become public.

**Impact on FV:** None. Immaterial dilution.

Arguments AGAINST market buy at EUR 43.62:

US volumes declining, prices softening, H1 2025 margins compressing. The thesis is to buy Buzzi when the cycle TURNS, not while it is still deteriorating. EUR 42 gives us the margin of safety to absorb another quarter of bad data.

If FY2025 full results (expected March 2026) show stabilization in US volumes or pricing, AND the stock is still in the EUR 42-44 range, that would be a stronger buy signal.
