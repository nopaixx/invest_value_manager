# Counter-Analysis: BCG.L (Baltic Classifieds Group PLC)

## Date: 2026-03-21

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis presents a compelling "small cap monopoly being ignored" narrative with 44% MoS. However, the thesis has several material vulnerabilities: (1) the QS adjustment of +12 points is the largest single adjustment in our portfolio history and warrants skepticism, (2) the FV of 260 GBp relies on forward growth re-acceleration that is management guidance, not demonstrated fact, (3) the TAM ceiling for a 6-7M person market creates structural growth limits that the thesis underestimates, and (4) the broader classifieds sector is experiencing a structural de-rating driven by AI disruption fears that may not be temporary. The thesis survives basic scrutiny -- this IS a good business trading cheaply -- but the FV is too aggressive and needs to be anchored closer to the conservative methods.

## Phase 0.5: Calibration + Market Anchor

**Reverse DCF:** Market at 180 GBp implies 0.3% FCF growth. Historical 3Y FCF CAGR: 40.9%. Revenue CAGR 3Y: 17.6%. The gap is enormous (17.2pp on revenue, 40.5pp on FCF).

**DA Accuracy Stats:** Historical average DA correction: -15.7%. Median: -13.0%. DA has NEVER increased a FV across 25 corrections. My corrections have been systematically directionally correct (all FVs were too high). No outcomes measured yet (first review Aug 2026).

**ANCHOR:** The market prices this business at 15.3x EV/EBIT and 20x P/E. For a monopoly classifieds business with 78% EBITDA margins, this IS cheap if growth sustains. But the market has seen the growth decelerate from 15% to 7% and is not convinced by management's re-acceleration guidance. The FA must prove the market is wrong on growth, not assume it.

## Key Assumptions Challenged

### 1. QS Adjustment: +12 Points (71 -> 83, Tier B -> Tier A)

- **FA's assumption:** Tool scored 0/8 on market position, 2/7 on ROIC persistence, and 0/5 on GM trend. Manual adjustments of +8, +1, and +3 respectively are justified.
- **Evidence in counter:**
  - **The +12 adjustment is the LARGEST QS adjustment in our portfolio history.** Previous large adjustments: ERIE +20 (DA reduced to +12 as STRONG COUNTER), and most other adjustments are +3 to +5. A +12 adjustment that jumps an entire Tier (B->A) demands extraordinary evidence.
  - **Market Position +8/8 (from 0):** The market position data IS strong (6-36x leads), and I concede this adjustment has the most evidence. However, the tool scored 0 because it cannot verify the claim -- meaning we're relying on company-reported data and thesis narrative. The claim that "Autoplius.lt is 6x larger than nearest competitor" comes from BCG's own presentations. Independent verification is limited.
  - **ROIC Persistence +1 (from 2 to 3):** Only 4 years of public data. ROIC went 1.3% -> 6.7% -> 9.8% -> 13.3%. This trajectory is impressive but (a) it started from near-zero because Apax had loaded the company with acquisition costs and (b) the ROIC is now barely above WACC (13.3% vs 11% = +2.3pp). Claiming "persistence" from 4 data points starting at 1.3% is weak. I would keep this at +0.
  - **GM Trend +3 (from 0 to 3):** The operating margin expansion from 27% to 65% in 4 years is real but largely reflects POST-IPO cost stripping, not organic business improvement. Management now guides EBITDA margin compression to "mid-70s." If margins are about to compress, scoring "stable" for GM trend is generous.
- **My adjusted QS:** 71 + 8 (market position, conceded) + 0 (ROIC persistence) + 1 (GM data gaps, not expansion) = **80/100 (still Tier A, but barely)**. The thesis conclusion doesn't change at 80 vs 83, but the sizing and MoS implications do.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Accept +8 for market position, reduce ROIC persistence to +0, reduce GM trend to +1. QS 80 Tier A.

### 2. Growth Re-Acceleration to "Double Digits" by FY2027

- **FA's assumption:** H1 FY2026 7% growth is temporary (Estonian auto tax), management guides re-acceleration to 12-15% by FY2027.
- **Evidence in counter:**
  - **Management guidance is NOT evidence.** The thesis uses management's own guidance ("double digit re-acceleration") as the PRIMARY basis for the 12-15% growth assumption. This is a Level 2/3 source at best. Management has incentive to be optimistic.
  - **September 2025 guidance CUT:** Management itself cut FY2026 guidance by 3-4pp just 3 months before H1 results. If management already missed once, why should we trust the FY2027 re-acceleration guidance?
  - **Auto segment structural headwind:** Estonia's auto tax is NOT a one-time shock -- it's a PERMANENT new tax regime that entered force Jan 2025. The annual tax and registration fee are ongoing. While transaction volumes may partially recover, they will settle at a permanently lower level than pre-tax. The thesis claims "Car transactions don't disappear -- they repriced" but the data shows transactions HALVED. Some of those transactions moved to private/informal channels that bypass the platform.
  - **ARPU growth is the bull case lever, but has limits:** Real estate ARPU grew 25% in H1 FY2026 -- impressive. But in a market of 7M people with already high platform penetration, ARPU cannot grow 20%+ annually for long. At some point you hit dealer budget constraints. Western European classifieds (the comp set) took 15+ years to reach their ARPU levels and had 10-30x the addressable market.
  - **The growth deceleration pattern:** FY2023 18%, FY2024 19%, FY2025 15%, H1 FY2026 7%. This is a consistent deceleration curve, not a one-time dip. Even stripping out the Estonian auto impact, the trajectory was decelerating BEFORE the tax.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** Use 8-10% as base-case growth (not 12-15%). The thesis mid-term assumption should be lowered.

### 3. Fair Value of 260 GBp Relies on Forward FCF at 18-20x Multiple

- **FA's assumption:** FV = 260 GBp based on FY2028 FCF (2 years forward) at 18-20x multiple.
- **Evidence in counter:**
  - **The FA's OWN conservative methods produce 186-205 GBp.** The reconciliation table gives a weighted average of 205 GBp. The thesis then overrides this with "BUT -- considering the growth re-acceleration thesis... I believe the true intrinsic value is higher, likely 220-260 GBp." This is the FA talking themselves UP from their own analysis -- classic bullish bias.
  - **260 GBp requires BOTH growth re-acceleration AND multiple expansion.** At current 15x EV/EBIT, you need the market to re-rate to 18-20x AND growth to re-accelerate. Two things have to go right simultaneously.
  - **Sector-wide de-rating in classifieds:** Rightmove -22%, REA Group -13%, Scout24 -15% since Oct 2025. This is NOT BCG-specific -- the entire classifieds sector is being de-rated due to AI disruption fears. JP Morgan's global classifieds report positions the sector with lower multiples going forward. The thesis dismisses AI threat as "LOW" but the market is pricing it as material across ALL classifieds companies.
  - **Small cap + Baltic geography discount is STRUCTURAL, not temporary.** The thesis applies only -3x from peer median for "small cap, Baltic geography, growth deceleration." In reality: (a) GBP 828M market cap has material liquidity risk, (b) Baltic states are EU periphery with minimal analyst coverage (11 analysts, but most are regional specialists), (c) the stock has NO US listing/ADR, limiting the buyer base, (d) a company this size could be delisted or taken private at any time. These factors deserve -5x or more from peer median, not -3x.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** FV should be anchored to the weighted average of 205 GBp, not the aspirational 260 GBp.

### 4. TAM Ceiling: 6-7M People Is a Hard Constraint

- **FA's assumption:** "Small market = moat, not limitation." BCG captures increasing share at increasing prices.
- **Evidence in counter:**
  - **Total Baltic online classifieds market is approximately EUR 100-120M.** BCG captures ~EUR 83M (FY2025) = already ~70% of the total market. There is a ceiling.
  - **7M people is the population of a mid-sized European city.** For comparison: AUTO.L serves 67M people (UK), RMV.L serves 67M, Scout24 serves 83M (Germany). BCG serves 10x fewer people. The ARPU runway is shorter because the total dealer/agent/employer base is smaller.
  - **Baltic population is DECLINING.** Lithuania lost 1.5M people (28%) since 1990 due to emigration. Latvia lost 27%. Estonia's population is flat. The addressable market is slowly shrinking, not growing.
  - **GDP per capita is lower:** Baltic GDP per capita (EUR 22-28K) is 40-60% of Western European levels. This limits ARPU expansion vs. Western comparables because businesses simply have smaller budgets.
  - **The thesis mentions ARPU "still well below Western European classifieds platforms" as upside.** But Baltic businesses have LOWER revenue per transaction, so comparable ARPU levels may never be achievable. A Lithuanian real estate agent earning EUR 3K commission cannot pay the same platform fee as a UK agent earning EUR 15K commission.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Growth beyond 10% requires ARPU expansion that has natural limits in a low-GDP, declining-population market. Long-term growth should be modeled at 6-8%, not 8-10%.

### 5. GBP/EUR FX Mismatch Is Unhedged Risk

- **FA's assumption:** "GBP weakness = higher GBp price" -- presented as neutral/positive.
- **Evidence in counter:**
  - **Revenue 100% EUR, stock priced in GBP.** If GBP strengthens vs EUR (which is possible in a UK rate advantage scenario), the GBp-denominated value of all EUR earnings falls mechanically.
  - **GBP/EUR has ranged from 1.10 to 1.20 in the past 3 years.** At 1.16 today, a move to 1.20 would reduce the GBp FV by ~3.4%. A move to 1.10 would increase it by ~5.5%. This creates a +/-5% FX noise on top of fundamental valuation.
  - **The company has NO natural GBP revenue to offset this.** Unlike a company like Unilever (multi-currency revenue), BCG has 100% EUR revenue, 100% EUR costs, and the GBP listing is purely because it was floated on LSE. This means the stock price carries permanent FX noise that obscures fundamentals.
- **Severity:** LOW (affects price, not business)
- **Resolution:** Note as risk factor; consider 2-3% FV haircut for FX uncertainty.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- The historical FCF CAGR of 41% makes the reverse DCF look extremely compelling, but this was driven by a one-time margin expansion event (post-IPO cost stripping). I'm uncertain whether the market is pricing in "growth has ended" (0.3% implied) or "margins are peaking + growth is decelerating + AI risk." The market could be overly pessimistic OR correctly seeing structural headwinds I'm underweighting.
- I cannot independently verify BCG's competitive lead claims (6x, 27x, 36x larger). These come from company presentations. SimilarWeb or third-party traffic data would be valuable but I don't have access.
- The AI disruption question for classifieds is genuinely unresolved. ChatGPT/AI property search could reduce portal traffic OR it could increase it (portals with the best data feed AI agents). The direction is unclear.

### Limitations of This Analysis
- No access to BCG's H1 FY2026 earnings call transcript (would clarify management's re-acceleration guidance specifics)
- Limited short interest data for BCG.L (FCA data may have positions but insider_tracker showed N/A)

### Suggestions for the System
- The QS adjustment protocol should have a hard cap or escalating evidence requirements: +0-5 = standard evidence, +6-10 = quantitative evidence + DA sign-off, +11+ = exceptional evidence + committee review. A +12 jump crossing a Tier boundary is too consequential to be unilateral.
- For companies in very small markets (<10M population), consider a "TAM ceiling" modifier in the growth projection framework. Baltic GDP is ~EUR 120B total -- even capturing 0.1% of GDP is only EUR 120M in revenue. Growth projections should be sanity-checked against absolute TAM limits.
