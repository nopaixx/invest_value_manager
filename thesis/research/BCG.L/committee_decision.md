## Precedentes Consultados

1. **AUTO.L (S150):** Tier B (QS 71adj), digital classifieds (UK autos), 12.1% E[CAGR], 5% sizing, sold for capital rotation. Relevance: SAME sector (digital classifieds), same platform dynamics. BCG.L has STRONGER competitive position (6-36x leads vs AUTO.L's narrowing moat) and HIGHER margins. AUTO.L sold at Tier B; BCG.L enters at Tier A.

2. **IHP.L (S143):** Tier A (QS 80), UK small-cap digital platform, 19.5% E[CAGR], added to 11.4%. Relevance: SAME QS (80), SAME geography (UK-listed), similar market cap range. IHP.L was sized aggressively (11.4%) due to high E[CAGR] and high conviction. BCG.L E[CAGR] is lower (13.5%) and conviction is MEDIUM, so sizing should be smaller.

3. **TW (S143):** Tier A (QS 84adj), data monopoly, 13.2% E[CAGR], 5.7% sizing. Relevance: SIMILAR E[CAGR] (~13%), Tier A, monopoly business with basket-filling rationale. TW was sized at 5.7% despite moderate E[CAGR] because of basket importance. BCG.L at 2.5-3.0% is consistent with lower conviction and smaller cap.

**Deviation from precedents:** BCG.L sizing at 2.5-3.0% is BELOW TW (5.7%) and IHP.L (11.4%) for similar Tier A companies. This is justified by: (a) MEDIUM conviction vs Medium/High for those positions, (b) Baltic geographic concentration risk, (c) growth deceleration uncertainty, (d) small cap liquidity concerns. The lower sizing is conservative relative to precedents, which is appropriate for a novel market (first Baltic investment).

## Gate 9: Autocritica + Edge -- PASS (with reservations)

**Unvalidated assumptions:**
1. Management's "double digit re-acceleration" guidance for FY2027 -- this is the core bull case and is NOT yet demonstrated
2. ARPU runway "30-50% below Western European peers" -- based on BCG presentations, not independently verified
3. Estonian auto tax impact is "transitory" -- it's actually a permanent new tax regime; the question is whether transactions partially recover

**Biases recognized:**
- [x] Popularity bias: BCG.L is NOT a popularity bias risk -- it's a small cap with limited coverage. The risk is actually the OPPOSITE: we found it through systematic screening, which is anti-bias.
- [x] Confirmation bias: The R1 thesis presents BCG as "AUTO.L but better." This comparison may be anchoring us to a favorable frame. BCG operates in a fundamentally different market (7M Baltics vs 67M UK) with different economics.
- [x] Recency bias: The -53% drawdown makes the stock FEEL cheap. But it dropped for reasons (growth deceleration, guidance cut) that may not be fully resolved.

**Kill conditions (7, from R3):**
1. KC#1: Revenue growth negative for 2 consecutive quarters (ex-M&A)
2. KC#2: EBITDA margin falls below 65% for 2 quarters
3. KC#3: Apax Partners sells >50% of remaining stake
4. KC#4: Facebook Marketplace captures >10% of any BCG vertical
5. KC#5: Estonian auto transactions fail to recover above 60% of pre-tax levels by FY2027
6. KC#6: Net debt exceeds 2.0x EBITDA
7. KC#7: Real estate ARPU growth decelerates below 10% for 2 consecutive quarters

**Committee additions:**
8. KC#8: Lithuania-specific regulatory action affecting platform pricing power (DA recommendation, accepted)
9. KC#9: Total Baltic population decline accelerates >1.5%/year across combined LT+LV+EE (demographic risk)

**Edge Test -- What do I know that the market doesn't?**
The market prices 0.3% implied FCF growth. I believe growth will re-accelerate to 8-10% because: (1) auto segment maintained revenue FLAT despite transactions halving (pricing power absorbed volume), (2) real estate ARPU expanded 25% and has runway, (3) bolt-on acquisitions (Untu.lt, GetAPro) add revenue layers. The market is treating temporary growth deceleration as permanent. An insider confirmed this view with GBP 100K purchase at ~190p.

**Falsifiability -- What would make this thesis wrong?**
If H2 FY2026 growth stays at 7% or lower (no re-acceleration), and real estate ARPU growth decelerates below 15%, the "temporary deceleration" thesis is wrong and the stock could de-rate further to 150-160p. This is the primary risk and should be monitored closely at H2 results (expected Jun 2026).

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas sobre esta decision
- The bare Tier A status (QS 80, only 5 above threshold) with a +9 adjustment is the weakest Tier A classification in the portfolio. If the tool data improves (more years of ROIC data), the adjusted score may change. At 80 it's borderline -- at 79 it would be Tier B.
- The 0% MoS vs bear case (180p vs bear 175p) means there is effectively NO downside cushion in the bear scenario. If growth disappoints AND multiples compress, the stock could trade at 145-155p (DA bear range), representing 17-20% downside.
- The capital source (Mar 26 surplus) depends on MONY.L sale + NVO trim executing successfully. If either is delayed, BCG.L capital may not be available.

### Debilidades del analisis recibido
- R1 thesis had significant bullish bias (FV 260 vs reconciliation 205) that was correctly identified and reduced by DA. The R1 analyst talked themselves UP from their own conservative analysis -- this is the systematic +15.7% bullish bias documented in the DA audit.
- The competitive position claims (6-36x leads) come from BCG's own presentations. No independent verification (SimilarWeb, etc.) was performed. This is a data gap.
- Baltic population decline (Lithuania -28% since 1990, Latvia -27%) is mentioned but not deeply modeled. This is a genuine long-term headwind that could compress the TAM ceiling faster than the ARPU expansion can offset.

### Sugerencias de mejora
- The quality_scorer.py tool should flag when market position is scored 0 and the company is in a known monopoly vertical (classifieds, exchanges, etc.) -- suggesting manual review rather than silent 0.
- For companies in <10M population markets, the projection framework should include a TAM ceiling sanity check: max revenue = [population * GDP per capita * industry revenue intensity]. This would bound the growth projections.
- The sector view (digital-marketplaces.md) should be updated to include BCG.L as a portfolio candidate/comparable.
