## Assumptions Challenged

### 1. MSA Concentration Risk Remains the Existential Overhang (SEVERITY: HIGH)

**R1/R3 assumption:** MSA runs through 2033, switching costs are prohibitive, relationship is "deeply symbiotic." R3 adjusted FV down by $10-15 for this risk.

**Updated evidence against:**

- **Short interest surged 33% MoM (825K to 1.1M shares).** Short % of float now 5.3% with 3.3 days to cover. This is the highest level since thesis initiation. Sophisticated short sellers are increasing bets AGAINST LOPE, likely because the MSA risk has not been priced out.

- **DOE's 2019 analysis found LOPE receives 60% of revenue for 28% of services.** This finding was never formally refuted -- the DOE simply accepted GCU's nonprofit status without ruling on MSA fairness. As GCU matures as an independent nonprofit with its own board, the pressure to renegotiate toward market-rate terms will grow. GCU has never solicited competing bids for the services LOPE provides.

- **The $35M qui tam settlement (Nov 2025) signals ongoing legal exposure.** While the FTC case was dismissed and the $37.7M DOE fine was rescinded, LOPE agreed to pay $35M to settle the qui tam case alleging incentive compensation violations. This is real cash leaving the business to settle conduct-based allegations.

- **Non-GCU diversification is glacial.** 22 partners but GCU still ~85% of revenue. At current trajectory, KC#6 (non-GCU >20% by 2028) will be extremely difficult to hit. The prior DA proposed this KC -- it remains the most likely kill condition to trigger.

**Severity: HIGH** (unchanged from prior DA -- risk is structural and unmitigated)

**Resolution:** The $10-15 discount applied in R3 may be insufficient given rising short interest and advancing litigation. Consider additional $5-10 haircut.

### 2. One Big Beautiful Bill Graduate Loan Caps -- NEW RISK Not in Thesis (SEVERITY: MODERATE-HIGH)

**Thesis assumption:** Regulatory risk is "diminishing" post-DOE nonprofit recognition.

**New evidence against:**

- **Effective July 2026, Grad PLUS loans will be eliminated.** Graduate students will be capped at $20,500/year ($100,000 aggregate). Professional students capped at $50,000/year ($200,000 aggregate). This is a STRUCTURAL change to higher education funding.

- **GCU's doctoral programs are directly in the crosshairs.** The DOE previously found that "fewer than 2% of GCU doctoral program graduates complete within the advertised cost." If doctoral programs at GCU require $40K-$80K+ and students can only borrow $20.5K/year for graduate studies, many prospective doctoral students will be priced out or forced to find private financing.

- **Management acknowledged one program category already fails.** On the Q4 2025 earnings call, management stated the Master's in Counseling category "failed" under the new framework. While management claimed "little to no impact" on bachelor's programs, graduate and doctoral programs face genuine enrollment headwinds.

- **The thesis DOES NOT MENTION the One Big Beautiful Bill at all.** This is a material omission given that GCU's doctoral programs have been the source of multiple lawsuits, an FTC investigation, and a $37.7M fine (rescinded). The loan caps create a new constraint on exactly the programs that have been most controversial.

- **Impact quantification:** If doctoral/graduate enrollment declines 10-15% due to reduced federal loan access, and these programs represent a disproportionate share of tuition revenue (doctoral tuition is higher per credit), the revenue impact could be 2-4% of total GCU tuition, flowing through to LOPE at 60% = $13-26M revenue reduction.

**Severity: MODERATE-HIGH** -- This is a new material risk not addressed in R1 or R3.

**Resolution:** This risk should be added as a Kill Condition and incorporated into bear case scenarios. Recommend: "GCU graduate/doctoral program enrollment declines >15% for 2 consecutive years post-OBBBA implementation (July 2026)."

---

### 3. RICO Lawsuit Advancing -- Material Litigation Risk (SEVERITY: MODERATE)

**Thesis assumption:** FTC case dismissed, DOE fine rescinded. Legal risks resolved.

**New evidence against:**

- **Smith v. Grand Canyon Education RICO lawsuit survived motion to dismiss (May 6, 2025).** U.S. District Court Judge allowed the case to proceed on 4 of 5 counts, including the RICO (Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act) claim. This is a CLASS ACTION with potentially thousands of affected students.

- **Allegations are serious:** Students allege GCU induced enrollment in doctoral programs by lying about costs, that 98% of doctoral students paid more than advertised, and that "continuation courses" were used to extract additional tuition. The DOE's own investigation (before being rescinded) confirmed these factual claims.

- **RICO claims carry treble damages.** If the class action succeeds, damages could be 3x actual harm. With 7,500+ affected students identified by DOE, even modest per-student damages ($10-20K) could result in $75-150M in initial damages, trebled to $225-450M. LOPE's market cap is $4.7B -- a $225-450M judgment would be 5-10% of market cap.

- **The FTC dismissal was political, not exonerative.** The FTC voted to dismiss under the Trump administration, which has systematically reduced regulatory enforcement. The factual basis of the allegations (98% of students paying more than advertised) has not been disproven in any court proceeding.

- **Former students filed a separate "worthless degrees" complaint (Feb 2026).** Alleging counselors lied about accreditation and licensure eligibility. This is a REPUTATIONAL risk that could affect future enrollment even if litigation is ultimately settled.

**Severity: MODERATE** -- Litigation outcome uncertain, but the RICO survival and treble damages possibility create material tail risk.

**Resolution:** Add Kill Condition: "RICO class action results in certified class AND discovery reveals systematic deceptive enrollment practices with damages potential exceeding $200M."

### 4. Growth Assumptions Still Carry Residual Bullish Bias (SEVERITY: MODERATE)

**R3 assumption:** 8% expected growth (revenue 6-7% + margin expansion 0-25bps + buyback 1-2%).

**Updated evidence against:**

- **Revenue per student is flat to declining.** Q4 2025 showed revenue per student declined YoY due to contract modifications reducing revenue share AND mix shift to online students with lower tuition. If LOPE is already seeing revenue share compression within the MSA, the 6-7% revenue CAGR depends entirely on enrollment volume growth.

- **FCF declined 5.3% in 2025 despite 7.1% revenue growth.** Cash flow conversion weakened from 123% (2024) to 107% (2025). The thesis's OEY-based valuation depends on FCF growing at least with revenue -- 2025 broke this pattern.

- **Enrollment demographic headwinds are intensifying.** The demographic cliff (declining 18-24 cohort) is not hypothetical -- it is already occurring. While LOPE's focus on adult learners (25+) provides some insulation, the thesis assigns this sensitivity as "LOW-MODERATE" when the data supports "MODERATE."

- **The buyback yield calculation is overstated.** The thesis claims 5-6% buyback yield ($265M on $4.7B market cap). But $35M went to the qui tam settlement, and if OBBBA headwinds or RICO litigation require reserves, buyback intensity could decline. KC#10 (buyback intensity >$100M/yr) is already monitoring this, but the base case should not assume peak buyback intensity persists.

**Severity: MODERATE** (prior DA already reduced growth from 10% to 8% -- residual bias is smaller)

**Resolution:** 8% growth assumption is borderline. If Q1 2026 shows continued FCF weakness or revenue per student decline, should be reduced to 7%.

## META-REFLECTION

### Doubts/Uncertainties
- The OBBBA graduate loan cap impact on GCU specifically is difficult to quantify without knowing the breakdown of enrollment by degree level (bachelor's vs master's vs doctoral) and the average loan burden per student. Management claimed "little to no impact" on bachelor's programs but acknowledged one master's category failed. The actual impact may be smaller or larger than my 2-4% revenue estimate.
- The RICO lawsuit is in early stages. Survival of a motion to dismiss is not uncommon and does not predict outcome. However, the factual basis (DOE confirmed 98% of students paid more than advertised) is strong for plaintiffs.
- Short interest surging 33% could reflect: (a) a sophisticated bear thesis we do not see, (b) macro-driven sector rotation out of education, or (c) arbitrage/technical positioning. Without knowing the shorts' thesis, I cannot assess whether their conviction is well-founded.
