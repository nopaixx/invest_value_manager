## Business Understanding

### What Does Games Workshop Do?

Games Workshop designs, manufactures, and sells miniature figurines and related products for tabletop wargaming, primarily under the **Warhammer 40,000** and **Warhammer: Age of Sigmar** brands. The company operates a vertically integrated model:

1. **Core Business (~96% of revenue):** Design, manufacture (in-house in Nottingham UK + Memphis USA), and sell miniature kits, paints, rulebooks, and accessories through:
   - Own retail stores (Warhammer stores globally, 71 countries)
   - Independent trade accounts (hobby stores)
   - Direct online (games-workshop.com)

2. **Licensing (~4% of revenue but growing):** Licensing the Warhammer IP for video games (Space Marine 2 sold 7M+ copies), Amazon TV adaptation (Henry Cavill attached), books, merchandise.

### Why This Is a Quality Compounder

**The moat is the IP and community, not manufacturing.** Warhammer is not a product — it is a hobby ecosystem. A customer who enters the Warhammer hobby faces:

- **Massive switching costs:** Hundreds of hours painting miniatures + building armies. Leaving means abandoning that investment. The average hobbyist's army represents 500+ hours of assembly and painting.
- **Network effects:** You play Warhammer with friends who also play Warhammer. The local gaming community is built around Warhammer rules and tournaments.
- **Emotional lock-in:** 40+ years of lore, novels, video games, and now TV. The IP has cult-like devotion.
- **No substitute at scale:** No competitor has anything close to Warhammer's ecosystem breadth (miniatures + rules + stores + tournaments + digital + licensing).

This creates the financial profile we see: 72% gross margins (vs 35% sector median), 105% ROIC, and pricing power demonstrated by the 3.5% H1 price increase with no volume loss.

## Why Is It "Expensive" (Not Cheap)?

### Market Narrative

The market is pricing GAW.L for:
1. Continued 14-17% revenue growth from the core hobby
2. The Amazon Warhammer TV series as a transformative licensing catalyst
3. Expansion of the digital ecosystem (My Warhammer app, Warhammer+)
4. Margin expansion from operating leverage and pricing power

At 27x P/E and 21x EV/EBIT, the stock is priced for perfection. The market is NOT wrong about the quality — it is correctly recognizing one of the UK's best businesses.

### My Assessment

The market's thesis is largely correct. The question is whether the price adequately compensates for risks:

- **Amazon deal timeline uncertainty:** Still in pre-production. No showrunner. No release date. Could be 2028+. The market may be front-running optionality that is 2-3 years away.
- **Tariff headwind:** GBP 12M annual impact, offset by price increases. Manageable but erodes margin.
- **Licensing revenue volatility:** H1 FY2026 licensing down 47% YoY due to Space Marine 2 comp. This lumpiness will create volatile headline numbers.
- **Payout ratio 90%:** Limits reinvestment capacity. The business throws off cash but returns almost all of it.
- **Single-IP concentration:** Warhammer IS the business. No meaningful second franchise.

## Risks

1. **Amazon deal execution risk:** Pre-production only. No showrunner confirmed. If the show is mediocre or cancelled, the licensing premium in the stock evaporates. However, licensing is only ~4% of revenue — core business is unaffected.

2. **US tariff escalation:** GBP 12M current impact could grow if tariffs increase further (Section 301 probes ongoing). GAW manufactures primarily in UK — exports to US face full tariff exposure. 3.5% price increase absorbed so far, but repeated increases risk volume impact.

3. **Single-IP concentration:** Warhammer is effectively 100% of the business. While the IP is extremely strong, any reputational damage (e.g., community backlash over pricing, rules changes, or "selling out" to Amazon) could erode the hobby ecosystem.

4. **3D printing technology:** Long-term, improving 3D printers could allow hobbyists to produce their own miniatures. Currently mitigated by: (a) community/tournament rules require official models, (b) quality gap persists, (c) paint and accessories still needed. But this is a 5-10 year structural risk.

5. **High payout ratio (90%):** Limits the company's ability to invest aggressively in growth. The business model doesn't require heavy capex, but it also means limited M&A capacity or investment in new IP development.

6. **Receivables growth anomaly:** 57.7% receivable growth vs 17.5% revenue growth in FY2025 needs monitoring. Could be timing, could be quality deterioration in trade accounts.

7. **Macro sensitivity:** While Warhammer is a "hobby" with passionate devotees, it IS discretionary spending. Severe recession + oil crisis could compress hobby budgets. H1 FY2026 showed no impact yet, but the Hormuz crisis is recent.

## Kill Conditions

1. **KC#1: Core revenue growth turns negative for 2 consecutive reporting periods.** This would signal hobby demand saturation. Current: +17.3% H1 FY2026.

2. **KC#2: Gross margin falls below 65% for 2 consecutive periods.** Would indicate pricing power loss or input cost absorption failure. Current: 69.4% H1 core GM (impacted by tariffs, still strong).

3. **KC#3: FCF margin falls below 20% sustainably (2+ years).** FCF is the lifeblood. Sub-20% would indicate capex/working capital problems. Current: 33.4%.

4. **KC#4: Amazon deal formally cancelled or abandoned.** While licensing is only ~4% of revenue, cancellation would destroy a significant portion of the growth premium in the stock price.

5. **KC#5: CEO Kevin Rountree departs AND begins selling shares.** Rountree has been the architect of GAW's transformation. His departure combined with selling would be a material negative signal. Note: departure alone (planned succession) is not a kill — only departure + selling.

6. **KC#6: Major community revolt resulting in >15% decline in My Warhammer active users.** The community IS the moat. A sustained user exodus (not seasonal fluctuation) would signal moat erosion. Current: 790K active users, growing.

7. **KC#7: 3D printing quality reaches parity with injection-molded miniatures AND becomes widely adopted in tournament play.** This is the long-term structural threat. Monitor annually.
