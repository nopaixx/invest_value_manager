## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

Sherwin-Williams manufactures, distributes, and sells paints, coatings, and related products globally. The business operates through three segments:

**Paint Stores Group (PSG) -- 58% of revenue, 22.5% margin:**
- 4,773 company-operated stores in US/Canada/Caribbean
- Sells exclusively to professional painters, contractors, property managers, and industrial/commercial customers
- This is the crown jewel -- vertically integrated distribution that competitors cannot replicate
- Same-store sales +1.7% FY2025, +1.0% Q4. Opened 80-100 stores/year

**Performance Coatings Group (PCG) -- 29% of revenue, 13.9% margin:**
- Industrial wood, coil, packaging, automotive refinish, general industrial coatings
- Sells through direct sales and distribution
- More cyclical, tied to industrial production and automotive
- Protective & Marine segment grew high-single digits

**Consumer Brands Group (CBG) -- 13% of revenue, 16.1% margin:**
- DIY retail channel through Lowe's, Ace, and international retailers
- Brands: Valspar, HGTV, Purdy, Minwax, Thompson's WaterSeal
- Recently acquired Suvinil (Brazil, ~$165M/quarter contribution)
- Core DIY demand remains soft

### Unit Economics

- Paint costs = ~15% of a contractor's total job cost --> extreme switching cost tolerance
- CAC effectively near zero for PSG (store proximity + relationship selling)
- LTV/CAC extremely high: pro painters are repeat buyers (daily/weekly purchases)
- Capital intensity: moderate. Capex $798M (3.4% of revenue), but includes growth capex (new stores, Suvinil)
- Maintenance capex estimated ~$500M (depreciation $628M x ~0.8, net of growth stores)
- FCF margin: 11.3% FY2025 (trailing 3yr avg 10.6%)

Gross margin expansion driven by raw material deflation (2023-2024) and pricing actions. Operating margin expansion slower due to SG&A investments (store openings, Suvinil integration, restructuring charges).

## Moat Assessment

**Rating: WIDE**

1. **Proprietary Distribution (Primary Moat):** 4,773 company-operated stores -- 6x larger than PPG's network. This is a 100+ year asset that cannot be replicated. The store network creates convenience for pro painters who need product availability within 15-minute drive radius. New store economics are attractive: payback in 3-4 years.

2. **Switching Costs (Pro Painter Lock-in):** Paint = 15% of job cost. Switching to a cheaper product risks callback costs, reputation damage, and productivity loss (different application characteristics). Pro painters are habitual -- they use what they know works.

3. **Vertical Integration:** SHW manufactures its own products, controls the full supply chain from raw materials to retail. This allows margin optimization that competitors selling through third-party retail cannot match.

4. **Scale Economies:** $23.6B in revenue provides procurement leverage on TiO2, resins, and solvents that smaller competitors cannot match. Largest paint buyer in the world.

5. **Brand Portfolio:** Sherwin-Williams, Valspar, HGTV, Dutch Boy, Minwax, Thompson's -- multi-channel brand coverage.

**Gross Margin Premium:** 48.8% vs sector median 25.0% = +23.8pp. This is among the strongest moat signals in any industrial company.

**ROIC Persistence:** ROIC > WACC for 10+ consecutive years (15-18% ROIC vs 10% WACC consistently).

## Valuation

### Method 1: DCF (60% weight) -- Tier B primary

**Parameters derived from business analysis:**

- Revenue growth: 3.5% base (TAM growth 3-4% for coatings, stable/gaining share, 1-2% pricing)
- FCF margin: 11% (trailing 3yr avg 10.6%, modest improvement from restructuring)
- WACC: 9.0% (Ke 11.3%, Kd 3.3% after-tax, D/V ~15%)
- Terminal growth: 2.5% (paint demand grows with housing stock, population)
- Projection: 5 years

**DCF tool output:**
- Bear case (2% growth, WACC 10.5%): $94 per share
- Base case (3.5% growth, WACC 9%): $132 per share
- Bull case (5% growth, WACC 8%): $187 per share

**CRITICAL NOTE:** The DCF produces FVs dramatically below market. This reveals that the market prices SHW as a compounder with significant FCF growth beyond what trailing FCF alone justifies. The DCF assumes FCF margin stays ~11%, but:
- SHW is actively expanding margins through restructuring and pricing
- Share buybacks (4.8M shares/yr at ~$1.6B) compound per-share FCF growth at ~2% additional
- The DCF at face value says SHW is 60-70% overvalued

**Sensitivity:** FV Spread 110%, TV 74.5% of EV --> HIGH SENSITIVITY. DCF is unreliable as a point estimate. Use as a range and anchor on EV/EBIT.

### Method 3: Reverse DCF (sanity check)

The market at $322.71 implies 22.4% annual FCF growth for 5 years. With revenue growing at 2-4%, this requires dramatic margin expansion or massive buyback acceleration. Historical FCF CAGR was 27.7% (3yr), but this was driven by post-COVID raw material deflation boosting gross margins from 42% to 49%. This tailwind is largely exhausted. The implied growth rate seems aggressive given forward fundamentals.
