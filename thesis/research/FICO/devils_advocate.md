## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis correctly identifies FICO as a high-quality business and appropriately concludes the stock is overvalued at then-price of $1,363. Since R1, the stock has fallen further to $1,128 -- a 17% decline in 5 weeks. However, the thesis still contains several critical blind spots: (1) the VantageScore competitive threat has ACCELERATED since R1, with bureaus now pricing at $1.00/pull in a full-scale price war; (2) FICO just issued $1B in new debt at 6.25%, INCREASING leverage into the storm; (3) the DOJ has opened an active investigation for "exclusionary conduct"; (4) the March 12 crash (-9%) on analyst downgrades confirms the market is still re-pricing the monopoly erosion. Even the R1's own $900 FV may be too generous given the structural shift from monopoly to duopoly. My independent bear-case FV is $550-650.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "VantageScore adoption will be slow (years, not months)"

- **FA assumption:** VantageScore adoption takes 3-5 years to materially impact FICO. Only 1-2% annual market share erosion starting FY2027.
- **Evidence against:** Since the R1 was written (Feb 11), the competitive landscape has deteriorated materially:
  - Credit bureaus slashed VantageScore pricing to $1.00/pull in some channels (vs FICO $10.00) -- a 10:1 price differential
  - March 12, 2026: FICO shares plunged 9% on VantageScore pricing war escalation + UBS downgrade to Neutral
  - VantageScore market penetration surged 166% YoY in 2025, with 3,700 lenders adopting
  - The bureaus are giving VantageScore away FREE to certain customers through 2026
  - FHFA "Lender Choice" model went into full implementation Jan 1, 2026 -- the clock is ticking
  - VantageScore estimates $649M in first-year savings from full adoption -- lenders have massive financial incentive
- **Why this matters:** The FA's "slow adoption" thesis rests on switching costs and lender conservatism. But when a product is 10x cheaper AND the three companies that control the data pipeline (Experian, TransUnion, Equifax) own the competitor AND are actively subsidizing its adoption, switching cost barriers erode much faster than historical precedent suggests. This is not a normal competitive entry -- it is a coordinated assault by FICO's own distribution partners.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution:** The investment committee should require actual GSE submission data showing VantageScore at <5% of mortgage decisions before accepting the "slow adoption" thesis. Without this data, the thesis is built on hope, not evidence.

### 2. "FICO's pricing power remains intact"

- **FA assumption:** FICO is INCREASING prices and launching Direct License. Revenue growing 16% YoY. Pricing power not yet eroded.
- **Evidence against:**
  - Senator Hawley formally called for DOJ investigation of FICO for "anticompetitive practices" (April 2025, renewed 2026)
  - DOJ Antitrust Division has opened a CIVIL INVESTIGATION into "potential exclusionary conduct" by FICO -- this is no longer theoretical
  - UBS downgraded to Neutral, cut PT from $1,500 to $1,350, explicitly citing pricing power erosion
  - FICO's own pricing strategy is contradictory: $10/score through bureaus but $4.95 Direct License -- effectively admitting the $10 price is unsustainable
  - The fact that FICO launched Mortgage Direct at HALF the bureau price ($4.95 vs $10) is a tacit admission that bureau channel pricing was too aggressive
  - Equifax publicly stated FICO's 2x price increase costs the industry ~$100M annually
  - Housing affordability is a bipartisan political issue -- "monopoly charges $10 per credit score during housing crisis" is a political vulnerability
- **Why this matters:** The FA correctly notes FICO doubled prices to $10/score. But this is exactly the behavior that triggered the regulatory and competitive backlash. The pricing power is REFLEXIVE: the more FICO raises prices, the more incentive competitors and regulators have to destroy that pricing power. Revenue growth from pricing CANNOT be extrapolated because the pricing itself is generating its own destruction.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution:** Model Scores revenue under a scenario where bureau-channel pricing reverts to $5 (Direct License level) and mortgage volume share drops 15%. This produces a very different revenue trajectory.

### 3. "QS 70 (Tier B) is an appropriate quality rating"

- **FA assumption:** QS Tool 75 adjusted to 70 for VantageScore threat, leverage, and capital allocation concerns.
- **Evidence against:**
  - The $1B new debt issuance at 6.25% (March 2026) was NOT in the R1 analysis -- total debt is now ~$4.2B, not $3.2B
  - Net Debt/EBITDA has likely risen from 3.0x to approximately 4.0x+ with the new issuance
  - The R1 scored leverage at 2/10; with $4.2B debt and negative equity, this should be 0/10
  - Insider selling: 127 sales, 0 purchases in 6 months. Capital allocation score should be further penalized.
  - Short interest at 6.8% and RISING (+5% MoM) -- market participants are actively betting against
  - Akre Capital holds 5.1% of portfolio in FICO -- but Akre is a quality/growth fund that bought at lower prices. This is NOT a contrarian signal.
  - R&D/Revenue DECLINING: 10.7% (2022) to 9.5% (2025) -- the company is CUTTING R&D investment during a period of maximum competitive threat
- **Why this matters:** QS 70 (Tier B) still implies "quality value" -- a business with moderate risk. Given the active DOJ investigation, coordinated competitive attack, leveraged balance sheet (now $4.2B debt), declining R&D, and unanimous insider selling, QS 65 (Tier B lower end) would be more appropriate. The -5 point adjustment was insufficient.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Adjust QS to 65 maximum. With Tier B at 65, MoS required increases to 25-30% per precedents.

### 4. "Fair Value ~$900 with range $650-$1,004"

- **FA assumption:** Weighted FV ~$900 using OEY (50%), EV/EBIT (30%), and Reverse DCF directional (20%).
- **Evidence against:**
  - Reverse DCF shows the market at $1,128 implies 25.9% FCF growth for 5 years -- vs 13.7% historical. Gap of 12.2pp. Even at $900, implied growth far exceeds historical delivery.
  - The EV/EBIT method used 27.5x multiple (midpoint of 25-30x). But FICO's peers with WIDER moats trade at: Equifax 14x forward, TransUnion 16x forward. Even SPGI (37x) and MSCI (42x) have no active DOJ investigation and no $1/pull competitor.
  - The R1 acknowledged DCF produces $570-590 but DISMISSED it as "too low" because DCF "struggles with high-growth businesses." This is selection bias -- dismissing the method that gives the lowest FV while keeping the methods that give higher FVs.
  - The asymmetry analysis from reverse DCF is damning: Bull return +37.7% vs Bear return -61.8%. Asymmetry ratio 0.61x (unfavorable). Equal-weight expected return: -12.1%.
  - Alpha Spread intrinsic value model: $944 base case, overvalued by 14% even at today's lower price
  - The $1B new debt issuance increases EV by $1B, reducing equity FV by ~$42/share across all methods
- **Why this matters:** The FA's $900 FV is anchored to the OEY + EV/EBIT methods, which produce higher values than DCF. But the DCF is the most mechanically rigorous method. The real question is: which multiple does FICO deserve? If the answer is "25-35x" (competitive but not monopolistic), then FV is $650-850. If "15-20x" (Equifax/TransUnion tier as moat fully erodes), FV is $400-550.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution:** The committee should examine whether the R1's EV/EBIT multiple (27.5x) is defensible when direct competitors with similar data-access advantages trade at 14-16x. The 13-14x multiple gap between FICO and Equifax/TransUnion reflects the monopoly premium -- if the monopoly is eroding, the gap should narrow.

### 5. "The software segment provides diversification and downside protection"

- **FA assumption:** Software Platform ARR growing 33% (Q1 FY2026). Software provides independent moat via switching costs.
- **Evidence against:**
  - Total Software revenue grew only +2% in Q1 FY2026 (vs Scores +29%). The 33% Platform ARR headline is misleading because non-platform revenue is declining 8-13%.
  - Platform ARR is only $303M of $766M total Software ARR (39.5%). The majority of software revenue is still legacy on-prem DECLINING.
  - Net Software growth of +2% with one component at +33% and another at -13% means the transition is at PARITY, not growth mode
  - The moat assessment correctly notes this but the thesis overweights the narrative. At current trajectory, it takes until FY2028-2029 for Platform to become majority of software revenue.
  - CEO Lansing (since 2012) was characterized as a "maintainer, not transformer" by Paragon Intel analysis -- raising execution risk on the platform pivot
- **Why this matters:** If Scores revenue decelerates (pricing pressure + VantageScore share loss) AND Software only grows 2-5%, FICO's total revenue growth drops to 5-8% -- far below the 10-15% that supports even a 25-30x multiple.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** Model a scenario where total revenue growth drops to 5-7% by FY2028. What multiple does that growth rate merit? Historically, 5-7% growth data companies trade at 15-22x forward P/E.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- The VantageScore "$1/pull" pricing from bureaus could not be independently verified against primary sources. This comes from financial news articles (T2/T3 sources). If the actual pricing is $4-4.50 (as some earlier reports suggest), the price differential is less extreme.
- The $1B debt issuance uses of proceeds include refinancing $400M of existing 5.25% notes due 2026. The NET increase in debt is therefore ~$600M, not $1B. However, the coupon rate is higher (6.25% vs 5.25%), increasing interest expense regardless.
- I could not find actual GSE submission data showing VantageScore's share of mortgage decisions. All adoption figures come from VantageScore's own PR (T3 source -- incentivized to overstate). The actual adoption could be much lower than the headlines suggest.
- The capex/depreciation ratio surge (0.3x to 2.6x) in narrative_checker.py output is anomalous for an "asset-light" business but could reflect FICO Platform buildout investment (positive, not negative). Requires investigation.
