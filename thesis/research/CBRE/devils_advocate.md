# Counter-Analysis: CBRE

## Fecha: 2026-03-20

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis for CBRE is built on a compelling narrative -- sector misclassification, asset-light services model, ValueAct governance -- but it rests on the LARGEST QS adjustment in the system's history (+47 points) and produces a FV ($130) that is BELOW the current market price ($133). The thesis effectively acknowledges there is no margin of safety at current levels. My investigation reveals that (1) the +47 QS adjustment is partially justified but materially overstated on at least 3 of 8 line items, (2) GAAP-to-Core earnings gap conceals real economic costs that will recur, (3) the CRE refinancing wall ($936B maturing in 2026) creates a significant macro headwind the thesis underweights, and (4) declining gross and operating margins suggest the business mix shift toward BOE is dilutive, not accretive, to quality. At $133, the stock is priced for a recovery that macro conditions may delay materially.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. QS Adjustment +47 Points (Tool 15 to Adjusted 62)

- **Evidencia en contra:** This is the largest QS adjustment EVER in the system. The thesis adjusts 8 individual line items, each with stated rationale. However, several adjustments are GENEROUS:

  **a) ROIC Spread: Tool 0 to 8 (+8).** The thesis argues tangible ROIC ~23% by excluding $8.9B goodwill. But goodwill represents REAL CAPITAL DEPLOYED for acquisitions. If you paid $1.3B for Turner & Townsend, that capital is consumed -- you cannot earn returns on it twice. Excluding goodwill from invested capital is standard for OPERATIONAL analysis but misleading for RETURN ON INVESTMENT analysis. A fair adjustment would be +4 (ROIC spread ~0-5pp including partial goodwill), not +8.

  **b) EPS CAGR: Tool 0 to 10 (+10).** The thesis uses "Core EPS" CAGR of ~15%. But Core EPS excludes: M&A amortization ($300-400M/yr), restructuring costs, carried interest compensation, and integration costs. For an ACQUISITIVE company that has done $5B+ in M&A in 5 years, these are NOT non-recurring -- they are the COST OF THE GROWTH STRATEGY. A company that grows by acquisition and then excludes acquisition costs from earnings is flattering results. Fair adjustment: +6 (acknowledge some adjustment valid but not full credit for 15% CAGR).

  **c) Leverage: Tool 2 to 8 (+6).** The thesis uses company-defined net leverage 1.24x. But the tool uses ND/EBITDA 3.8x with GAAP EBITDA. Company-defined metrics use "adjusted" EBITDA which adds back the same items excluded from Core EPS. The truth is between: probably 2.0-2.5x on a normalized basis. Fair: +4, not +6.

  **d) Market Position: Tool 0 to 8 (+8).** This adjustment is FULLY JUSTIFIED. CBRE is clearly #1 globally. No dispute.

  **e) FCF Margin: Tool 0 to 5 (+5).** The net revenue approach (10% FCF margin vs 2.9% total) is conceptually correct but note that FCF margin on net revenue has been DECLINING (from the thesis's own data: FCF fell from $1.4B to $1.2B while revenue grew 13.4%). Fair: +4.

- **Revised DA estimate of fair QS:** Applying more conservative but still justified adjustments: +4 (ROIC) + 4 (FCF) + 4 (leverage) + 6 (EPS) + 4 (GM) + 8 (market position) + 3 (ROIC persistence) + 3 (shareholder returns) = +36 on top of Tool 15 = **QS 51 (Tier C)**, not 62 (Tier B).

- **Severidad:** **HIGH** -- A Tier C classification vs Tier B changes the required MoS from ~20-25% to ~30-40%, making the entry price dramatically different.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Committee should scrutinize each QS adjustment individually. Goodwill exclusion from invested capital and Core EPS CAGR credit are the two most debatable items. If QS lands at 51-55, the required MoS increases materially.

### 2. GAAP vs Core EPS: Recurring Non-Recurring Items

- **Evidencia en contra:** CBRE's Core EPS adjustments exclude:
  - Amortization of acquisition intangibles (~$300-400M/yr)
  - Integration and restructuring costs
  - Carried interest incentive compensation
  - Write-off of financing costs

  For a company whose STATED STRATEGY is growth-by-acquisition (T&T, Trammell Crow, Industrious), these costs are as recurring as revenue growth. CBRE has spent $5B+ on acquisitions in the past 5 years. The amortization will continue for 10-20 years.

  **GAAP EPS trajectory tells a DIFFERENT story:** 2022: $4.36, 2023: $3.20, 2024: $3.16, 2025: $3.88. GAAP EPS CAGR = -3.8%. GAAP P/E = 34.6x. This is NOT cheap.

  The thesis dismisses GAAP as "distorted" but GAAP exists precisely to prevent companies from flattering earnings by excluding the costs of their growth strategy.

- **Severidad:** **HIGH** -- The entire valuation (Core P/E 20.9x, FV $130) depends on accepting Core EPS as the "true" earnings. If you weight GAAP 40% and Core 60%, the blended P/E is ~26x, and FV drops to ~$105-110.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Committee should calculate FV using BOTH Core EPS and GAAP EPS as primary metrics, then weight the results. The divergence between GAAP and Core EPS should be treated as a RISK FACTOR, not a data quality issue.

### 3. CRE Refinancing Wall and Macro Headwinds

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis mentions macro headwinds but underweights a critical structural risk: $936B in CRE loans mature in 2026 (19% more than 2025). Key data points:
  - Average CRE loan rate at origination: 3-4%. Current refinance rate: 6.24% (nearly 2x)
  - CMBS delinquency rate: 7.29% -- nearly 6x higher than bank loans
  - Rolling 12-month troubled CRE volume: $13.8B (up from $1.1B in early 2020)
  - "Extend and pretend" has pushed $hundreds of billions into the 2026 window

  **Why this matters for CBRE:** While CBRE doesn't own buildings, it ADVISES on transactions and MANAGES properties. If a wave of CRE distress hits:
  - Advisory fees drop (fewer transactions, lower values, stressed clients)
  - Property management contracts at risk (building owners under financial pressure may bring management in-house or negotiate lower fees)
  - Investment Management AUM declines (portfolio markdowns)
  - Sentiment contagion: CBRE's "Real Estate" classification means ETF selling intensifies during CRE stress events

  The thesis argues "services not REITs" but CBRE's fortunes are inextricably linked to CRE market health. In 2023, when CRE was at its trough, FCF collapsed to $175M (from $1.4B the prior year). That is NOT resilience.

- **Severidad:** **HIGH** -- The $936B maturity wall is a concrete, dated macro risk that could suppress CRE transaction volumes through 2026-2027, directly contradicting the "+16% transaction volume" assumption in the thesis.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** The thesis should stress-test the bear case with a delayed recovery scenario where transaction volumes remain flat or decline through 2027 due to refinancing distress.

### 4. Margin Deterioration: Mix Shift Toward Low-Margin BOE

- **Evidencia en contra:** The narrative_checker data shows a clear and concerning trend:
  - **Gross margin declining:** 21.4% (2022) to 18.7% (2025) -- 270bps compression in 3 years
  - **Operating margin declining:** 4.3% (2022) to 3.2% (2025) -- 110bps compression
  - **FCF margin declining:** 4.4% (2022) to 2.9% (2025) -- 150bps compression

  The thesis argues this is explained by mix shift toward BOE (57% of revenue, cost-plus contracts). But this mix shift IS the problem: CBRE is growing by adding low-margin, capital-intensive facilities management revenue. This makes the company LARGER but not more PROFITABLE.

  The thesis claims "net revenue" is the right denominator, but even on net revenue, the trend is DECLINING FCF ($1.4B to $1.2B while net revenue grew).

  **Receivables growing faster than revenue (18.3% vs 13.4%)** -- this is a yellow flag for potential revenue recognition aggressiveness or deteriorating collection quality, as the thesis itself acknowledges.

- **Severidad:** **MODERATE** -- Margin compression is real and measurable. It may stabilize as the mix shift matures, but the trajectory contradicts the thesis's implicit assumption of stable-to-improving margins.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Committee should model FCF explicitly for 2026-2028 using the declining margin trend, not the stable 10-11% net revenue FCF margin the thesis assumes.

- **Severidad:** **LOW** -- Absolute short interest is minimal. The MoM increase is notable but not alarming. CFO sale needs 10b5-1 verification before drawing conclusions.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Verify whether CFO sale is 10b5-1 scheduled. Monitor short interest trend in next monthly report.
