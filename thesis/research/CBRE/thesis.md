## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

CBRE is the world's largest commercial real estate services and investment firm. The business operates across four segments:

1. **Advisory Services** ($8.84B, 22% of revenue): Leasing, property sales, debt/equity financing, appraisal, consulting. This is the cyclical, high-margin segment. SOP margin ~24% (Q4: $709M on $2.9B).

2. **Building Operations & Experience** ($23.2B, 57% of revenue): Enterprise facilities management, property management, Industrious flexible workspace. Mostly cost-plus contracts with management fees. Low margin but highly recurring and predictable. Growing 15% YoY.

3. **Project Management** ($7.66B, 19% of revenue): Turner & Townsend combined business. Program management, cost consultancy across real estate, infrastructure, and energy. 20,000+ employees in 60+ countries. Growing at 20%+ CAGR since T&T acquisition.

4. **Real Estate Investments** ($0.88B, 2% of revenue): Investment management ($155B AUM) and development. Lumpy but high-margin.

**Revenue model:** ~75-80% recurring/contractual (BOE + Project Management), ~20-25% transactional (Advisory + REI). This mix has shifted dramatically from 50/50 a decade ago, making CBRE far more resilient through cycles.

**The problem CBRE solves:** Corporations outsource CRE management because it's non-core, complex, and benefits from scale/data. CBRE manages 7.4B+ sq ft globally. Once embedded in a corporate client's operations (FM, project management), switching costs are high (12-18 month transition periods, data migration, relationship disruption).

### Por Que Esta Barata (Relative to Peak)

Stock at $133 vs 52wH $174 = -23.5% from highs.

**Market narrative (why it's down):**
1. **Oil crisis / stagflation fears** → CRE transaction volumes depend on economic confidence. If recession hits, Advisory segment (20% of revenue, ~40% of profits) gets crushed.
2. **Interest rate uncertainty** → Higher-for-longer rates depress CRE valuations, reduce transaction volumes, slow investment activity.
3. **"Real Estate" sector classification** → ETF selling/index rebalancing treats CBRE like a REIT when it's actually an asset-light services company. Passive flows hurt.
4. **Valuation stretched on GAAP metrics** → P/E 34.6x (GAAP) looks expensive. Core P/E 20.9x is more reasonable.

**Mi contra-tesis:**
1. **Recession fear overdone for CBRE specifically:** 75-80% of revenue is recurring/contractual (BOE + PM). Even in 2023 (CRE trough), revenue was flat at $31.9B — did not decline. The cyclical Advisory piece can drop 20-30% but the resilient businesses keep growing.
2. **Rate sensitivity is real but priced in:** CRE transaction volumes already recovering (+16% expected 2026). Cap rate compression beginning. The cycle is turning, not peaking.
3. **Services not REITs:** CBRE doesn't own buildings. It charges fees. Rising rates hurt CRE owners, not CRE service providers (whose clients need more help navigating complex markets).
4. **Secular outsourcing trend:** Corporations continue outsourcing CRE management. CBRE's BOE segment grew 15% in a soft CRE market. Data centers, infrastructure (Turner & Townsend), and sustainability consulting are secular growth drivers.
