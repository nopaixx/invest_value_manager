## Business Understanding

### Business Model

**Problem solved:** Businesses need clean, compliant workwear, facility supplies (first aid, fire protection, restroom), and safety services. Managing in-house is expensive and distracting. Cintas provides it as a recurring managed service.

**Revenue model:** Route-based rental/service. ~80% recurring revenue. Customers sign 3-5 year contracts. Cintas delivers, picks up, launders, and returns uniforms on weekly routes. Cross-sells first aid, safety, restroom, and fire protection on the same route stops -- near-zero incremental delivery cost.

**Revenue breakdown (H1 FY2026):**
- Uniform Rental & Facility Services: $4.28B (77%, +8.3% YoY)
- First Aid & Safety + All Other: $1.28B (23%, +12.8% YoY)

**FY2026 guidance (raised Dec 2025):**
- Revenue: $11.15B - $11.22B (+7.8% to +8.5% YoY)
- EPS: $4.81 - $4.88 (+9.3% to +10.9% YoY)

**Unit Economics:**
- Customer retention: 97% (exceptionally high)
- Contract duration: 3-5 years, 92% renewal rate
- Route density: adding customer to existing route has near-zero incremental delivery cost
- No-programmer TAM: 15M+ US businesses not yet renting uniforms (massive unpenetrated market)
- LTV/CAC: Not disclosed, but 97% retention + multi-year contracts implies very high ratio

### UniFirst Acquisition (NEW - Mar 12, 2026)

**Deal summary:**
- Enterprise value: $5.5B ($310/share, cash + stock)
- UniFirst revenue: ~$2.4B, EBITDA ~$290M (pre-synergy)
- Multiple: 19x EBITDA pre-synergy, 8.0x including $375M synergies
- Expected close: H2 calendar 2026
- Financing: Cash + $2.85B bridge facility (Morgan Stanley, KeyBank, Wells Fargo)
- Croatti family (2/3 voting control) signed support agreement

**Strategic impact:**
- Increases laundry facilities by ~50%
- Adds ~20% to revenue ($2.4B)
- $375M annual synergies within 4 years (route optimization, procurement, facility consolidation)
- EPS accretive by end of Year 2 post-close
- Combined market share: ~36%+ (from ~31%)
- Eliminates #3 competitor, widens gap to remaining competition

**Risks of the deal:**
- Leverage will increase materially (from 1.1x to estimated 2.5-3.0x ND/EBITDA)
- Regulatory risk (antitrust review -- combined share ~36% but market is fragmented enough that approval is likely)
- Integration execution risk -- CTAS has M&A track record but this is their largest deal ever
- Distraction from organic growth execution during 2-3 year integration period

### Why It Is Expensive (NOT cheap)

CTAS trades at:
- P/E: 41.3x (vs S&P 500 ~21x, vs business services ~26x)
- EV/EBIT: 33.8x
- FCF yield: 2.3%

**The market narrative is CORRECT.** The market recognizes CTAS as an exceptional business and prices it accordingly. There is no contra-thesis -- the market is not wrong about the quality. The question is whether the PRICE overshoots fair value for this quality.

The UniFirst acquisition adds a near-term catalyst (synergies) but the stock is already pricing in successful execution. At 8.0x run-rate EBITDA including synergies, the deal itself is accretive -- but the CTAS stock multiple already embeds premium expectations.

### Moat Assessment: WIDE

1. **Route density / scale economies (10/10):** Cintas operates 11,500+ delivery routes across 330+ cities. Adding customers to existing routes has near-zero marginal cost. This creates natural local monopoly economics. With UniFirst, facility count grows ~50%, further densifying the network.

2. **Switching costs (8/10):** Changing uniform provider requires new sizing, new contracts, disrupts daily routines for hundreds of workers. 97% retention confirms switching costs are real and formidable.

3. **Cross-sell platform (4/5):** Route relationship enables cross-selling first aid, safety, fire protection, restroom services on the same truck stop. First Aid segment growing 13%. Competitors offering single category cannot match this integrated proposition.

4. **Scale advantage (5/5):** 4-6x larger than next competitor (now even larger with UniFirst). Superior procurement, technology investment (route optimization AI/analytics), and brand in B2B space.

## Valuation

### Anti-Bullish-Bias Protocol (S202)

Per the protocol, I calculate bear FV FIRST, then base, and weight 60% bear / 40% base.

### Method 1 (Primary, 60%): Owner Earnings Yield

```
FCF FY2025: $1.8B
Depreciation: ~$380M
Maintenance Capex: ~$420M (D&A * 1.1)
Owner Earnings = $1.8B - $420M + $380M = $1.76B

Owner Earnings Yield = $1.76B / $76.9B market cap = 2.3%
Expected sustainable growth: 9%
OEY + Growth = 2.3% + 9% = 11.3%

vs WACC 9.0% = spread of +2.3pp
```

**Interpretation:** The spread is positive but THIN. For Tier A compounders, precedents show we want OEY+Growth - WACC >= 4pp (ADBE at purchase had ~5.4pp spread, BYIT.L similar).

**What price gives 4pp spread?**
- Need OEY >= 4% (4% + 9% = 13% vs 9.0% WACC = 4pp spread)
- OEY 4% = $1.76B / X = $44B market cap
- $44B / ~401M shares = ~$110/share

**OEY-implied Fair Value: $110**

### Method 2 (Secondary, 40%): EV/EBIT Normalized

```
EBIT FY2026E: ~$2.6B (23.5% margin on $11.2B revenue, standalone pre-UniFirst)
Post-UniFirst EBIT (FY2028E, with synergies): ~$3.5B

STANDALONE VALUATION (current):
Sector median EV/EBIT: ~20x for industrials
Quality premium:
  + Superior ROIC (23.9% vs sector ~12%): +3x
  + Wide moat (route density, #1): +2x
  + Above-avg growth (9% vs 5%): +2x
  + Expanding margins: +1x
  = 28x

Bear (22x): EV = $2.6B * 22x = $57.2B → Equity $54.2B → FV $135
Base (28x): EV = $2.6B * 28x = $72.8B → Equity $69.8B → FV $174
```

### Reconciliation (Anti-Bullish-Bias: 60% Bear + 40% Base)

**OEY Method:**
- Bear: $110 (4pp spread target)
- Base: ~$145 (2.5pp spread, moderate)
- Anti-bias weighted: $110 * 0.60 + $145 * 0.40 = $66 + $58 = $124

**EV/EBIT Method:**
- Bear: $135 (22x)
- Base: $174 (28x)
- Anti-bias weighted: $135 * 0.60 + $174 * 0.40 = $81 + $70 = $151

**Final Weighted FV (60% OEY + 40% EV/EBIT):**

|--------|-------------|--------|----------|
| OEY (anti-bias) | $124 | 60% | $74 |

## 🔄 META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The DCF tool dramatically undervalues CTAS ($54-92 range) because the 5-year projection period does not capture the long-duration compounding of route economics. I compensated with EV/EBIT as secondary method. A 10-year DCF projection would give a more realistic value for this type of compounder.
- UniFirst integration risk is hard to quantify. CTAS has M&A experience (G&K Services 2016, $2.2B) but UniFirst at $5.5B is 2.5x larger. The $375M synergy target is aggressive -- depends heavily on route overlap which we cannot verify from public data.
- Receivables growth (13.9%) significantly exceeds revenue growth (7.7%) in FY2025. This could be benign (enterprise customer mix, timing) or early quality deterioration signal. Need to track in Q3/Q4 FY2026 filings.
- The price_checker shows yield 93.0% which is a data error (actual dividend yield ~0.9%). This is a known tool anomaly for CTAS.
- Post-UniFirst leverage will rise to 2.5-3.0x from 1.1x. This temporarily reduces the financial quality profile, but CTAS has strong FCF to deleverage within 2-3 years.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The DCF tool should consider a "compounder mode" with 10-year projections for businesses with QS >=75 and ROIC persistence 10/10. The standard 5-year projection systematically undervalues these companies.
