## Business Understanding

### What United Rentals Does

United Rentals rents construction, industrial, and specialty equipment to contractors, industrial companies, and government entities across North America. The company operates ~1,650 locations and manages a fleet valued at approximately $22B at original cost.

**Revenue mix (2025):**
- General Rentals: ~75% of rental revenue (earthmoving, aerial, general construction)
- Specialty Rentals: ~25% (trench safety, power/HVAC, fluid solutions, mobile storage)
- Equipment sales + services: ~14% of total revenue ($2.3B)
- Rental revenue: 86% of total revenue ($13.8B)

**The core value proposition:** Contractors rent equipment instead of buying because: (a) avoids $200K-$1M+ upfront cost per unit, (b) eliminates maintenance burden, (c) provides flexibility to scale up/down with project needs, (d) access to latest technology/emissions-compliant equipment. URI's scale advantage means better fleet utilization, broader geographic coverage, and superior customer service vs. smaller operators.

### Unit Economics

- **Fleet cost per unit:** ~$150K average (ranges from $20K hand tools to $1M+ cranes)
- **Average rental duration:** ~3-4 months per contract
- **Time utilization:** ~70% (industry standard for large operators)
- **Dollar utilization (fleet productivity):** ~45-50% annually (rental revenue / OEC)
- **Customer retention:** Very high for National Accounts (~50% of revenue); URI's 1,650 locations provide "one-stop shop" nationwide coverage that small operators cannot match
- **EBITDA margin:** 45.5% (FY2025) — exceptional for any industrial business, reflects the inherent operating leverage of a rental model (high fixed costs in fleet, variable revenue per utilization)

### Revenue Model: Recurring with Cyclical Overlay

Equipment rental is NOT subscription, but has subscription-like qualities:
- Projects last 6-24 months, creating predictable utilization windows
- National Account contracts provide visibility
- Specialty rentals (25% and growing) are higher margin, more recurring, less cyclical
- Used equipment sales provide a floor on fleet residual value

## Valuation

### CRITICAL NOTE: DCF Tool Limitation

The standard DCF tool produces $0 fair value because it uses reported FCF ($662M) against a $62B enterprise value. This is STRUCTURALLY WRONG for rental businesses where "capex" is fleet investment. I must use EV/EBITDA as the primary method, supplemented by adjusted FCF analysis.

### Method 1: EV/EBITDA (Primary — 60% weight)

**Normalized EBITDA:** $7.3B (FY2025 actual). 2026E: $7.7B (guidance midpoint).

**Multiple derivation:**
- URI 5-year average EV/EBITDA: ~9-10x
- Current: 62.2B / 7.3B = 8.5x (below historical average)
- Peers: Sunbelt Rentals trades ~8-9x, Herc Rentals ~7-8x
- URI deserves premium for: #1 position, best margins, specialty exposure, scale
- Fair multiple: 9.0x on 2026E EBITDA (conservative, below 5yr average)

**Bear case:** 8.0x on $7.5B EBITDA = $60.0B EV - $15.2B net debt = $44.8B equity / 63.6M shares = **$704**
**Base case:** 9.0x on $7.7B EBITDA = $69.3B EV - $15.2B net debt = $54.1B equity / 63.6M shares = **$851**
**Bull case:** 10.0x on $8.0B EBITDA = $80.0B EV - $15.2B net debt = $64.8B equity / 63.6M shares = **$1,019**

**Anti-bullish-bias FV (60% bear + 40% base):**
FV = 0.60 x $704 + 0.40 x $851 = $422 + $340 = **$763**

### Method 2: Adjusted FCF Yield (Secondary — 40% weight)

**Adjusted FCF:** $2.18B (company-reported, 2025)
**FCF Yield at current price:** $2.18B / $47.0B = 4.6%
**Sustainable growth:** 7% (revenue) + buyback yield ~3% = 10% total return
**Total return:** 4.6% + 10% = 14.6% (attractive for Tier B)

**Valuation via target yield:**
- Bear case: Require 6% FCF yield → MCap = $2.0B / 6% = $33.3B → $524/share
- Base case: Require 5% FCF yield → MCap = $2.2B / 5% = $44.0B → $692/share
- Bull case: Require 4% FCF yield → MCap = $2.4B / 4% = $60.0B → $943/share

**Anti-bullish-bias FV (60% bear + 40% base):**
FV = 0.60 x $524 + 0.40 x $692 = $314 + $277 = **$591** (very conservative)

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The QS adjustment from 47 to 62 (+15 points) is meaningful. The FCF margin distortion is well-documented and quantitative, but I want to flag that the ROIC spread is genuinely thin (+0.8pp on reported, +2.3-2.8pp on normalized WACC). This is NOT a wide-moat business in the traditional sense -- it is a scale-advantage business with high barriers to entry but not pricing power.
- The DCF tool is essentially useless for rental/fleet businesses. This is a known limitation. EV/EBITDA is the standard industry metric and more appropriate.
- Margin compression trend (gross margin down 470bps in 3 years) is real and concerning. If this continues to EBITDA, the thesis weakens materially.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The quality_scorer.py should have an optional `--business-type rental` flag that uses adjusted FCF (after growth capex normalization) instead of reported FCF for scoring. This would prevent systematic underscoring of capital-intensive rental/leasing businesses.
- The dcf_calculator.py should detect when FCF margin is very low (<5%) relative to EBITDA margin (>30%) and flag "RENTAL/CAPEX-INTENSIVE MODEL -- consider EV/EBITDA valuation instead."

### Preguntas para Orchestrator
1. Should we create a dedicated "equipment-rental" or "capital-leasing" sector view? URI + potential Sunbelt/Herc coverage would benefit from a dedicated view rather than lumping into "industrials."
2. The H&E acquisition failed. Herc Rentals (HRI) could be a complementary play with lower valuation. Worth an R1?

### Anomalias Detectadas
- Inventory growth (+20%) significantly outpacing revenue growth (+4.9%) per narrative_checker. For most companies this is a red flag (channel stuffing). For URI, this likely reflects fleet equipment held for sale (used equipment inventory). Should verify this is not a genuine operational concern.
- The reverse DCF implies >50% FCF growth needed to justify the price, which seems absurd. This confirms the tool cannot handle rental business models and should not be cited in committee review.
