# Counter-Analysis: URI (United Rentals, Inc.)

## Fecha: 2026-03-20

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis identifies a legitimate secular trend (own-to-rent shift) and a quality franchise, but materially underweights three risks: (1) the gross margin compression is structural, not temporary, driven by specialty segment degradation and logistics inflation, (2) the ARA industry forecast of only 2.3% rental revenue growth in 2026 directly contradicts the thesis's 6-8% organic growth assumption, and (3) Sunbelt Rentals' NYSE listing in March 2026 creates a newly public, well-capitalized competitor with nearly equal scale. The QS adjustment from 47 to 62 (+15 points) is aggressive -- the thin ROIC-WACC spread (+0.8pp reported) and declining margins are red flags the tool correctly identified. The thesis survives as a reasonable investment idea but the FV of $830 should be reduced to ~$720-750 to reflect margin headwinds and slower industry growth.

---

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "This is NOT a cyclical peak -- demand is supported by STRUCTURAL drivers (IIJA, data centers, reshoring)"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The ARA (American Rental Association) -- the industry's own trade body and most authoritative source -- forecasts equipment rental revenue growth of only 2.3% in 2026, DECELERATING from 3.3% in 2025. The ARA explicitly states that IIJA and CHIPS Act spending "is winding down as the peak investment cycle from that legislation passes." The AIA (American Institute of Architects) Consensus Construction Forecast projects just 1.0% nominal construction spending growth in 2026, which after inflation represents a real decline. Manufacturing construction spending is forecast to DECLINE 3.9% in 2026. The thesis projects 6-8% organic growth for URI vs. 2.3% industry growth -- implying 4-6pp of market share gains annually, which would be unprecedented.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** The committee should reconcile the thesis's 6-8% organic growth assumption with ARA's 2.3% industry forecast. Either URI is taking massive share (needs evidence) or the thesis overstates growth. Adjust revenue projections to 3-5% organic growth (market + modest share gain from specialty).

### 2. "Gross margin decline from 42.9% to 38.2% is manageable -- EBITDA margins stable at 45.5%"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The 470bps gross margin compression over 3 years is NOT just cyclical/temporary. Bernstein specifically flagged this as potentially structural: (1) Specialty margins declined 520bps YoY in Q4 to 40.3%, and specialty is supposed to be the HIGHER-margin segment, (2) delivery costs are rising structurally due to mega-projects being more geographically dispersed, (3) ancillary revenue (lower-margin) is growing 2x faster than core rental. The thesis correctly notes EBITDA margins are more stable (45.5%), but this is partly because SG&A leverage is masking gross margin erosion. If gross margin compression continues at ~150bps/yr, EBITDA margins will eventually follow. Barclays issued a rare SELL rating on Jan 30, 2026, specifically citing margin compression concerns.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Model a scenario where EBITDA margins compress to 43-44% by 2028 (consistent with current gross margin trajectory). This changes the bear case materially.

### 3. "QS adjustment from 47 to 62 (+15 points) is justified by FCF margin distortion"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The FCF margin adjustment (+8 pts) has partial merit -- rental businesses do have structurally higher capex. However, the thesis uses the company's OWN adjusted FCF definition ($2.18B), which separates growth vs. maintenance capex. This is management's number, not independently verified. More importantly: the ROIC adjustment (+2 pts based on normalizing beta from 1.65 to 1.2-1.3) is cherry-picking. URI's business IS cyclical -- the stock dropped 28% from its 52-week high, confirming the market treats it as cyclical. A beta of 1.65 is not "inflated by stock price behavior" -- it IS the stock's behavior, reflecting the underlying business cyclicality. The market position adjustment (+5 pts) is more defensible, but 5 points for being #1 in a fragmented, capital-intensive industry with thin ROIC spreads is generous. Net: I would allow +8-10 pts (FCF + market position), not +15. Adjusted QS: ~55-57, still Tier B but at the low end.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Accept QS 55-57 (low Tier B) and apply the higher MoS precedent for low-Tier-B positions (~20-25%).

### 4. "Management guidance is conservative -- they raised 3x during 2025"

- **Evidencia en contra:** Past conservatism does not predict future conservatism. In 2025, URI benefited from the H&E acquisition closing in Q1 which added ~$2B revenue inorganically. Stripping out H&E, 2025 organic growth was closer to ~2-3%, not the headline 4.9%. The 2026 guidance of $16.8-17.3B (4-7% growth) is now entirely organic, and the ARA's 2.3% industry growth forecast suggests the midpoint may actually be aggressive, not conservative. Q4 2025 EPS already missed consensus ($11.09 vs $11.80 expected), a 6% miss, suggesting the "always conservative" narrative may be breaking down.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Do not assume guidance is sandbagged. Use guidance midpoint ($17.05B) as base case, not a floor.

### 5. "H&E acquisition failure is a non-event"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis is partially correct that URI does not NEED H&E. However, the loss matters for two reasons: (1) URI was willing to pay ~$5B for H&E, suggesting management felt organic growth alone was insufficient to meet targets -- this contradicts the "structural growth" narrative, and (2) Sunbelt Rentals (now NYSE-listed as SUNB since March 2, 2026) is URI's primary competitor with $30B+ market cap, 1,600+ locations, and $19B fleet. Sunbelt's US primary listing gives it improved access to US capital markets for aggressive M&A and fleet expansion. The competitive landscape is intensifying, not stable.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Factor Sunbelt's NYSE listing as a competitive risk. The duopoly narrative is correct, but Sunbelt now has equal capital market access for fleet investment and M&A.
