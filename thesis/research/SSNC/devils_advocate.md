# Devil's Advocate: SSNC (SS&C Technologies)

## Fecha: 2026-03-21

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The SSNC thesis identifies a solid financial infrastructure business with sticky clients, but the fair value determination is problematic. The FA produced an FV of $82 through a convoluted valuation process that departed from the S202 protocol -- the mechanical S202 calculation yielded $63-67, but the FA overrode this to $82 using forward P/E on adjusted EPS. This override is the thesis's most vulnerable point. The +16 QS adjustment (42 to 58, Tier C to Tier B) is the second-largest in our DA history and deserves scrutiny, particularly the ROIC adjustment (+8 points). Additionally, the thesis underweights key man risk (Bill Stone, age ~70, 14% insider), the Morningstar moat downgrade to "none," and the risk that 23% FCF margin in FY2025 represents a spike, not a new baseline (trailing 3yr avg: 19.9%). At current price ($71.38 vs S202-mechanical FV of $63-67), the stock offers marginal MoS at best and is potentially overvalued on a conservative basis.

**Market anchor:** SSNC at $71.38 implies 4.2% FCF growth per year (from reverse DCF). Historical FCF CAGR: 15.9%. The gap of 11.7pp suggests the market prices in growth deceleration. Key question: is 15.9% FCF CAGR sustainable, or was it acquisition-driven and now normalizing to 4-5% organic?

**DA historical stats:** 25 corrections, avg -15.7%, median -13.0%. No outcomes measured yet.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. QS Adjustment of +16 Points (42 to 58, C to B) -- Largest Component: ROIC +8

- **The thesis claim:** ROIC spread of -1.4pp (ROIC 7.6% vs WACC 9.0%) is distorted by $7.8B goodwill. Adjusted ROIC on tangible capital is "substantially higher." Adjustment +8 points (0/15 to 8/15).
- **Evidence against:**
  - Goodwill is not an accounting fiction -- it represents the PRICE PAID for acquisitions. If SS&C paid $7.8B in acquisition premiums over 30 years, that capital is consumed. The return on TOTAL invested capital (including goodwill) IS the economically relevant return, because the capital was spent. Adjusting ROIC for goodwill is equivalent to saying "if I ignore what I paid, the returns look great."
  - Morningstar DOWNGRADED SS&C's moat to "none" in early 2026. While the FA dismisses this as potentially wrong, Morningstar's analysts have access to the same data and reached a different conclusion about the durability of competitive advantage. The moat downgrade undermines the +5 market position adjustment.
  - The FA uses ROE (11.6%) as evidence that "real returns exceed cost of capital." But ROE on heavily leveraged equity is inflated BY the leverage. With 2.8x net debt/EBITDA, ROE is amplified by financial engineering, not operational excellence. An unlevered ROE would be materially lower.
  - FCF yield of 8.2% is cited as evidence of "real economic profit." But FCF yield is a PRICE metric (FCF/market cap), not a QUALITY metric (ROIC). A company can have high FCF yield because the stock is cheap, not because the business generates excess returns.
  - The +16 total adjustment is the joint-largest we have seen in our system. The EEFT DA (S75) caught a +13 adjustment that was reduced. Adjustments this large should face proportionally higher scrutiny.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** The ROIC adjustment should be +4-5 (not +8), reflecting that tangible ROIC is better than total ROIC but that goodwill represents real capital consumed. Market position should be +3-4 (not +5) given the Morningstar moat downgrade. This would produce QS 49-51 (Tier C), not 58 (Tier B). Tier C treatment would require 30-40% MoS, which is nowhere close to current pricing.

### 2. FV of $82 Overrides S202 Protocol

- **The thesis claim:** S202 mechanical FV is $63-67 (the thesis explicitly calculates this). But the FA overrides to $82 using "mid-range of 2026 adj EPS guidance ($6.86) at conservative 12x P/E."
- **Evidence against:**
  - The S202 protocol exists PRECISELY to prevent bullish bias in FV calculations. The FA's own calculation showed S202 FV of $63-67. Overriding to $82 is a +22-30% deviation from the protocol.
  - The thesis shows the FA's internal struggle: the S202 FV "feels appropriately conservative" but is then abandoned because it "seems overly conservative" for this quality level. This is textbook anchoring bias -- the FA wants a higher number and finds reasons to get there.
  - The $6.86 adjusted EPS uses management's NON-GAAP adjustments. GAAP EPS is lower. Using non-GAAP EPS on a P/E multiple compounds the bullish bias.
  - A 12x P/E on adjusted EPS for a company growing organically at 5% with 2.8x leverage is not obviously "conservative." FIS trades at ~12x on similar growth. Broadridge at ~18x but with much lower leverage and higher margins.
  - The DCF base case ($74.89) -- the tool's own output -- is the closest thing to an independent verification, and it is $8 below the FA's FV.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** The committee should use the S202 mechanical FV of $65-67, not the overridden $82. At the mechanical FV, SSNC at $71.38 is ~7-10% OVERVALUED, not undervalued. The entire thesis conclusion changes.

### 3. FCF Margin Spike in FY2025 (23.0% vs 3yr avg 19.9%)

- **The thesis claim:** FCF margin of 23.0% is the new baseline, with modest forward estimate of 22%. Trailing 3yr avg is 19.9%.
- **Evidence against:**
  - A 310bps jump in one year (19.3% to 23.0%) is unusual. The FA's own META-REFLECTION flags this as potentially "working capital timing rather than structural."
  - Using 22% forward FCF margin (which is ABOVE the 3yr average of 19.9%) for a valuation is inherently optimistic, even with the S202 label.
  - If FCF margin reverts to the 3yr average of 20%, FY2026 FCF would be $1.35B (on $6.73B revenue), not $1.48B. This is a 9% reduction in the key valuation input.
  - The Calastone acquisition ($1B) will likely create integration costs in 2026 that could DEPRESS FCF margin temporarily, not expand it.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Use 20% FCF margin (trailing 3yr average) rather than 22-23% for base case valuation. This directly reduces DCF FV by ~8-10%.

### 4. Bill Stone Key Man Risk (Age ~70, No Named Successor)

- **The thesis claim:** KC#3 covers Stone departure. 14% insider ownership is "aligned."
- **Evidence against:**
  - Bill Stone is approximately 70 years old (born ~1955-56, graduated HS 1973). He has been CEO for 40 years since founding in 1986. No named successor has been publicly identified.
  - 14% insider ownership is a double-edged sword: it means Stone IS the company. If he retires, becomes incapacitated, or dies, the company loses its strategic architect AND 14% of the float may be sold by estate or succession holders.
  - The SS&C proxy statement mentions succession planning as a "top priority" but does not name a successor. This is boilerplate corporate governance language.
  - Serial acquirer companies led by founder-CEOs (Danaher/Culp, Constellation Software/Leonard) typically see multiple compression and strategic uncertainty during succession transitions. SS&C's entire M&A strategy (46+ acquisitions) is driven by Stone's judgment. A successor may not have the same acquisition acumen.
  - The thesis lists this as KC#3 but does not discuss the probability or timeline. At age ~70, the actuarial probability of a CEO transition within 5 years is non-trivial.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** This is a genuine risk that should be discussed with more depth. Recommend: check whether SS&C has formal CEO succession plan beyond boilerplate. If not, apply a key-man discount of 5-10% to FV, or require additional MoS.

### 5. Morningstar Moat Downgrade to "None"

- **The thesis claim:** The FA mentions this but dismisses it as "potentially an analytical error" since it contradicts 95%+ retention and 14% insider ownership.
- **Evidence against:**
  - Morningstar's moat methodology is rigorous and consistently applied across thousands of companies. They downgraded SSNC as part of a broader AI-disruption review in early 2026 (along with FactSet). This is not a random error.
  - The AI disruption risk to fund administration is real: if AI can automate NAV calculation, compliance reporting, and investor reporting, SS&C's services component (~55% of revenue, labor-intensive) faces margin compression.
  - 95%+ retention is backward-looking. It tells you about existing contracts, not about pricing power on renewal or new client acquisition costs.
  - The FA's QS adjustment gives +5 for market position and +8 for ROIC despite the moat downgrade. The moat downgrade DIRECTLY contradicts both adjustments.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** The Morningstar moat downgrade should be taken seriously, not dismissed. It does not mean SS&C has zero moat, but it suggests the moat is narrowing, which should reduce the QS adjustment (particularly market position) and increase required MoS.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- The ROIC-ex-goodwill debate is philosophically complex. For serial acquirers, goodwill IS the business model -- they pay premiums to build a platform. Excluding goodwill shows the acquired businesses generate good returns on their tangible assets. Including goodwill shows the total investment returns are mediocre. Both perspectives have validity. The question is which one matters more for FORWARD returns. I lean toward including goodwill because the capital is already spent and cannot be recovered.
- The Morningstar moat downgrade was part of a systematic AI disruption review. Morningstar may be over-weighting AI disruption risk for fund admin (which is more about operational complexity than data processing). But their methodology is rigorous and should not be dismissed without counter-evidence.
- I could not independently verify the 95%+ client retention figure. This is management-reported and not audited in the same way as financial statements.
