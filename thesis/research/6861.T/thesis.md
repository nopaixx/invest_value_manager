## Business Understanding

### Model of Business

**Problem solved:** Manufacturing companies need to detect defects, measure precision, mark products, and automate quality inspection. These are mission-critical functions -- a defective sensor on an automotive line can halt production costing $100K+/hour.

**Revenue model:** Hardware sales (sensors, vision systems, laser markers, measuring instruments, digital microscopes) + direct sales force. No subscription/SaaS model, but extremely high repeat purchase rates because:
1. Products embed into production lines (switching costs)
2. New factory lines require new sensors
3. ~30% of revenue from products launched in the last 3 years (constant innovation cycle)

**Fabless model:** KEYENCE designs and engineers products in-house but outsources 100% of manufacturing to contract manufacturers. This is why gross margins are 84% -- no factory overhead, depreciation, or capex drag.

**Direct sales force (THE moat):** Unlike every major competitor (Cognex uses distributors, Omron uses channel partners, Sick AG uses distributors), KEYENCE employs its own field sales engineers who visit customer sites directly. This creates three structural advantages:
1. **Customer intelligence:** Salespeople record detailed data on customer operations into an internal database. This feeds back directly to R&D, ensuring 70% of new products are "world-first" or "industry-first" innovations.
2. **Margin capture:** Eliminating the distributor layer saves 30-50% on margins. KEYENCE list prices are 10-15% above competitors, but they can discount up to 80% in competitive situations and STILL profit.
3. **Speed of response:** Problems resolved in hours, not days. Customers deeply loyal.

**Geographic mix (FY2025 Q3):**
- Japan: 31% (growing ~0.3% YoY -- mature market)
- Americas: 20% (growing 15.0% YoY -- fastest absolute growth region)
- Asia (ex-Japan): 30% (growing 18.9% YoY -- China ~15% of total)
- Europe & Others: 19% (growing 2.2% YoY -- weakest)
- Overseas total: 69.3% of sales (up from 65.9% YoY)

### Unit Economics

- LTV/CAC: Not publicly disclosed, but proxy metrics are extraordinary. KEYENCE's sales force generates ~JPY 43M revenue per employee (one of the highest in any industry globally). The direct model means CAC is essentially the cost of a sales engineer visit (~1-2 hours) vs LTV of a manufacturing customer who buys sensors for every production line for decades.
- FCF Margin: 37.3% (best-in-class for industrial companies; typical peers 10-15%)
- Capital intensity: Ultra-light. Fabless = capex ~2-3% of revenue (maintenance + R&D facilities only). No factories to maintain.

**Why operating margin declined from 55% to 52% despite gross margin expanding:**
The gap is entirely SG&A-driven. KEYENCE is aggressively investing in international expansion -- hiring and training sales engineers in the Americas (+15% revenue growth) and Asia (+19% revenue growth). SG&A as % of revenue increased from ~27% to ~32% as overseas headcount grows. This is GOOD spending -- it is the investment that drives the international growth engine. As overseas offices mature and revenue per salesperson normalizes, operating margins should stabilize at 50-53% (not return to 55%, as the geographic mix is permanently shifting toward lower-margin international markets).

## Valuation

### Method 1: Owner Earnings Yield (Primary -- Tier A, 60% weight)

```
FCF FY2025: JPY 395B
Depreciation: ~JPY 25B (estimated, asset-light)
Maintenance Capex: JPY 25B * 1.1 = JPY 27.5B
Owner Earnings = JPY 395B - JPY 27.5B + JPY 25B = JPY 392.5B

Market Cap: JPY 59,080 * 242M shares = JPY 14,297B
Net Cash: JPY 1,334B
Enterprise Value = JPY 14,297B - JPY 1,334B = JPY 12,963B

OE Yield = JPY 392.5B / JPY 14,297B = 2.75%
OE Yield (ex-cash) = JPY 392.5B / JPY 12,963B = 3.03%

OEY + Expected Growth = 2.75% + 10% = 12.75%
vs WACC 7.7% = spread +5.05pp
```

**OEY interpretation:** The 2.75% OEY + 10% growth = 12.75% is adequate but not compelling for a Tier A compounder. For context, our precedent HLNE (QS 86) was bought with E[CAGR] 13.1%, and DOCS (QS 79) at 13.8%. KEYENCE at 12.75% is at the lower end of what we've accepted for Tier A.

**Fair Value via OEY (Target 4.0% OEY ex-cash for Tier A compounder with 10% growth):**
- FV = Owner Earnings / Target OEY = JPY 392.5B / 4.0% = JPY 9,812B market cap

## Kill Conditions

1. **Gross margin falls below 78% for 2 consecutive quarters** -- Would signal structural deterioration of the fabless model or severe competitive pricing pressure. Current: 83.8%.
2. **ROIC falls below WACC (7.7%) for 2 consecutive years** -- Would indicate moat erosion. Current: 15.4%.
3. **Founder family (Takizaki) sells more than 5% of holdings** -- 22.8% insider ownership is a core alignment signal. Material selling = loss of alignment. Takizaki stepped down as chairman in 2015 but remains honorary chairman.
4. **China revenue drops >30% YoY without offset from other regions** -- China is 15% of revenue. A forced exit from China (US-China decoupling) would cut ~JPY 160B revenue.
5. **Revenue declines YoY for 2 consecutive fiscal years** -- KEYENCE has never had 2 consecutive years of revenue decline. This would signal secular, not cyclical, issues.
6. **Management deploys cash pile (JPY 1.3T) on value-destructive M&A** -- The cash pile is a POSITIVE for discipline and optionality. A large, dilutive acquisition would change the thesis.
7. **Operating margin falls below 45% for 2 consecutive quarters** -- Would indicate international expansion costs are out of control or competitive dynamics have shifted. Current: 51.9%.
8. **Direct sales model abandoned or materially diluted (e.g., using distributors for >20% of sales)** -- THE moat is the direct sales force. If management shifts to a channel/distributor model, the competitive advantage erodes.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The WACC of 7.7% (using JGB yield as risk-free rate) produces relatively generous valuations. If I used USD-based WACC (~10%), the FV would be substantially lower. Since KEYENCE reports in JPY and we'd hold the JPY-denominated stock, using JGB-based WACC is methodologically correct, but the FX risk (JPY/EUR) is real and not captured.
- Capital allocation remains a wildcard. JPY 1.3T cash (>9% of market cap) is suboptimal. TSE governance reforms COULD force buybacks/dividends, which would be a catalyst. But KEYENCE has resisted for decades. Payout ratio is only 21%.
- The OEY method gives a relatively narrow FV range because both bear and base produce similar numbers. The EV/EBIT method is doing the heavy lifting on scenario differentiation. This makes the weighted FV more dependent on multiple assignment than I'd prefer.
