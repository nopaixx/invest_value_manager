## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

**Problem solved:** Wingstop provides an affordable, flavor-forward chicken wing dining experience through a highly efficient, digital-first franchise system. It solves the "flavor variety at value pricing" need for a primarily delivery/pickup customer base.

**How it generates revenue (FY2025 $696.9M):**

| Revenue Stream | Estimated % | Description |
|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| Royalty revenue | ~45-50% | 6% of franchisee gross sales on 2,586 domestic + 470 international units |
| Advertising fees | ~35-40% | 5.5% of system-wide sales (raised from 5.3% in FY2025) |
| Company-owned restaurants | ~10-12% | ~60 company-owned locations, actual food/labor costs |
| Franchise fees & other | ~3-5% | Initial franchise fees, transfer fees, development fees |

**Key insight:** The FRANCHISOR revenue model means Wingstop collects ~11.5% of every dollar of system-wide sales ($5.3B) as royalties + advertising, with virtually no food cost, labor cost, or real estate exposure. System-wide sales of $5.3B generate $696.9M in Wingstop Inc. revenue -- a ~13% take rate.

### Unit Economics (CRITICAL)

**Franchisee level (the customer's economics):**
- Initial investment: $298K-$1.01M (avg ~$400K)
- Domestic AUV: $2.0M (Q4 2025, down from $2.138M prior year)
- Estimated franchisee cash-on-cash return: ~70% on $400K investment
- Payback period: <2 years
- 95% of new domestic openings from EXISTING franchisees (reinvestment signal)

**Franchisor level (our investment):**
- Revenue per unit (Wingstop Inc.): ~$228K/unit (FY2025 $696.9M / 3,056 units)
- FCF per unit: ~$34.6K/unit ($105.7M / 3,056)
- Incremental unit cost to Wingstop: near zero (franchisee bears all capital)
- Each new unit = incremental royalty stream at zero capital

**LTV/CAC Analysis:**
- CAC (cost to acquire a franchise unit): Near-zero for Wingstop (franchisee pays $20K-$40K initial fee + all buildout costs)
- LTV (lifetime royalty from a unit): Assuming $2.0M AUV x 6% royalty x 20-year life = $2.4M NPV at 10% discount
- LTV/CAC ratio: Effectively infinite from franchisor perspective
- This is the most capital-efficient growth model in QSR

### Structure de Margenes

```
Gross Margin:    48.1% (FY2024, stable trend 47.8%-49.9% over 4 years)
Operating Margin: 26.3% (FY2024, improving trend)
Net Margin:      ~24.9% (FY2025 $174.3M / $696.9M)
FCF Margin:      ~15.2% (FY2025 $105.7M / $696.9M)
Adj EBITDA Margin: ~35.0% (FY2025 $244.2M / $696.9M)
```

Note: Gross margin is ~48% because company-owned restaurant COGS are included. The pure franchise revenue streams (royalties + ad fees) carry near-100% incremental margins. As the company opens more units, the franchise revenue grows while SG&A scales more slowly, creating natural operating leverage.

**FCF Margin trajectory:** 7.4% (2021) -> 14.6% (2022) -> 17.6% (2023) -> 16.9% (2024). The 2024 slight decline reflects higher SBC and capex. Trailing 3yr avg: ~16.4%.

### Capital Requirements

- **Asset-light:** Capex/Revenue = 2.7x depreciation (FY2024), which looks capital-intensive but is misleading -- the actual capex is modest in absolute terms (~$26M on $626M revenue = 4.2%). Much of this is company-owned restaurant maintenance and tech investment (Smart Kitchen rollout).
- **Working capital:** Minimal. Franchise model means Wingstop collects royalties monthly on franchisee sales. No inventory to manage. Negative working capital model (collect before you spend).
- **Share count:** 27.54M diluted shares. $91.3M remaining buyback authorization. SBC ~3.5% of revenue = moderate.

### Digital-First Advantage

- 73.2% of system-wide sales are digital (Q4 2025)
- Smart Kitchen technology deployed across ALL 2,586 domestic locations in 10 months
- Digital ordering = lower labor costs, higher accuracy, better data for marketing
- This is NOT table stakes -- most wing competitors lack this digital penetration
- Digital enables delivery partnerships (DoorDash, UberEats) which expand TAM without adding seats

## Por Que Esta Barata (WHY IS IT CHEAP?)

### Narrative del Mercado

The stock declined from $388.14 (52wH in June 2025) to $204 (52wL in December 2025), a -47% decline. Currently at $246.67, it's -36% from the high. Here's what the market believes:

**1. First same-store sales decline in 22 years (-3.3% domestic, -5.8% in Q4):**
- Market narrative: "The growth story is over. SSS is the most important metric."
- My counter-thesis: SSS decline follows +18.3% (2023) and +19.9% (2024) -- extraordinary comps that were always unsustainable. The 2023-2024 period benefited from post-COVID dining normalization and favorable wing pricing. A -3.3% comp on a +20% prior year is effectively flat on a 2-year stack. Management guides flat to low-single-digit domestic SSS for 2026 -- a normalization, not a collapse.

**2. Revenue miss in Q4 ($175.7M vs $181.1M consensus):**
- Market narrative: "They can't meet expectations anymore."
- My counter-thesis: The revenue miss was driven by the domestic SSS decline, which was already known from Q3. But net income rose 60.3% for the full year, and 493 net new units opened. Unit growth is the primary driver, not SSS.

**3. Valuation concern at 40x P/E:**
- Market narrative: "Even after -36% drop, it's still expensive. Growth stocks are out of favor."
- My counter-thesis: 40x trailing P/E is misleading because it includes one-time items and a large deferred tax benefit in FY2025. On FY2025 adjusted EPS of $4.08, the P/E is ~60x. This IS expensive -- but for a business growing units at 19% with 40% ROIC, terminal value is substantial. The question is whether unit growth sustains, not whether SSS rebounds.

**4. Chicken wing commodity risk:**
- Market narrative: "If wing costs spike again, franchisee economics deteriorate."
- My counter-thesis: WING as franchisor does NOT bear food costs. Franchisees do. Wingstop only collects royalties on gross sales. Higher wing prices actually INCREASE system-wide sales (via menu price increases) and therefore INCREASE royalty revenue, even if franchisee margins compress temporarily. This is a structural hedge. Additionally, wing prices have fallen from $3.22/lb to ~$1.00/lb currently -- near historic lows.

**5. Low insider ownership (0.72%):**
- Market narrative: "Insiders are selling. No skin in the game."
- My counter-thesis: This is a legitimate concern. No concentrated insider ownership post-Roark Capital exit. CEO and management have stock grants but not large personal stakes. This lowers conviction.

## Kill Conditions

1. **KC#1: Domestic SSS negative for 4+ consecutive quarters (structural, not cyclical).** FY2025 had 3 negative quarters (Q2-Q4). If Q1 FY2026 is also negative, this triggers a re-evaluation. Two consecutive years of negative SSS would indicate structural demand destruction.

2. **KC#2: Net new unit openings fall below 300/year (indicating franchisee economics deterioration).** FY2025 was 493. If this drops below 300, it means franchisees are no longer reinvesting, which undermines the entire growth thesis.

3. **KC#3: Domestic AUV falls below $1.7M.** Current $2.0M. A sustained decline to $1.7M would impair franchisee economics (payback period extends beyond 3 years, franchisees stop expanding).

4. **KC#4: Gross margin falls below 44% for 2 consecutive quarters.** Current 48.1%. A 4pp compression would signal structural cost issues or pricing power loss.

5. **KC#5: International unit closures exceed 5% of international base in any year.** Currently 470 international units. If >23 close, the international expansion thesis is in trouble.

6. **KC#6: Interest coverage falls below 4x.** Current 7.9x. The WBS debt structure requires minimum coverage ratios. Below 4x signals potential covenant risk.

7. **KC#7: Existing franchisee reinvestment rate falls below 80%.** Current 95%. This is the most important leading indicator of franchisee economics health.

8. **KC#8: Management abandons the 7,000-unit long-term target.** If they lower this materially (to say 5,000), it signals they see saturation or cannibalization.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The standard DCF framework produces wildly divergent results for WING ($25/share to $310/share) depending on assumptions. This makes me less confident in any point estimate of fair value. The OEY + Growth approach is more appropriate but still requires a growth assumption that is inherently uncertain.
- The 48% gross margin seems low for a 98% franchise model. This is because company-owned restaurant COGS are included. I could not find a clean breakout of franchise-only economics from Wingstop Inc.'s perspective. The pure franchise revenue streams should have near-100% incremental margins, but the overall margin is diluted by ~60 company-owned units.
- The yield anomaly flagged by price_checker (49.0%) is a data error -- likely yfinance misinterpreting special distributions or the payout ratio calculation. Actual yield is ~0.49% ($1.20 annual / $246.67).

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- **DCF tool limitation for high-growth franchisors:** The dcf_calculator.py uses a standard 5-year projection with terminal value. For companies with 10+ year growth runways (like franchise rollouts), a 10-year DCF or explicit two-stage model would be more appropriate. Consider adding a `--years 10` parameter.
- **Quality scorer leverage scoring for franchise models:** WBS (whole-business securitization) debt in franchise models has fundamentally different risk profiles than corporate debt in capital-intensive businesses. The 0/10 leverage score for WING at 4.9x is overly punitive. A franchise-adjusted leverage metric (ND/franchise revenue) would be more meaningful.
