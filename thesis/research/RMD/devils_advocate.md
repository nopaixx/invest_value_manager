## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis presents RMD as an effective CPAP monopolist trading at a modest discount due to misunderstood GLP-1 fears. The core argument -- that GLP-1 is complementary to CPAP -- is built on ResMed's own corporate data, which directly conflicts with independent real-world evidence (Komodo Health) showing 83% fewer CPAP starts among tirzepatide users. The thesis survives on business quality and moat strength, but the GLP-1 risk is materially understated, the receivables/inventory divergence warrants monitoring, and the anti-bias FV of $237 already provides minimal upside at $231. After DA adjustment, the stock is approximately fairly valued at market price.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "GLP-1 is COMPLEMENTARY, not cannibalistic" -- The Thesis's Central Claim

- **What the thesis says:** ResMed's 3-year real-world data shows GLP-1 patients are 11% MORE likely to initiate PAP therapy and have higher resupply rates. Probability market is wrong: 60%.

- **Evidencia en contra (HIGH):**

  **Independent data from Komodo Health (post-FDA approval real-world analysis, Jan 2026):**
  - OSA patients who received tirzepatide initiated CPAP therapy **83% LESS often** than those who did not receive the drug
  - Only **4% of tirzepatide-initiating OSA patients** started CPAP within 6 months, vs **22% of controls**
  - Among existing CPAP users, **26% of tirzepatide users** showed a 6-month gap with no CPAP claims, vs **15% of controls** (75% relative increase in CPAP abandonment)
  - Nearly **30% of newly diagnosed OSA patients** starting tirzepatide did so within 30 days of diagnosis -- GLP-1 is being positioned as FIRST-LINE alternative, not adjunct
  - Tirzepatide uptake among OSA patients increased 16% in first 6 months post-FDA approval

  **JAMA Network Open study (January 2026):**
  - Obese patients with T2D on GLP-1 were **8% less likely** to need CPAP after ~1 year follow-up

  **SURMOUNT trial data:**
  - Mean AHI reduction of -25.3 to -29.3 events/hour (vs -5.3 to -5.5 placebo)
  - Up to **50% of patients** on Zepbound for 1 year improved enough to potentially stop CPAP

  **Critical source analysis:** The thesis relies on ResMed's OWN corporate data (Level 2 source with direct financial incentive) to argue GLP-1 is complementary. The Komodo Health analysis (independent, real-world claims data from post-FDA-approval period) directly contradicts this. ResMed's data may reflect pre-FDA-approval period when GLP-1 for OSA was off-label and rare. Post-approval dynamics have shifted dramatically -- physicians now have a non-device option to offer patients first.

  **Why this matters structurally:** The thesis assigns 60% probability the market is wrong about GLP-1. The independent evidence suggests the opposite -- the market may be UNDERPRICING the GLP-1 risk. If 83% fewer new CPAP starts is the trend, this is not a complementary relationship; it is direct therapeutic substitution for the obese OSA population (~60% of all OSA patients).

- **Severidad:** **HIGH**
- **Resolucion sugerida:** The committee must weigh ResMed's corporate data against independent claims data. The thesis's 60% probability should be revised to 35-40% at best. GLP-1 bear case probability should increase from 25% to 35-40%.

### 2. "Philips re-entry impact is manageable (8-10pp share loss)"

- **What the thesis says:** Philips re-entry will be slow, brand is damaged, 8-10pp share loss modeled. Probability of >15pp share loss: 20%.

- **Evidencia en contra (MODERATE):**

  Respondents in a Jefferies survey expect ResMed's market share to decrease to between 49% and 52% in the next 1-3 years with Philips re-entry. This is consistent with the thesis's 8-10pp loss modeling. However:

  - Philips has shifted to silicone-based materials (addressing the foam issue) and is actively preparing for re-entry in the 2026-2028 window
  - The consent decree process is underway; regulatory path is clearer than 12 months ago
  - Fisher & Paykel Healthcare is also gaining share as a serious #3 competitor, not just Philips
  - The combined effect of Philips returning AND Fisher & Paykel growing could push RMD closer to 45% rather than the thesis's 48%

  **The real issue is TIMING:** The thesis models normalized growth rates (7-9% in FY2028-2029) but the transition period may see a 1-2 year device revenue DECLINE as Philips re-enters with aggressive pricing to rebuild share. The thesis doesn't model a negative device growth quarter scenario.

- **Severidad:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Add a scenario where device revenue growth turns negative for 2-3 quarters during Philips re-entry phase. Adjust base case market share to 46-48% (not 48%).

### 3. FCF Trajectory: Extraordinary Expansion as "Normal"

- **What the thesis says:** FCF margin expanded from 5.5% to 32.1% in 3 years. Uses 28% as conservative base case.

- **Evidencia en contra (MODERATE):**

  - FY2022 FCF of $195M was depressed by supply chain disruption AND the Philips recall windfall was just beginning. Using FY2022 as the base makes the growth look supernatural (103.8% CAGR)
  - The REAL underlying FCF margin trajectory, stripping out the one-time Philips vacuum effect, is probably 18-22% normalized (pre-recall FY2020-2021 FCF margins were in the 15-20% range)
  - As Philips returns and competitive pricing normalizes, FCF margin is more likely to settle at 24-26%, not the thesis's 28%
  - The DCF tool correctly warns: CV=1.6 (FCF highly volatile). Terminal value = 74.5% of EV -- the valuation is extremely sensitive to growth/margin assumptions
  - **Narrative checker confirms:** Receivables growing 12.2% vs revenue 9.8% (+2.4pp divergence). Inventory growing 12.8% vs revenue 9.8% (+3.0pp divergence). Both signal either (a) channel build ahead of demand, (b) Philips-return-related inventory buildup, or (c) modest revenue quality deterioration. Not alarming alone, but combined they suggest FCF margin may face working capital headwinds

- **Severidad:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Use 25% FCF margin for bear case (not 25% as thesis does -- which is already conservative) and 26% for base case (not 28%). The Philips-era margin expansion is one-time, not structural.

### 5. Growth Deceleration Trajectory

- **What the thesis says:** Revenue growth slowed from 18% to 11% to 10%. Market implies 8.1% FCF growth. Projects 8-9% base case revenue CAGR over 5 years.

- **Evidencia en contra (MODERATE):**

  - Revenue growth trajectory: 18% (FY2023) -> 11% (FY2024) -> 9.8% (FY2025) -> Q2 FY2026 at ~11%. The thesis treats the Q2 reacceleration as sustainable, but it may reflect timing of competitive bidding wins and resupply automation gains that are one-time in nature
  - Baird downgraded to Neutral citing "high-single-digit" forward growth -- which is EXACTLY what the thesis models but at 19.6x EV/EBIT, HSD growth is not cheap
  - The CPAP devices market is growing at 8.76% CAGR (Mordor Intelligence). If RMD grows at market rate while losing share to Philips, organic growth could be 6-7%, not 8-9%
  - GLP-1 impact on new CPAP starts (83% reduction per Komodo data) would primarily affect the device segment (~45% of revenue). If new starts drop even 15-20% in the obese OSA population over the next 3-5 years, device revenue growth could approach zero while masks/SaaS carry the load

- **Severidad:** **MODERATE** (compounded with GLP-1 challenge)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Growth projection should be 7-8% base, not 8-9%. The combination of GLP-1 new-start reduction + Philips re-entry share loss + market growth = net 6-8% revenue CAGR, not 8-9%.

### 7. Receivables and Inventory Divergence

- **What the thesis says:** The thesis META-REFLECTION notes receivables growing 12.2% vs revenue 9.8% as "worth monitoring" and suggests "timing of large DME orders" as explanation.

- **Evidencia en contra (HIGH):**

  The thesis header mentions +43.7% receivables growth vs +30.7% revenue growth, which is a different (larger) divergence than the narrative_checker's 12.2% vs 9.8%. Let me reconcile:
  - Narrative checker shows the annual divergence (12.2% vs 9.8% = 2.4pp gap)
  - The thesis header figure may reference quarterly or TTM data
  - Additionally, inventory is growing 12.8% vs revenue 9.8% (3.0pp gap)
  - Combined receivables + inventory growing faster than revenue is a CLASSIC early warning of revenue quality issues

  While operating cash flow remains strong (OCF/NI = 1.3x), the divergence pattern is worth flagging because:
  - If it persists for 2+ quarters, it signals channel stuffing or demand softening
  - The Philips re-entry preparation could involve inventory build (legitimate) but receivables build is harder to explain away
  - In context of GLP-1 headwinds on new starts, growing receivables could mean longer collection cycles from DMEs facing their own demand questions

- **Severidad:** **HIGH** (combined with broader demand uncertainty)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Add KC-8: "Receivables/Revenue divergence persists >2pp for 3+ consecutive quarters." Monitor in next earnings.
