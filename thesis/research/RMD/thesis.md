## Business Understanding

### What ResMed Does

ResMed designs, manufactures, and sells medical devices and cloud-based SaaS solutions for the treatment of sleep-disordered breathing (primarily obstructive sleep apnea/OSA), COPD, and other chronic respiratory conditions. The core product is CPAP (Continuous Positive Airway Pressure) devices and masks.

**Problem solved:** OSA affects ~1 billion people globally, with ~80% undiagnosed. Untreated OSA causes hypertension, stroke, heart failure, diabetes, and cognitive decline. CPAP is the gold-standard first-line treatment for moderate-to-severe OSA.

**Revenue model:**
- **Devices (flow generators):** ~45% of revenue. One-time sale of CPAP/BiPAP machines. Replacement cycle 3-5 years.
- **Masks & accessories:** ~40% of revenue. HIGH recurring -- masks replaced every 3-6 months. Resupply programs drive repeat purchases. Growing 16% YoY in Americas Q2 FY2026.
- **Software/SaaS:** ~15% of revenue. AirView (clinician platform, 22M+ patients monitored), myAir (patient app), Brightree (HME/DME management software). Recurring, high-margin.

**Revenue mix (FY2025 ~$5.1B):**
- Americas: ~57%
- Rest of World: ~43%
- Recurring/resupply: estimated 55-60% of total revenue

### Unit Economics

- **Customer acquisition:** Primarily through sleep labs, pulmonologists, and primary care physicians. Patients are "prescribed" RMD devices -- low direct CAC.
- **LTV/CAC:** Extremely favorable. A CPAP patient generates: device sale ($500-1,500) + 5-10 years of mask resupply ($200-400/year) + software SaaS fees. Estimated LTV $3,000-5,000 per patient.
- **Payback:** Immediate on device sale; resupply is pure margin.

### Why It's Cheap (-21% from 52wH)

**Market narrative (bear case):**

1. **GLP-1 drugs will kill CPAP demand.** Zepbound (tirzepatide) FDA-approved for moderate-to-severe OSA in obese adults. Studies show 50% of patients on Zepbound for 1 year improved enough to potentially stop CPAP. Market fears a secular decline in CPAP addressable market.

2. **Philips returning to US market.** After 4+ years out of the US CPAP market due to the foam degradation recall, Philips is expected to re-enter in late 2026-2027. Analysts estimate RMD could lose ~1,000bps of market share (from ~55-60% back to ~45-50%).

3. **Growth deceleration.** Revenue growth slowed from 18% (FY2023) to 11% (FY2024) to 10% (Q2 FY2026). Baird downgraded to Neutral citing "high-single-digit" forward growth.

4. **Short interest elevated.** 9.4% of float short, 9.2 days to cover, increasing MoM (+6.4%). Bears are actively betting against.

**My contra-thesis:**

1. **GLP-1 is COMPLEMENTARY, not cannibalistic.** ResMed's own 3-year real-world data (presented at major conferences): patients on GLP-1 are 11% MORE likely to initiate PAP therapy and have HIGHER resupply rates (+3.1% year 1, +6.2% year 3). Why? GLP-1 patients are health-motivated, see doctors more often, and get diagnosed with OSA they didn't know they had. The "GLP-1 kills CPAP" narrative ignores that: (a) 80% of OSA is undiagnosed -- GLP-1 prescriptions trigger weight/health assessments that FIND new OSA patients, (b) GLP-1 addresses obesity-related OSA but not the ~40% of OSA patients who are not obese, (c) CPAP remains first-line treatment per medical guidelines even WITH GLP-1, (d) GLP-1 adherence drops significantly after year 1 (weight regain = OSA return). **Probability market is wrong: 60%.**

2. **Philips return is slow and damaged.** Philips has been out of the US for 4+ years. Their brand is severely damaged (15M unit recall, FDA consent decree, class action lawsuits, $1.1B+ in settlements). Even when they re-enter: (a) patients and physicians have switched to RMD and built workflows around AirView/myAir, (b) Philips must rebuild distribution and physician trust from scratch, (c) DME providers have retooled for RMD, (d) Philips' re-entry will take 2-3 years to meaningfully impact share. RMD may lose 5-10pp share but from a monopoly position -- they'll still be dominant. **Probability of material share loss (>15pp): 20%.**

3. **Growth deceleration is priced in at 8% implied.** The reverse DCF shows the market implies only 8.1% FCF growth -- below even the decelerated 10% revenue growth. The market is pricing in bear-case growth as base case. If RMD delivers even mid-single-digit growth with margin stability, it's undervalued.

## Moat Assessment: WIDE

### Moat Sources

1. **Installed Base Lock-in (PRIMARY):** >15M connected devices in the field running AirView cloud platform. Physicians manage patients through AirView -- switching to a competitor means retraining clinical workflows, losing patient data history, and disrupting care protocols. This is the CPAP equivalent of EHR switching costs.

2. **Regulatory Barriers:** FDA 510(k) clearance required for new CPAP devices. Philips recall shows regulatory risk is REAL -- new entrants face years of testing and compliance. ResMed's clean regulatory record is a competitive asset.

3. **Resupply Ecosystem:** myAir app (patient engagement), Brightree (DME provider software), resupply automation = patients automatically receive replacement masks. Once in the ecosystem, patient lifetime value is captured through multi-year mask purchases. This is a razor/blade model with digital lock-in.

4. **Scale & Distribution:** Global manufacturing, established DME/HME distribution partnerships, physician relationships built over 30+ years. New entrant (or returning Philips) must rebuild this from scratch.

5. **Data Moat (Emerging):** 15M+ connected devices generating real-world sleep data. AI-powered adherence tools (Dawn AI assistant). This data advantage compounds over time -- no competitor has this scale of clinical data.

### Moat Durability

- Switching costs: HIGH (5+ years to rebuild physician workflows)
- Network effects: MODERATE (more data = better AI tools = better outcomes = more prescriptions)
- Scale advantages: HIGH (global manufacturing, regulatory compliance infrastructure)
- Brand: MODERATE-HIGH (strong among physicians, less among consumers but that's irrelevant in a prescribed market)

**ROIC Persistence:** 19.3% -> 16.3% -> 19.5% -> 23.7% over 4 years. ROIC > WACC in ALL years. Spread WIDENING (+14.4pp latest). This is textbook moat evidence.

## Risk Assessment

### High Impact Risks

1. **GLP-1 class expansion (MEDIUM probability, HIGH impact):** If next-gen GLP-1s (or combinations) show >80% OSA resolution rate in large trials, medical guidelines could shift away from CPAP as first-line. Current data suggests this is 5+ years away, but drug development can accelerate. **Mitigation:** RMD's own pivot to "GLP-1 as acquisition channel" strategy; SaaS business less device-dependent.

2. **Philips US re-entry (HIGH probability, MEDIUM impact):** Philips WILL return. The question is timing and market share recapture. Modeling 8-10pp share loss over 2-3 years. RMD's installed base, data ecosystem, and physician loyalty provide structural defense. **Mitigation:** Entry price accounts for normalization to ~48% share.

### Medium Impact Risks

3. **Tariff/trade war impact:** RMD manufactures in Singapore, Malaysia, Australia, and US. New tariffs on medical devices could compress margins. 70%+ of revenue is US -- mostly domestically manufactured. **Mitigation:** Diversified manufacturing; medical device exemptions historically likely.

4. **Reimbursement cuts:** CMS/Medicare could reduce CPAP reimbursement rates. This would pressure DME providers who pass cost pressure to RMD. **Mitigation:** CPAP is cost-effective vs untreated OSA comorbidities; strong health-economic evidence base.

5. **Technology disruption from Inspire Medical:** Hypoglossal nerve stimulation (Inspire) is an implantable alternative to CPAP for patients who can't tolerate masks. Growing but still niche (<5% of treatment market). **Mitigation:** Different patient population (CPAP-intolerant); RMD's AirFit masks have dramatically improved comfort/compliance.

### Low Impact Risks

6. **FX headwinds:** 43% revenue outside Americas. USD strength compresses reported revenue. **Mitigation:** Diverse geographic exposure; local manufacturing.
