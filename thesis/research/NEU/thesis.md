## Business Understanding

### What NewMarket Does

NewMarket Corporation operates through two segments:

**1. Petroleum Additives (93% of revenue via Afton Chemical)**
Afton Chemical develops and manufactures additives that are blended into petroleum products (engine oils, transmission fluids, gear oils, fuels, industrial lubricants). These chemical packages are essential -- without them, engines and transmissions would fail within weeks. The market is a tight oligopoly: Lubrizol (#1, Berkshire Hathaway), Afton Chemical (#2, NewMarket), Infineum (#3, ExxonMobil/Shell JV), and Chevron Oronite (#4). Together, these four control ~85% of the global lubricant additives market.

**2. Specialty Materials (7% of revenue via AMPAC + Calca)**
- AMPAC: Sole North American manufacturer of ammonium perchlorate (AP), the critical oxidizer in solid rocket propellant. Customers include DoD and NASA. $100M capacity expansion underway, +50% capacity by end 2026.
- Calca Solutions (acquired Oct 2025, >$200M): Leading US manufacturer of UltraPure hydrazine, used in satellite/spacecraft propulsion. ~30 employees, <$100M revenue. Defense + space critical.

### Competitive Advantage / Moat Analysis

**Moat Type: Narrow-to-Wide (Oligopoly + Switching Costs + Regulatory)**

1. **Oligopoly Structure:** Four players control ~85% of global lubricant additives. No new entrant in 30+ years. The last meaningful competitive change was Infineum's formation in 1999 as ExxonMobil/Shell JV. Barriers:
   - R&D investment: $130M/yr for Afton alone; formulating additive packages requires decades of proprietary data
   - OEM approval cycles: 12-24 months per specification
   - Regulatory compliance: EPA, REACH, global environmental standards
   - Scale: blending and distribution infrastructure across 6 continents

2. **Switching Costs:** Once an OEM approves an additive package for an engine specification, switching mid-lifecycle is extremely costly. The additive maker is embedded in the product design. Engine oil changes happen with the same additive package for the entire engine generation (5-7 years).

3. **ROIC Persistence:** ROIC has exceeded WACC for the entire observable history. This IS the hallmark of a durable moat.

4. **Gross Margin Premium:** +6.5pp vs specialty chemicals sector median, and EXPANDING (23% to 31.5% in 3 years). This indicates pricing power, not commodity competition.

5. **AMPAC Monopoly:** Sole North American manufacturer of ammonium perchlorate. Literal single-source supplier to the DoD. This is arguably a WIDER moat than petroleum additives.

**Moat Risk:** EV transition is the long-term erosion vector. But the moat is not being breached by competitors -- it's that the addressable market SLOWLY shrinks. This is critical distinction: the business remains highly profitable during the decline, generating massive FCF for reallocation (as management is already doing with AMPAC/Calca).
