## Business Understanding

### What NewMarket Does

NewMarket Corporation operates through two segments:

**1. Petroleum Additives (93% of revenue via Afton Chemical)**
Afton Chemical develops and manufactures additives that are blended into petroleum products (engine oils, transmission fluids, gear oils, fuels, industrial lubricants). These chemical packages are essential -- without them, engines and transmissions would fail within weeks. The market is a tight oligopoly: Lubrizol (#1, Berkshire Hathaway), Afton Chemical (#2, NewMarket), Infineum (#3, ExxonMobil/Shell JV), and Chevron Oronite (#4). Together, these four control ~85% of the global lubricant additives market ($19B TAM in 2025).

**2. Specialty Materials (7% of revenue via AMPAC + Calca)**
- AMPAC (acquired Jan 2024 for ~$700M): Sole North American manufacturer of ammonium perchlorate (AP), the critical oxidizer in solid rocket propellant. Customers include DoD and NASA. Board approved $100M capacity expansion (+50% capacity by end 2026).
- Calca Solutions (acquired Oct 2025 for ~$200M+): Leading US manufacturer of UltraPure hydrazine, used in satellite/spacecraft propulsion. Defense + space critical.
- Combined ~$1B committed to specialty materials since 2024.

Revenue is predominantly B2B, contract-based with major oil companies and lubricant blenders. Customers include Shell, ExxonMobil, BP, Castrol, Valvoline, and hundreds of smaller blenders. Switching costs are HIGH: each additive package must be reformulated and tested for specific OEM specifications (12-24 months). Once approved by an OEM (e.g., GM, Toyota), the spec is locked in for that engine generation (5-7 years).

### Competitive Advantage / Moat Analysis

**Moat Type: Narrow-to-Wide (Oligopoly + Switching Costs + Regulatory)**

1. **Oligopoly Structure:** Four players control ~85% of global lubricant additives. No new entrant in 30+ years. Last meaningful competitive change was Infineum's formation in 1999 (ExxonMobil/Shell JV). Barriers:
   - R&D investment: $130M/yr for Afton alone; formulating additive packages requires decades of proprietary data
   - OEM approval cycles: 12-24 months per specification
   - Regulatory compliance: EPA, REACH, global environmental standards
   - Scale: blending and distribution infrastructure across 6 continents

2. **Switching Costs:** Once OEM approves additive package for engine spec, switching mid-lifecycle is extremely costly. Additive maker is embedded in product design. Locked in for entire engine generation (5-7 years).

3. **ROIC Persistence:** ROIC has exceeded WACC for the entire observable history. Hallmark of durable moat.

4. **Gross Margin Premium:** +6.5pp vs specialty chemicals sector median, EXPANDING. Indicates pricing power, not commodity competition.

5. **AMPAC Monopoly:** Sole North American manufacturer of ammonium perchlorate. Literal single-source supplier to DoD. Arguably WIDER moat than petroleum additives.

**Moat Risk:** EV transition is the long-term erosion vector. But the moat is not being breached by competitors -- the addressable market SLOWLY shrinks. Critical distinction: business remains highly profitable during decline, generating massive FCF for reallocation.
