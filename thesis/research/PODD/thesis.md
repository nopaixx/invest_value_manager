## Business Understanding

### What Problem Does Insulet Solve?

Insulet solves the daily burden of insulin delivery for people with diabetes (Type 1 and increasingly Type 2). Traditional insulin delivery requires either multiple daily injections (MDI) or tethered insulin pumps with tubing. Both impose friction, stigma, and compliance challenges. Omnipod is a small, tubeless, waterproof pod worn on the body that delivers insulin automatically -- the only FDA-cleared tubeless automated insulin delivery (AID) system in the world.

### Revenue Model

**Consumable/razor-razorblade model (highly recurring):**
- Revenue comes overwhelmingly from Omnipod pods (disposable, replaced every 3 days)
- One-time controller sale (Omnipod 5 uses smartphone app, reducing hardware cost)
- ~95%+ recurring revenue from pod replacement
- Pharmacy channel distribution (vs traditional durable medical equipment) -- lower friction, faster adoption, insurance-friendly

**FY2025 Revenue: $2.7B (+30.7% YoY)**
- U.S. Omnipod: ~$1.65B
- International Omnipod: ~$1.05B (+46.5% reported, +39.9% CC)
- Drug Delivery (legacy): minimal

### Unit Economics

- 600,000+ active Omnipod users globally
- Revenue per user: ~$4,500/year (pods replaced every 3 days = ~122 pods/year)
- Gross margin per pod: ~72% (and expanding)
- Customer acquisition: pharmacy channel makes acquisition cheaper than DME channel
- Retention: very high -- once a patient is on Omnipod and integrated with CGM, switching requires physician involvement and new training
- LTV/CAC: estimated >10x given multi-year usage and high retention

### Margin Structure & Trajectory

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026E |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| Revenue ($B) | 1.30 | 1.69 | 2.06 | 2.70 | 3.30-3.35 |
| Gross Margin | 61.7% | 68.3% | 69.8% | 71.6% | ~72.5% |
| Operating Margin | 2.9% | 13.0% | 14.9% | 17.5% | ~18.5% |
| FCF Margin | -2.9% | 2.2% | 14.3% | 12.9% | ~14-15% |

**Key observation:** Gross margin has expanded 1,000bps in 3 years (61.7% to 71.6%). Operating leverage is powerful -- opex grows slower than revenue. Operating margin went from 2.9% to 17.5% in 3 years. This trajectory has significant room to continue as the company scales.

### Capital Requirements

- Capex/Depreciation: 2.4x in FY2025 (investing heavily in manufacturing capacity)
- R&D: 11.1% of revenue (down from 13.8% in FY2022 -- R&D leverage)
- SBC: 2.3% of revenue (declining, reasonable)
- Working capital: receivables grew 43.7% vs 30.7% revenue growth -- watch this
- Goodwill: only 1.6% of assets (no acquisition-driven bloat)
- Net cash position possible by end of FY2026 given FCF trajectory

## Why Is It Cheap? (43% Below 52wH, AT 52-Week Low)

### Market Narrative

1. **Omnipod 5 recall (March 13, 2026):** Voluntary correction for ~1.5% of pod production due to internal tubing defect causing insulin leakage. 18 serious adverse events (DKA hospitalizations, no deaths). Stock dropped ~7% on disclosure. Cost: ~$40M in FY2026.

2. **Securities fraud lawsuit (March-April 2026):** Multiple law firms (Levi & Korsinsky, Pomerantz) investigating whether management knew about the defect before the March 12 disclosure. CEO McEvoy's Feb 18 earnings call comments ("reliability, consistency and broad appeal") are under scrutiny. No class certification yet.

3. **Deceleration fear:** Guidance for FY2026 at 20-22% CC growth vs 30.7% in FY2025. The "growth deceleration" narrative scares momentum investors even though 20%+ sustained growth is exceptional.

4. **GLP-1 overhang:** Market fears GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic, Mounjaro) will reduce need for insulin pumps. This is the most persistent bear narrative.

5. **Valuation compression:** Stock went from $355 (Sep 2025) to $203 -- multiple compression from ~80x to ~34x trailing P/E as growth expectations moderate.

6. **Rising short interest:** 6.1% of float short as of March, up 45.8% MoM. Bears pressing.

### My Counter-Thesis

**On the recall:** This is a manageable manufacturing issue affecting 1.5% of production. $40M cost is <1.5% of revenue. Medical device recalls are common in medtech -- Medtronic has had far worse with MiniMed. This is a lot-level issue (specific production run), not a design flaw. Management has replacement program in place and expects no disruption to new patient starts.

**On the securities fraud lawsuit:** These are standard ambulance-chaser filings. Levi & Korsinsky files these routinely after any significant stock drop + news event. Key facts: (1) no evidence of concealment has been presented, only speculation about timeline; (2) no class certification; (3) CEO McEvoy BOUGHT $1.03M of stock at $239 on Feb 20, two days after earnings -- this is INCONSISTENT with knowing about a concealed defect. Per our 10b5-1 Verification Protocol: this was an open-market discretionary purchase, not a pre-scheduled plan. A CEO buying $1M while knowingly concealing a defect that would tank the stock would be securities fraud itself. The buy signal is POSITIVE, not neutral.

**On deceleration:** 20%+ CC growth for 10 consecutive years is extraordinary. The company's own long-term guidance (2025-2028) is ~20% CC revenue CAGR with 25%+ EPS CAGR. This is normalization, not deceleration.

**On GLP-1:** GLP-1s are COMPLEMENTARY, not cannibalistic:
- GLP-1 drugs do not cure diabetes -- they delay progression but don't stop beta cell decline
- Available for T2D since 2005 -- 20 years of coexistence
- >40% of new US Omnipod starts are now T2D patients -- FASTEST T2D adoption ever during GLP-1 peak
- GLP-1s raise awareness of diabetes management, driving more patients into active treatment
- RMD's data confirmed GLP-1 is complementary for sleep -- same dynamic applies

**On valuation:** The compression from 80x to 34x trailing P/E is healthy. At ~27x FY2026E earnings for a 20%+ grower with expanding margins, it's approaching fair value.

## Competitive Advantage / Moat Analysis

### Moat Sources (WIDE)

**1. Product Differentiation -- Only Tubeless AID System (PRIMARY MOAT)**
Omnipod is the ONLY FDA-cleared tubeless automated insulin delivery system in the world. No tubing = less stigma, waterproof, simple 3-day pod changes, smartphone-controlled.

**2. Regulatory Barriers**
FDA process takes years. Omnipod 5 algorithm enhancements (approved Dec 2025) continually improve. International approvals in 19+ countries. Tandem "Sigi" not expected until late 2026/2027.

**3. Ecosystem Lock-in**
Integration with Dexcom G7, Abbott Libre CGMs. Physician prescribing habits. Pharmacy channel infrastructure. Patient data history.

**4. Switching Costs**
New prescriptions, retraining, insurance re-authorization, emotional attachment to working glucose management.

**5. Scale Advantages**
600K+ active users, manufacturing scale (GM 62% to 72% in 3 years), R&D leverage (11% declining), international infrastructure in 19+ countries.

### Moat Durability

**Primary threat:** Tandem "Sigi" patch pump (late-stage development, launch late 2026/2027). First credible threat to tubeless monopoly. However: 600K+ installed base, physician familiarity, historically very low pump switching rates (10-15% annual churn). New entrants may expand TAM more than steal share.

**Moat verdict:** WIDE, with some narrowing expected 2027+ as Tandem enters tubeless.
