## Business Understanding

### What Problem Does Insulet Solve?

Insulet solves the daily burden of insulin delivery for people with diabetes (Type 1 and increasingly Type 2). Traditional insulin delivery requires either multiple daily injections (MDI) or tethered insulin pumps with tubing. Both impose friction, stigma, and compliance challenges. Omnipod is a small, tubeless, waterproof pod worn on the body that delivers insulin automatically -- the only FDA-cleared tubeless automated insulin delivery (AID) system in the world.

### Revenue Model

**Consumable/razor-razorblade model (highly recurring):**
- Revenue comes overwhelmingly from Omnipod pods (disposable, replaced every 3 days)
- One-time controller sale (Omnipod 5 uses smartphone app, reducing hardware cost)
- ~95%+ recurring revenue from pod replacement
- Pharmacy channel distribution (vs traditional durable medical equipment) -- lower friction, faster adoption, insurance-friendly

**FY2025 Revenue: $2.7B (+30.7% YoY)**
- U.S. Omnipod: ~$1.65B
- International Omnipod: ~$1.05B (+46.5% reported, +39.9% CC)
- Drug Delivery (legacy): minimal

### Unit Economics

- 600,000+ active Omnipod users globally
- Revenue per user: ~$4,500/year (pods replaced every 3 days = ~122 pods/year)
- Gross margin per pod: ~72% (and expanding)
- Customer acquisition: pharmacy channel makes acquisition cheaper than DME channel
- Retention: very high -- once a patient is on Omnipod and integrated with CGM, switching requires physician involvement and new training
- LTV/CAC: estimated >10x given multi-year usage and high retention

### Margin Structure & Trajectory

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026E |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| Revenue ($B) | 1.30 | 1.69 | 2.06 | 2.70 | 3.30-3.35 |
| Gross Margin | 61.7% | 68.3% | 69.8% | 71.6% | ~72.5% |
| Operating Margin | 2.9% | 13.0% | 14.9% | 17.5% | ~18.5% |
| FCF Margin | -2.9% | 2.2% | 14.3% | 12.9% | ~14-15% |

**Key observation:** Gross margin has expanded 1,000bps in 3 years (61.7% to 71.6%). Operating leverage is powerful -- opex grows slower than revenue. Operating margin went from 2.9% to 17.5% in 3 years. This trajectory has significant room to continue as the company scales.

### Capital Requirements

- Capex/Depreciation: 2.4x in FY2025 (investing heavily in manufacturing capacity)
- R&D: 11.1% of revenue (down from 13.8% in FY2022 -- R&D leverage)
- SBC: 2.3% of revenue (declining, reasonable)
- Working capital: receivables grew 43.7% vs 30.7% revenue growth -- watch this
- Goodwill: only 1.6% of assets (no acquisition-driven bloat)
- Net cash position possible by end of FY2026 given FCF trajectory

## Why Is It Cheap? (33% Below 52wH)

### Market Narrative

1. **Omnipod 5 recall (March 2026):** Voluntary correction for ~1.5% of pod production due to internal tubing defect. 18 serious adverse events (DKA hospitalizations, no deaths). Stock dropped ~7% on the news. Cost: ~$40M in FY2026.

2. **Deceleration fear:** Guidance for FY2026 at 20-22% CC growth vs 30.7% in FY2025. The "growth deceleration" narrative scares momentum investors even though 20%+ sustained growth is exceptional.

3. **GLP-1 overhang:** Market fears GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic, Mounjaro) will reduce need for insulin pumps. This is the most persistent bear narrative.

4. **Valuation compression:** Stock went from $355 (Sep 2025) to $225 -- pure multiple compression from ~80x to ~45x trailing P/E as growth expectations moderate.

5. **Rising short interest:** 6.1% of float short, up 45.8% MoM. Bears are pressing.

### My Counter-Thesis

**On the recall:** This is a manageable manufacturing issue affecting 1.5% of production. $40M cost is <1.5% of revenue. Medical device recalls are common in medtech -- the key is whether it indicates systemic quality problems. Given this is a specific lot issue (not a design flaw), it is TEMPORARY. Management has replacement program in place.

**On deceleration:** 20%+ CC growth for 10 consecutive years is extraordinary. The company's own long-term guidance (2025-2028) is ~20% CC revenue CAGR with 25%+ EPS CAGR. This is not deceleration -- it's normalization from an unusually strong 2025. The T2D expansion provides a new growth leg.

**On GLP-1:** This is the most nuanced risk. The evidence suggests GLP-1s are COMPLEMENTARY, not cannibalistic:
- GLP-1 drugs do not cure diabetes -- they delay progression to insulin but don't stop beta cell decline
- GLP-1 drugs have been available for T2D since 2005 -- 20 years of coexistence with insulin therapy
- >40% of new US Omnipod starts are now T2D patients -- the GLP-1 narrative coincides with Insulet's FASTEST T2D adoption ever
- Insulet's TAM actually EXPANDS because GLP-1s raise awareness of diabetes management, driving more patients into active treatment including pumps
- RMD's data (different device category but similar dynamic) showed GLP-1 patients are MORE likely to initiate device therapy

**On valuation:** The compression from 80x to 45x trailing P/E is healthy. At ~36x FY2026E earnings for a 20%+ grower, it's closer to fairly valued but not cheap enough for adequate MoS.

## Competitive Advantage / Moat Analysis

### Moat Sources (WIDE)

**1. Product Differentiation -- Only Tubeless AID System (PRIMARY MOAT)**
Omnipod is the ONLY FDA-cleared tubeless automated insulin delivery system in the world. This is not a minor feature -- it fundamentally changes the user experience:
- No tubing = less stigma, easier to conceal
- Waterproof = exercise, swimming without concern
- Simple pod change every 3 days vs complex infusion set changes
- Smartphone-controlled (Omnipod 5) = no separate device to carry

**2. Regulatory Barriers**
- FDA 510(k)/PMA process takes years for new entrants
- Omnipod 5 algorithm enhancements (approved Dec 2025) continually improve the product
- International regulatory approvals in 19+ countries create cumulative advantage
- Tandem's "Sigi" (nearest tubeless competitor) not expected until late 2026/2027

**3. Ecosystem Lock-in**
- Integration with Dexcom G7, Abbott Libre 2+/3+ CGMs
- Physician prescribing habits and training
- Pharmacy channel infrastructure (unique to Insulet)
- Patient data and outcomes history in the Omnipod ecosystem

**4. Switching Costs**
- Patients must get new prescriptions, train on new system
- Physicians must learn new device workflows
- Insurance formulary/prior authorization process
- Emotional attachment after successful glucose management

**5. Scale Advantages**
- 600K+ active users generating recurring revenue
- Manufacturing scale driving GM from 62% to 72% in 3 years
- R&D amortized over growing revenue base (11% of rev, declining)
- International infrastructure in 19+ countries

### Moat Durability Assessment

**Primary threat:** Tandem "Sigi" patch pump (late-stage development, potential launch late 2026/2027). This is the first credible threat to Insulet's tubeless monopoly. However:
- First-mover advantage of 600K+ users and physician familiarity is substantial
- Tandem must prove equivalent or superior glucose outcomes
- Tandem's track record in hardware manufacturing is weaker
- Insulin pump switching rates are historically very low (10-15% annual churn)

**Secondary threat:** Medtronic diabetes spin-off could increase competitive intensity, but Medtronic's pump reputation suffered from MiniMed quality issues.

**Moat verdict:** WIDE, with some narrowing expected 2027+ as Tandem enters tubeless. But installed base, pharmacy channel, and ecosystem advantages provide durable differentiation.

## Kill Conditions

1. **Omnipod 5 quality escalation:** If recall expands beyond 1.5% of production, or if additional design-level (not lot-level) defects emerge, indicating systemic manufacturing quality degradation. Threshold: second recall within 12 months OR cumulative >5% of production affected.

2. **Tandem Sigi launch success:** If Tandem's tubeless pump launches and captures >10% market share within 12 months, demonstrating Insulet's tubeless moat is narrower than assumed. Monitor Tandem earnings for Sigi revenue breakout.

3. **T2D adoption stall:** If T2D share of new US starts falls below 30% for 2 consecutive quarters (vs >40% current), indicating the TAM expansion thesis is failing. This would fundamentally reduce the growth runway.

4. **Revenue growth below 15%:** If CC revenue growth drops below 15% for 2 consecutive quarters before 2028, indicating structural deceleration beyond guidance normalization.

5. **Gross margin contraction below 68%:** If gross margin reverses its expansion trend and drops below 68% (vs 71.6% current), indicating pricing pressure, manufacturing issues, or competitive dynamics eroding the moat.

6. **GLP-1 clinical evidence of pump displacement:** If large clinical studies (not anecdotal reports) demonstrate that GLP-1 drugs reduce insulin pump initiation rates by >20% in T2D patients. Current data suggests complementary, not substitutive.

7. **Management departure:** CEO Ashley McEvoy (or successor) resignation, or CFO Pease departure, would remove execution continuity during critical T2D expansion phase.
