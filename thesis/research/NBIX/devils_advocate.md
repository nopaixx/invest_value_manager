# Devil's Advocate: NBIX (Neurocrine Biosciences)

## Date: 2026-04-06

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis survives scrutiny but with material adjustments required. The core business quality is real (98% gross margins, growing FCF, dominant TD franchise), but the FA's FV of $155 is anchored more to bullish scenario-blending than conservative fundamentals. Three moderate concerns -- DOJ investigation risk underpriced, CRENESSITY peak sales competition from Crinetics' atumelnant, and a QS adjustment that flips the Tier designation -- require committee attention. The thesis does NOT have critical flaws, but the margin of safety at current price ($131.60) is thinner than presented.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. INGREZZA Concentration Risk -- "Patent Protection to Mid-2030s" Is Real, But Irrelevant to the Nearer-Term Threats

- **FA's assumption:** INGREZZA has patent protection to mid-2030s; generic risk is "NOT imminent." Four generic entrants settled for March 1, 2038 launch.
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - The patent cliff is indeed far out (2038). This is confirmed. However, the thesis UNDERSTATES competitive threats from Austedo XR. Teva's once-daily formulation (FDA cleared May 2024) removes a key adherence disadvantage. Market share data shows INGREZZA at ~60% and Austedo at ~40%, but Austedo's IRA-negotiated pricing gives it a cost advantage with Medicare patients.
  - AbbVie's $8.7B acquisition of Cerevel Therapeutics brings tavapadon closer to TD trials, introducing a new mechanism with less dopaminergic liability. This is NOT in the thesis.
  - The TD market is a **duopoly** today, but the thesis treats it as if INGREZZA's 60% share is safe for a decade. The realistic risk is gradual share erosion (60% to 50-55%) as Austedo XR gains ground on cost, not a cliff event.
  - Receivables growing 43.4% vs revenue growth of 21.4% (narrative_checker.py) -- this suggests potential channel stuffing or deteriorating collection terms. This is a yellow flag for revenue quality.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Committee should model INGREZZA share declining to 52-55% by 2028 (from 60% today) as a realistic base, not maintaining 60%+. Revenue projections should be haircut 5-8% on the INGREZZA line.

### 2. DOJ Investigation -- "Manageable" Underestimates Tail Risk and Litigation Costs

- **FA's assumption:** DOJ CID will resolve with a consent decree or modest fine ($50-200M). Categorized as "manageable."
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - The DOJ announced FCA settlements exceeding $6.8 billion in FY2025 -- a record year. The enforcement environment is HARSHER than at any time in the statute's history.
  - Comparable pharma marketing settlements range widely: Biohaven (Pfizer) paid $59.7M for kickbacks, Gilead paid $176M for speaker program abuse, McKinsey paid $323M for OxyContin-related claims. For a company with $2.9B revenue, a $200-500M range is plausible, not just $50-200M as the thesis suggests.
  - The CID was received August 2025. The FDA issued a WARNING LETTER in September 2025 about misleading INGREZZA advertising. Two separate regulatory actions in two months is NOT typical of minor cases. This is a coordinated regulatory response.
  - The company declined to provide details beyond "cooperating" -- standard legal strategy, but tells us the scope may be broader than promotional materials alone.
  - A consent decree restricting INGREZZA sales practices could structurally reduce new patient acquisition rates, even if no fine is paid. The thesis does not model this operational risk.
  - INGREZZA represents 88% of revenue. Any restriction on marketing/promotion of the dominant product is existential-adjacent, not "manageable."
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** Bear case should model $300-500M fine (not $300M as thesis base), PLUS 6-12 months of restricted promotional activity reducing new patient starts by 10-15%. This alone drops the bear FV from $109 to approximately $95-100.

### 3. QS Adjustment: +4 Points to Cross the Tier A Threshold Is Not Justified

- **FA's assumption:** Tool gives QS 74 (Tier B). Manual +8 for market position correction, -4 for regulatory risk = net +4 = 78 (Tier A). This changes the MoS requirement and deployment framework.
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - The QS tool scored Market Position 0/8 because it requires manual input. The FA awards 8/8 (full marks) for being #1 in TD. But TD is a $3.5B duopoly market. Being #1 with 60% share in a two-player market with ZERO barriers to switching (Austedo is also VMAT2 inhibitor) is NOT the same as being #1 in a multi-player market with network effects.
  - For comparison: MELI gets market position credit for being #1 in LatAm e-commerce (a market with massive network effects, logistics moats, and payment ecosystem lock-in). TW gets credit for being #1 in US Treasury electronic trading (regulatory moats, liquidity network effects). NBIX being #1 in TD has NO network effects -- a doctor switches patients from INGREZZA to Austedo with zero switching cost.
  - The 8/8 score for market position should be 5/8 at most -- dominant share but in a small duopoly with low switching costs and price competition. Net adjustment should be +5 (not +8), leading to QS 75 at best. With the -4 for regulatory, that is QS 75 -- still borderline Tier A but with less room.
  - The thesis itself acknowledges: "Tier: A (borderline -- at 78, just above threshold. Treat with Tier B discipline on MoS.)" This is a self-contradiction. If you need to "treat with Tier B discipline," the position IS Tier B for deployment purposes.
  - Capital Allocation at 1/10 is strikingly low. No buybacks, no dividends, 1.1% insider ownership, net insider selling. For a company generating $750M FCF/year, zero capital return is a legitimate concern about management alignment.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Treat as Tier B for all deployment, MoS, and sizing decisions. QS 74-75, not 78. This means MoS requirement is ~20-25%, not ~15%. At $131.60 vs FV $155, MoS is only 15.1% -- INSUFFICIENT for Tier B.

### 4. CRENESSITY Peak Sales -- Competition Arriving Sooner Than Thesis Assumes

- **FA's assumption:** CRENESSITY scales to $500M+ in 2026-2027, with "blockbuster potential" ($1B+). First-in-class with 7-year orphan exclusivity.
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - Crinetics Pharmaceuticals' atumelnant (ACTH receptor antagonist) showed **88% of patients achieving physiologic dose levels** at 12 weeks in Phase 2 -- reported as beating CRENESSITY in head-to-head comparisons of key efficacy metrics. Phase 3 has initiated.
  - Atumelnant uses a DIFFERENT mechanism (ACTH receptor antagonist vs CRF1 antagonist) -- not a me-too. If Phase 3 succeeds, CRENESSITY faces a differentiated competitor within 3-4 years of launch.
  - The addressable CAH market is approximately 20,000 US patients. CRENESSITY reached ~2,050 patients in its first year (~10%). An October 2025 report pegged the CAH market at ~$500M in 2024, growing to ~$1B by 2035. The thesis's $500M peak for CRENESSITY implies capturing ~50% of a $1B market by 2027 -- aggressive.
  - Neurocrine DECLINED to provide 2026 CRENESSITY guidance, noting the market is "still being characterized." This is unusual for a company that guided $2.7-2.8B for INGREZZA. It suggests uncertainty about adoption velocity.
  - Analyst estimates range $1.2-1.8B for CRENESSITY peak GLOBAL sales, but this requires international approvals not yet obtained. US-only peak is more like $700M-$1B.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution:** Reduce CRENESSITY 2027-2028 projections by 15-20%. Use $450M for 2027 (not $700M) and $650M for 2028 (not $850M). Peak US sales ceiling around $800M, not $1B+.

### 5. CEO Transition Risk

- **FA's assumption:** Mentioned but barely explored. Kevin Gorman retired, Kyle Gano is new CEO.
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - Kyle Gano has been at Neurocrine for 23+ years, was Chief Business Development and Strategy Officer since 2020, and has a PhD in Organic Chemistry plus an MBA from UCLA. He was appointed President and CEO in October 2024. This is an INTERNAL succession -- the lowest-risk type.
  - Gano managed partnerships with AbbVie, Sanofi, Takeda, Xenon, and others. His track record is in business development, not clinical/R&D.
  - The former CEO Kevin Gorman remains on the Board (received 64K share award Feb 2026), providing continuity.
  - **Honest assessment:** This is a LOW risk. Internal promotion, board continuity, deep institutional knowledge. The thesis should have said so more clearly, but the DA finds no material risk here.
- **Severity:** LOW
- **Resolution:** No adjustment needed. CEO transition is well-managed.

## Recomendacion al Investment Committee

1. **Reclassify as Tier B** (QS 74-75). The +4 adjustment to cross the Tier A threshold is not defensible given the low switching costs in the TD duopoly and zero capital return policy. This changes MoS requirements.

2. **Lower FV to $135-140 range.** The FA's $155 relies on scenario-blending gymnastics (60/40, then averaged with 25/50/25, then "rounded up"). A cleaner approach: 2026E EBIT $875M (conservative margin) * 16x = $14B + $1B cash = $15B / 100.3M = $150 base, then 60% bear + 40% base = $130. Add pipeline optionality premium of $5-10 = $135-140.

3. **Investigate DOJ scope more deeply.** The thesis assumes $50-200M fine. Recent FCA precedents and the dual CID+FDA warning letter pattern suggest $200-500M is more realistic. Model the operational impact of restricted promotional activity on new patient starts.

4. **Monitor Crinetics atumelnant Phase 3 progress.** If Phase 3 data confirms Phase 2 superiority, CRENESSITY's peak sales ceiling drops by 20-30%. This is a 2027-2028 risk, not immediate.

5. **Set entry at $115-120** (not $125). At $120 vs $135 FV, MoS = 11% -- still thin for Tier B with DOJ overhang. At $115, MoS = 15% -- minimum acceptable. The stock has traded as low as $84.23 in the past 52 weeks, so $115-120 is achievable if DOJ news deteriorates temporarily.

6. **Explain receivables growth anomaly** (43% vs 21% revenue growth) before committing capital. This could indicate channel dynamics, delayed collections, or revenue quality deterioration.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The anti-bullish protocol (60% bear + 40% base) needs clearer guidance on WHERE in the valuation process it applies. The FA applied it within methods, then across methods, then averaged -- three layers produced a mechanical result ($131) that was then overridden to $155. The protocol should produce ONE number, applied ONCE.
- For pharma positions with active government investigations, consider a dedicated "regulatory risk premium" in the WACC (e.g., +1-2pp) rather than a haircut to the multiple. This is more methodologically clean.
