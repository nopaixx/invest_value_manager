## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis reaches a sensible WATCHLIST verdict at $33-36 entry, acknowledging CELH is approximately at fair value with no MoS. However, several critical assumptions deserve deeper scrutiny: (1) the thesis underweights the structural margin dilution from running a 3-brand platform vs. the single-brand model that generated all the historical quality metrics, (2) the PepsiCo dependency is framed as both moat AND risk but the asymmetry of the relationship favors PepsiCo, (3) Q4 2025 actual results (now available) show gross margin compressed to 47.4% and a troubling GAAP-vs-scanner disconnect for the CELSIUS brand, and (4) the consensus analyst PT of $61-68 vs FA's $44 FV means the FA has a bearish-vs-consensus view but frames it as neutral. The thesis survives scrutiny because the WATCHLIST verdict and negative MoS assessment are already conservative. The main risk is that the $33-36 entry target may never arrive, rendering this a perpetual watchlist item.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "Normalized operating margin will recover to 14-17%"

- **FA Assumption:** Operating margin collapsed from 20.2% to 11.5% due to temporary factors (integration costs, PepsiCo destocking). Will recover to 14-17% by Q2 2027.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Q4 2025 ACTUAL results show gross margin compressed further to 47.4% (from 50.2%), not the 50-52% the FA projected for steady state. Tariffs on aluminum cans (25% raised to 50% in June 2025) are a STRUCTURAL headwind, not temporary.
  - Management guided S&M at 23-25% of revenue -- this is a PERMANENT cost of running 3 brands across different demographics. Monster operates at 30% operating margin but has 40 years of brand equity and spends only ~12% on S&M.
  - Rockstar Energy has been a DECLINING brand for years. Its inclusion dilutes margins without proportional growth. PepsiCo sold it to Celsius precisely because it was underperforming within PepsiCo's portfolio.
  - The FA correctly identifies 20.2% as anomalous, but the floor may be lower than 14%. Multi-brand CPG companies with <5 years of integration history typically see margin normalization at 2-3pp below initial expectations.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Committee should model a 12-14% operating margin as base case (not 14-17%). The difference is material: at 12% op margin on $3.4B revenue vs 16%, the EBITDA delta is ~$136M, which at 18x = ~$9.50/share or ~23% of current price.

### 2. "QS Adjusted 62 (Tier B) is the correct quality assessment"

- **FA Assumption:** Tool QS 79 adjusted down to 62 via -17 point adjustment. The -17 is justified by acquisition distortion, declining EPS, ROIC volatility.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - The FA's adjustment is DIRECTIONALLY correct but may not go far enough. The tool QS 79 is based on a company that NO LONGER EXISTS (single-brand CELH with hypergrowth). The entity today is a leveraged multi-brand platform with $870M debt, declining core brand EPS, and unproven integration.
  - However, the counter-argument is also valid: the FA may be too harsh on EPS CAGR (2/10). The Q4 2025 adjusted EPS of $0.26 beat consensus by 37%, and full-year adjusted EBITDA of ~$450M suggests the business IS generating real earnings from the combined platform.
  - Net: the QS 62 is reasonable. Neither too generous nor too punitive.
- **Severidad:** LOW
- **Resolucion sugerida:** QS 62 Tier B is defensible. No change recommended.

### 3. "PepsiCo distribution is both moat AND controllable risk"

- **FA Assumption:** PepsiCo distribution is a strong moat (99.5% ACV, 250K+ outlets). PepsiCo's 11% ownership + 2 board seats = alignment. Risk probability 10-15% over 5 years.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - PepsiCo accounted for 43.2% of total revenue and 46.2% of total receivables in FY2025 (10-K data). This is EXTREME customer concentration. In any other context, a company with 43% revenue from one customer would be classified as HIGH RISK.
  - PepsiCo's alignment is CONDITIONAL. PepsiCo invested $550M in convertible preferred at ~$75/share equivalent. With CELH now at $41.51, PepsiCo is underwater on its investment. This changes the power dynamic -- PepsiCo has LESS incentive to help CELH succeed if the stock remains depressed and more incentive to extract economic value from the relationship (higher distribution fees, shelf space charges).
  - The "moat" is actually a DEPENDENCY. Celsius cannot feasibly switch distributors without catastrophic revenue disruption. PepsiCo can apply pricing pressure knowing Celsius has no alternative. This is a textbook supplier-buyer power asymmetry (Porter's framework).
  - PepsiCo also distributed Rockstar for years and allowed the brand to DECLINE before selling it. This is not evidence of a nurturing distributor.
  - The 10-15% risk probability over 5 years is reasonable for COMPLETE relationship breakdown but ignores the higher probability (30-40%) of PARTIAL relationship deterioration: higher fees, reduced priority, shifted marketing support.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** PepsiCo dependency should be treated as a STRUCTURAL VULNERABILITY, not a moat. The moat is distribution access; the vulnerability is that access is controlled by a single entity with superior bargaining power. Kill Condition #1 should be expanded to include "PepsiCo increases distribution fees by >200bps" as a trigger.

### 4. "Celsius brand organic growth of +44% in Q3 2025 signals rebounding growth"

- **FA Assumption:** Organic brand growth rebounding from +3% FY2024. CELSIUS brand +44% Q3 2025, +13% scanner growth.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Q4 2025 ACTUAL results reveal a troubling disconnect: CELSIUS brand GAAP sales DECLINED 7.7% while scanner data showed +12.8% growth. Management attributed this to "timing and distribution transition dynamics." This is the SAME language used to describe channel stuffing in other CPG companies.
  - The +44% Q3 2025 growth was lapping the Q3 2024 PepsiCo destocking debacle (an extremely easy comp). Underlying normalized growth is likely 10-15%, consistent with the FA's own estimate.
  - The receivables/revenue divergence flagged by the FA (47.2% vs 2.9% in FY2024) has NOT been resolved and may have WORSENED given the Q4 GAAP-vs-scanner disconnect.
  - Energy drink category growth has slowed to 5-6% from 7-8% previously, and brands have become more promotional in convenience channels.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolucion sugerida:** The GAAP-vs-scanner divergence in Q4 2025 is a RED FLAG that warrants investigation before any entry. The FA's KC#4 (receivables/revenue gap) should be actively monitored against Q4 2025 10-K data. The organic growth assumption should be lowered to 8-12% (not 10-15%).

### 5. "Fair Value of ~$44 (weighted) is approximately correct"

- **FA Assumption:** Weighted FV of $44 based on Forward EV/EBITDA (50%), Forward P/E (30%), Reverse DCF (20%).
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Using UPDATED Q4 2025 data: FY2025 revenue $2.515B, gross margin 47.4% (declining), adjusted EBITDA ~$450M.
  - The FA used Forward EV/EBITDA 18x on 2027E EBITDA of $650M. But at 47.4% gross margin (not 50-52%) and 12-14% operating margin (not 14-17%), normalized 2027E EBITDA is closer to $530-580M. At 18x, EV = $9.5-10.4B, Equity = $8.8-9.7B, FV/share = $34-38.
  - The FA's consensus P/E multiple of 30x for a Tier B company with integration risk is generous. The S202 anti-bullish-bias protocol requires starting from bear case and weighting 60% bear / 40% base.
  - Applying S202: FV = 60% * $30 (bear) + 40% * $44 (base) = $18 + $17.60 = **$35.60**
  - This aligns with the FA's own entry target of $33-36, suggesting the FA's instincts on entry are better than the stated FV.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Post-DA FV should be adjusted to $36-40 range, incorporating Q4 2025 actual margins and S202 bear-weighting protocol.
