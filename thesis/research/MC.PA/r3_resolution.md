## 2. Resolution — Point by Point

### 2.1 QS Adjustment: DA partially wins → QS 66

R1 adjusted +18 points (54 → 72). The DA argues maximum +10-12 (64-66).

The R1's adjustments:
- Market position +8: **Valid.** LVMH IS #1 global luxury conglomerate. Louis Vuitton + Dior are undeniable franchises.
- "Cyclical distortion" +8: **Partially invalid.** This assumes the cycle turns — which is the thesis, not a fact. Margins have declined 4 consecutive years. ROIC has fallen from 15.7% to 11.7%. You can't credit cyclical recovery as a QS adjustment; it belongs in FV scenarios.
- Other +2: Minor.

**Resolution:** Market position +8 is fair. Cyclical distortion adjustment halved to +4 (give some credit for luxury resilience, but not full recovery). QS Tool 54 + 12 = **QS 66 Tier B.**

This matters: Tier B requires ~20-25% MoS (precedents), not Tier A's 10-15%.

### 2.2 China: DA wins — model as flat, not recovering

The evidence is overwhelming:
- Aspirational consumer base contracted 70M in 3 years (Bain data)
- China luxury spending flat-to-declining 2024-2025
- Bain expects "broadly flat" 2026
- Property crisis continues to suppress wealth effect
- 65% domestic consumption reduces high-margin travel retail

The R1's recovery assumption has zero supporting data. The DA's "flat for 2-3 years" is more defensible.

**Resolution:** China assumed flat through 2027. Any recovery is upside surprise, not base case.

### 2.3 W&S: DA wins — structural decline, not temporary

- Revenue -5% FY2025, Cognac -15% H1
- Workforce cut 13% to 2019 levels (companies don't do this for temporary issues)
- Consumer preference shift toward tequila/Japanese whisky is secular
- 200% US alcohol tariff threat hanging over Hennessy

**Resolution:** Model W&S as declining 3-5% annually for 3+ years. Strip any recovery premium.

### 2.4 Tariff Risk: DA wins — must be in base case

- Currently 15% on EU goods, 200% on alcohol products floated
- Morgan Stanley estimates -150bps margin on F&LG division from current 15% tariff
- June 2026 escalation deadline is real
- US manufacturing pivot failing (Texas factory at 30% capacity, quality issues)
- "Made in France" IS the luxury moat — manufacturing in US undermines brand value

**Resolution:** 15% tariff as base case. 25% as high-probability scenario. 200% alcohol tariff as kill condition. This is NOT tail risk.

### 2.5 Pricing Power / Margins: DA mostly wins

4 consecutive years of GM decline (68.8% → 66.2%) and OpM decline (26.5% → 21.9%) is not "cyclical trough" — it's a trend. Introducing "accessible" products (bag charms, beauty line) is volume desperation, not pricing power.

**However:** F&LG division alone maintains ~40% OpM. The blended margin decline is partly mix (W&S drag). Louis Vuitton's individual pricing power is still real — it's the conglomerate mix that dilutes it.

**Resolution:** Model blended OpM stabilizing at 21-22% (not recovering to 25%+). F&LG may hold, but W&S and other divisions drag the average.

### 2.6 ROIC Trajectory: DA wins on trend

15.7% → 16.2% → 13.0% → 11.7%. Only +3.5pp vs WACC 8.2%. For a "quality compounder," this is thin and deteriorating.

**Resolution:** ROIC trajectory is genuinely concerning. Must stabilize above 12% for 2 quarters before any upgrade. The +3.5pp spread provides limited buffer if WACC rises (rates staying higher for longer).

## 3. R3 Fair Value Calculation

### Method 1: EV/EBIT Normalized (60% weight)

**Bear case:**
- Normalized EBIT: EUR 16.5B (FY2025 EUR 17.8B, haircut for continued margin compression + tariff drag + W&S decline)
- Multiple: 12x (declining margins, near-zero organic growth, tariff overhang)
- Net debt: EUR 23.3B
- Shares: 496M
- EV = EUR 198B, Equity = EUR 175B
- **Bear FV = EUR 353/share**

**Base case:**
- Normalized EBIT: EUR 18.0B (FY2025 level holds, F&LG resilient, W&S stabilizes)
- Multiple: 14.5x (quality franchise premium, but discounted for growth headwinds)
- Net debt: EUR 23.3B
- EV = EUR 261B, Equity = EUR 238B
- **Base FV = EUR 480/share**

**Anti-bullish-bias (60% bear / 40% base):**
FV = (353 × 0.60) + (480 × 0.40) = 212 + 192 = **EUR 404**

### Method 2: FCF Yield (40% weight)

FCF FY2025: EUR 14.2B. FCF margin 17.6% (strong and stable).

**Bear OEY** (target 5.5%, compressed growth): EUR 14.2B / 0.055 = EUR 258B equity / 496M = **EUR 520** → minus net debt adjustment → **EUR 473**

Wait — this produces a HIGHER FV than the price. The FCF is genuinely strong. Let me use Owner Earnings more carefully:

- Owner Earnings = FCF - maintenance capex excess = ~EUR 13B (conservative)
- Bear OEY target 5.5%: EUR 13B / 0.055 = EUR 236B → equity EUR 213B / 496M = **EUR 430**
- Base OEY target 4.5%: EUR 13B / 0.045 = EUR 289B → equity EUR 266B / 496M = **EUR 536**

**Anti-bullish-bias:** (430 × 0.60) + (536 × 0.40) = 258 + 214 = **EUR 472**

### Reconciliation

| Method | Anti-Bias FV | Weight | Weighted |
|--------|-------------|--------|----------|
| EV/EBIT Normalized | EUR 404 | 60% | EUR 242 |
| Owner Earnings Yield | EUR 472 | 40% | EUR 189 |
| **Weighted Average** | | **100%** | **EUR 431** |

The two methods diverge modestly (EUR 404 vs 472). The EV/EBIT method penalizes declining margins more heavily; the OEY method gives credit for strong FCF generation.

**R3 Fair Value: EUR 430** (rounding from EUR 431, tilted slightly toward EV/EBIT given margin concerns).

This is notably below the R1's EUR 540 (-20%) and close to the DA's post-DA recommended range of EUR 400-430. The DA's analysis on China structural decline, margin erosion, and tariff risk was compelling.

## 8. Verdict

**WATCHLIST — Iconic franchise, wrong price, wrong time.**

LVMH is a world-class luxury conglomerate with genuine franchise value (Louis Vuitton, Dior, Tiffany, Sephora), EUR 14.2B FCF, and 50% Arnault family alignment. The DA did NOT break the franchise thesis. What the DA demonstrated convincingly is:

1. **Margins are in structural decline**, not cyclical trough — 4 consecutive years
2. **China recovery is unproven** — 70M fewer aspirational consumers is structural
3. **Tariff risk is base case**, not tail risk — June 2026 deadline looms
4. **QS 72 was inflated** — QS 66 (Tier B) is more defensible

At EUR 467, the stock offers zero MoS against the R3 FV of EUR 430. The market is pricing in a modest recovery that hasn't materialized. At EUR 355 (~24% below current), the franchise value provides a genuine margin of safety even if margins don't recover.

**The luxury cycle WILL turn eventually.** The question is timing and magnitude. LVMH at EUR 355 prices in enough pessimism that the cycle turn becomes upside. LVMH at EUR 467 prices in a recovery that isn't happening.

**Do not advance to R4 at current price.** If price reaches EUR 380-400, consider an accelerated R4 review.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres
- The biggest uncertainty is whether the aspirational consumer contraction is truly structural (DA view) or cyclical (R1 view). If China luxury spending rebounds 10%+ in 2027, FV jumps to EUR 500+. I don't have an edge on predicting this.
- Tariff outcome by June 2026 is binary and unpredictable. If tariffs are removed/reduced, LVMH re-rates sharply. If escalated to 25-50%, FV drops to EUR 350-380.
- The OEY method produced EUR 472 vs EV/EBIT EUR 404. The gap reflects LVMH's genuine FCF generation power vs its declining margin trajectory. Both are "correct" — the question is which matters more for the next 3 years.

### Sugerencias
- MC.PA belongs in the luxury-goods sector view watchlist with trigger at EUR 355
- Consider Hermès (RMS.PA) as the stronger luxury compounder — 41% OpM, 9% organic growth, pricing power intact. May be better candidate for any luxury basket allocation, though likely at a premium valuation.
- The European Pricing Power basket (DEATH_WATCH per DA) should NOT be filled with MC.PA at current price. Error #57 applies.

- QS Tool score of 54 (Tier C) for the world's largest luxury company is surprising. The tool heavily penalizes declining ROIC/margins. This is "correct by construction" but may underweight franchise value for iconic brands.
