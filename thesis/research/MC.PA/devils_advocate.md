## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis assigned EUR 540 FV to LVMH with QS adj 72 (Tier B), implying 14% upside from current EUR 474 and entry at EUR 430 (-20.4% below FV). The DA finds this FV is anchored to a recovery scenario that faces multiple structural headwinds: (1) the aspirational luxury consumer base has contracted by 70 million people in 3 years -- this is not cyclical, it is a structural repricing of the luxury market; (2) Wines & Spirits is in genuine structural decline with workforce cuts to 2019 levels; (3) tariff risk of 150bps margin headwind in 2026 is quantified but could escalate dramatically under 200% alcohol tariff threat; (4) ROIC is declining (15.7% in 2022 to 11.7% in 2025) and margins are compressing, challenging the "pricing power" thesis. The market at EUR 474 is implying 5.8% FCF growth -- already optimistic vs 3.7% historical FCF CAGR and 0.7% recent revenue growth. The FA's EUR 540 requires a full luxury recovery that evidence does not support. At EUR 474, LVMH is approximately fairly valued in the base case, with meaningful downside in the bear case. The entry price of EUR 430 provides minimal true safety.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "LVMH Has Exceptional Pricing Power That Will Drive Recovery"

**FA Assumption:** LVMH's brand portfolio commands pricing power that will restore margins and growth as China recovers. QS adjusted from 54 to 72 (+18 points) partly on "market position" and "GM stability."

**Evidence Against:**
- Gross margin has DECLINED 4 consecutive years: 68.8% (2023) to 66.2% (2025). This is not "stability" -- it is erosion.
- Operating margin compressed from 26.5% (2022) to 21.9% (2025) -- a 460bps decline over 3 years.
- LVMH's own management acknowledged moving away from "blanket price increases" -- they are losing the ability to pass through uniformly.
- The aspirational consumer base has shrunk by 70 million people globally (from 400M to 330M in 3 years). "Extravagant price increases" identified as the primary driver behind this mass departure ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2026/chinas-personal-luxury-market-contracts-35-in-2025-but-shows-signs-of-recovery/)).
- LVMH is now introducing MORE ACCESSIBLE products (Louis Vuitton beauty line, bag charms) -- this is the opposite of pricing power; it is volume desperation that dilutes brand exclusivity.
- Hermes, the true pricing power exemplar, grew 9% organically in 2025 with 41% operating margin vs LVMH's -1% and 22%. The gap is widening, not closing.

**Severity: HIGH**
- The +18 point QS adjustment (54 to 72) is the largest driver of the Tier B classification. If pricing power is eroding rather than stable, the adjustment is at least partially unwarranted. A more conservative +10 adjustment (QS 64, still Tier B but low-end) would be more defensible.

**Resolution:** Committee should require evidence of margin stabilization (not just revenue stabilization) before accepting EUR 540 FV. Q1 2026 showed 3% organic growth, but margin data is the real test.

---

### 2. "China Luxury Slowdown Is Cyclical, Not Structural"

**FA Assumption:** China will recover, driving LVMH back to historical growth rates. Entry at EUR 430 implicitly prices a cyclical trough.

**Evidence Against:**
- Bain data: China personal luxury goods market contracted 3-5% in 2025, after an 18-20% collapse in 2024 that reverted to 2020 levels. This is a 2-year double decline, not a single-year blip.
- The contraction of the aspirational consumer base is STRUCTURAL: "years of aggressive price elevation have now collided with slower income growth, rising financial pressures, and a more cautious social climate" ([China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/chinas-luxury-market-outlook-stabilization-strategic-reset-and-the-new-consumer-landscape/)).
- 65% of Chinese luxury consumption now happens domestically (consumption repatriation), reducing the high-margin travel retail channel where LVMH's DFS and European flagships profit most.
- Bain expects "broadly flat" China luxury spending in 2026 -- NOT the rebound the EUR 540 FV requires.
- Asia ex-Japan organic sales fell -4% in LVMH's 2025 results. Deutsche Bank expects sequential deceleration across most luxury brands in China.
- Property market crisis continues to suppress wealth effect for Chinese middle class.

**Severity: HIGH**
- LVMH has ~22-25% revenue exposure to Greater China (direct + travel retail). If China is flat (not recovering) for 2-3 more years, the revenue base does not support 5-6% growth assumptions.

**Resolution:** Model China as FLAT for 2026-2027 (not recovering). Only accept recovery assumption with evidence of 2+ consecutive quarters of positive organic growth in Asia.

---

### 3. "Wines & Spirits Weakness Is Temporary"

**FA Assumption:** W&S headwinds will pass as destocking normalizes and tariffs are resolved.

**Evidence Against:**
- W&S division organic revenue fell -5% in FY2025, with H1 spirits down -15% and Cognac revenue down -15% YoY in H1.
- LVMH is cutting W&S workforce from 9,400 to 8,200 -- levels not seen since 2019. Companies do not cut 13% of a division's workforce for temporary issues.
- The US 200% tariff threat on EU alcohol is not priced into any scenario. If enacted, it would effectively shut the US cognac market for Hennessy (US is ~40% of Hennessy volume).
- S&P Global: "The cognac issue remains" (Jan 2026 headline). Not "is resolving."
- LVMH is deprioritizing smaller spirits brands (Volcan de mi Tierra, Eminente rum) -- strategic retreat, not temporary pause.
- Consumer preference shift: younger demographics moving toward tequila, Japanese whisky, and mezcal -- away from cognac. This is a secular preference change, not destocking.

**Severity: MODERATE**
- W&S is ~9-10% of LVMH revenue and ~6% of profit. It is not the core thesis, but its decline is real, structural, and worse than "temporary." A EUR 540 FV that assumes W&S stabilization is too generous.

**Resolution:** Model W&S as flat-to-declining for 3+ years. Strip any recovery premium from this segment.

---

### 4. "Tariff Risk Is Manageable"

**FA Assumption:** LVMH can absorb or pass through tariffs without material margin impact.

**Evidence Against:**
- Current 15% EU tariff already estimated to cost -150bps on F&LG division margin in 2026 (Morgan Stanley).
- Tariffs could escalate to 25% by June 2026 if no EU-US agreement reached. 50% rate has been floated by Trump administration.
- 200% tariff on alcohol products would devastate Hennessy (Newsweek: "hit billionaire ally" headline).
- LVMH's US manufacturing strategy has FAILED so far: Texas factory at <300 workers vs 1,000 target, with productivity shortfalls and quality control problems. "Made in France" IS the product for luxury -- US manufacturing undermines the brand proposition.
- Morgan Stanley explicitly downgraded LVMH to Equal Weight partly on tariff risk, cutting PT from EUR 635 to EUR 565.
- If LVMH raises US prices to offset tariffs, it accelerates the aspirational consumer exodus already documented.

**Severity: HIGH** (elevated from MODERATE due to escalation risk)
- US is ~25% of LVMH revenue. A 25-50% tariff with limited price pass-through could reduce group operating profit by 3-5%.

**Resolution:** Any FV must haircut for tariff risk as a base case (not tail risk). Include 200% alcohol tariff as a kill condition scenario.

---

### 5. "ROIC Decline Is Cyclical"

**FA Assumption:** ROIC will recover with the luxury cycle. QS tool gives 4/15 for ROIC spread (only +3.5pp vs WACC).

**Evidence Against:**
- ROIC trajectory: 15.7% (2022) to 16.2% (2023) to 13.0% (2024) to 11.7% (2025). Three consecutive years of decline.
- ROE trajectory: 25.6% (2022) to 16.1% (2025) -- a near-40% decline in return on equity.
- ROIC-WACC spread at only +3.5pp is thin for a supposed "quality compounder." A normal luxury company with genuine pricing power should sustain 10%+ spread.
- Capital allocation into DFS (travel retail), Tiffany integration, and hotel expansion (Cheval Blanc) requires significant capex that may not generate returns matching the core F&LG business.
- The decline is not just cyclical -- LVMH's conglomerate model means returns are diluted by lower-ROIC divisions (W&S, Retail, Other).

**Severity: MODERATE**
- ROIC is still positive and above WACC. But the trend is clearly negative and the QS tool correctly captures this at 54. The +18 manual adjustment seems excessive given the declining trajectory.

**Resolution:** QS adjustment should be +10-12 maximum (QS 64-66), not +18 (QS 72). ROIC must stabilize above 12% for 2 consecutive quarters before upgrading.
