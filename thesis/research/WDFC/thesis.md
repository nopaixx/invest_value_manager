## Business Understanding

### What WD-40 Does

WD-40 Company manufactures and markets maintenance products, homecare, and cleaning products worldwide. The business is deceptively simple:

1. **Maintenance Products (95% of revenue):** The iconic WD-40 Multi-Use Product plus WD-40 Specialist line (degreasers, lubricants, corrosion inhibitors). Sold in 176+ countries. The formula is a trade secret (not patented -- patents expire, secrets don't).

2. **Homecare & Cleaning (5% of revenue):** 3-IN-ONE, GT85, and other niche brands. Declining segment, not strategic.

**Revenue model:** Consumable repeat-purchase product. Users buy cans regularly. Distribution through hardware stores, auto parts retailers, industrial distributors, and increasingly e-commerce (22% growth in Q1 FY2026).

**Geographic mix:** Americas 45%, EIMEA (Europe, India, Middle East, Africa) 38%, Asia-Pacific 17%.

### Unit Economics & Margin Structure

| Gross Margin | 49.1% | 51.0% | 53.4% | 55.1% | Expanding (+600bps) |

**Critical observation:** Gross margin expanded 600bps (49.1% -> 55.1%) over 4 years, yet operating margin was FLAT at ~16.7%. This means ~$37M of incremental gross profit was absorbed by SG&A and other operating expenses. The company is investing heavily in geographic expansion, digital transformation, and the "Must-Win Battles" strategy. This is either:
- (a) Smart investment that will pay off in operating leverage later, or
- (b) Structural cost inflation from a more complex global operation

Management guidance suggests FY2026 EPS of $5.75-6.15, which at midpoint ($5.95) is BELOW FY2025's $6.70. This is a DECLINE, not growth.

### Capital Intensity

- Capex/Depreciation: ~1.0-1.2x (maintenance level)
- Net Debt/EBITDA: 0.5x (fortress balance sheet)

This is genuinely asset-light. The brand IS the asset, not factories. Manufacturing is increasingly outsourced.

### Moat Assessment

**MOAT: WIDE -- Brand + Trade Secret + Distribution**

1. **Brand recognition:** WD-40 is one of the most recognized brands in maintenance products globally. The name IS the category. "Grab the WD-40" is how customers refer to ANY penetrating oil. This creates mindshare that no competitor can replicate.

2. **Trade secret formula:** Unlike patents (which expire), WD-40's formula has been a trade secret since 1953. This provides indefinite protection. Competitors can create similar products but cannot replicate the exact formula.

3. **Gross margin premium:** +30.1pp vs sector median (55.1% vs 25.0%). This is an enormous premium that signals extraordinary pricing power and brand value.

4. **Distribution network:** Present in 176+ countries. The logistics and retail relationships built over 70+ years create high barriers to entry for new competitors.

5. **Switching costs (moderate):** While any consumer CAN switch, brand loyalty is very high. The product costs $5-15 per can -- low enough that price sensitivity is minimal. Nobody comparison-shops penetrating oil.

**Moat risks:**
- PFAS regulation could force formula changes (monitoring)
- Bio-based alternatives gaining niche traction
- No technology moat -- the product is chemically simple

### ROIC Analysis

ROIC of 29.8% with WACC at 5.4% = spread of +24.4pp. This is exceptional. The ROIC has been consistently above 20% for the past decade, demonstrating durable competitive advantage. The low capital requirements (asset-light model) plus high margins naturally produce outstanding ROIC.

## Risks

### Primary Risks

1. **Valuation compression risk (HIGH):** At 34.6x P/E, any growth disappointment causes disproportionate price decline. Q1 miss already shows vulnerability. Risk is asymmetric at these multiples.

2. **FX headwinds (MODERATE):** 55% of revenue is non-US. Strong USD (trade war driven) compresses reported results. Q1 showed +0.6% reported but -2% constant currency -- FX flattered the number.

3. **Tariff exposure (MODERATE):** Blanket 10% tariff on imports + Section 301 probes. WDFC manufactures in US, UK, Australia, China. Cross-border supply chain exposes them to tariff friction. Products are low-ASP, so tariff % impact is proportionally larger on margins.

4. **Input cost inflation from oil (MODERATE):** Petroleum-based products. WTI at $98+, Brent $103+. Raw material costs could compress gross margins that have been expanding. Management has historically passed through costs, but timing lag hurts quarters.

### Secondary Risks

5. **PFAS regulatory risk (LOW-MODERATE):** Emerging regulation on PFAS chemicals. WD-40 has not disclosed specific PFAS content in products. If products contain PFAS, reformulation costs + potential market restrictions in EU/US states.

6. **Management transition risk (LOW):** Current CEO Steve Brass since 2021. No imminent succession issues but culture-driven company where leadership matters.

7. **E-commerce channel shift (LOW):** Disintermediation of traditional retail could disrupt established distribution advantages, though WDFC is leaning into e-commerce (+22% growth).

- FCF margin declining from 17% to 13.5% over 3 years while the tool scores FCF consistency at 4/5. The tool looks backward; the forward trajectory matters more for investment decisions.
- FY2025 had negative FCF in the first year of the 4-year lookback, which artificially suppresses the CAGR. Adjusting to only positive FCF years gives a healthier picture, but FY2022 was not a one-time event -- it included real working capital and capex needs.
