### Finding 1: FV $140 too high → $130-135 (HIGH)
**DA argument:** Growth 7-8% should be 6-7% (total revenue guide, not recurring-only). Governance discount -2.5% weakly justified (grants ≠ open-market buys). Thesis creep after 16% rally.
**Resolution: ACCEPT with modification.** FV $140 → $135.
- Growth: 7-8% → 7% (compromise: management guides 6-7% total but 7-8% recurring; PAYC historically beats guide by ~100bps. Use 7% as midpoint).
- Governance discount: restore to -5% (DA correct that grants are compensation, not conviction buys. CEO/Chair duality + $211M comp persists).
- OEY at 5.75% target yield (compromise between FA's 5.5% and DA's 6.0%).
- DCF base at 7% growth / 9% WACC = ~$145. OEY at 5.75% = ~$140. Peer 20x FCF = $148. Weighted 50/25/25 = $144. Apply -5% governance = $137. Round to $135 (conservative lean given no informational edge).

### Finding 2: Rippling threat understated (HIGH)
**DA argument:** $600M ARR stale. Rippling at $570M Feb 2025, projected $1B by end-2026. $3B KC threshold too distant.
**Resolution: ACCEPT.** KC#6 modified: "Rippling reaches $1.5B ARR OR Rippling client count >50K in mid-market segment." Also add KC#13 (NEW): "Rippling IPO with proceeds >$2B earmarked for mid-market expansion."

### Finding 3: Revenue deceleration structural (HIGH)
**DA argument:** 30%→23%→11%→9%→guide 6-7%. FCF growth (20%) is peak, not sustainable. EPS declined -9% YoY.
**Resolution: PARTIALLY ACCEPT.** Revenue deceleration IS structural — the hyper-growth era is over. But PAYC is transitioning to a mature compounder model: 6-7% revenue + 10% buyback + 1.1% yield = ~17-18% total shareholder return if margins hold. The EPS decline was distorted by FY2024 SBC anomaly. Use 7% as steady-state growth assumption (not 8%), and monitor Q1 FY2026 for confirmation.

### Finding 4: Morningstar moat downgrade to NONE (MODERATE)
**DA argument:** AI-driven headcount reduction + seat-based pricing vulnerability.
**Resolution: NOTE but DO NOT OVERWEIGHT.** Morningstar's moat framework penalizes seat-based pricing models generically. PAYC's switching costs are real (implementation cycles, data migration, payroll-error-intolerance). Our moat assessment gave NARROW 18/25, which is more nuanced. The downgrade is a sentiment headwind, not a fundamental change.

### Finding 5: No informational edge (MODERATE)
**DA argument:** Our FV $130-135 is BELOW consensus mean ($153) and median ($137). Per Error #49, no edge.
**Resolution: ACKNOWLEDGE.** This is the most important DA finding. At $132, we are saying the stock is roughly fairly valued. Our edge is NOT informational — it is structural: we can be patient and buy at $105-115 when others can't. The 10.6% SI creates potential for sharp drawdowns that provide entry. Our edge is discipline + patience, not analysis.
