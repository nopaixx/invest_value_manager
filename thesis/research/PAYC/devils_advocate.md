## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 REFRESH thesis ($140 FV) has three persistent weaknesses that the Mar 11 DA identified and which have NOT been resolved by the R3 resolution ($135): (1) growth assumption of 7% is optimistic relative to management's 6-7% total revenue guidance; (2) the governance discount reduction from -5% to -2.5% was based on stock grants mischaracterized as insider buying; (3) the Rippling competitive KC#6 threshold ($3B ARR) is dangerously high given Rippling's >30%/yr trajectory. However, at $123.47 (vs $132 at prior DA), the stock now trades BELOW the DA's own mild bear FV of $137 and near the severe bear of $109. The quality fundamentals (24.5% ROIC, $404M FCF, net cash, 83.2% GM) are genuine and NOT disputed. The question is whether the structural growth deceleration (30% -> 9% -> 6-7%) has been adequately priced. At 2.2% implied FCF growth vs 6-7% guided revenue growth and 8-10% guided EBITDA growth, the market appears to be pricing in a scenario worse than even my bear case. This creates genuine upside asymmetry.

**Net assessment: The thesis has gaps, but the stock at $123 is approaching a level where even conservative assumptions produce adequate MoS.**

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "Growth is rebasing at 7-8% -- the deceleration is behind us"

- **FA's claim:** FY2025 delivered 9% revenue, 10.3% recurring, 20% FCF growth. Client growth accelerated from 2% to 4%. Guide of 6-7% is deliberately conservative (Paycom historically guides low, delivers high). Sustainable growth is 7-8%.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - FY2026 total revenue guidance of $2.175-2.195B = 6.0-7.0% growth. Recurring revenue guidance: 7.4% growth (vs analyst expectations of 9.4%). This is the FIFTH consecutive year of deceleration: FY2022 ~28%, FY2023 ~22%, FY2024 11.2%, FY2025 9.0%, FY2026E 6-7%.
  - Jefferies noted "disappointing 2026 growth outlook will likely overshadow the results" and that results "largely validate company-specific and industry concerns."
  - CEO Richison himself admitted Paycom needs to "improve sales execution" and has been "retraining the entire sales organization over the past three months." If the best sales team in mid-market HCM needs retraining, the product-market fit is shifting.
  - The deceleration trajectory points toward 4-5% by FY2027-2028 unless a genuine growth catalyst (IWant? International?) materializes. There is zero evidence of reacceleration in the FY2026 guide.
  - FCF growth of 20% in FY2025 was peak, driven by layoffs + margin expansion + capex reduction. Revenue is the sustainable growth driver, and it is 6-7%.
  - EPS actually DECLINED from $8.93 (FY2024) to $8.13 (FY2025) due to the SBC/Revenue anomaly normalization in FY2024. This undermines the "EPS compounder" narrative.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolution sugerida:** Use 6-6.5% as the base growth assumption, not 7-8%. The FA anchors to the optimistic end. Management guides 6-7% total revenue. The DA should use the midpoint of management's own guidance, not exceed it.

### 2. "Rippling is years away from being a threat (KC#6 at $3B ARR)"

- **FA's claim:** Rippling at ~$600M ARR is well below KC#6 threshold of $3B. Kill condition not approaching.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - The $600M figure is stale. Rippling reached $570M ARR in Feb 2025 and is projected to reach $1B by end-2026. At >30% growth, $2B by 2028-2029, $3B by ~2030.
  - The $3B threshold is ARBITRARY. Rippling does not need $3B ARR to damage Paycom. It needs to capture enough greenfield mid-market growth to slow Paycom's new client additions below the churn rate. With 20,000+ customers (doubled from 2023) and Gartner ratings of 4.9/5 vs Paycom's 4.1/5, this is already happening.
  - Rippling's architecture is structurally superior: unified employee graph integrating HR+IT+Finance with 650+ tool integrations. Paycom's closed ecosystem (no inbound third-party data) is increasingly perceived as a liability in a market that demands integration. Paycom's own comparison page admits architectural differences.
  - ADP and Paychex also compete aggressively on pricing in mid-market. The "intense competition in a soft demand environment" narrative from analyst calls is consistent.
  - The PEPM (per-employee-per-month) pricing model across the HCM sector is under structural threat from AI-driven headcount reduction at client companies. This is not Rippling-specific -- it is industry-wide.
- **Severidad:** HIGH
- **Resolution sugerida:** Lower KC#6 to "$1.5B Rippling ARR OR Paycom client growth turns negative." Add industry-level KC: "Average PEPM revenue per client declines for 2+ consecutive quarters ex-recession."

### 3. "FV $140 (R1 REFRESH) / $135 (R3 resolved) is well-supported"

- **FA's claim:** Three valuation methods (OEY, DCF, Peer) weighted 50/25/25 produce $145, minus 2.5% governance = $140 (R1 REFRESH). R3 resolved at $135 with entry $105-115.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - **Governance discount:** The reduction from -5% to -2.5% is poorly justified. Stock grants (198.8K shares) are compensation, not open-market conviction purchases. The historical pattern is 128 sells to 1 buy over 5 years. CEO/Chair duality, $211M compensation, clawback waiver on 5.3M shares, 500+ layoffs via text at 5am, 61% Glassdoor CEO approval -- the governance discount should remain -5% or increase, not decrease.
  - **OEY growth assumption:** At 5.5% OEY target yield, FV = $147. But OEY + Growth = 5.8% + 7.5% = 13.3%. Using 6.5% growth instead: 5.8% + 6.5% = 12.3%. At 6.0% target OEY (appropriate for 6.5% grower): FV = $135. This is BEFORE governance discount.
  - **DCF base case:** The tool produces $138.52 at base case, which essentially equals the current price -- no margin of safety from DCF alone.
  - **Peer comparison:** Thesis uses 20x FCF for "modest re-rate." But at 6-7% revenue growth and 18% FCF margin, PAYC is NOT a growth compounder. Paychex (PAYX) trades at 22x FCF but has 30% FCF margin. ADP at 25x has 18% FCF margin but $15B revenue and 6% growth. Paylocity at 30x grows 15%. PAYC's 18x is appropriate for its growth/margin profile, not a discount.
  - **Morningstar FV $130** (moat = NONE). Cantor Fitzgerald PT $115. Mizuho $120. Jefferies $130. The FA's $140 is the HIGHEST among these bearish analysts.
  - **My independent calculation:** OEY at 6.0% yield = $135 * 0.95 governance = $128. DCF base at 5% growth / 9% WACC = $138 (tool). Peer at 18x FCF = $133. Weighted 50/25/25 = $132. With -5% governance = $125.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolution sugerida:** Post-DA FV should be $125-130. The R3's $135 was reasonable; the R1 REFRESH at $140 overcorrected upward. At $123 market price, MoS vs $125-130 is only 2-5% -- still insufficient for a Tier A borderline position requiring 15-20% MoS. Entry at $105-115 remains appropriate.

### 4. "Revenue retention improving to 91% validates the thesis"

- **FA's claim:** Retention improved from 90% to 91% in FY2025. Beti cannibalization is largely resolved. GM recovered to 83.2%.
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - 91% is GROSS retention. For mid-market SaaS, 91% = 9% annual dollar churn. Elite SaaS companies (VEEV, NOW) maintain 95%+ gross retention. The trajectory was 94% (2021) -> 91% (2022) -> 90% (2023-2024) -> 91% (2025). The improvement from 90% to 91% is within measurement noise; the 3pp structural decline from 94% is the signal.
  - Beti-specific retention is 99%. This means non-Beti client retention is SIGNIFICANTLY below 91% (possibly 85-87%). As Beti adoption saturates, the blend should improve -- but the non-Beti segment reveals the competitive vulnerability of the broader product without the Beti lock-in effect.
  - Deferred revenue declined 5.7% while revenue grew 8.9% in FY2025. Receivables grew 14.5% vs revenue 8.9%. Both signal deteriorating revenue quality and weaker forward bookings.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolution sugerida:** Do not treat 91% as thesis confirmation. Maintain KC#9 at 89%. Add monitoring flag: if retention does not reach 92%+ by FY2027, the "improving trajectory" narrative is falsified.

### 5. "Insider activity has shifted to grants, reversing the sell pattern"

- **FA's claim:** Recent insider activity is "almost entirely stock GRANTS" with 198.8K shares granted vs 5K sold. The sell:buy ratio "has fundamentally changed."
- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - **Stock grants are NOT open-market purchases.** They are compensation. The board granted Richison 72K shares (Feb 18) and 43K more (Feb 10). These are RSU vests or awards -- they cost Richison nothing and dilute shareholders. Characterizing them as reversing the 128:1 sell:buy ratio is methodologically incorrect (Error #51 on insider analysis -- 10b5-1 verification protocol applies).
  - The actual open-market activity remains bearish: CFO Foster sold 1K shares at $162.66 (Dec 10), COO Peck sold 2K shares at $165-166 (Dec 5). These are small but 100% of open-market transactions were SELLS.
  - Over 18 months: net sales of 549,300 shares. Direction of voluntary, cash-in-pocket activity remains consistently outward.
  - Richison still holds 5.94M shares (~12.6%) worth ~$730M. Alignment is real despite selling. But the DIRECTION of open-market activity does not support reducing the governance discount.
- **Severidad:** MODERATE
- **Resolution sugerida:** Maintain governance discount at -5% (not -2.5%). The insider activity argument was the weakest part of the R1 REFRESH's FV increase from $115 to $140.
