## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. DBRS Credit Ratings Growth is Sustainable at 15%+ (CRITICAL)

**FA Assumption:** DBRS grew 21.7% in FY2025, Credit maintains 15%+ in base case, regulatory NRSRO status creates moat.

**Evidence Against:**
- **Bloomberg (Feb 2, 2026) documented that DBRS ratings are ON AVERAGE ONE FULL NOTCH HIGHER than S&P, Moody's, and Fitch.** This is the textbook definition of ratings inflation.
- **"Ratings shopping" is accelerating:** 111 single-rated deals in Canada in 2025 (more than previous two years combined). DBRS handled 80%+ of them. Companies choose DBRS specifically BECAUSE it gives higher grades.
- **Quote from Bloomberg/CMT:** "It is concerning that the primary rating agency they use, DBRS, seems to systematically offer better ratings... this could artificially inflate the credit quality of Canadian bond indices while offering firms opportunities to arbitrage ratings in a way that disadvantages investors."
- **This is the EXACT dynamic that destroyed ratings credibility pre-2008.** If regulators (OSFI, CSA, or SEC) begin investigating ratings shopping or if a wave of DBRS-rated Canadian bonds default, the reputational damage extends to ALL of Morningstar.
- **Severity: CRITICAL** -- DBRS is 14% of revenue and the fastest-growing segment. If growth was fueled by ratings lenience rather than genuine competitive advantage, the 21.7% growth rate is unsustainable AND creates tail risk.

**Resolution:** Investment committee should investigate: (a) What % of DBRS revenue comes from single-rated Canadian deals? (b) Has DBRS experienced higher default rates vs peers on equivalently-rated bonds? (c) Is there any regulatory scrutiny underway? Until answered, DBRS growth should be modeled at 8-10% (industry rate), not 15-21%.

### 2. PitchBook Moat is Intact vs S&P Preqin ($3.2B) (HIGH)

**FA Assumption:** PitchBook maintains 8.6% growth, 90%+ retention, strong moat in private markets data.

**Evidence Against:**
- **PitchBook renewal rate decelerated from 112% (2023) to 107% (2024) to 103% (2025).** That is a 9pp decline in two years. At this trajectory, net revenue retention hits 100% (zero growth from existing customers) by 2027.
- **BlackRock acquired Preqin for $3.2B in 2024**, integrating it into the world's largest asset manager. Preqin + BlackRock's distribution network + Aladdin platform creates a competitor with access to proprietary data (actual LP returns) that PitchBook cannot replicate.
- **S&P Capital IQ continues to close feature gaps**, and with LSEG/Refinitiv also investing heavily in private markets data, PitchBook faces pricing pressure from both above (Bloomberg Terminal bundles) and below (emerging niche competitors).
- **PitchBook is 27% of revenue** -- a deceleration from 8.6% toward 5% would reduce consolidated growth by ~1pp.

**Severity: HIGH** -- The renewal rate trend is empirically documented (company's own filings). The S&P/Preqin competitive threat is structural, not speculative.

**Resolution:** Model PitchBook growth at 5-6% (not 8.6%), which still implies positive but decelerating contribution. Monitor Q1 2026 renewal rates closely.

### 4. CRSP Accretion of $14/share is Speculative (MODERATE)

**FA Assumption:** CRSP adds ~$55M revenue at 15-20% CAGR, justifying a $14 premium over the $181 weighted methodology FV.

**Evidence Against:**
- CRSP acquisition closed Feb 2, 2026. There is ZERO post-acquisition financial data. The $55M revenue base and 15-20% growth are management estimates, not proven.
- **Integration risk is real:** Rebranding CRSP indexes under Morningstar carries brand transition risk. CRSP has 97 years of academic heritage. Vanguard signed an agreement, but terms and duration are not fully disclosed.
- **Vanguard concentration risk:** A significant portion of CRSP's $4.2T in linked assets comes from Vanguard. If Vanguard decides to build proprietary indexes (as BlackRock has done with iShares), the revenue base could shrink materially.
- **The thesis explicitly noted DCF sensitivity is HIGH (TV 74.5% of EV, FV Spread 85%).** Adding a speculative $14 premium on top of an already uncertain base estimate is aggressive.
- **$365M acquisition at 6.6x revenue** for an index business is not cheap. S&P/MSCI/FTSE Russell trade at similar or lower revenue multiples with much larger scale.

**Severity: MODERATE** -- The CRSP deal is reasonable strategically, but adding $14 to FV before ANY post-acquisition data is available violates conservative valuation discipline.

**Resolution:** Remove the CRSP accretion premium from FV until Q2 2026 results demonstrate actual revenue contribution. Base FV = $181, not $195.

- The thesis notes this risk as "MEDIUM" but the deceleration from ~8% (2022-2023) to 5.4% (2025) is a TREND, not noise. If Direct Platform growth falls to 3-4%, consolidated organic growth drops below 7%.
- **Morningstar's own research** (their AI moat analysis of 132 companies) established that AI commoditizes proprietary data and workflows. They are writing their own bear case.

**Severity: MODERATE** -- The deceleration is gradual, not catastrophic. But it challenges the "9% organic growth" assumption.

**Resolution:** Model Direct Platform at 3-4% growth (mature product in competitive market), reducing consolidated organic growth to ~7%.
