### Why It Fell 42% from $315

The decline from $315 (mid-2024 peak) to $183 is driven by:

1. **Multiple compression from euphoria:** MORN traded at 35-40x P/E in 2024, pricing in aggressive margin expansion. At 21x P/E today, the multiple has normalized to historical levels -- this is healthy, not alarming.
2. **Sustainalytics restructuring:** ESG demand softened globally. Morningstar retired its second-party opinions product (Q1 2026). Revenue declining in this segment.
3. **Broad fintech/data derating:** Sector peers also corrected: FDS -52%, FICO -33%. Not MORN-specific.
4. **CRSP acquisition debt:** Debt rose from $699M to $1.07B to fund $365M CRSP deal + $787M buybacks. Leverage 1.2x ND/EBITDA is manageable but represents a step-up.
5. **Growth deceleration concern:** Revenue growth slowed from 11.6% (2024) to 7.5% (2025). Market may fear further deceleration.
6. **Geopolitical/tariff overhang:** Broad US equity weakness in Q1 2026 amplified the decline.

**My contra-thesis:** The 42% decline is primarily multiple compression from an inflated 2024 peak, NOT fundamental deterioration. Operating margins expanded, Credit grew 21.7%, PitchBook maintained 8.6% growth, and CRSP acquisition transforms the index business. At 21x P/E, the market prices in very modest growth (~5.6% implied FCF growth) while the company delivers 8-9% organic. The risk/reward has improved materially.

## Moat Assessment

**Moat: WIDE**

1. **Proprietary Data (STRONG):** 75+ year track record of investment research data. Morningstar Star Ratings are industry standard for fund evaluation. CRSP data goes back to 1925 -- irreplaceable academic heritage.

2. **Switching Costs (STRONG):** Direct Platform and PitchBook have 90%+ retention. Institutional workflows built around Morningstar data. Retraining costs, API integrations, regulatory reporting dependencies.

3. **Network Effects (MODERATE):** Credit ratings benefit from network effects -- more issuers using DBRS means more investors demanding DBRS coverage. Index licensing: more AUM linked to Morningstar/CRSP indexes = higher switching costs for fund providers.

4. **Brand/Trust (STRONG):** 47% insider ownership (founder still holds 14.6M shares) signals alignment. "Morningstar" is synonymous with independent investment research. Brand trust is critical in ratings (post-2008 lesson).

5. **Regulatory Moat (MODERATE):** NRSRO designation for DBRS creates regulatory barrier. SEC-registered. Compliance requirements deter new entrants.

**Moat Risks:**
- AI could commoditize basic star ratings and fund analysis (LOW probability, 3-5yr)
- Bloomberg/Capital IQ aggressive AI tools (MEDIUM probability for Direct Platform competitive pressure)
- PitchBook faces emerging competitors in private markets data (Preqin, recently acquired by S&P)

**DCF is HIGH SENSITIVITY.** FV Spread >60% and TV >70% of EV means the DCF output is UNRELIABLE as a single point estimate. I will use it as a range ($138-$235) and weight the bear-base portion more heavily per S202 anti-bullish-bias protocol.

**My DCF adjustment (anti-bullish 60/40):**
- Bear FV: $138

### Method 2: Owner Earnings Yield + Growth (40% weight) -- Quality compounder approach

```
Owner Earnings = FCF - Maintenance Capex
FCF (FY2025): $443M
Depreciation: ~$120M (from financial statements)
Maintenance Capex = Depreciation x 1.1 = $132M
Actual Capex = ~$147M
Owner Earnings = $443M - $132M + $120M = $431M (using FCF directly as it already nets out capex)

Actually: OE = Net Income + D&A - Maintenance Capex
Net Income = $365M (from EPS $8.87 x ~41.1M shares)
D&A = ~$120M
Maintenance Capex = ~$132M
OE = $365M + $120M - $132M = $353M

OE Yield = $353M / $7.5B market cap = 4.7%
Expected Growth = 9%
OE Yield + Growth = 13.7% vs WACC 9.5% = +4.2pp spread

Implied FV using OEY target of 4.0% (compounder with 9% growth):
FV = $353M / 0.04 = $8.83B market cap
FV/share = $8.83B / 41.1M = ~$215
```

But applying anti-bullish 60/40:
- Bear OEY FV (5.5% yield target): $353M / 0.055 = $6.42B / 41.1M = $156
- Base OEY FV (4.0% yield target): $215
- 60/40: $156 x 0.6 + $215 x 0.4 = $93.6 + $86 = **$180**

Wait -- the 60/40 on OEY produces $180, which is close to DCF method 60/40 of $154. These two methods diverge meaningfully. The DCF is more pessimistic because of its high sensitivity to terminal value assumptions.

### Method 3: EV/EBIT Normalized (cross-check)

```
EBIT (FY2025 adjusted): $583M
Sector EV/EBIT range for data/analytics: 15-22x
MORN quality (Tier B, 47% insider, WIDE moat): justified at 17-18x

EV = $583M x 17.5x = $10.2B
Equity = $10.2B - $732M net debt = $9.47B
FV/share = $9.47B / 41.1M = $230

Bear (14x): EV $8.16B, Equity $7.43B, FV = $181
Base (17.5x): $230
60/40 bear+base: $181 x 0.6 + $230 x 0.4 = $108.6 + $92 = $201
```
