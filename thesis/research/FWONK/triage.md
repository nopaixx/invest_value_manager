## FWONK -- Liberty Formula One Group
**QS Tool:** 28/100 (Tier D)
**QS Adjusted:** 40/100 (Tier C) -- Adjustment: +8 for monopoly market position (unscored by tool, F1 is the only premier open-wheel series globally), +4 for GM premium structural distortion (team prize money ~50% of revenue is contractual cost, not competitive weakness; underlying OIBDA margin 27% is strong for sports media). Total +12 points. Tier D to Tier C.

**Price:** $87.17 (EUR 74.01) | **52wH:** $109.36 | **52wL:** $75.26
**FV:** $80-85 (midpoint ~$82) | **MoS:** -3% to -6% (slightly overvalued)
**E[CAGR]:** ~6% (insufficient -- below 12% Tier A and 15% Tier B thresholds)

**Business:** Liberty Formula One Group is a tracking stock for Liberty Media's ownership of Formula One, the world's premier open-wheel racing series. F1 is a monopoly scarcity asset with 750M+ global fanbase, 24 races per year across 20+ countries, and contracted revenue streams from media rights (~38%), race promotion fees (~28%), and sponsorship (~34%). Revenue grew from $2.1B (2021) to $3.7B (2024), CAGR +19.6%, driven by new media deals (ESPN in US, international expansion), race calendar growth, and surging sponsorship from the Netflix "Drive to Survive" effect.

**Unit Economics:** F1 distributes ~50% of revenue to teams as prize money (contractual via Concorde Agreement, renewed through 2030+). Remaining ~50% goes to F1 as gross profit. Adjusted OIBDA margin has expanded from ~16% (2022) to ~27% (H1 2025). Low capex needs (~$55M H1 2025). FCF conversion improving.

**Bull:** 2026 is a catalyst-rich year -- new technical regulations (more exciting racing), Cadillac entry as 11th team (US growth), Audi takeover of Sauber, Disney licensing deal, PepsiCo through 2030, F1 TV subscriber growth. Revenue could reach $4.5-5B by 2026-2027. New Concorde Agreement (2026+) is "financially attractive to all parties." Contracted revenue streams provide visibility.

**Bear:** ROIC persistently below WACC (2.1% vs 7.4%). Heavy leverage at 4.5x ND/EBITDA. EPS erratic (negative in 2021 and 2024). Goodwill ~35% of assets from acquisitions. No dividends, no buybacks -- shareholders rely entirely on price appreciation. Current EV/EBITDA of 25-30x prices in significant growth already. Liberty Media holding structure adds complexity (tracking stock, not pure-play). MotoGP acquisition (July 2025) adds integration risk and more debt.

**KCs:**
1. EV/EBITDA expands above 30x without corresponding EBITDA growth (bubble territory)
2. ROIC fails to reach WACC within 3 years (capital destruction)
3. 2026 regulation changes REDUCE viewership/sponsorship instead of increasing it (competitive balance disruption backfires)

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres
- Liberty Media's tracking stock structure makes financial analysis imprecise. Consolidated figures mix F1, Liberty Live, and corporate overhead.
- The "right" EV/EBITDA multiple for a monopoly sports franchise is debatable. Traditional 15-20x or scarcity-premium 25-30x? The answer dramatically changes FV.
- ROIC may structurally never exceed WACC due to goodwill from Liberty's acquisition of F1. This is an accounting artifact, not operational reality -- but it IS real capital that was deployed.

### Anomalias
- QS tool scored 0/8 for market position because it requires manual input. For a global monopoly, this is a significant gap. The tool should perhaps flag "manual input needed" rather than defaulting to 0.
- P/E shows as N/A because EPS was negative in 2024 (-$0.13). Yet the business generated $492M FCF. This EPS/FCF disconnect is typical of media companies with heavy D&A.

### Sugerencias
- For sports/entertainment monopolies, the QS framework structurally underscores (low ROIC from goodwill, team payments reduce GM). A "scarcity/monopoly" adjustment category might be worth considering, though it risks becoming a loophole for justifying expensive stocks.
