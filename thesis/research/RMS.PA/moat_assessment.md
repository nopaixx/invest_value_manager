# Moat Assessment: RMS.PA (Hermes International)

## Fecha: 2026-03-06

## Clasificacion: WIDE

**Note on "Ultra-Wide":** The R1 thesis calls this "Ultra-Wide." I classify it as WIDE -- the highest formal tier in our framework -- but resist the temptation to invent a new category. The moat is genuinely exceptional, among the strongest in global equities. But I have found evidence of early erosion signals (Birkin resale premium compression) that the R1 thesis understated. The moat is wide, not invincible.

## Birkin Secondary Market: Early Erosion Signal (INDEPENDENT FINDING)

This is where my assessment DIVERGES from the R1 thesis, which mentions secondary market premium but does not quantify the decline.

| Metric | 2018 | 2022 (Peak) | Nov 2025 | Trend |
|--------|------|-------------|----------|-------|
| Avg Birkin/Kelly resale multiple vs retail | 1.9x | 2.2x | 1.4x | DOWN 36% from peak |
| Birkin Togo 30 resale premium | ~70% | ~70% | 0% (1.0x retail) | PREMIUM COLLAPSED |
| Overall Hermes value retention | 150%+ | 160%+ | 138% | Still best in class but declining |

**What this means for the moat:**

The Birkin Togo 30 -- the most iconic, standard Birkin -- now resells at approximately RETAIL PRICE. The secondary market premium that the R1 thesis cites as "50-100%" has compressed dramatically for standard models. Only rare/exotic/mini models retain strong premiums (Kelly Mini II at 282%, Sellier Birkin at 183%).

**This is NOT a moat destruction signal, but it IS an erosion signal.** The reasons:

1. **Supply increase on secondary market**: More bags in circulation from the post-COVID supercycle (2021-2023) are now hitting resale platforms.
2. **Gen Z preferences**: 72% of Gen Z luxury shoppers in a Vogue Business survey preferred a Walmart "Wirkin" dupe to a real Birkin. This generation does not yet have the wealth to participate, but their cultural values may differ from prior generations.
3. **End of luxury supercycle**: Bain reports the luxury market contracted 3% in early 2025, losing ~50 million customers globally.
4. **Aspirational luxury pullback**: The decline is concentrated in ASPIRATIONAL luxury. Hermes is positioned at the TOP of ultra-luxury, which is more resilient -- but the resale premium compression suggests even ultra-luxury is not immune.

**My interpretation:** The primary moat (brand, scarcity, allocation) remains intact. The secondary market premium is a DERIVATIVE of the moat, not the moat itself. The premium can compress while the primary moat holds -- but it is a leading indicator worth monitoring. If the premium continues compressing toward zero across ALL models (not just standard Togo), that would signal demand approaching supply, which would be the first sign of moat impairment.

## Moat Source Deep Dive

### Source 1: Intangible Assets (PRIMARY -- Brand Heritage + Governance)

**Strength: 5/5**

The Hermes brand is 188 years old. No competitor can replicate this. The brand advantage has two dimensions:

1. **Heritage as authentication**: In luxury, age = legitimacy. Louis Vuitton (1854), Chanel (1910), Gucci (1921) are the closest peers in age. Hermes (1837) predates them all. This heritage cannot be manufactured.

2. **Commandite par actions structure**: This is the GOVERNANCE moat that most analysts miss. The commandite structure means the Hermes family (H51 holding, 67%) controls ALL management decisions. Public shareholders have economic participation but zero governance power. This prevents: (a) hostile takeover (LVMH tried in 2010-2014, failed), (b) activist pressure to "maximize short-term value" by expanding production, (c) any strategic pivot away from scarcity. The commandite is the structural lock on the moat -- without it, a future management team COULD destroy the moat by overproducing.

**Durability: 20+ years.** Heritage deepens with time. Governance structure is legally protected. The only risk is family fragmentation across three branches (Dumas, Guerrand, Puech).

### Source 2: Switching Costs (via Allocation System)

**Strength: 4/5**

The allocation system creates REAL economic switching costs:

- Customers spend EUR 5,000-50,000 on "qualifying purchases" (scarves, home goods, RTW) over 1-3 years before being OFFERED a Birkin.
- Walking away means losing that investment and starting over (with no guarantee of Birkin access elsewhere, since no competitor has an equivalent product).
- The antitrust lawsuit (filed March 2024, dismissed Sept 2025, appealed Feb 2026) specifically challenges this as an illegal "tying arrangement." The court DISMISSED the case, finding plaintiffs failed to prove market power abuse. This legal validation actually STRENGTHENS the moat -- courts have said this practice is legal.

**Key risk:** If the Ninth Circuit REVERSES on appeal and forces Hermes to sell Birkins without qualifying purchases, this would erode switching costs materially. Probability: LOW (15-20%). The district court dismissal was well-reasoned.

**Durability: 15-20 years** assuming legal status quo.

### Source 3: Network Effects (Partial -- Social Status Network)

**Strength: 2.5/5**

This is NOT a traditional network effect. It is a social signaling loop:

```
More visible Birkins -> More aspirational desire -> Longer wait lists ->
More exclusivity perception -> Higher secondary market value -> More visible Birkins
```

This is a VIRTUOUS CYCLE, but it is weaker than platform network effects (Visa, Google) because:
- It depends on CULTURAL RELEVANCE, which can shift (see Gen Z data)
- It has no technological lock-in
- The cycle can reverse if the brand becomes "over-exposed" or "old money" in a negative sense

**Durability: 10-15 years.** Cultural preferences shift in generational cycles. The current cohort (Gen X, Millennials) fuels the cycle. Gen Z's relationship with luxury status symbols is more ambivalent.

| Amenaza | Probabilidad | Impacto | Horizonte | Detail |

| **1. Birkin resale premium continued decline** | MEDIUM-HIGH (50%) | MEDIUM | 2-5 years | Already underway. If Togo 30 trades below retail consistently, the "investment" narrative dies. This removes one pillar of demand (financial buyers). Core demand (UHNW status buyers) would remain. |

However: Gen Z has lower CURRENT wealth; their preferences may shift as they accumulate wealth. |
| **3. China structural slowdown** | MEDIUM (30-40%) | HIGH | 3-7 years | APAC ex-Japan is 42% of revenue. China luxury market contracted 3-5% in 2025 (Bain). If "common prosperity" policies persist and Chinese UHNW spending on Western luxury structurally declines, this is Hermes's largest revenue risk. |
| **4. Antitrust (Ninth Circuit appeal)** | LOW (15-20%) | MEDIUM-HIGH | 1-3 years | If court forces Hermes to decouple Birkin access from qualifying purchases, switching costs erode and the allocation system (a core moat source) is dismantled. |
| **5. Family governance fragmentation** | LOW (10-15%) | VERY HIGH | 10-20 years | Three family branches must stay aligned. Generational transfer to 7th generation introduces risk. If H51 fractures, the commandite structure could be unwound, opening door to hostile takeover or production expansion. |
| **6. Counterfeiting + AI-replica quality** | LOW (10%) | LOW-MEDIUM | 5-10 years | Physical craftsmanship is hard to replicate, and Hermes customers buy from STORES, not open market. The risk is mostly to brand perception, not direct sales. |

**#1 Threat: The convergence of resale premium decline + Gen Z taste shift.** Either alone is manageable. Together over 10-15 years, they could shift the demand curve from "always exceeds supply" to "matches supply," which would fundamentally alter the moat. I rate this convergence scenario at 20-25% probability over 15 years.

## Escenarios de Erosion

### Scenario 1: Gradual Normalization (MOST LIKELY, 40% probability)

The Birkin secondary premium continues compressing toward 0-20% for standard models. Gen Z partially adopts luxury aspiration as they gain wealth, but at lower intensity than Millennials. Growth decelerates to 5-7% CXR. Margins hold at 39-41%. P/E compresses to 30-35x. Hermes remains an excellent business but no longer trades at "invincibility premium." Moat NARROWS slightly (from Wide to "Strong Wide") but remains intact.

**Moat rating in this scenario: WIDE (but lower-end of Wide)**

### Scenario 2: Demand-Supply Equilibrium (TAIL RISK, 15% probability)

China luxury collapse + Gen Z indifference + resale market saturation combine to reduce demand growth to 2-3%. Production capacity catches up. Wait lists shrink from 2+ years to 6-12 months. Allocation system becomes less necessary. Birkins become "available" -- still expensive, still beautiful, but no longer scarce. The Veblen dynamic breaks.

**Moat rating in this scenario: NARROW (brand remains, but scarcity premium and switching costs erode)**

## Comparison: Hermes vs Ferrari (RACE.MI)

Both are in our luxury-pricing-power basket pipeline. Key structural comparison:

| Dimension | Hermes (RMS.PA) | Ferrari (RACE.MI) |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Moat classification** | WIDE | WIDE |
| **Primary moat source** | Brand heritage + scarcity (demand management) | Brand + scarcity (supply capping at ~15K units/yr) |
| **ROIC** | 49.2% (declining from 64%) | 24.2% (improving from 19%) |
| **ROIC trajectory** | DOWN (cash accumulation + growth decel) | UP (operating leverage + pricing) |
| **Gross Margin** | 71.1% (stable) | 51.7% (expanding) |
| **Pricing power mechanism** | Veblen goods + allocation system | Limited editions + personalization + wait lists |
| **Customer base risk** | Generational shift risk (Gen Z ambivalence) | Lower risk (car enthusiasm is more durable across generations) |
| **Geographic concentration risk** | APAC 42% (China heavy) | More balanced (Europe ~35%, Americas ~30%, APAC ~25%) |
| **Resale market health** | DETERIORATING (premium compressing) | HEALTHY (used Ferraris appreciate) |
| **Balance sheet** | Net cash EUR 9.9B | Net debt 0.6x EBITDA |
| **Insider ownership** | 67% (family, commandite) | 30.5% (Exor/Agnelli) |
| **Valuation (P/E)** | 48.6x | ~50x |
| **Moat trajectory** | Stable but with early erosion signals | STRENGTHENING (EV transition as moat deepener) |

**Key comparative insight:** Ferrari's moat is STRENGTHENING because (a) margins are expanding, (b) the EV transition creates a new exclusivity layer (limited EV production), and (c) car enthusiasm has proven more durable across generations than fashion/accessories aspiration. Hermes's moat is STABLE but with early erosion signals that Ferrari does not face.

**For the basket:** Both deserve inclusion in a luxury-pricing-power basket. However, Ferrari offers a BETTER moat trajectory (improving) vs Hermes (stable-to-slightly-declining). Hermes offers a WIDER moat today (higher ROIC, higher margins, stronger governance lock). The allocation between them should reflect this: Ferrari for forward momentum, Hermes for current quality (but only at appropriate entry price).

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- **ROIC decline causation**: Is the 64% -> 49% ROIC decline purely mechanical (cash pile growth) or does it contain a real operational component from APAC deceleration? The truth is likely both, but I cannot cleanly separate the two without detailed invested capital breakdowns.
- **Gen Z data reliability**: The "72% prefer dupes" survey is a single data point from Vogue Business. This is a Nivel 3 source (opinion/survey). I flag it as directionally informative but not definitive. The actual purchasing behavior of Gen Z as they accumulate wealth in 10-15 years is unknowable today.
- **Birkin resale data**: Rebag and Bernstein data show premium compression, but this could partly be a normalization from a COVID-era bubble (2021-2022 luxury supercycle) rather than structural decline. Both explanations may be partially true.

### Discrepancias con Thesis
- See table above. The R1 thesis is broadly correct but overly optimistic on moat invincibility. I believe the moat is Wide with a 20-25% probability of erosion to Narrow over 15 years, primarily driven by the convergence of resale premium decline + generational taste shift + China structural risk.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- **Luxury moat framework addition**: For luxury companies, add "secondary market health" as a moat indicator. Resale premium trends are a leading indicator of brand desirability. This is specific to Veblen goods companies and would complement the existing 5-source framework.
- **Generational taste shift as moat threat category**: The current framework lists "disruption," "regulation," "new entrants," "commoditization," and "preference change." For luxury specifically, "generational taste shift" deserves explicit tracking with 10-20 year horizon assessment.
