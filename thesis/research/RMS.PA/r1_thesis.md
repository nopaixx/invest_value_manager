## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

Hermes International is a 188-year-old French luxury house operating as a Societe en Commandite par Actions (limited partnership by shares). This corporate structure is critical: the Hermes family (three branches -- Dumas, Guerrand, Puech -- united in H51 holding company) controls ~67% of shares and all management decisions through the commandite structure. Public shareholders have economic rights but limited governance influence.

**What problem it solves:** Hermes sells social signaling, craftsmanship heritage, and time-tested exclusivity to the ultra-wealthy. A Birkin bag is not a handbag -- it is a store of value, a social currency, and an investment that appreciates on the secondary market.

### The Scarcity Model (WHY Hermes is Different)

Hermes operates a fundamentally different model from every other luxury company:

1. **Production intentionally below demand.** Hermes trains artisans for 2+ years before they produce a single bag. There are ~5,800 leather artisans, each bag takes 15-20 hours by hand. Production capacity is the constraint, not demand.

2. **No wholesale.** 100% distribution is company-controlled (stores or hermes.com). No department stores, no multi-brand, no discounting.

3. **Allocation system.** You cannot walk in and buy a Birkin. You must develop a relationship with a store, purchase other items over time, and eventually be "offered" the opportunity to buy one. This inverts the traditional buyer-seller dynamic.

4. **Secondary market premium.** Birkin bags trade at 50-100% premiums on the secondary market. A Birkin 25 retails for ~EUR 10,000 and resells for EUR 15,000-20,000+. This is unique -- the product appreciates, making it a Veblen good.

5. **Pricing power is unlimited in practice.** Hermes raised prices 8-10% in 2024-2025 with zero demand impact. Because demand >> supply, price increases actually INCREASE desirability.

### Risk Detail

**1. China Structural Slowdown:**
Asia-Pac ex-Japan is 42% of revenue. China is the largest component of this. FY2025 growth of +4.9% CXR in this region (vs +12% Americas, +14% Japan) already shows pressure. Bain reports China personal luxury market contracted 3-5% in 2025. If this becomes structural (secular decline in Chinese luxury consumption due to common prosperity, wealth destruction, or cultural shift), Hermes loses its marginal growth engine. However: (a) Hermes is the MOST resilient luxury brand in China (Birkin demand unaffected), (b) UHNW Chinese population continues to grow, (c) Hermes is diversified geographically (58% non-APAC).

**2. Succession/Family Governance:**
Axel Dumas (6th generation) is CEO since 2013. The commandite structure protects family control. However: (a) family has 3 branches that must remain aligned, (b) H51 holding + Krefeld Invest multi-family office are governance structures, but generational transfer always introduces risk, (c) no public succession plan. The corporate structure (commandite par actions) means even if family disagreement occurs, public shareholders cannot influence. This is both protection (no hostile takeover) and risk (no accountability mechanism). Bloomberg reported (Feb 12, 2026) Epstein connection to Axel Dumas though Dumas said Epstein "kept trying to target him" -- reputational risk to monitor.

**3. Multiple Compression:**
P/E has fallen from ~55x (2023 peak) to 44.2x (was 48.6x on Feb 20 -- compression continuing as predicted). If market re-rates further to 35x (still premium), that is ~21% downside from current multiple. The sector is in a de-rating cycle driven by China concerns and rotation away from growth.

**4. Revenue Growth Deceleration:**
FY2025 revenue growth was 8.9% CXR (5.5% reported). This compares to 15.7% (2023) and 13.0% (2024). If growth normalizes to 6-8% (post-COVID normalization), the 44.2x P/E is harder to justify.

**5. US Tariffs:**
Americas is 19.2% of revenue. 25% tariff on EU imports could impact, though Hermes (like Ferrari) has demonstrated ability to pass through tariffs given demand >> supply. The US tariff risk is LOW for Hermes specifically because customers are price-inelastic.

**6. Iran/Hormuz Crisis (NEW -- Mar 2026):**
Operation Epic Fury, Hormuz near-total halt, oil $92 WTI. Impact on Hermes: **LOW**.
- UHNW clients do not cancel luxury purchases over geopolitical events
- Oil wealth effect is actually POSITIVE for Middle East UHNW spending (+14.9% growth in ME region)
- Hermes production is 100% in France (no supply chain disruption from Hormuz)
- Secondary effects via risk-off market selloff already partially reflected in -8.9% price decline
- If Iran crisis extends to broader recession (oil >$100 sustained), ultra-luxury dips 5-10% max historically
- **Net assessment: Iran crisis is a BUYING OPPORTUNITY catalyst for Hermes, not a thesis risk**
