## Risk 1: China Structural Slowdown (HIGH)

**What:** Asia-Pacific ex-Japan represents 42% of revenue (EUR 6.7B). China is the dominant market within APAC. The China personal luxury goods market contracted 3-5% in 2025 (Bain). If this becomes structural (not cyclical), Hermes loses its marginal growth engine.

**Evidence:**
- APAC ex-Japan grew only 4.9% CXR in FY2025 (slowest region)
- Bain reports 3-5% contraction in China personal luxury in 2025
- China property crisis, high youth unemployment, "common prosperity" policies
- CEO Dumas: "positive signs" but acknowledges "aspirational customers pulled back"

**Mitigants:**
- Hermes targets UHNW, not aspirational (most resilient segment)
- Local customer base (not tourist-dependent like European competitors)
- Supply discipline: if demand wavers, Hermes moderates output, does not discount
- 58% of revenue is non-APAC, providing geographic diversification
- Middle East (+14.9%), Americas (+12.4%), Japan (+14.1%) compensate

**Kill Condition trigger level:** China revenue decline >10% for 2 consecutive half-years

## Risk 2: Multiple Compression (MEDIUM-HIGH)

**What:** P/E has compressed from ~55x (2023 peak) to 44.2x and continues declining. The de-rating cycle is driven by China concerns, rotation away from growth, and luxury sector normalization.

**Evidence:**
- P/E trajectory: 55x (2023) -> 48.6x (Feb 2026) -> 44.2x (Mar 2026)
- Luxury sector broadly de-rating (-30% from 2023 peaks)
- Reverse DCF: current price implies 22.4% FCF growth vs 8.7% historical
- If P/E compresses to 35x (still premium): ~21% downside from current

**Mitigants:**
- The compression IS what creates our eventual entry opportunity
- Quality justifies premium: QS 96, ROIC 49%, 71% GM
- At 30-35x, Hermes would be attractively valued on absolute metrics
- The "patience path" (2-3 years of compression + earnings growth) leads to entry

**Impact on thesis:** This is the PRIMARY driver of our waiting strategy. The compression is EXPECTED and DESIRED for us.

## Risk 3: Succession/Family Governance (MEDIUM-HIGH)

**What:** Axel Dumas (6th generation) leads since 2013. Three family branches (Dumas, Guerrand, Puech) are united in H51 holding. Generational transfer to 7th generation introduces fragmentation risk.

**Evidence:**
- No public succession plan
- Nicolas Puech (5-6% individual stake) has expressed intention to donate shares to foundation (potential alignment risk)
- Commandite structure protects from external threats but creates internal dependency on family cohesion
- Bloomberg: Epstein connection to Dumas (reputational, Dumas said Epstein "kept trying to target him")

**Mitigants:**
- Commandite par actions structure is legally robust
- H51 holding company unifies family stakes
- Family has managed generational transitions for 188 years
- Professional management layer exists alongside family leadership
- 67% family ownership = massive skin in the game

**Kill Condition trigger level:** Family ownership drops below 50%

## Risk 4: Revenue Growth Deceleration (MEDIUM)

**What:** Revenue growth has decelerated from 15.7% (2023) to 13.0% (2024) to 8.9% CXR (2025). Post-COVID normalization may settle growth at 6-8% CXR sustainably.

**Evidence:**
- Clear downtrend: 15.7% -> 13.0% -> 8.9% (CXR)
- TAM growing only 2-3% (mature market)
- China deceleration is the primary drag
- Pricing power alone contributes 5-6% (floor for growth)

**Mitigants:**
- 8.9% CXR is still EXCELLENT for a EUR 16B business
- Pricing power provides 5-6% floor (protected by moat)
- Market share gains add 1-2% (4.3% -> still growing)
- New store openings in Middle East, India provide growth optionality
- Leather goods (+13.1%) remains the growth engine

**Impact on thesis:** Growth deceleration is ALREADY in our projections (8-9% base case). If it drops below 6%, FV estimate would decline by ~10-15%.

## Risk 5: Birkin Resale Premium Erosion (MEDIUM)

**What:** The secondary market premium for standard Birkin models has compressed dramatically from 2.2x retail (2022 peak) to 1.4x average, with Birkin Togo 30 now at 1.0x retail (zero premium).

**Evidence (from Moat Assessment):**
- Birkin Togo 30: ~70% premium (2022) -> 0% premium (Nov 2025)
- Overall Hermes value retention: 160%+ (2022) -> 138% (Nov 2025)
- More supply hitting secondary market from post-COVID supercycle
- Aspirational buyer pullback reducing speculative demand

**Mitigants:**
- Rare/exotic models retain strong premiums (Kelly Mini II 282%, Sellier 183%)
- Primary sales unaffected (demand still exceeds supply)
- Resale premium is derivative of moat, not moat itself
- Normalization from COVID-era bubble may explain most of decline
- Hermes does not earn revenue from secondary market

**Kill Condition trigger level:** Birkin secondary market premium collapses below 10% across ALL models

## Risk 6: Antitrust (Ninth Circuit Appeal) (LOW-MEDIUM)

**What:** The Birkin allocation system (requiring qualifying purchases before being offered a Birkin) was challenged as an illegal tying arrangement. District court dismissed Sept 2025. Plaintiffs appealed Feb 2026 to the Ninth Circuit.

**Evidence:**
- Lawsuit filed March 2024
- District court dismissed with well-reasoned opinion
- Appeal filed February 2026
- If court forces decoupling, allocation system (core moat source) is dismantled

**Mitigants:**
- District court dismissal was thorough
- No precedent for luxury allocation being ruled anti-competitive
- Even if allocation is forced to change, the scarcity model (production constraints) remains
- Other luxury houses would also be affected (industry-wide risk, not Hermes-specific)

**Impact if realized:** Switching costs component of moat erodes. Margin impact minimal (allocation drives qualifying purchases, which would decrease). Revenue impact 5-10% medium-term.

## Risk 7: US Tariffs Escalation (LOW-MEDIUM)

**What:** Americas is 19.2% of revenue. 25% tariff on EU imports could impact.

**Evidence:**
- Current 15% EU surcharge in effect
- US-EU tariff deadline June 1, 2026
- Ferrari demonstrated full pass-through (10% price hike, zero demand impact)

**Mitigants:**
- Hermes customers are price-inelastic (UHNW clientele)
- Demand exceeds supply -- tariff pass-through is structurally easier
- EUR/USD at 1.16 partially offsets
- Only 19% revenue exposure (diversified)

## Risk 8: Generational Taste Shift (LOW -- very long-term)

**What:** Vogue Business survey: 72% of Gen Z luxury shoppers preferred Walmart "Wirkin" dupe to real Birkin. If this generation never develops Birkin aspiration, demand pipeline shrinks in 10-15 years.

**Evidence:**
- Single survey data point (Nivel 3 source -- directional, not definitive)
- Gen Z currently lacks wealth to be Hermes customers
- Cultural shift toward experiences over physical luxury goods

**Mitigants:**
- Preferences may shift as Gen Z accumulates wealth (historically, luxury aspiration correlates with wealth)
- Gen Alpha is TBD
- UHNW population growing 5% annually regardless of generational preferences
- 10-15 year horizon exceeds our typical investment timeframe

## Convergent Risk Scenarios

### Scenario A: "Controlled Deceleration" (50% probability)
Growth normalizes to 6-8% CXR. Multiple compresses to 30-35x over 2-3 years. Margins stable. Hermes remains excellent but re-rates to fair value. Stock reaches EUR 1,400-1,600 by 2028.
**Impact:** Creates our entry opportunity via the "patience path."

### Scenario B: "China + Gen Z Convergence" (15% probability)
China luxury market structurally contracts. Gen Z never adopts ultra-luxury aspiration. Growth drops to 4-5%. Multiple compresses to 25-28x. Secondary market premiums collapse. Stock reaches EUR 900-1,100.
**Impact:** Entry opportunity BUT moat partially impaired. Would need to reassess FV.

### Scenario C: "Status Quo" (30% probability)
China stabilizes. Market maintains 38-42x multiple. Revenue grows 9-10%. Stock stays EUR 1,800-2,200. We never reach entry.
**Impact:** We miss Hermes. This is the opportunity cost of discipline.

### Scenario D: "Crisis Crash" (5% probability)
Iran escalation + recession + luxury sector crash. Stock drops 30-40% to EUR 1,150-1,350. Fundamentals unchanged.
**Impact:** Dream entry. BUY immediately.

## Value Trap Assessment

**Value Trap Score: 0/10** -- Zero red flags.

This is NOT a value trap situation because Hermes is not cheap. The question is not "is this a trap?" but "when does the premium become reasonable?" The risks identified are all about OVERVALUATION and GROWTH TRAJECTORY, not about quality deterioration.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The Birkin resale premium data is from Rebag/Bernstein (Nov 2025). More recent data would be valuable to confirm if compression has continued, stabilized, or reversed.
- Gen Z taste data is a single survey. This is a weak data point that could be confirmation bias for the bear case. Would benefit from 2-3 additional data sources.
- The China risk is well-documented but Hermes management insists their UHNW clientele is distinct from the broader luxury market. This could be accurate (Hermes really is different) or could be management complacency.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- For luxury stocks, add "secondary market health" as a standard risk indicator (resale premium tracking)
- Track generational taste data as a long-term moat threat indicator for brands
