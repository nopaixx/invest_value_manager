# Counter-Analysis: CPRT (Copart, Inc.)

## Date: 2026-02-25

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The R1 thesis presents CPRT as a high-quality compounder (QS 83, Tier A) in an unassailable duopoly with secular tailwinds. After independent adversarial research, I find the thesis has **material gaps in three areas**: (1) the competitive threat from RB Global/IAA is significantly underestimated -- Progressive is actively routing volume away from Copart, and RB Global is targeting a return to 50/50 market dynamics from current 35/65; (2) the operating margin compression (39.3% -> 34.7% most recent quarter) may not be purely "growth capex" as claimed but partially structural/competitive; and (3) the OEY valuation method (50% weight, producing $44 FV) is aggressively parameterized and creates an upward bias. The thesis survives scrutiny as a quality business, but the fair value of $40 is optimistic. My independent bear valuation produces $28-30, and the market at $35.28 is pricing the business reasonably given current headwinds.

The market is pricing in growth that is ABOVE recent delivery but below long-term history. This is not obviously cheap.

## Assumptions Key Challenged

### 1. "Unassailable Duopoly with ~40% Market Share"

- **Claim:** R1 states CPRT is "one half of an unassailable duopoly" and IAA integration into RB Global "may cause share GAINS for CPRT."
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - **RB Global is actively rebuilding and gaining share.** After two years of post-IAA integration disruption, 2026 is RB Global's first "clean year." Bank of America upgraded RBA to Outperform, projecting mid-single-digit GTV growth in 2026 with stronger gains in 2027. ([Transportation Today](https://transportationtodaynews.com/news/36872-rb-global-rebuild-takes-hold-as-shifting-progressive-behavior-puts-new-pressure-on-copart/))
  - **Progressive Insurance is routing MORE volume to RB Global/IAA.** RB Global now holds approximately 90% of Progressive's salvage unit volume (up from ~75%). Progressive is the fastest-growing major auto insurer. This is a MATERIAL shift the R1 thesis completely ignores.
  - **RB Global has secured multiyear agreements with its two largest partners.** This provides greater future volume visibility and supports their strategy to increase market share.
  - **National Bank Financial upgraded RBA** specifically on market share gains thesis.
  - **RB Global's target is to push back toward 50/50** from current 35/65. Even a shift to 40/60 means CPRT loses ~5pp market share -- a kill condition (#3) that the R1 thesis assigned 40% probability to IAA "fumbling," when the evidence suggests the OPPOSITE.
  - **IAA Total Loss Predictor (AI tool)** launching in 2026 for dynamic vehicle routing -- competitive innovation that reduces carrier costs.
  - **JPMorgan lowered CPRT price target to $34** (below current price), citing competition and volume declines.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution suggested:** The R1 thesis should reverse its assumption about IAA integration. Rather than assuming "disruption helps CPRT," the evidence strongly suggests RB Global is recovering and actively taking share. KC#3 (5pp share loss) should be monitored much more closely. The probability of competitive dynamics shifting should be raised from 40% to 55-60%.

### 2. "Total Loss Frequency is a 20-Year Secular Tailwind at 24.2%"

- **Claim:** R1 relies heavily on TLF rising from 15.6% (2015) to 24.2% (2025) as an enduring secular trend.
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - **EV battery costs declining rapidly.** Battery pack prices reached ~$80/kWh in 2026, projected $69/kWh by 2030 (BloombergNEF). Module-level repairs ($2,000-$5,000) and refurbished batteries (30-50% savings) are becoming viable alternatives to total loss declarations. As EVs grow from ~8% to 20-30% of the fleet by 2030, cheaper battery repairs could REVERSE the TLF trend for the EV portion.
  - **ADAS is reducing accident frequency.** A 2023 study forecasted ADAS could prevent 37 million crashes in the US between 2021-2050. A JAMA Surgery study projects 3.6% reduction in traffic-related injuries between 2025-2035. While ADAS increases repair cost per accident (supporting TLF), it REDUCES total accident count -- which reduces total loss VOLUME absolutely.
  - **The interaction effect is uncertain.** The R1 thesis assumes ADAS net-helps CPRT (more expensive repairs = more total losses). But the math depends on the RATE of accident reduction vs the RATE of repair cost increase. If ADAS reduces accidents 15% by 2030 while only increasing TLF by 2pp (24.2% to 26.2%), TOTAL volume falls. Fewer cars * higher TLF could net to FEWER total losses in absolute terms.
  - **Vehicle age 12.8 years in 2025** -- older vehicles are LESS likely to have expensive ADAS sensors, reducing the "complexity = total loss" dynamic for a large portion of the fleet.
- **Severity:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolution suggested:** The secular TLF thesis is real but the R1 overstates its certainty by ignoring EV battery cost declines and ADAS accident reduction. The net effect over 5 years is genuinely uncertain. TLF should not be treated as a guaranteed perpetual tailwind. Include as a risk factor rather than a certainty.

### 3. "Operating Margin Compression is Growth Investment, Not Structural"

- **Claim:** R1 states margin decline from 39.3% to 36.5% is "driven by GROWTH capex in land/facilities, not deterioration."
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - **Most recent quarter (Q2 FY2026) op margin dropped to 34.7%** from 36.6% prior year. This is a 190bps YoY decline in a single quarter -- worse than the already concerning multi-year trend.
  - **Facility operations costs increased 13.7% YoY** with $56M in additional CAT-related costs in FY2025.
  - **Competitive pricing pressure.** RB Global's aggressive pricing to win back insurance carrier contracts adds cost pressure for CPRT to retain clients.
  - **International expansion is dilutive to margins.** International operations are less mature and operate at lower margins than US operations. As international grows from 18% to 25%+ of revenue (as the thesis projects), this will structurally compress blended margins.
  - **The capex/depreciation ratio of 2.6x is NOT definitively growth capex.** Without a detailed breakdown of maintenance vs growth capex from management, the R1 assumption that "most is growth" is an assertion, not a proven fact. Some portion of this elevated capex is maintaining and upgrading an aging yard network.
  - The R1 then projects margins EXPANDING from 36.0% to 38.0% by FY2030 -- against a 4-year trend of compression and with competitive headwinds intensifying. This is aspirational, not evidence-based.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution suggested:** Do not project margin expansion without strong evidence. At minimum, use flat margins (36-37%) in the base case. Margin expansion should be the bull case, not the base case.

### 4. "Q2 FY2026 Miss Was CAT Comp, Not Structural"

- **Claim:** R1 dismisses the Q2 miss as purely a catastrophe comparison issue.
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - **Q1 FY2026 also showed flat revenue growth** ($1.16B YoY) despite 4.9% gross profit rise. This is NOT a CAT comp issue -- Q1 had no exceptional CAT comps.
  - **Insurance volumes down ~10% YoY** in the latest quarter (JPMorgan data). US insurance unit volumes declined ~2.8% even before CAT adjustments.
  - **Global insurance unit sales fell 8.4% in Q1 FY2026,** with US declines of 9.5%. Two consecutive quarters of declining insurance volumes is a trend, not a one-off.
  - **Consumers reducing insurance coverage and raising deductibles** -- this is not just a CAT comp dynamic. Fewer insured vehicles = fewer claims = fewer total losses. This could persist in a soft insurance market through 2027.
  - **Progressive routing more volume to IAA** compounds the organic volume decline with competitive share loss.
  - **The R1 "ex-CAT US revenue FLAT" defense** actually proves the concern: FLAT is not growth. For a company priced at 21.6x P/E with expectations of 12%+ FCF growth, flat revenue is a problem.
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution suggested:** Acknowledge that the weakness is partly cyclical (CAT comp) AND partly competitive/structural (insurance volume decline + RB Global share gains). The base case should not assume "CAT comp normalization" produces automatic growth reversion. Q3 FY2026 (reports ~May/Jun) is the real test.

### 5. "OEY Valuation at $44 (50% Weight) is Justified"

- **Claim:** R1 uses OEY as primary method (50% weight) producing $44 FV, with OE Yield target of 3.5%.
- **Evidence in contra:**
  - **A 3.5% OE Yield target for a "10% growth compounder" is aggressive.** This implies the market should value CPRT's owner earnings at ~28.5x. For context, the S&P 500 earnings yield is ~4.5%. Applying a growth premium to get to 3.5% requires the market to assign CPRT a significant premium, which it currently does NOT (21.6x P/E).
  - **The OE Yield method produced the HIGHEST value by far** ($44 vs $36 rDCF vs $33 EV/EBIT), and then received the HIGHEST weight (50%). This is a classic bias: the most generous method gets the most weight.
  - **The DCF tool's base case ($27.49) is rejected** by the R1 analyst because "it uses short trailing data." But the DCF tool uses 4 years of data and applies normalized WACC. The R1's objection is essentially: the tool gives a number I don't like, so I'll use a different method.
  - **Sensitivity analysis shows FV range of $21-$38.** At 50% weight on the $44 OEY, the weighted FV ($40) lands ABOVE the top of the DCF sensitivity range. This is a red flag.
  - **The EV/EBIT at 16x "base case"** includes a +3-4x quality premium and +2-3x growth premium on top of the 8-12x sector range. This is generous -- the justification cites ORLY/AZO (18-22x) but those are auto parts retailers with DIFFERENT business models (retail vs auction).
- **Severity:** **HIGH**
- **Resolution suggested:** Reduce OEY weight to 30%, increase EV/EBIT and rDCF weights. Use 4.0-4.5% OE Yield target (not 3.5%). Use 15x EV/EBIT (not 16x). This produces a weighted FV closer to $33-35.

1. **RB Global/Progressive dynamic is completely absent** from the R1 analysis. This is the most significant omission. Progressive actively routing volume to IAA is a competitive shift, not integration fumbling.

4. **Insider selling is noteworthy.** $16M+ in sales with zero buys is not what you expect from management at a 52-week low if they believe the stock is significantly undervalued.

The thesis does NOT face a STRONG COUNTER because:
- The business quality is genuinely high (ROIC 21%, FCF positive, net cash)
