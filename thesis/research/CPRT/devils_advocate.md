CPRT remains a genuine high-quality business (QS 83 Tier A, ROIC 21%, $5.1B net cash) operating in a real duopoly. However, three weeks after the original R2/R3, the stock has fallen ANOTHER 4% to $33.88 -- now within $0.35 of a fresh 52-week low ($33.53) -- and the bear case evidence has STRENGTHENED, not weakened. RB Global's competitive recovery is now being tracked by multiple analysts with concrete data (Progressive routing 90% of salvage to IAA, RB Global unit growth +4.7% vs CPRT -2.8%). JPMorgan cut PT to $34 and Barclays to $32 -- both BELOW market price. The R3-resolved FV of $37 still offers only 9.2% upside from $33.88, with HIGH uncertainty on competitive dynamics and insurance cycle duration. The thesis survives as a quality business play, but the margin of safety at current prices is thin and the entry should be adjusted downward.

## Assumptions Key Challenged

### 1. Competitive Threat from RB Global/IAA (SEVERITY: HIGH)

- **R3 position:** Accepted as genuine risk, probability raised to 55%, applied -$1 FV haircut.
- **New evidence since R3:**
  - RB Global unit growth is now confirmed at +4.7% YoY vs Copart at -2.8% (Yipit data, multiple analyst citations). This is a 7.5pp DELTA in volume growth between the two halves of the duopoly.
  - Progressive Insurance (fastest-growing major US auto insurer) routing ~90% of salvage volume to IAA, UP from ~75%. Progressive's growth means MORE of the incremental insurance market flows to IAA, not CPRT.
  - RB Global secured multiyear agreements with its two largest carrier partners, providing volume visibility.
  - 2026 is RB Global's first "clean year" post-IAA acquisition. Mid-single-digit GTV growth expected.
  - National Bank Financial upgraded RBA specifically on market share gains thesis.
  - IAA launching AI-based "Total Loss Predictor" tool for dynamic vehicle routing -- competitive innovation.
- **Why this matters:** The R1 thesis ASSUMED "IAA integration is disruptive, if anything" and assigned 40% probability to "IAA/RB Global fumbling." The evidence shows the OPPOSITE. The duopoly is intact, but the share split is shifting from 65/35 (CPRT/IAA) toward 60/40 or potentially 55/45. Even a 5pp shift represents ~$250M in annual fee revenue migrating to IAA.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution:** KC#3 (>5pp share loss) should be monitored quarterly. Current trajectory suggests 2-3pp shift already underway. R3 probability adjustment to 55% was appropriate; consider 60% if Q3 FY2026 shows continued volume divergence.

### 2. Insurance Volume Decline is Deeper Than CAT Comp (SEVERITY: HIGH)

- **R3 position:** Accepted as valid. Two quarters of weak volume is a trend.
- **Updated data:**
  - Q2 FY2026: Global insurance units -9% (-4% ex-CAT). US insurance units -10.7% (-4.8% ex-CAT).
  - Q1 FY2026: Global insurance unit sales fell 8.4%. US insurance units -9.5%.
  - Even ex-CAT, US insurance unit volumes are declining ~4-5% for two consecutive quarters.
  - Management attributed softness to "shifts in policies in force" and "softer overall claims activity" tied to consumers reducing coverage and raising deductibles.
  - Management claims this is cyclical. But the combined ratio for auto insurance is expected to worsen to ~99% in 2026, which could prolong the soft cycle through 2027.
  - JPMorgan cut CPRT PT to $34, specifically citing insurance volume headwinds.
  - Barclays cut PT to $32, below current market price.
- **Why this matters:** The R1 base case projects 8% revenue growth starting FY2027. This requires a full recovery from -4.8% ex-CAT insurance unit declines to positive 8%+ within 12 months. That is a ~13pp swing in volume trajectory -- possible in a hard insurance market, but the current insurance market shows NO signs of hardening. If soft market persists through FY2027, revenue growth stays at 2-4%, not 8%.
- **Severity:** HIGH

### 3. Valuation: R3 FV of $37 Still Offers Thin MoS (SEVERITY: MODERATE)

- **Current situation:** At $33.88, MoS vs R3 FV ($37) = 8.4%. This is BELOW the minimum 10-15% for Tier A under the E[CAGR] framework.
- **E[CAGR] at market price:**
  ```
  Rerating: ($37/$33.88)^(1/3) - 1 = 3.0%
  + Growth: 8% (thesis assumption)
  + Yield: 0%

  ```
  E[CAGR] 11.0% is BELOW the 12% Tier A threshold at current prices. The R3 entry of $31 would produce 14.1%, but the stock is $2.88 ABOVE that entry.
- **If growth is 5-6% (bear case insurance cycle) instead of 8%:**
  ```
  E[CAGR] = 3.0% + 5.5% = 8.5% -- well below threshold
  ```
- **The R3 entry of $31 is now 8.5% below market.** Given the stock made a new 52wL at $33.53 this month, $31 requires another 8.5% decline -- plausible in a continued sell-off but not certain.
- **Severity:** MODERATE

### 4. Operating Margin Trajectory (SEVERITY: MODERATE)

- **R3 position:** Use 36-37% terminal margins instead of 38%.
- **Updated evidence:**
  - Q2 FY2026 operating margin: 34.7% (down from 36.6% YoY). This is a FURTHER compression from the already concerning trend.
  - 5-quarter trend: 38.0% -> 37.1% -> 36.5% -> 36.6% -> 34.7%.
  - Management is cutting capex (slashed nearly in half in Q2) -- which should help FCF margins but may indicate they are responding to competitive pressure rather than simply pausing growth investment.
  - International operations (18% of revenue, growing) are structurally lower-margin than US. As international mix grows, blended margins face structural headwind.
- **Severity:** MODERATE

### 5. Insider Activity Pattern (SEVERITY: LOW -- REVISED DOWN)

  - Net insider summary: 425K shares purchased (mostly option exercises held), 149K shares sold on open market. Net POSITIVE share count.
  - Institutions hold 86.6%, insiders 8.7%. Ownership structure is healthy.
- **Severity revised to LOW** (original R2 said MODERATE, but 10b5-1 verification changes the signal from "concerning" to "neutral for CEO, mildly noteworthy for Tryforos").
