## Business Understanding

### The Business Model

**Problem solved:** Moving freight across North America's three countries on a single railroad network. This eliminates interchanges (handoffs between railroads), cutting transit times by up to 48 hours and reducing damage, cost, and complexity for shippers.

**Revenue model:** Freight transportation services. Revenue by commodity:
- Grain & fertilizers: ~25% (largest, Canadian harvest dependent)
- Intermodal: ~20% (containers, growing cross-border)
- Energy/chemicals/plastics: ~15%
- Automotive: ~10% (fastest growing post-merger)
- Forest products, metals, minerals, coal: ~30% combined

**Revenue characteristics:** Volume-driven (Revenue Ton-Miles), with pricing power from rail duopoly structure. ~60% of revenue is contractual with annual price escalators. Revenue is ~40% Canadian-origin, ~35% US-origin, ~25% Mexico-origin.

### Unit Economics

- **Operating Ratio:** 59.9% (FY2025 core adjusted) -- industry benchmark, record for CPKC
  - OR = operating expenses / revenue. Lower = better. Best Class I railroads target 55-60%.
  - Improving from 64.4% (FY2024), 65.3% (FY2023). Clear trajectory toward sub-58%.
- **Capital intensity:** HIGH. Capex ~$2.65B (2026E) = ~17% of revenue. But D&A covers ~60% of capex.
- **FCF conversion:** OCF/NI = 1.3x (good). FCF margin 14.4% (depressed by merger integration capex).
- **Network effect:** Each additional customer/route on the network increases value for ALL customers (more origin-destination pairs). This is a natural monopoly characteristic.

### Competitive Moat (WIDE)

CPKC's moat is among the widest in North American business, based on three pillars:

1. **Regulatory barriers to entry:** Building a new Class I railroad is physically and legally impossible. The Surface Transportation Board (STB) regulates mergers and new trackage. Even the CP-KCS merger took 3+ years. No new Class I railroad has been created in over a century.

2. **Efficient scale:** Rail is 3-4x more fuel-efficient than trucking per ton-mile. The fixed-cost nature of the network means incremental volume has very high margins. CPKC's 20,000-mile network is the ONLY one connecting all three USMCA countries.

3. **Switching costs:** Shippers build facilities on railroad sidings. Switching railroads means physically relocating or building new transload facilities -- costing millions and taking years. Once a shipper is on CPKC's network, they are effectively captive.

**Moat durability:** The UNP-NSC proposed merger ($85B, filed Feb 2026) could create a transcontinental competitor on the EASTERN half of North America. If approved (2027+ timeline), this would intensify competition for east-west traffic but would NOT threaten CPKC's unique north-south Mexico corridor. CPKC's Mexico franchise is irreplaceable.

### The Merger Synergy Story

The 2023 CP-KCS merger ($31B) is the defining event:
- **Synergies running at $1.2B/yr** (exiting 2025), target $1.4B by end-2026
- Split: ~60% cost savings, ~40% revenue synergies (new routes, cross-border traffic)
- Ultimate target: C$1.2B in ANNUAL synergies by 2027 (~$900M USD)
- Key new services: Mexico Midwest Express (intermodal), automotive Dallas terminal, Canadian grain to Mexico
- Network 13% faster than pre-merger, car velocity 14% higher
- Record Canadian grain harvest of 85M metric tonnes handled (previous record: 78M)

### Why It Appears Cheap (It Doesn't -- But Why the Opportunity May Come)

At $78.46 and 23.8x P/E, CPKC is NOT cheap by traditional metrics. The stock is down ~12% from 52-week high of $89.42 due to:

1. **Tariff fear:** Trump's 25% tariff on Mexican imports (with USMCA exemptions) threatens CPKC's unique cross-border advantage. Mexico is ~25% of revenue origin. Analysts cut 2025/2026 EPS estimates by 5-10%.
2. **Revenue growth deceleration:** FY2025 organic revenue growth was just 3.7% (stripping out merger consolidation effects from 2023-2024).
3. **General industrial/trade war uncertainty:** Broad Section 301 probes into 16+ economies.
4. **Oil/energy costs:** Fuel is ~10-12% of operating costs. Oil at $95-100 WTI is a headwind.

**My contra-thesis to each:**
1. Tariffs: USMCA-compliant goods are EXEMPT. ~70% of cross-border rail traffic is USMCA-compliant. The tariff impact is real but overstated by the market. Near-shoring actually INCREASES Mexico rail traffic long-term as manufacturing moves from China to Mexico.
2. Revenue deceleration: Expected post-merger consolidation effect. Synergy-driven margin improvement matters more than top-line growth at this stage. OR trajectory 65.3% --> 59.9% in 2 years = massive operating leverage.
3. Trade uncertainty: Railroads have operated through every recession and trade war in 150 years. The network is permanent. Volume is cyclical, but the asset is not.
4. Oil: Fuel surcharges are embedded in rail contracts, partially offsetting higher costs. And higher trucking costs (from oil) make rail MORE competitive vs trucking.
