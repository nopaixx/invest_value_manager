## Resumen Ejecutivo

The ORLY thesis survives scrutiny as a fundamentally sound business, but the $108 FV is inflated by an aggressive +24-point QS adjustment and an OEY valuation method that produces results far above the DCF and EV/EBIT anchors. The thesis correctly identifies ORLY as a high-quality compounder, but understates near-term margin headwinds, inventory accumulation risk, and the narrowness of the informational edge vs consensus (FA FV $108 vs consensus PT $111). The counter-cyclical narrative, while directionally correct, is overstated -- DIY customers ARE deferring repairs under tariff-driven cost inflation, which contradicts the "non-discretionary" claim. Post-DA suggested FV: $95.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. QS Adjustment of +24 Points (58 -> 82, Tier B -> Tier A)

- **Evidencia en contra:** This is the largest QS adjustment in the portfolio's history. While each individual adjustment has some basis, the cumulative effect is extraordinary. Specific challenges:
  - **Market Position +8:** Justified -- ORLY is #1/#2. No challenge.
  - **Shareholder Returns +5:** Partially justified. Buybacks are real ($27B+), but they are DEBT-FUNDED. ORLY has negative equity ($-1.8B book value). The buyback program is funded entirely via debt issuance. This is financial engineering, not organic capital return. If rates stay elevated, the cost of continuing this program rises.
  - **FCF Margin +5:** The thesis treats 2025 FCF margin (9.0%) as a trough. But FCF has declined for 3 consecutive years: $2.6B -> $2.0B -> $2.0B -> $1.6B. FCF margin went from 17.9% (2022) to 9.0% (2025). Management guides $1.8-2.1B for 2026 -- the MIDPOINT ($1.95B) would still be below 2023/2024 levels. The "normalization" thesis requires evidence that capex will decline, which is uncertain given accelerating store openings (225-235 vs ~200 historically) and Mexico/Canada expansion.
  - **Insider Ownership +3:** Adjusting insider ownership score based on institutional holders is methodologically questionable. Insider ownership is meant to measure management skin-in-the-game, not institutional validation. Additionally, Akre and Wedgewood also hold many other stocks -- this is not unique conviction.
  - **Revenue CAGR +3:** Adjusting a trailing metric based on FORWARD guidance (store acceleration, Mexico) conflates historical data with projections. The tool score of 58 reflects actual delivered results. Forward projections belong in the growth estimate, not the QS.
- **Net justified adjustment:** +8 (Market Position) + 3 (Shareholder Returns, partial) + 2 (FCF Margin, partial) = +13. Adjusted QS: 71, Tier B. Not Tier A.
- **Severidad:** **HIGH**
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Committee should use QS 71 (Tier B) and apply Tier B MoS requirements (~20-25%). At current $89, the stock needs FV > $111 to clear 20% MoS.

### 2. FCF Declining 3 Consecutive Years -- Investment or Deterioration?

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis frames the FCF decline as "investment-driven, not structural." But the data shows:
  - FCF: $2.6B (2022) -> $2.0B (2023) -> $2.0B (2024) -> $1.6B (2025). Three consecutive years of decline.
  - FCF margin: 17.9% -> 12.8% -> 12.1% -> 9.0%. Persistent deterioration.
  - OCF/NI ratio declining: 1.4x -> 1.3x -> 1.3x -> 1.1x. Cash conversion weakening.
  - Operating margin compressed from 20.5% (2022) to 19.5% (2025). This is NOT capex-related; it's SG&A pressure (labor, insurance, legal costs).
  - Management's 2026 FCF guide of $1.8-2.1B has a wide range ($300M spread). The low end ($1.8B) would represent only marginal improvement.
  - Inventory growing 12.5% vs revenue 6.4% -- nearly 2x the rate. This absorbs working capital and suppresses FCF. AP-to-inventory ratio declined from 128% to 124%.
- **The "investment cycle" narrative may be partially true (DC buildout) but it masks real margin pressure from labor, insurance, and legal costs that management flagged as persistent, especially in H1 2026.**
- **Severidad:** **HIGH**
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Use trailing 3-year average FCF ($1.87B) rather than 2026E normalized ($2.0-2.2B) as base case. This reduces OEY-derived FV materially.

### 3. Counter-Cyclical Narrative Overstated

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis claims ORLY benefits from recession because consumers keep old cars. But CEO Brad Beckham directly contradicts the "non-discretionary" claim: tariffs and cost inflation are causing DIY customer deferrals on larger-ticket jobs. This means:
  - DIY segment (~57% of revenue) IS discretionary for larger repairs. Consumers facing cost pressure DEFER brake jobs, defer timing belt replacements.
  - In a true recession with job losses + tariff inflation, DIY deferrals could accelerate rather than creating "pent-up demand."
  - The DIFM segment (~43%) is more resilient but lower-margin and growing more slowly.
  - ORLY guided 2026 comps at 3-5%, BELOW the 4.7% achieved in 2025. If ORLY were truly counter-cyclical, guidance should be improving into economic uncertainty, not softening.
- **The thesis correctly notes fleet aging is a tailwind. But the counter-cyclical label is misleading -- ORLY is recession-RESISTANT (not recession-PROOF or recession-POSITIVE). There IS demand sensitivity, particularly in DIY.**
- **Severidad:** **HIGH**
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Model bear case with 2% comps (low end of guide), not 3-5%. Acknowledge DIY deferral risk as real, not temporary.

### 4. No Informational Edge vs Consensus

- **Evidencia en contra:** FA FV of $108 vs analyst consensus PT of ~$111-112. The FA thesis essentially agrees with Wall Street. Per Error #49: if our FV converges to consensus without independent reasoning, we have no edge.
  - The thesis arguments (fleet aging, counter-cyclical, FCF normalization, conservative guidance) are well-known sell-side themes. Every analyst covering ORLY makes these same arguments.
  - Akre and Wedgewood holding ORLY is public information -- not an edge.
  - At $89 with consensus PT $111, the "upside" is already priced into the consensus expectation. We are not seeing something the market does not see.
- **Severidad:** **MODERATE**
- **Resolucion sugerida:** The committee should ask: "What specific insight do we have that the 21 analysts covering ORLY do not?" If the answer is "longer time horizon" -- that is not edge, that is hope.

### 5. Valuation Method Divergence and OEY Concerns

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis acknowledges 36% divergence between OEY ($109) and EV/EBIT ($80) methods. This is a significant spread that signals uncertainty in the FV estimate.
  - The thesis weights OEY at 60% because it "captures buyback compounding." But OEY uses Owner Earnings of $2.2B (OCF $2.8B - maintenance capex $0.6B), which ignores growth capex. For a company spending heavily on DCs and international expansion, this overstates available cash.
  - The DCF tool produces FV of $23 (base). Even acknowledging FCF trough distortion, this massive gap ($23 vs $108) should create pause.
  - EV/EBIT at 24.1x trailing is already a premium multiple. The thesis uses 20-25x range for fair value, but the historical average is 21-23x.
  - P/E at 30.1x on EPS of $2.97 is at the high end of ORLY's historical 25-30x range. Multiple expansion from here is unlikely; contraction is the risk.
- **Severidad:** **LOW** (methodology is reasonable, but the OEY weighting inflates the blended FV)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Equal-weight OEY and EV/EBIT (50/50) rather than 60/40. This produces blended FV of ~$95.

### 6. EV Transition: Long-Term Structural Risk

- **Evidencia en contra:** The thesis dismisses EV risk as "10-15 year horizon." Research data:
  - EV maintenance costs ~60% lower than ICE vehicles. EVs have fewer than 20 moving parts vs hundreds in ICE drivetrains.
  - Over 150 component categories are eliminated in BEVs (engine, exhaust, fuel system, drivetrain parts).
  - US EV fleet penetration is still <5%, but growing. By 2030, ICE will be ~92% of vehicles on road. Beyond 2030, the impact accelerates.
  - The kill condition (#4) sets the threshold at 25% EV fleet penetration -- but structural demand impact begins well before 25%. At 10-15% EV fleet, parts demand growth STALLS even if total fleet ages.
  - This is a 7-10 year risk, not 10-15 years. The thesis is overly dismissive.
- **Severidad:** **LOW** (not imminent, but thesis underestimates timeline)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Adjust KC #4 from "25% EV fleet" to "15% EV fleet OR EV-adjusted aftermarket growth turns negative."

### 7. Inventory/Receivables Growing Faster Than Revenue

- **Evidencia en contra:**
  - Inventory +12.5% vs revenue +6.4% (2025). Nearly 2x gap.
  - Receivables +9.2% vs revenue +6.4%.
  - AP-to-inventory ratio declined from 128% to 124%.
  - Management intentionally increased per-store inventory +5% for service levels. But excess inventory above this (~7.5% growth) is concerning.
  - Receivables growth likely reflects DIFM mix shift (professional customers buy on credit), but the gap bears monitoring.
  - These trends compress working capital and contribute to FCF decline.
- **Severidad:** **LOW** (partially explained by strategy, but warrants monitoring)
- **Resolucion sugerida:** Add kill condition: "Inventory growth exceeds revenue growth by >5pp for 3+ consecutive quarters AND AP/inventory ratio falls below 115%."
