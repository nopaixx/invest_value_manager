## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

O'Reilly Automotive is the largest US aftermarket auto parts retailer, operating 6,300+ stores across 48 US states, Mexico (~100 stores), and Canada. The business serves two customer segments:

1. **DIY (Do-It-Yourself):** ~57% of revenue. Individual consumers buying parts (brake pads, oil, filters, batteries) to repair their own vehicles. Lower ticket, higher margin.

2. **DIFM (Do-It-For-Me / Professional):** ~43% of revenue. Professional mechanics and repair shops ordering parts for customer vehicles. Higher ticket, relationship-driven, growing faster.

**Revenue model:** Transaction-based with high repeat purchase. Average vehicle requires $400-600/year in maintenance. The parts themselves are non-discretionary -- a broken alternator must be replaced regardless of the economy.

**Competitive advantage:** ORLY operates a proprietary distribution network (33 distribution centers + hub stores) that enables same-day/next-day delivery to professional customers. This distribution infrastructure is nearly impossible to replicate and creates a structural moat against both Amazon (which can't match parts expertise + same-day local delivery) and independents (who can't match inventory breadth).

### Unit Economics

- Gross margin: 51.6% (2025) -- remarkably stable, +16.6pp vs sector median
- Operating margin: 19.5% (2025) -- slight compression from 20.5% (2022) due to labor/SG&A investment
- FCF margin: 9.0% (2025, depressed by capex) / normalized ~11-12%
- ROIC: 36.0% (2025) -- extraordinary, even after declining from 42% peak
- ROIC-WACC spread: +28.7pp
- Capital intensity: Medium (stores + distribution centers, but minimal heavy equipment)
- SBC/Revenue: 0.2% -- negligible dilution

### 33 Consecutive Years of Comp Sales Growth

This is one of the most remarkable records in US retail. No other retailer of this scale has matched 33 straight years of positive comparable store sales. This reflects: (a) aging fleet tailwind, (b) consistent execution, (c) market share gains from independents and weaker chains (Advance Auto Parts is structurally disadvantaged).
