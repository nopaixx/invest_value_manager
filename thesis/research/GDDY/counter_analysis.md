# Counter-Analysis: GDDY (GoDaddy Inc.)

## Fecha: 2026-03-08

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis survives scrutiny but with notable gaps. The R1 analyst built a compelling FCF machine narrative but MINIMIZED three material risks: (1) systematic insider selling with zero insider buying in 5 years, (2) the "vibe coding" revolution making professional websites trivially easy for a broader population than just micro-SMBs, and (3) the QS adjustment of +10 points that elevates a Tier B company to Tier A on arguable grounds. The FV of $140 is AGGRESSIVE given 6% guided growth and should be reduced. However, the core thesis -- that the market prices -8.4% FCF decline when FCF is growing -- is directionally correct. This is a MODERATE COUNTER with an FV adjustment recommendation.

---

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. "GoDaddy IS the AI play for micro-SMBs, not the victim"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The R1 frames AI as a tailwind via Airo. But the competitive landscape has shifted dramatically in 2026. Wix launched "Harmony" in January 2026 as a direct challenge to vibe-coding platforms. CodeDesign, Readdy, Hostinger Horizons, and dozens of AI website builders now let anyone create a professional website by describing what they want in natural language. The R1 says "micro-SMBs will NEVER vibe-code their own site" -- but vibe coding IS the one-click solution. It is not coding. It is literally telling AI what you want, exactly what Airo does. The differentiation between "Airo generates your website" and "Bolt.new generates your website" is razor-thin. Airo's advantage is that it bundles domain + hosting + email in one purchase. But that bundle advantage erodes if customers can get a better website from a competitor and just use GoDaddy for the $12/year domain.

- **What the R1 got right:** Airo metrics are genuinely impressive -- 50% of new paid subs, Airo cohorts growing in high-teens with 30% faster second-product attach. The data supports that Airo is working TODAY.

- **What the R1 missed:** The competitive moat around AI website generation is ZERO. Any platform with access to GPT-5 or Claude can generate websites. GoDaddy's only defensible advantage is the existing customer base + domain infrastructure. If Airo is the growth engine and Airo has no technical moat, the growth engine is replicable.

- **Severidad:** MODERATE -- Airo is working now but the barrier to competitive replication is low. The risk is not immediate but structural.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Committee should acknowledge that Airo's competitive advantage is distribution (20M customers), not technology. Growth assumptions should assume competitors match Airo capabilities within 12-18 months.

### 2. "FCF margin 31.8% is sustainable and expanding"

- **Evidencia en contra:** R&D spending declined from 19.4% to 17.0% of revenue over 3 years. In absolute terms, R&D grew only 1.6% YoY while revenue grew 8%. This is a company that is CUTTING R&D as a proportion of its business during the most disruptive period in web technology (the AI revolution). Starboard Value's activism is explicitly focused on margin expansion through cost discipline. The question is whether this is "operating leverage" (benign) or "underinvestment" (destructive). The R1 calls it "efficiency." But GoDaddy's gross margin (63.6%) is BELOW competitors per StockStory analysis, which means GoDaddy has LESS room to invest in R&D and marketing than peers. If product quality degrades relative to Wix/Squarespace (which are investing heavily in AI), the land-and-expand model breaks.

- **What makes this concerning:** The margin expansion is almost entirely from OpEx reduction, not gross margin improvement. Gross margin has been FLAT at ~63.5% for 4 years. This means GoDaddy is not becoming a better business (higher value products) -- it is becoming a leaner one (fewer people doing the same work). Leanness has diminishing returns.

- **Historical precedent:** Companies that cut R&D under activist pressure frequently show margin expansion for 2-3 years followed by competitive deterioration. IBM (2013-2018 under Buffett) is a classic example -- margins expanded while market position eroded.

- **Severidad:** MODERATE -- The 31.8% FCF margin is likely near peak. Sustainable FCF margin is probably 27-29%, not 32%+.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Use 28-29% FCF margin in bear case instead of 25%. The 25% kill condition is probably too generous -- by the time it hits 25%, competitive damage may already be severe.

### 3. "QS 78 (Tier A) is justified"

- **Evidencia en contra:** The tool scored 68 (Tier B). The analyst added +10 points: +8 for market position and +3 for buyback-driven shareholder returns, minus -1 for rounding. While the market position adjustment is factual (GoDaddy IS #1), the buyback adjustment is debatable. The buybacks are funded by DEBT. GoDaddy has negative equity precisely because it borrowed to buy back shares. This is financial engineering, not organic capital return. Giving +3 points for debt-funded buybacks while the company has $2.7B net debt is inconsistent. Furthermore, 0.7% insider ownership with ZERO insider purchases in 5 years (CEO made 48 transactions -- ALL sells) should arguably REDUCE the QS, not leave it unchanged.

- **My independent QS assessment:** Market position +8 is valid. Buyback adjustment should be +1 (not +3) because debt-funded. Insider ownership should be -2 (not 0) because active selling is worse than passive low ownership. Net adjustment: +7, giving QS 75 -- still Tier A but barely, and arguably Tier B at 73-75 depending on rounding.

- **Why this matters:** Tier A allows lower MoS (~10-15%) per framework precedents. Tier B requires ~20-25%. If GDDY is properly Tier B at QS 68-73, the required entry price changes materially.

- **Severidad:** HIGH -- The Tier classification directly affects entry decision and sizing.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Committee should classify GDDY as Tier B (QS 68-73) and apply Tier B MoS requirements unless strong evidence supports Tier A.

### 4. "Buyback-driven EPS growth is sustainable"

- **Evidencia en contra:** GoDaddy has been buying back shares aggressively -- $1.6B in 2025 alone, $4B+ since 2022, reducing share count from 178M to 133M. This is funded by a combination of FCF and debt. The company announced a new $3B buyback authorization through 2027. But consider: at $94/share, $1.8B in FCF buys back ~19M shares (~14% of float). This is extraordinarily accretive AT THESE PRICES. But the thesis assumes the stock stays cheap enough for buybacks to be this accretive. If the stock re-rates to $140 (the thesis FV), $1.8B buys back only ~13M shares (~10%). And if Starboard pushes for even more buyback funded by additional debt, Net Debt/EBITDA could exceed 3.0x.

- **The deeper problem:** The EPS trajectory is erratic -- $2.22 (2022), $9.39 (2023), $6.63 (2024), $6.34 (2025). The R1's own anomaly section flags this. The 41.9% EPS CAGR is calculated from a distorted low base. Normalized EPS growth is ~15-20%, which is good but not exceptional. The buyback math works best when the stock is depressed -- creating a perverse incentive to keep expectations low (which is exactly what Starboard's "prudent guidance" strategy does).

- **Severidad:** MODERATE -- Buybacks are real and accretive but the magnitude of accretion declines as the stock recovers, and debt-funded buybacks carry rate risk.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Model buyback accretion at 8-10% share reduction per year (not 14%) to account for higher future stock price. Monitor Net Debt/EBITDA closely.

### 5. "Insider selling is immaterial"

- **Evidencia en contra:** This is where the R1 was most dismissive. The thesis says "Director sold $56K (immaterial). No pattern." But my independent research reveals a CLEAR pattern:

  - CEO Bhutani: 48 transactions in 5 years. ZERO buys, 48 sells. Most recent: March 4, 2026 -- sold 34,148 shares at $88.99 ($3.0M) plus 5,231 shares for tax coverage. He received 170K shares in awards the same week.
  - CFO McCaffrey: Sold 17,406 shares at $88.99 ($1.5M) on March 4, 2026. Same pattern -- receive awards, sell immediately.
  - CAO and other officers: Same sell-only pattern.

  In total, insiders hold 0.7% of shares. The CEO's direct holdings are 530K shares (~$50M at $94) -- but his annual comp is ~$20M. He has 2.5 years of compensation in stock. This is NOT deep alignment. He is a RENTER, not an OWNER.

  The R1 said "No pattern." There IS a pattern: receive RSUs, sell immediately, never buy with personal capital. This is the pattern of a professional manager extracting value, not building it. Combined with Starboard's activist presence (financial engineering focus), there is no one at GoDaddy whose personal wealth is deeply tied to long-term share price appreciation.

- **Severidad:** HIGH -- Systematic insider selling with zero buying is a material negative signal that the R1 completely minimized. Per the skin-in-the-game framework, this is a RED flag.

- **Resolucion sugerida:** Add kill condition: "CEO or CFO begin selling shares at a rate exceeding annual RSU grants (net seller of accumulated shares, not just tax-related sales on new grants)." Adjust QS downward by 2 points for insider alignment.

## Conflictos con Otros Analisis

**Moat Assessment vs. Reality:**
The moat assessment scored 20/25 and classified moat as "NARROW-TO-WIDE." I find this generous. The switching costs argument is strong for domains but WEAK for website builder (GoDaddy's growth engine). The moat assessment says "AI coding tools are a LOW threat" to GoDaddy's target market. In March 2026, with Wix Harmony, Bolt.new, and dozens of vibe-coding platforms live, this assessment may be stale within 12 months. I would score the moat at 16-18/25 (Narrow, not Narrow-to-Wide).

**Risk Assessment gaps:**
The risk assessment is solid but did not investigate: (1) debt maturity schedule, (2) CEO selling pattern depth, (3) law firm inquiry, (4) SBC impact on true FCF. These are not CRITICAL omissions but represent incomplete risk mapping.

## FV Adjustment Recommendation

**Post-DA recommended FV: $120** (down from $140, a -14.3% correction)

Rationale:
1. Reduce EV/EBITDA from 13x to 11.5x (more appropriate for 6% grower, below peer group)
2. Reduce P/FCF from 14x to 12x (reflecting leverage, insider misalignment, and AI competition risk)
3. Use FCF margin of 30% (not 31.8%) for forward estimates -- slight reversion toward mean
4. Maintain OEY method but adjust for SBC: true owner earnings closer to $1.3B

At $120 FV and $94 price: MoS = 21.6% (appropriate for Tier B)
E[CAGR] at $94 vs $120 FV: (120/94)^(1/3) - 1 + 3% buyback accretion = ~11.6%

This is still attractive but BELOW the 12% Tier A deployment threshold, making it a solid WATCHLIST candidate at $94, not an immediate market buy.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- I could not verify GoDaddy's debt maturity schedule. If there is a significant maturity wall in 2027-2028, the buyback sustainability thesis weakens materially. The R1 flagged this but did not investigate it.
- The "vibe coding" threat is difficult to quantify. In March 2026 these tools are nascent for SMBs. By March 2027, they could be mainstream. The speed of AI adoption makes 12-month forecasts unusually uncertain.
- I could not find GoDaddy-specific customer churn data (beyond domain renewal rates). The SaaS benchmark of 7.5% annual SMB churn implies ~15% of GoDaddy's non-domain customers leave each year -- but this is estimated, not verified.

### Limitaciones de Este Analisis
- No access to GoDaddy 10-K for debt maturity details, segment-level FCF, or detailed customer cohort data
- Peer multiples for WIX/SQSP are inflated by their higher growth profiles -- a direct comparison overstates the multiple discount GDDY "deserves"
- SBC adjustment to FCF is standard but debatable -- some analysts treat SBC as a cost of growth, not a deduction from owner earnings

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The quality_scorer.py should have a NEGATIVE adjustment option for systematic insider selling (CEO with 0 buys in 5 years should trigger automatic flag)
- DA accuracy tracker shows historical average correction of -15.7%. My -14.3% correction on GDDY is consistent with historical pattern.

### Preguntas para Orchestrator
