## TL;DR

GoDaddy is the world's #1 domain registrar (82M+ domains, ~21-30% global share) and a leading web presence platform for 20M+ micro/small businesses. The stock dropped -52% from ATH on slowing guidance ($5.2-5.3B, +6%) and AI disruption fears, creating a massive valuation gap: the market prices -8.4% FCF decline while FCF has been growing +20% CAGR. At $94, the FCF yield is 12.4% ($1.58B FCF / $12.7B market cap), making this a compelling cash flow machine at a distressed valuation. The key question is whether 6% revenue growth is a terminal condition or a temporary deceleration with margin expansion and buyback-driven EPS growth creating total shareholder returns of 15%+.

## Business Understanding

### Model de Negocio

GoDaddy solves a fundamental problem: micro and small businesses need an online presence but lack technical expertise and budget. GoDaddy provides a single platform covering the full lifecycle: domain registration, website building, hosting, security, email, commerce, and payments.

**Revenue segments:**
- **Core Platform ($3.1B, 62% of revenue, +5% YoY):** Domains, hosting, security. Mature, predictable, high retention. Domains have 85%+ renewal rates. This is the cash cow and the moat anchor.
- **Applications & Commerce ($1.9B, 38% of revenue, +14% YoY):** Website builder (Airo AI), commerce/payments, email marketing. Growth engine with higher LTV customers. Segment EBITDA margin 47%.

**Revenue model:** Subscription-based with high renewal rates. Average revenue per user (ARPU) growing as customers adopt more products (land-and-expand within the micro-SMB base). ~65% of revenue is subscription/renewal, creating a predictable base.

**Unit Economics:**
- Customer base: 20M+ micro/small businesses
- Customer retention: 85%+ domain renewal rate
- FCF margin: 31.8% (exceptional for a platform at this scale)
- Capital intensity: Very low (Capex/Depreciation 0.2x = negative net capex effectively)
- LTV/CAC: Not publicly disclosed but implied to be strong given 85% retention and rising ARPU

### Structure de Margenes

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Trend |
|--------|------|------|------|------|-------|
| Gross Margin | 63.7% | 63.0% | 63.9% | 63.6% | Stable |
| Op Margin | 12.6% | 15.0% | 20.4% | 23.0% | EXPANDING strongly |
| FCF Margin | 22.5% | 22.8% | 27.6% | 31.8% | EXPANDING strongly |
| R&D/Rev | 19.4% | 19.7% | 17.8% | 17.0% | Declining (efficiency) |
| SBC/Rev | 6.5% | 7.0% | 6.6% | 6.4% | Stable/declining |

The margin story is compelling: operating margin nearly doubled in 3 years (12.6% to 23.0%) while gross margin stayed flat. This means the improvement is from operating leverage and cost discipline, NOT from pricing or mix. FCF margin at 31.8% is extraordinary -- higher than many pure-play SaaS companies. Starboard Value's activism appears to have been effective in driving cost rationalization.

**Deferred Revenue:** Growing at 7.3% YoY (to $2.4B), slightly faster than revenue -- a positive signal for revenue visibility.

### Por Que Esta Barata

**The market narrative (multiple headwinds converging):**

1. **Guidance disappointment (MAIN DRIVER):** FY2026 guided to ~6% revenue growth vs market expectations of 8-10%. Q4 2025 bookings of $1.28B missed expectations. Market re-rated from growth to value.

2. **AI disruption fears:** Can AI website builders (Wix AI, Squarespace, Cursor, Replit) eliminate the need for GoDaddy's website builder? Will AI agents eventually replace the need for a traditional web presence?

3. **Core Platform deceleration:** Core Platform (domains/hosting) growing only +3-5%, the mature cash cow is barely growing.

4. **Applications & Commerce bookings deceleration:** A&C bookings growth decelerated from 14% revenue growth to 11% bookings growth -- market feared momentum fading.

5. **Multiple analyst downgrades:** RBC cut PT from $200 to $100. Barclays, Morgan Stanley also cut significantly.

6. **Iran war + macro risk-off:** General VIX spike and growth stock compression (not GDDY-specific but amplified the selloff).

**My contra-thesis (for each point):**

1. **Guidance:** 6% revenue growth is the floor, not the ceiling. Management has a pattern of conservative guidance (Starboard pushed for "prudent" guidance). The real return engine is not revenue growth -- it is FCF growth (guided $1.8B = +14% YoY) and buyback-driven EPS growth (+25%+ CAGR). Total shareholder return = 6% rev growth + margin expansion + buyback accretion = 15%+ total.

2. **AI disruption:** GoDaddy IS the AI play for micro-SMBs, not the victim. Airo drives 50% of new paid subscriptions. The real question is: does the micro-SMB customer WANT to use Cursor/Replit/vibe-code their own site? No -- they want a one-click solution, which is exactly what Airo provides. GoDaddy's moat is not the website builder per se -- it's the domain registration + customer relationship + integrated platform. AI enhances rather than threatens this.

3. **Core Platform maturity:** Domains have an 85%+ renewal rate and a 62M+ installed base. This is a toll road that generates $3.1B/year with minimal churn. Slow growth (3-5%) in a near-zero-capex business is fine -- it's cash generation, not growth that matters here.

4. **A&C deceleration:** 11% bookings growth supporting 14% revenue growth suggests pricing/upsell momentum is strong. Bookings can fluctuate; revenue retention is what matters for a subscription business.

5. **Analyst downgrades:** Consensus PT is now $143.67 -- but I will derive my FV independently per Error #49.

### Consensus Analysis (Reverse DCF)

The market at $94 implies:
- FCF growth rate: **-8.4% per year** for 5 years
- This requires FCF to decline from $1.58B to approximately $1.0B
- Historical FCF CAGR: +19.7%
- **Gap: 28 percentage points.** The market is pricing this company as if FCF will shrink materially, while it has been growing 20% per year

Even in a bear case where revenue grows 0% and margins contract, FCF would remain around $1.2-1.3B. The implied -8.4% FCF decline requires BOTH revenue decline AND margin compression -- a double-negative scenario with very low probability given the subscription/renewal model.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The FCF margin trajectory (22.5% to 31.8% in 3 years) is extraordinary. Is this sustainable or has Starboard-pushed cost-cutting gone too far (R&D declining from 19.4% to 17.0%)? Underinvestment risk is real but hard to quantify
- Goodwill at 45% of assets -- the negative equity from buybacks makes traditional leverage metrics distorted. The "real" leverage may be higher than Net Debt/EBITDA 2.2x suggests
- Insider ownership at 0.7% is a structural concern. Who is aligned? Starboard (7.8%) is aligned but they are an activist -- they may exit after value extraction

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The quality_scorer.py market position field defaults to 0/8 for all companies -- this systematically penalizes market leaders. A lookup table or flag for "verify market position manually" would prevent Tier misclassification
- The tool should capture buyback-driven shareholder returns, not just dividends

### Preguntas para Orchestrator
1. Does technology.md sector view sufficiently cover GDDY (domain registrar/web services), or should a new sector view be created?
2. Given the "NO FURTHER DEPLOYMENT" macro stance, should this be fast-tracked as a standing order candidate or deferred entirely?

### Anomalias Detectadas
- EPS CAGR of 41.9% reported by tool, but actual EPS trajectory is erratic: 2022: $2.22, 2023: $9.39, 2024: $6.63, 2025: $6.34. The CAGR is calculated from a low base ($2.22) and inflated by non-recurring items in 2023. Normalized EPS growth is more like 15-20%, still strong
