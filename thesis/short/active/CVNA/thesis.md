## Business Understanding

### Model of Business

Carvana is an online used car retailer that generates revenue through three streams:

1. **Vehicle sales (~80% of revenue):** Buy, recondition, and sell used vehicles online with home delivery. Average selling price ~$34K. Low-margin activity -- retail gross profit per unit (GPU) under pressure.

2. **Wholesale vehicle sales (~12%):** Vehicles not meeting retail standards sold at auction. Lower margin than retail.

3. **Finance and other (~8%):** Originate auto loans at point of sale, package into ABS (asset-backed securities), and sell to investors. This is the CRITICAL profit engine: gain-on-sale income was $541M in FY2024 (26% of gross profit), and an estimated 70-80% of Q4 2025 "clean" profit came from this channel.

**The core insight:** Carvana is marketed as a tech-enabled used car retailer, but its real profit driver is a consumer finance operation -- originate, package, sell subprime auto loans at a premium. When credit markets tighten, this engine stalls.

This is the hallmark of a business that requires incremental capital to grow (inventory, logistics, loan origination) without proportional FCF improvement. Diseconomies of scale.

## Why This Stock Is Overvalued: The Short Thesis

### 1. Earnings Quality: The DTA Mirage

FY2025 GAAP net income of $2.08B includes a $685M one-time deferred tax asset valuation allowance release. This is NON-RECURRING, NON-CASH, and will NOT repeat in FY2026. Stripping it out:

- Adjusted Net Income: ~$1.27B
- Shares outstanding: ~219M
- Adjusted EPS: ~$5.80
- At $281.28: Adjusted P/E = ~48x

The market's GAAP P/E of 33.3x significantly understates the true earnings multiple. Investors anchoring to headline EPS of $10.22 are pricing in a one-time accounting event as if it were sustainable.

### 2. The Gain-on-Sale Dependency

Carvana's profitability is structurally dependent on its ability to originate auto loans and sell them into securitization markets at a premium. This is not a car business -- it is a consumer finance business wearing a retail mask.

Key vulnerabilities:
- **93% of YTD net income** comes from gain-on-sale of originated loans (per Abelian Analysis research)
- **100% stated income loans** -- no income verification, creating subprime risk regardless of FICO score
- **39% of collateral underwater** -- borrowers owe more than the car is worth
- **Subprime auto delinquencies at 6.74%** (60+ day, per Fitch), highest since early 1990s
- If ABS spreads widen 100-150bps, gain-on-sale premiums compress or disappear entirely
- The company books NO loss reserves on $553M of loans held on balance sheet

In a credit tightening scenario (elevated for 2026 with rates stuck), this profit engine is at risk of seizing.

### 3. EBITDA Margin Compression: Diseconomies of Scale

The sequential quarterly EBITDA margin trajectory tells the story:

| Q1 2025 | Q2 2025 | Q3 2025 | Q4 2025 |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| 12.0% | 12.0% | 11.3% | 9.1% |

This is the OPPOSITE of what the bull narrative promises. As Carvana scales toward its 3M unit target, margins are compressing, not expanding. Possible drivers:
- Higher reconditioning costs per unit as they move beyond core markets
- Logistics costs scaling non-linearly with geographic expansion
- Mix shift toward lower-GPU wholesale channel
- Competitive pressure on pricing from new car supply normalization

Management guided to "significant growth" for 2026 without providing specific margin targets -- a red flag when margins are declining.

### 4. The $5.6B Debt Wall and PIK Transition

Carvana restructured its debt in September 2023, converting unsecured notes to secured notes with PIK (payment-in-kind) interest features. This DEFERRED cash interest payments, flattering FCF during 2024-2025.

**Mid-2026 is the inflection point:** PIK period expires and Carvana must begin paying over $500M/year in CASH interest. This is a direct hit to free cash flow:
- FY2025 FCF: $889M
- Less incremental cash interest: ~$500M
- Implied FY2026 FCF (all else equal): ~$389M
- At $61.6B market cap: FCF yield compresses from 1.4% to 0.6%

Interest coverage is already NEGATIVE (-0.8x). The transition to cash interest in a rising-rate environment with declining margins creates a genuine solvency concern if operations deteriorate.

### 5. Governance and Related-Party Risk

- **Garcia family controls 40%** through dual-class structure (Class A: 1 vote, Class B: 10 votes)
- **DriveTime/Bridgecrest related-party transactions:** Gotham City Research alleges $1B+ in overstated earnings via undisclosed support from entities controlled by Ernie Garcia II (CEO's father)
- **DriveTime leverage 20-40x earnings** (vs historical cap of 10.3x pre-2023) -- potential bailout candidate from Carvana
- **SEC subpoena** escalated to active investigation phase
- **Multiple class action lawsuits:** Robbins Geller defeated motion to dismiss; Block & Leviton, Rosen Law, Pomerantz all pursuing
- **Management SILENCE** on Gotham City allegations during Q4 earnings call
- **Insider selling:** CFO Jenkins $5.2M, COO Huston $4.1M (exercise-and-sell pattern)

### 6. Macro Headwinds: Oil + Rates + Recession

The macro environment is maximally hostile for used car demand:
- Oil at $95-99 WTI: gas ~$3.80-4.00/gal, direct wallet squeeze on lower-income used car buyers
- Fed rate cuts OFF TABLE for 2026: auto financing rates remain elevated at 9-10% for used cars
- Recession probability 45-55%: consumer discretionary contraction expected within 30-60 days
- Hormuz crisis ongoing: oil likely sustained at $95-110 range
- New car supply normalizing: reduces the trade-in premium arbitrage that supported used car pricing

## Risks to the Short Thesis

1. **CVNA has been a "widow-maker" short.** Historical pattern: bears get convinced, stock rallies 50%+. The operational turnaround from 2023 is genuine. Revenue growth is real. Units are scaling. This is not a zero.

2. **Stock split retail inflows.** $56 post-split price could attract significant retail buying in May-June, creating short-term pressure against the short.

3. **Tariffs on new cars could BOOST used car demand.** If new cars become 10-25% more expensive due to tariffs, some buyers shift to used -- partially offsetting oil/rate headwinds.

4. **Management could stabilize margins.** Q4's 9.1% EBITDA margin could be seasonal (Q4 typically weaker in auto retail). Q1 recovery to 11%+ would weaken the thesis.

5. **Short interest declining.** Bears are covering (14.8M to 13.3M shares, -10.7% MoM). If ALL bears exit, reflexive buying pressure could drive a rally regardless of fundamentals.

6. **Edge is marginal.** The DTA/margin compression thesis is KNOWN to the market. Gotham City, Hindenburg, and multiple short sellers have published similar analysis. Our informational advantage is limited.
