## 1. Por que el precio actual no se sostiene

### The Core Problem: $40B Market Cap for a Company Burning $322M/Year with Zero Profit

RKLB trades at $70.11 ($39.8B market cap) -- 66x trailing revenue. To put this in context:

| Metric | RKLB | SpaceX (private est.) | Airbus | Boeing |
|--------|------|----------------------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | $602M | ~$15B+ | EUR 69B | $66B |
| P/S | 66x | ~8-10x (implied) | ~1.5x | ~1.3x |
| FCF | -$322M | ~$3-5B+ (profitable) | EUR 4.4B | -$4B |
| Operating Margin | -38% | Est. 15-20%+ | 8.5% | Negative |

RKLB's P/S of 66x is **22x the aerospace industry average of 2.9x** and **6-8x SpaceX's implied multiple** -- and SpaceX is PROFITABLE with dominant market share.

The market is pricing RKLB as if Neutron succeeds, achieves reusability, captures meaningful market share from SpaceX, AND the company reaches profitability -- all simultaneously. Each of these is uncertain individually; together they form a fragile chain of assumptions.

### Structural Fragility #1: Neutron is the ENTIRE Growth Thesis -- And It's Broken

Neutron suffered a **Stage 1 tank rupture during hydrostatic testing on January 21, 2026**. The failure was traced to a manufacturing defect in a critical structural joint. First launch has been pushed to Q4 2026 at the earliest -- already a delay from the hoped-for 2025 debut.

This is not a minor setback. Neutron IS the story:
- Current revenue ($602M) comes from Electron (small sat launcher) + Space Systems (satellite components/spacecraft). These are real businesses but they are NOT $40B businesses.
- Electron launches ~15-20 times/year at $7.5M per launch = ~$112-150M launch revenue. This is a niche market.
- Space Systems is growing ($400M+ in 2025) but at margins similar to defense contractors, not SaaS companies.
- Without Neutron, RKLB is a $5-8B company at best (20-30x launch revenue + 2-3x Space Systems).

### Structural Fragility #2: Cash Burn is ACCELERATING, Not Improving

| Year | Revenue | FCF | FCF Margin | Direction |
|------|---------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| 2022 | $211M | -$149M | -70.6% | -- |
| 2023 | $245M | -$154M | -62.8% | Slight improvement |
| 2024 | $436M | -$116M | -26.6% | Improving |
| 2025 | $602M | -$322M | -53.5% | **SEVERE DETERIORATION** |

The 2024 improvement was a mirage. In 2025, FCF went from -$116M to -$322M -- a 2.8x worsening. Capex/Depreciation surged to 3.6x (from 2.0x in 2024), meaning RKLB is investing massively in Neutron and Space Systems expansion. With $1.0B cash on hand, at the 2025 burn rate, the company has approximately 3 years of runway. But Neutron development costs are front-loaded, and any further delays would accelerate cash consumption.

Deferred revenue DECLINED 9.6% in 2025 ($216M to $195M) despite revenue growing 38%. This is a yellow flag -- it suggests the pipeline of prepaid contracts may be thinning relative to revenue recognition.

### Structural Fragility #3: Competition Will Intensify, Not Diminish

In the medium-lift market (Neutron's target):
- **SpaceX Falcon 9**: Already dominates with ~50+ launches/year, reusable, proven, cost ~$30-50M per launch
- **SpaceX Starship**: Making progress toward operational capability -- if fully reusable, could drop launch costs to $10-20M, DESTROYING Neutron's value proposition before it flies
- **Blue Origin New Glenn**: First launch completed (or imminent), backed by Jeff Bezos's deep pockets ($1B+/year personal investment)
- **ULA Vulcan**: Flying, backed by Boeing + Lockheed
- **Ariane 6**: ESA's new launcher, operational

Neutron targets 13 metric tons to LEO at ~$50M per launch. If Starship achieves even partial reusability at $20-30M for 100+ tons, Neutron is obsolete before its first flight. The window for Neutron may be closing, not opening.

## 2. Dependencia oculta

The $40B valuation depends on a chain of assumptions that ALL must hold:

```
$40B valuation
  |-- Neutron flies successfully in Q4 2026 (first delay already occurred)
       |-- Neutron achieves reusability (never been done by anyone except SpaceX)
            |-- Neutron captures meaningful launch market share from SpaceX
                 |-- SpaceX/Starship does NOT dramatically reduce launch costs first
                      |-- RKLB reaches FCF breakeven (currently -$322M/yr and worsening)
                           |-- No dilutive capital raise needed (only $1B cash vs $322M/yr burn)
                                |-- Space Systems margins expand (currently low-margin)
```

If ANY link in this chain breaks:
- Neutron delayed to 2027 -> valuation should compress 30-50% (no medium-lift revenue for another year+)
- Neutron fails/cancelled -> RKLB is a $5-8B company (current business only)
- Starship succeeds -> Neutron's economics are uncompetitive; medium-lift segment may not exist
- Dilutive raise -> at current burn, $500M+ raise = 1.3%+ dilution from current share count

The market is pricing the OPTIMISTIC path through this entire chain. One failure mode collapses the thesis.
