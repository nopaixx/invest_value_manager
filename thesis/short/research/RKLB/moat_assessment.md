## Detailed Source Analysis

### 1. Electron (Small Launch): Niche Position, NOT a Moat

**What market says:** Electron is the #2 most-launched orbital rocket globally (behind Falcon 9), with 21 launches in 2025 at 100% success. "Second most frequently launched US rocket."

**What data says:**
- Electron captures ~10.2% global small launch market share, ~21% in US/Japan
- BUT: the relevant competitive frame is NOT "small dedicated launchers." It's ALL small-sat deployment options, including SpaceX Transporter rideshare
- **Cost comparison devastates Electron's economics:**
  - Electron: ~$25,000/kg (300kg to LEO for $7.5M)
  - SpaceX rideshare: ~$7,000/kg (and dropping as Falcon 9 amortizes further)
  - That is a 3.5x cost disadvantage
- Electron's value proposition is CONVENIENCE (dedicated orbit, schedule control), not cost
- The convenience premium narrows as SpaceX increases rideshare frequency and orbit options (now offering mid-inclination rides)
- Small-sat market is growing, but SpaceX's rideshare is capturing the majority of that growth

**Moat verdict for Electron:** No sustainable moat. Electron has a track record advantage (heritage, reliability) but faces an economically superior competitor (SpaceX) that can price below Electron indefinitely. Electron's market is a NICHE within a niche — customers willing to pay 3.5x premium for dedicated orbit access. That niche exists but is not expanding relative to rideshare.

### 2. Neutron (Medium Launch): Zero Moat — Product Does Not Exist

**What market says:** Neutron will compete with Falcon 9 at $50M per launch for 13,000kg to LEO.

**What data says:**
- Neutron has NEVER launched. Zero flights. Zero proven reliability.
- Timeline has slipped repeatedly: originally 2024 -> late 2025 -> Q1 2026 pad arrival -> now Q4 2026 at earliest (propellant tank failure in January 2026)
- Target competitor (Falcon 9) has 300+ successful launches, proven reusability, and established customer base
- Blue Origin's New Glenn already flying (launched Jan 2025) — another competitor Neutron must face
- Neutron target price ($50M) vs Falcon 9 (~$67M list, but SpaceX likely offers discounts to high-volume customers)
- Even IF Neutron works perfectly on first attempt (historically improbable), it will take YEARS to build the launch cadence and reliability record needed to win government contracts
- $1B+ invested in Neutron development with no revenue generated

**Moat verdict for Neutron:** ZERO. The product does not exist. Assigning any moat to an unlaunched rocket is pure speculation. The competitive landscape it enters (Falcon 9 with 300+ launches, New Glenn flying, Starship in development) is the most competitive in spaceflight history. The probability of Neutron achieving the market position priced into RKLB's valuation is LOW.

### 3. Space Systems: Competitive but Not Moated

**What market says:** Vertical integration (build satellite + launch it) is a unique advantage. $338M+ revenue in 2025. SDA contracts worth $1.3B+.

**What data says:**
- Space Systems revenue ~$338M (56% of total), growing well
- SDA Tranche 3 contract ($816M) won against Lockheed Martin ($1.1B), Northrop Grumman ($784M), L3Harris ($843M) — these are the REAL competitors
- RKLB is competing against defense primes with 50-100x its resources
- The SDA contracts are competed EACH tranche — no recurring moat, must win again
- Spacecraft bus manufacturing has moderate switching costs (qualification testing) but not high barriers to entry
- Components (reaction wheels, star trackers, solar arrays) face competition from multiple suppliers
- Vertical integration story is compelling BUT: SpaceX also vertically integrates (builds Starlink + launches it), and the defense primes manufacture satellites AND have launch partnerships

**Moat verdict for Space Systems:** NARROW at best — but driven by government contract wins (competed, not captive) and moderate switching costs from qualification processes. This is the ONLY segment with arguable competitive advantages, but they are nowhere near sufficient to justify the valuation.

## Escenarios de Erosion

### 1. Most Probable: Neutron Delays + SpaceX Pricing Pressure (60% probability)
Neutron continues to slip (history: every major milestone has been delayed). Meanwhile, SpaceX increases Transporter rideshare frequency to monthly, adds more orbit options, and drops pricing further. Electron revenue growth stalls as customers shift to rideshare for non-time-critical missions. Space Systems grows but at defense-contractor margins (10-15%), not tech margins. RKLB remains unprofitable through 2027-2028. At 66x P/S, any growth deceleration triggers massive multiple compression.

### 2. Tail Risk but Possible: Starship Disruption (25% probability, 3-5 year horizon)
SpaceX achieves regular Starship launches at sub-$20M per flight. At 150,000kg to LEO, the cost per kg drops below $200. This makes Electron economically obsolete and Neutron uncompetitive before it reaches operational maturity. The entire small/medium launch market restructures around Starship economics. Only Space Systems survives, but as a sub-scale defense contractor competing against primes.

### 3. Positive Tail (for short thesis): Neutron Catastrophic Failure
First Neutron launch fails, destroying the vehicle. Given the propellant tank testing failure in Jan 2026, this is not inconceivable. A RUD (Rapid Unscheduled Disassembly) would delay the program 12-18 months, require additional capital (dilution), and severely damage the credibility narrative that supports the 66x P/S multiple.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- **Space Systems moat strength:** I may be underestimating the switching costs in SDA-type defense contracts. Qualification processes for spacecraft buses are genuinely costly ($5-10M+ per platform). However, the SDA competes each tranche, so this is a competed advantage, not a captive one. Confidence: Medium.
- **Vertical integration narrative:** The "build the satellite AND launch it" story is genuinely differentiated vs. pure-play launchers. I may be underweighting this. However, SpaceX does the same thing (Starlink) at 50x the scale, and defense primes have launch partnerships. The moat from vertical integration is real but narrow and NOT unique to RKLB.
- **Neutron potential:** If Neutron works and achieves 10+ launches/year by 2028, the economics change dramatically. I cannot assign zero probability to this. However, pricing a moat for an unbuilt product is speculation, not analysis.

### Sugerencias de Mejora
- For future aerospace/defense moat assessments, include a "technology readiness level" (TRL) dimension — products at TRL 9 (proven operational) get full moat credit, products at TRL 5-7 (development) get discounted credit, products at TRL 1-4 get zero credit. This would formalize the Neutron discount.
