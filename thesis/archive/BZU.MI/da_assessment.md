# Counter-Analysis: BZU.MI (Buzzi SpA)

## Fecha: 2026-03-13

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The thesis identifies Buzzi as the most profitable cement company globally with exceptional family alignment and best-in-class US margins. After independent research, the thesis SURVIVES but with serious cyclical headwinds that the thesis only partially addresses. The most critical finding is that US cement demand is in its THIRD consecutive year of decline (10M+ ton drop since 2022), US revenue fell 7% in FY2025, and the industry faces a 2026 demand bottom at ~100Mt with "more downside risk than any recent forecast." At EUR 41.50, the market is pricing this weakness correctly. The thesis's EUR 57 FV assumes through-cycle normalization that may take 2-3 years to materialize, during which margins will compress further. The position is defensible as a long-term cyclical value play, but the timing is premature -- we may be buying into continued margin compression, not a trough.

## Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### 1. Through-Cycle Normalized EBIT of EUR 850M

- **FA assumption:** Through-cycle EBIT normalizes at EUR 850M (~20% operating margin), with 60% of 2023-2024 margin expansion being structural.
- **Evidence against:** FY2025 LFL EBITDA declined ~6%. US revenue fell 7% YoY. Italy revenue -3.3%. Rising production costs weighed on margins in Italy, Germany, AND the US. Cement selling prices "declined slightly in the final months" of 2025. Unit production costs worsened, driven by higher fixed costs and raw materials, despite lower energy costs. If EBITDA for FY2025 is EUR 1,230M on EUR 4,519M revenue, that's a 27.2% margin -- already compressing from 30% peak in 2024. The trajectory is DOWN, not stabilizing.
- **My estimate of through-cycle EBIT:** EUR 750-800M is more realistic. The 2021-2022 average was EUR 573M (trough). The 2023-2024 average was EUR 994M (peak). True mid-cycle is EUR 750-800M, not EUR 850M. The thesis assigns too much credit to "structural improvements."
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution suggested:** Reduce through-cycle EBIT to EUR 780M. This produces FV EUR 48-50 (EV/EBIT method), significantly lowering the MoS.

### 2. US Cement Demand Is Resilient (Infrastructure + Data Centers Offset Housing)

- **FA assumption:** US volumes +0-2% organic with infrastructure tailwinds. US pricing +3-5% annually.
- **Evidence against:** US cement consumption has declined for THREE consecutive years, dropping 10M+ tons since 2022. Ed Sullivan (American Cement Association chief economist) forecasts cement consumption falling 5% in 2025 and a further 0.2% in 2026, hitting a trough of ~100Mt. Data center construction accounts for less than 2% of total cement consumption -- it is NOT a meaningful offset. US housing permits fell 5.8% YoY in January 2026. Major cement producers are signaling FLAT or NEGATIVE price outlooks. Domestic capacity utilization is ~76%, below the preferred 80% threshold. Import terminal capacity is expanding to pre-2008 crisis levels despite falling demand.
- **Buzzi's specific US weakness:** US revenue fell 7% in FY2025 (EUR 1,606M vs EUR 1,727M). Texas demand continues to slow. Ready-mix concrete volumes confirmed weakness observed throughout the year.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution suggested:** US volume assumptions should be -2% to flat for 2026, not +0-2%. Pricing assumptions should be flat to +2%, not +3-5%. This materially reduces EBITDA projections.

### 3. Oil at $97 Impact Is Manageable

- **FA assumption:** Not explicitly addressed. The thesis notes energy costs are 30-40% of production costs but assumes normalization.
- **Evidence against:** Oil at $97/bbl (Hormuz crisis) directly impacts cement production costs. Pet coke and coal prices correlate with crude oil. Transport costs for both raw materials and finished products increase. Buzzi's FY2025 already showed "rising production costs" weighing on margins despite lower energy costs at that time. With oil now at $97 (significantly higher than the $65-80 range in 2025), the cost pressure will INTENSIFY in 2026. Cement is one of the most energy-intensive manufacturing processes. The March 2026 Iran conflict is specifically cited as adding pressure to cement sector costs globally.
- **Severity:** HIGH
- **Resolution suggested:** Add a kill condition for sustained oil >$100 impact on EBITDA margins. Model energy cost sensitivity: +$10/bbl oil = roughly -1pp EBITDA margin for cement producers.

### 4. The "Italian Discount" Is an Opportunity, Not a Structural Feature

- **FA assumption:** Italian HQ discount should be ~10-15%, not ~40% vs US peers. Kerrisdale calls it "prejudice."
- **Evidence against:** ISS Governance QualityScore shows Shareholder Rights score of 3/10 (vs Board 10/10, Audit 10/10). This is NOT prejudice -- it reflects REAL governance weakness in minority shareholder protection. Only 1 out of 10 board members comes from a minority shareholder list. The family controls 53% with no apparent intention to close the discount (no US relisting plans, no English investor days mentioned). CRH closed its discount by relisting on NYSE -- Buzzi has not. The Italian discount is a permanent feature for as long as the family maintains control and refuses structural governance improvements.
- **CRH's valuation premium provides context:** CRH trades at 18.1x P/E vs Buzzi's 9.5x. Heidelberg at 15.5x. The gap is persistent and may be justified by governance quality differences, not just listing venue.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution suggested:** The 15% catalyst probability for US relisting/spin-off (thesis) is optimistic. The Italian discount is structural, not catalytic. Fair multiple should stay at sector average minus Italian discount (10-10.5x, not higher).

### 5. Russia Exposure Is Contained at 7%

- **FA assumption:** Worst case is forced exit at EUR 300-400M writeoff (~EUR 2/share). Kill condition at >EUR 500M.
- **Evidence against:** Russia revenue GREW 3.1% in FY2025 (EUR 303M vs EUR 294M), suggesting Buzzi is maintaining and possibly investing in Russian operations despite sanctions pressure. The "spider effect" of sanctions is broadening -- being in Russia creates secondary sanctions risk for US operations (Buzzi's crown jewel). The kill condition of EUR 500M may be too high -- the entire Russian business is likely worth EUR 200-400M, so any forced exit would likely be below the KC threshold while still destroying EUR 1-2/share of value.
- **Severity:** MODERATE
- **Resolution suggested:** The Russia risk is correctly identified but the KC threshold may be too generous. Consider lowering to EUR 300M or adding a KC for US regulatory scrutiny of Russian operations.

### Interpretacion:

**MODERATE COUNTER.** The thesis has gaps in three areas: (1) the through-cycle EBIT normalization is too optimistic given accumulating evidence of demand weakness and margin compression, (2) the oil price impact at $97 is not modeled, and (3) the timing of entry may be premature given we're buying into continued cyclical deterioration rather than at a clearly identifiable trough.

## META-REFLECTION

### Dudas/Incertidumbres
- The exact through-cycle EBIT is the thesis's fulcrum and is inherently uncertain for cyclical businesses. My EUR 780M could be as wrong as the FA's EUR 850M. The true answer depends on how much of the 2023-2024 margin expansion is structural vs cyclical -- and this won't be clear until we see 2-3 years of "normalized" results.
- Oil at $97 is a new variable not present when the original R2 was done (Mar 6). Its impact on cement production costs is material but hard to quantify precisely because Buzzi uses a mix of fuels (coal, pet coke, alternative fuels) and has been increasing alternative fuel usage.
- The tariff situation is in flux (Supreme Court struck down most Trump tariffs). This could be positive (removes import barriers, which is BAD for domestic producers like Buzzi) or neutral (tariffs may be reimposed through alternative legal mechanisms).

### Limitaciones de Este Analisis
- I could not access Buzzi's Q3 2025 trading update PDF directly, relying on web summaries
- European competitor valuation multiples (Heidelberg, Holcim) were not available at granular operating-segment level
- I did not have access to sell-side analyst reports for their detailed EBIT forecasts
- The Russia risk assessment is qualitative; I lack data on exact sanctions exposure and secondary sanctions risk

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- For cyclical companies, the DA should always model a "time to trough" estimate with explicit probability -- not just a trough FV, but WHEN the trough earnings are expected and how long they persist
- Oil price sensitivity should be a standard DA module for energy-intensive businesses
