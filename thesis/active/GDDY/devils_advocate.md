## Counter-Thesis Summary

The GDDY thesis rests on the premise that the market is pricing a -10.7% FCF decline while FCF has been growing 20%. This framing is seductive but incomplete. The DA investigation reveals **six material concerns** that the thesis either minimizes or omits entirely:

1. **Securities fraud investigation** -- multiple law firms are actively investigating GDDY for potential federal securities violations after the Feb 24 Q4 earnings disclosure. The thesis does NOT mention this.
2. **FTC 20-year consent order** -- finalized May 2025. GoDaddy's security failures (14+ breaches since 2011) are not just "past incidents" -- they resulted in a federal enforcement action with a 20-year compliance monitoring period. The thesis buries this under KC#7 without acknowledging the FTC action.
3. **Starboard is actively exiting** -- reduced stake by 9.7% in Q4 2025, following a 1.8M share reduction in Q2 2024. This is a slow liquidation, not a stable activist position. KC#5 may be approaching faster than the thesis assumes.
4. **Insider selling is extreme and pattern-based** -- 48 sells / 0 buys from CEO over 5 years. CFO sold $1.5M on the same day (Mar 4, 2026). Zero open-market purchases across entire C-suite for 2+ years. The thesis called this "immaterial" in R1 before R3 upgraded it.
5. **Discounted .com bookings gambit** -- management admitted the promotional .com pricing "reduced upfront bookings and near-term revenue," directly causing the guidance miss. This is self-inflicted revenue quality degradation.
6. **FCF margin may be at structural peak** -- 22.5% to 31.8% in 3 years, driven by R&D cuts (19.4% to 17.0%). Underinvestment risk is real for a company facing AI disruption from multiple directions.

Despite these concerns, the thesis **does survive** at the current price of $82. The reverse DCF gap of 30.4pp provides substantial cushion. But the margin of safety is narrower than the thesis claims, and the risk profile is worse than presented.

## Key Findings

### 1. Securities Fraud Investigation (NOT in thesis -- OMISSION)

**Evidence:** On February 25-26, 2026, at least four law firms (Glancy Prongay & Wolke, Howard G. Smith, Frank R. Cruz, Portnoy Law Firm) announced securities fraud investigations into GoDaddy. The investigations focus on the Q4 2025 earnings disclosure where management revealed the .com promotional pricing impact on bookings and 2026 guidance.

**Severity: MODERATE**

**Rationale:** These "ambulance chaser" investigations are common after large stock drops and rarely result in material damages. However:
- The core allegation has substance -- management did not disclose the .com promotion impact until Q4 results
- If a class action lawsuit is certified, legal costs could be $50-100M+ and create management distraction
- The FTC consent order (below) establishes a pattern of regulatory/legal issues that could compound

**Resolution:** Investment Committee should acknowledge this risk. Not a deal-breaker at $82, but should be monitored as a KC: securities class action certified with damages claim >$200M.

---

### 2. FTC 20-Year Consent Order on Data Security (Minimized in thesis)

**Evidence:** The FTC finalized an enforcement action against GoDaddy in May 2025 for "misleading consumers by failing to implement data security protections." Key requirements:
- 20-year compliance monitoring period
- Mandatory comprehensive infosec program
- Independent biennial security assessments
- 180-day deadline to disconnect unsupported hardware/software
- Phishing-resistant MFA implementation for all employees/contractors

This stems from 14+ data breaches since 2011, with the SAME threat actors breaching GoDaddy repeatedly between 2019-2022 due to lack of basic security measures (no MFA, no monitoring).

**Severity: HIGH**

**Rationale:** The thesis mentions "past security incidents" as KC#7 but does NOT mention:
- The FTC enforcement action specifically
- The 20-year monitoring period
- That the same attackers breached GoDaddy repeatedly for 3+ years
- The compliance cost (biennial assessments, security infrastructure investment)
- That this directly contradicts the "award-winning security" marketing claims

For a company hosting 20M+ SMB websites and processing their data, a federal finding of systematic security negligence is material. The compliance costs will eat into the FCF margin expansion story. The reputational risk is ongoing under 20 years of FTC oversight.

**Resolution:** FCF margin bear case should incorporate $30-50M/yr in ongoing compliance costs (currently NOT in any scenario). KC#7 should be upgraded to specifically reference the FTC consent order.

### 3. Starboard Value Actively Reducing Position (Thesis understates)

**Evidence from 13F filings:**
- Q2 2024: Reduced GDDY stake by 1.8M shares
- Q4 2025: Reduced by another 303,993 shares (9.7% reduction)
- Current: 2,830,100 shares (2.02% of GDDY, down from ~6.5% initially)
- Position size: $558M (still their largest holding, but shrinking)

**Severity: MODERATE-HIGH**

**Rationale:** The thesis lists "Starboard exits completely" as KC#5. But the pattern shows Starboard IS exiting -- slowly and systematically. Their stake has gone from ~6.5% to ~2%. They are NOT a stable activist partner anymore; they are in harvest/exit mode. At $82, they are sitting on losses (entered ~$95 average).

This matters because:
- Starboard's margin improvement campaign was the KEY catalyst for the 12.6% to 23.0% op margin expansion
- Once Starboard is fully out, who holds management accountable on cost discipline?
- The "potential take-private by PE" bull case (15% probability in thesis) becomes less likely without an activist pushing for it
- Continued selling pressure from a 2% holder liquidating

**Resolution:** KC#5 should be recalibrated. "Starboard exits completely" is a binary kill condition, but the threat is gradual loss of activist oversight. A better KC: "Starboard stake falls below 1% AND no new activist takes a 5%+ position."

Bhutani has NEVER bought open-market shares. He chose to set up only SELL plans. Zero buying across the entire C-suite for 2+ years is not "diversification" -- it is systematic lack of conviction.

### 5. Discounted .com Bookings -- Self-Inflicted Revenue Quality Erosion (NOT in thesis)

**Evidence:** In the Q4 2025 earnings call (Feb 24, 2026), management disclosed:
- "Introduced a promotional price for .com domains with a 1-year term"
- "The shift in term mix, combined with the promotional price, reduced upfront bookings and near-term revenue"
- 2026 guidance: $5.195-5.275B (vs analyst expectations of $5.3-5.5B)
- Q4 bookings of $1.28B missed expectations

This disclosure triggered the 14.26% stock drop on Feb 25, 2026.

**Severity: MODERATE**

**Rationale:**
- The thesis frames guidance disappointment as "conservative guidance" influenced by Starboard. But management CHOSE to discount .com domains, directly sacrificing bookings quality
- This raises questions about competitive pressure: WHY did GoDaddy need to discount its highest-margin product?
- If domain pricing power is the moat anchor, discounting .coms is either desperate (bad) or strategic (needs evidence of higher LTV from promo customers)
- The securities fraud investigations suggest the market views the timing/disclosure of this promotion as problematic
- Revenue guidance of 6% YoY now includes a HEADWIND from this promotion -- organic growth may be even lower

**Resolution:** The thesis should address WHY management discounted .coms. If this is competitive response to Namecheap/Cloudflare domain pricing, it undermines the pricing power moat. If it is a customer acquisition strategy, the thesis should model the CAC/LTV implications.

### 6. FCF Margin at Structural Peak -- Underinvestment Risk (Partially addressed)

**Evidence:**
- FCF margin: 22.5% (2022) to 31.8% (2025) -- 930bps expansion in 3 years
- R&D/Revenue: 19.4% (2022) to 17.0% (2025) -- declining investment
- The FTC consent order MANDATES new security investments (not yet in guidance)
- SBC/Revenue stable at 6.4% -- means true "economic" FCF is $1.26B, not $1.58B
- Competitive landscape is intensifying: Wix scored higher on features, Squarespace on design, both investing heavily in AI

**Severity: MODERATE-HIGH**

**Rationale:**
- The margin expansion is the core driver of the "FCF machine" narrative
- But the expansion came partly from cutting R&D (240bps reduction), which is unsustainable for a company whose competitors are outspending on AI
- The FTC compliance costs (security program, assessments, hardware disconnection) will add $30-50M/yr minimum in OpEx
- If FCF margin reverts even partially (to 28-29%), the valuation math weakens significantly
- The Altman Z-Score reportedly at 1.17 (distress zone) is likely distorted by negative equity from buybacks, but the underlying signal is: this company has meaningful leverage and low equity cushion

**Resolution:** R3 already uses $1.3-1.4B as normalized FCF (vs $1.58B raw). This is correct. But the DIRECTION matters: is FCF margin expanding or peaking? The evidence suggests peaking, with FTC compliance costs as a new headwind.
