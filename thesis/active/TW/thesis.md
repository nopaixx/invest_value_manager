## Business Understanding

### Model of Business

**Problem solved:** Fixed income and derivatives markets are historically opaque, fragmented, and phone-traded. Tradeweb electronifies price discovery, execution, and post-trade processing, making $2.8T+ of daily volume more transparent, efficient, and cheaper to execute.

**Customer segments:**
- ~2,500+ institutional clients (asset managers, insurance companies, pension funds, central banks)
- ~70+ liquidity providers (major banks/dealers — Goldman, JPMorgan, Citi, etc.)
- Retail platforms (via Tradeweb Direct)
- LSEG/Refinitiv data distribution network

**Revenue model (FY2025: $2.1B):**

| Revenue Type | Description | Characteristics |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Variable transaction fees | Per-trade fees based on volume | ~55% of revenue. Grows with ADV. RPM declining due to mix shifts |
| Fixed subscription fees | Annual/multi-year platform access | ~$95.8M/quarter ($383M annualized). Sticky, recurring, growing |
| Market data fees | Data licensing | ~$105M/year (2026 guidance). Growing with LSEG contract renewal |
| Other (incl. Canton Network) | Digital assets/tokenization | ~$6.6M/quarter. Nascent but strategic |

**Why the model is powerful:**
1. **Two-sided network effects:** More clients attract more dealers, and vice versa. Once liquidity concentrates on a venue, switching costs become prohibitive because leaving means losing access to the best prices.
2. **ADV-driven revenue flywheel:** Revenue grows with Average Daily Volume, which grows with (a) market activity, (b) new product electronification, and (c) wallet share gains from competitors.
3. **Toll booth economics:** TW earns a fraction of a cent per $1M traded on $2.8T+ daily. The per-unit fee is invisible to clients but compounds to $2.1B/year.

### Unit Economics

- **CAC:** Effectively near-zero for established products. Dealers and clients are pre-existing; TW just needs to offer a better protocol (RFQ, portfolio trading, automation). The cost is in technology development, not customer acquisition.
- **LTV:** Extremely high. Client relationships span decades. 95%+ client retention per management commentary.
- **LTV/CAC:** Not traditionally calculable (network business, not SaaS), but the 67% gross margin and 95%+ retention imply an exceptional ratio.
- **Revenue per employee:** ~$2.1B / ~2,100 employees = ~$1M per employee — elite-level productivity.

### Electronification S-Curve: Where Are We?

This is the most critical business question. The electronification of fixed income is a multi-decade structural trend, and TW is at the center of it.

| Asset Class | Electronic Penetration (2024) | 2019 Level | Trajectory | TW Position |
|-------------|-------------------------------|------------|------------|-------------|
| US IG Credit | ~45% | ~25% | Accelerating (inflection) | #2 behind MKTX, gaining share |
| US HY Credit | ~30% | ~15% | Mid-S-curve | Growing, MKTX losing share |
| US Treasuries | ~70-80% | ~55% | Mature phase | #1, >50% share |
| Rates Derivatives (swaps) | ~30% | ~15% | Early-mid S-curve | #1, electronification up 90+bps YoY |
| EU Government Bonds | ~40-50% | ~25% | Accelerating | Record ADV in Q4, +36% YoY Jan 2026 |
| Repo/Money Markets | ~25-30% | ~10% | Early S-curve | Record repo revenues |
| MBS/Mortgages | ~20-25% | ~10% | Early S-curve | Growing, +16.7% Jan ADV |

**My assessment:** TW is in the EARLY-TO-MID portion of the S-curve across its aggregate product suite. US Treasuries are more mature (~70%+ electronic), but credit, derivatives, repo, and mortgages have massive room for electronification. The aggregate TAM for electronification is expanding — as more asset classes move electronic, cross-product synergies (portfolio trading, automation) accelerate the next product. This is the core of the long-term growth thesis.

## META-REFLECTION

### Incertidumbres/Dudas
- The FCF margin of 52% is exceptionally high. While I investigated and concluded it is real (not client money distortion), there may be working capital timing effects that inflate a single year. The 3-year average of 50% is more reliable as a base.
- LSEG's 80% voting control means governance is effectively determined by a single shareholder. If LSEG's strategic priorities change (e.g., post-Elliott activism), TW could be affected in unpredictable ways.
- Insider ownership at 0.2% is the lowest in our Tier A portfolio. CEO sold $14M in Feb 2026. While all sales were pre-planned (10b5-1), the ABSENCE of any insider buying is notable.
- RPM decline of 14% in credit is significant. If this trend persists or spreads to rates, revenue growth could disappoint even with strong ADV.

### Sugerencias para el Sistema
- The quality_scorer.py tool assigns 0/8 for Market Position when it cannot auto-detect. For well-known platform monopolies (TW, MKTX, exchanges), this creates a systematic -8 point bias. Consider allowing manual override input or sector-specific defaults.
- The DCF tool defaults do not match my derived growth rates. A way to pass custom growth/margin parameters directly would improve accuracy.
