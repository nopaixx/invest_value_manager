### What the Shorts Likely See

**THESIS 1: Regulatory contagion is structural, not episodic**
Italy (2025) -> Brazil (2025) -> Turkey investigation (2025) -> France reform (2026). The pattern is clear: governments worldwide are targeting the meal voucher oligopoly to reduce merchant costs. France chose transparency over caps THIS TIME, but the regulatory trajectory is toward lower fees everywhere. The shorts are betting that 2025 was the beginning, not the end, of regulatory normalization.

**THESIS 2: 2025 was the peak, 2026 is the cliff**
FY2025 record results are backward-looking. 2026 guidance is -8/-12% EBITDA. The shorts do not care that 2025 was great -- they are positioning for the 2026 earnings compression cycle. The earnings beat was the LAST good quarter.

**THESIS 3: The moat is regulatory capture, and it is being removed**
Edenred's margins (EBITDA 41%) are extraordinarily high for a payments processor. Compare: Visa 67%, Mastercard 58%, Adyen 49%, PayPal 22%, Worldline 18%. Edenred's margins are closer to Visa/MA than to normal payment processors. Why? Because the meal voucher system is a government-mandated closed-loop network with limited competition. When governments REMOVE that protection (Italy caps, Brazil interchange limits, Turkey antitrust probe), margins normalize toward Worldline/PayPal territory. A margin compression from 41% to 25-30% would halve the EBITDA.

**THESIS 4: iFood-Alelo deal creates a formidable new competitor in Brazil**
iFood (backed by Prosus) is in advanced talks to acquire Alelo for R$5-6 billion. Combined entity would have 35%+ market share in Brazil -- ahead of Edenred's Ticket. This creates a tech-native competitor with delivery platform integration that Edenred cannot match. In a deregulated, interoperable Brazil market, network effects flow to the PLATFORM player, not the legacy voucher company.

**THESIS 5: Negative equity + buybacks = financial engineering, not value creation**
Equity: EUR -1,046M (worsening from -809M in 6 months). EUR 600M buyback program continues while equity is deeply negative. This is financial engineering -- buying back shares with borrowed money while the balance sheet deteriorates. When EBITDA declines in 2026, leverage increases mechanically. The shorts may be betting on a credit event or forced buyback suspension.

## 2. Asunciones Clave Desafiadas

### Assumption 1: "90.5% of the business is unaffected by regulation"

- **Thesis claim:** Brazil meal vouchers = 9.5% of revenue. Rest is fine.
- **Counter-evidence:** Brazil TOTAL = 19% of operating revenue (per Edenred's own Q3 2025 filings). Italy has already imposed caps. Turkey is under antitrust investigation. France introduced a "transparency charter" (first step before regulation?). Belgium, Mexico could follow. The "unaffected" portion is shrinking.
- **Additionally:** The risk assessment from Feb 7 already flagged this: cumulative Italy + Brazil impact = EUR 270-320M = 22-27% of EBITDA. This is NOT 9.5% -- it is a quarter of profitability.
- **Severity: HIGH**
- **Resolution:** The thesis should explicitly state "affected revenue" as 30-35% (Italy + Brazil + Turkey) and model scenarios for further contagion.

### Assumption 2: "2027-2028 guidance of +8-12% EBITDA growth is reliable"

- **Thesis claim:** Management confirmed 2027-28 targets. Recovery is coming.
- **Counter-evidence:** Management held a Capital Markets Day ONE WEEK before the Brazil decree and did NOT incorporate it. Guidance was then cut from +2/+4% to -8/-12% for 2026 -- a 10-16pp swing. This is the same management now guiding +8/+12% for 2027-28. Their credibility on forward guidance is damaged. Additionally, this guidance assumes: (a) no further regulatory action, (b) Brazil impact is fully absorbed, (c) no new market regulation. None of these are guaranteed.
- **Autonomous Research rates UNDERPERFORM with PT EUR 17.50** -- below current price. They explicitly disagree with the recovery thesis.
- **Severity: HIGH**
- **Resolution:** Weight 2027-28 guidance at 60-70% probability, not 80%+. If 2027 delivers <+5%, KC #10 triggers.

### Assumption 3: "France KC#1 is CLEARED -- no more French regulatory risk"

- **Thesis claim:** France chose transparency over caps. KC#1 deactivated.
- **Counter-evidence:** France chose not to impose caps IN THIS ROUND. But:
  - A proposed 8% social levy on meal vouchers was discussed (source: Compound with Rene analysis)
  - France sovereign debt downgrade by Fitch creates fiscal pressure -- meal voucher tax benefits cost the French treasury real money
  - The Autorite de la Concurrence fined the sector EUR 415M in 2019 for anticompetitive practices
  - France explicitly transferred oversight from the dissolved National Commission to Banque de France -- a MORE powerful regulator
  - The reform mandates full digitalization by Feb 2027 and expands usage (supermarkets, Sundays) -- these INCREASE the political visibility of the meal voucher system
- **Severity: MODERATE** (not HIGH because no concrete current threat, but the thesis is premature in declaring this "cleared")
- **Resolution:** Reclassify KC#1 from "CLEARED" to "DORMANT." France risk is reduced but not eliminated. The 8% levy threat and Banque de France oversight are new variables.

### Assumption 4: "The market is pricing near-permanent destruction -- unrealistic"

- **Thesis claim:** Market implies -15.3% FCF decline for 5 years. Even in bear case, unrealistic.
- **Counter-evidence:** The market is NOT pricing permanent destruction. The market is pricing MARGIN NORMALIZATION. If Edenred's EBITDA margin compresses from 41% to 30% over 5 years due to regulatory normalization across markets, FCF would decline significantly from current levels and then stabilize at a lower base. This is not "permanent destruction" -- it is a secular margin compression thesis. At 30% EBITDA margin on 2024 revenue of EUR 2,856M, EBITDA would be EUR 857M vs current EUR 1,200M. At 8x EV/EBITDA, EV = EUR 6.9B, equity EUR 5.6B, FV = EUR 24/share. The market at EUR 18.79 is pricing this scenario at ~75% probability.
- **Severity: MODERATE**
- **Resolution:** The reverse DCF analysis should model margin compression scenarios, not just growth rate changes.

### Assumption 5: "Insider buying signals conviction"

- **Thesis claim:** Smart money profile shows Board member Kelly Richdale bought $50K on 2026-03-07.
- **Counter-evidence:** $50K from a Board member is trivial -- not meaningful skin in the game. CEO Bertrand Dumazy owns ~EUR 8.7M in shares (data possibly stale from 2019-2020), which is modest for a CEO of a EUR 4.4B company. Insider ownership at 0.1% is extremely low. No reported insider buying at significant scale during the 50%+ decline from EUR 34.75 to EUR 18.79. If insiders truly believed in EUR 29+ FV, why are they not buying aggressively at EUR 18?
- **Severity: LOW**
- **Resolution:** Absence of insider buying is a weak negative signal, not a kill condition.
