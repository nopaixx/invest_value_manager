## Summary

The $390 FV is defensible but optimistic. The thesis correctly identifies Adobe's exceptional fundamentals (89% GM, 53% ROIC, $9.9B FCF) and the market's overreaction to AI fears. However, the thesis **underweights three material risks**: (1) the competitive threat has intensified dramatically since Feb 10 -- Canva launched free Affinity (1M signups in 4 days), Figma launched AI credits, Goldman downgraded to SELL at $290; (2) the FTC risk is more structurally damaging than a one-time fine because mandatory cancellation ease could permanently compress lifetime value; (3) the "AI strengthens Adobe" narrative, while partially true, obscures the fact that AI is also commoditizing the lower end of creative work where future growth lives. The 37.5% MoS provides substantial cushion, but with consensus PT at $389-390 (essentially equal to our FV), we have **no informational edge on valuation**.

## Findings

### Finding 1: No Informational Edge -- FV Converges with Consensus
**Severity: HIGH**

**What the thesis claims:** FV $390 based on OEY ($408) + DCF ($362) weighted 60/40. Implies 37.5% upside.

**What the evidence shows:** Analyst consensus PT is $389-390 (median from 33 analysts). Mean is $389.47. Our FV is indistinguishable from consensus. This is a direct violation of Error #49 (anchoring to consensus) and the critical-thinking skill's Level 4 warning: "The consensus IS the price... If my FV converges to consensus without independent reasoning, I don't have edge."

The thesis acknowledges this in the pre-earnings section ("Consensus PT $403-418 is close to our FV $390. This means we do NOT have a strong informational edge vs consensus on valuation") but then does nothing about it. If the entire sell-side arrives at the same number, the upside is already priced into expectations. The only edge claimed is "conviction about AI disruption timing" -- which is subjective narrative, not data.

**Impact on FV:** No direct FV change, but the MoS is meaningless if consensus agrees. The stock is already trading at the consensus bear-case PT of $270. If consensus is right that $390 is fair, the stock should converge there naturally -- but it hasn't. That's information: the market is pricing something below consensus.

**Resolution:** The investment committee must articulate what SPECIFIC information we have that 33 analysts don't. If the answer is "nothing," the position is a consensus trade with no edge.

### Finding 2: Competitive Threat Intensified Since Thesis Date
**Severity: HIGH**

**What the thesis claims:** "Canva/Figma attack different markets. Adobe = professionals ($60/month); Canva = amateurs ($13/month). Not competing directly."

**What the evidence shows:** This was already outdated at thesis date (Feb 10) and is now materially wrong:

1. **Canva launched free Affinity (Feb 2026):** Merged Photo, Designer, and Publisher into one free app directly competing with Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign. 1 million signups in 4 days. This is NOT the $13/month Canva for amateurs -- this is a professional-grade tool at $0/month vs Adobe's $55-90/month. TechRadar calls it an "Adobe killer."

2. **Figma launched AI credit model (Mar 2026):** Directly targeting Adobe's design customer base with AI-native workflows. Figma went public Jul 2025, peaked at $68B valuation, now at ~$13B (down 81%) but still at 13x revenue vs Adobe's 5.8x.

3. **Goldman Sachs downgraded to SELL (Jan 12, 2026) at $290:** Applied 15x P/E (50% discount to peers) citing "reduced pricing leverage amid proliferation of AI tools." Goldman's Borges specifically flagged EPS pressure if revenue growth falls below 10%.

4. **BMO downgraded citing entry-level losses:** "Proprietary research indicating Adobe is losing ground in the entry-level segment to rivals like Canva, particularly among freelancers and students." This is where FUTURE professionals come from.

5. **Analyst sentiment most bearish since 2013** (Bloomberg). Only 20 buys vs 15 holds + 4 sells. This is not normal for a Tier A compounder.

The thesis line "Adobe has 17x more R&D spend than Figma" is true but irrelevant if the competition is free (Canva/Affinity) or venture-funded to grow (Figma).

**Impact on FV:** If pricing power erodes even 1-2% due to competitive pressure on the lower end (students, freelancers, prosumers), and churn increases 1-2pp from Canva/Affinity migration, the cumulative revenue impact over 5 years is $1.5-3B. This translates to approximately -$10-20/share on FV.

### Finding 3: Firefly Revenue -- Incremental or Cannibalistic?
**Severity: MODERATE**

**What the thesis claims:** "Firefly already generates $400M revenue, 11% of new ARR. Adobe IS the AI for creativos."

**What the evidence shows:** The $400M figure requires scrutiny:

1. **Definition unclear:** Adobe states "AI-influenced ARR is >1/3 of total business." That's ~$6.4B of $19.2B ARR. But "AI-influenced" means any subscription where AI features exist -- it doesn't mean customers are paying EXTRA for AI. The $400M "direct revenue" from Firefly credits is more meaningful but small relative to $24B total revenue (1.7%).

2. **Cannibalization risk:** If Firefly makes one designer 3x more productive, companies need fewer subscriptions. Adobe's TAM for seats could SHRINK even as productivity per seat grows. The thesis assumes AI grows TAM (more people creating), but Goldman and Jefferies argue it may SHRINK seats.

3. **Credit consumption tripling QoQ:** Impressive, but gen credits are bundled in existing plans. Users consuming more of included credits doesn't generate incremental revenue until they exceed limits. The question is: what % of users hit credit limits and pay overage? Adobe doesn't disclose this.

4. **CIO survey:** IT budget growth decelerating to 3.4% in 2026, with funds diverted FROM application software TOWARD AI infrastructure. Enterprise customers may consolidate tools, not expand.

**Impact on FV:** If Firefly is 60% cannibalistic (replaces existing seat growth rather than adding new revenue), the effective growth rate drops from 7% to 5.5-6%. At 5.5% growth, DCF base case = ~$370 vs $403 at 7%.

### Finding 4: FTC Structural Risk Underestimated
**Severity: MODERATE**

**What the thesis claims:** Expected cost $470M one-time + $334M/yr ongoing (churn). Settlement probability 55-65%.

**What the evidence shows:**

1. **NEW copyright lawsuit (Dec 2025):** Class-action alleging Adobe used pirated books (via SlimPajama/Books3 dataset) to train its SlimLM AI model. This is SEPARATE from the FTC ROSCA case. Anthropic settled a similar Books3 case for $1.5B (largest AI copyright settlement ever). The thesis doesn't mention this lawsuit AT ALL.

2. **Named executives create settlement pressure:** Sawhney and Wadhwani face personal liability. But Wadhwani was still receiving stock compensation as of Jan 2026 (insider tracker shows Jan 23 grant). Named executives with personal liability AND ongoing compensation creates misaligned incentives -- they benefit from settlement but may resist terms that harm stock price.

3. **FTC political dynamics:** The thesis estimates 55-65% settlement probability but doesn't account for the current political environment. FTC enforcement priorities shift with administrations. This is genuinely uncertain.

4. **Class action running parallel:** The thesis mentions this but doesn't price the separate copyright class action. If both cases proceed simultaneously, legal costs escalate and management distraction increases.

**Impact on FV:** Adding $100-200M for copyright lawsuit expected cost, and widening the uncertainty band on FTC outcome. Net: -$5-10/share additional risk vs thesis.

### Finding 5: Insider Selling Pattern
**Severity: MODERATE**

**What the thesis claims:** "Low insider ownership (0.2%) but no massive selling."

**What the evidence shows:**

1. **CFO Durn sold $485K on Jan 27, 2026** -- 2 months before earnings, just after buying $508K in Mar 2025. The buy-then-sell within 10 months at lower prices is an odd signal.

4. **0.2% insider ownership** is extremely low for a company this size. This means management has minimal skin in the game at current prices. Compare to other portfolio holdings where insider ownership is 5-10%.

**Impact on FV:** No direct FV impact, but the lack of insider buying at "52-week lows" and "37.5% MoS" is a soft bearish signal. If management believed in $390 FV, they'd be buying aggressively at $283.

### Finding 6: Growth Deceleration Trajectory
**Severity: MODERATE**

**What the thesis claims:** Revenue growth 6.5-7.5% sustainable. "TAM 5% + pricing 2% - FTC churn drag 0.5%."

**What the evidence shows:**

1. **FY2026 guidance: $25.9-26.1B** = 8.8-9.7% growth. But Q1 guidance specifically is $6.25-6.30B = 9.9% vs Q4's 11%. This is sequential deceleration within the guidance itself.

2. **Revenue growth trend:** 10.2% -> 10.8% -> 10.5% over last 3 years. Guidance for FY26 at ~9.2% midpoint. The thesis uses 6.5-7.5% which is BELOW management guidance. This is conservative but the question is whether the 7% is floor or reality.

3. **Receivables growing faster than revenue:** Receivables +13.1% vs revenue +10.5%. This gap (2.6pp) can indicate channel stuffing, longer payment terms, or quality-of-revenue issues. The thesis doesn't address this.

4. **Document Cloud dependency on AI:** The thesis expects Document Cloud to contribute, but if AI commoditizes PDF tools (Google's AI in Workspace, Microsoft's Copilot in Office), Document Cloud's 24% revenue share is vulnerable.

**Impact on FV:** If growth settles at 5% instead of 7% (adjusting for competitive pressure + FTC churn + AI cannibalization), the DCF base case falls from $403 to $387 (at 5% growth). Combined with the other adjustments, cumulative impact is material.

### Finding 7: Valuation Method Sensitivity
**Severity: LOW**

**What the thesis claims:** OEY method ($408) weighted 60% + DCF ($362) weighted 40% = $390.

**What the evidence shows:**

1. **OEY target yield of 5.5%:** The thesis requires a 5.5% OEY for a compounder with regulatory overhang. For context, the S&P 500 FCF yield is ~3.5%. A 5.5% OEY implies the market should pay a lower multiple than the S&P average, which seems reasonable for a stock with legal uncertainty and competitive risk. This assumption is fair.

2. **WACC of 12% in DCF:** The tool calculates WACC at 9.0% (auto) while the thesis uses 12%. Using 12% is conservative, which BENEFITS the DA (lower FV). But if the true WACC is 9-10%, the DCF FV would be $400-500+. The thesis is actually being conservative here in the investor's favor.

3. **Terminal growth 2.5%:** Reasonable for a software company. No issue.

**Impact on FV:** The methodology is sound. The OEY method is appropriately weighted for a Tier A compounder. The main vulnerability is the growth input (7% vs potentially 5-6%), not the method.

## Pre-Earnings Risk Assessment

### What Could Go Wrong Mar 12

1. **Revenue miss (<$6.15B):** If revenue disappoints, the growth deceleration narrative accelerates. Stock could test 52-week low $244.28. Given Iran crisis context (oil $92, VIX 27), enterprise IT budget caution is plausible.

2. **Churn commentary:** If management discloses churn approaching 12% (vs current ~9%), KC#2 approaches. This is the SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT metric to watch. FTC pressure may have already affected cancellation behavior.

3. **Firefly deceleration:** If gen credit consumption growth slows from "tripled QoQ" to "doubled" or "grew 50%," the AI narrative weakens. The market needs ACCELERATION in AI metrics to offset the competitive fears.

4. **Guidance cut or narrow:** If FY2026 guidance is narrowed to lower end ($25.9B) or cut due to macro/oil/enterprise caution, the stock could gap down 10-15%.

5. **Legal reserve increase:** If Adobe discloses a large litigation reserve (>$500M), this signals trial preparation rather than settlement, and the market will price in worst-case FTC outcome.

6. **CPI same-day:** If Feb CPI runs hot on the same day (Mar 12), rate-cut expectations die and software broadly sells off. ADBE could get caught in the crossfire regardless of its own results.

7. **Copyright lawsuit disclosure:** If the earnings call reveals the Books3/SlimLM copyright lawsuit is material, this is a NEW negative catalyst not in the current stock price.

### How DA Changes the Earnings Framework

The thesis's pre-earnings framework is well-structured. I would add ONE modification:

- **In MISS scenario:** The thesis says "HOLD" unless KC triggered. I agree, but with the caveat that if Firefly metrics DECELERATE simultaneously with a revenue miss, this is a STRUCTURAL signal (not macro noise). The thesis should define: "If revenue misses AND Firefly gen credit growth falls below 50% QoQ, the AI-counternarrative is weakened. Reduce FV to $340-350."

The thesis treats each risk independently; the correlation between them is the real danger.
