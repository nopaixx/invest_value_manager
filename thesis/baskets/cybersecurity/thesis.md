## Thematic Thesis

Every organization needs to defend against increasingly sophisticated cyber threats.
Cybersecurity spending is **non-discretionary** and growing 12-15% CAGR globally.
Global spend growing from $188B (2024) to $300B+ (2028).

The best companies have WIDE moats from:
1. **Switching costs** — ripping out security infrastructure is operationally terrifying
2. **Proprietary technology** — FTNT's custom ASICs, QLYS's cloud-native scanner
3. **Network effects** — threat intelligence improves with scale (more endpoints = better detection)

**Hormuz Crisis Tailwind:** Operation Epic Fury (Feb-Mar 2026) elevates state-sponsored
cyber threats. Iran, Russia, and affiliated groups target Western critical infrastructure.
Cybersecurity is the one IT budget that INCREASES during geopolitical crises.

## Why This Theme Wins Over 10+ Years

- Attack surface expanding (IoT, cloud, AI-generated threats, OT/ICS)
- Regulatory compliance mandates (SEC cyber rules, NIS2, DORA) force spend
- AI both amplifies threats AND creates new defense opportunities
- Consolidation trend benefits platform vendors (FTNT, Palo Alto)

## Shared Competitive Dynamics

- Market fragmenting AND consolidating simultaneously (fragmented vendors, consolidating platforms)
- SASE/SSE convergence creates winner-take-most dynamics for platform vendors
- CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Zscaler are primary competitors — all at premium multiples
- Our edge: finding quality at reasonable valuations (FTNT at 10% MoS, QLYS gated)

## Basket Kill Conditions

1. Global cyber spending growth decelerates to <5% CAGR for 2 consecutive years
2. 2+ positions hit individual KCs simultaneously
3. Major regulatory change that commoditizes cybersecurity (unlikely but catastrophic)

## Correlation Assessment

- **HIGH correlation:** All tied to global cybersecurity spend cycle
- **MODERATE correlation:** Similar customer base (enterprise IT buyers)
- **LOW correlation:** Different sub-sectors (network security vs cloud security vs vulnerability management)
- **Mitigation:** Sub-sector diversification within theme reduces correlation vs single-stock
