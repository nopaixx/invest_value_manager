# Sector: Promotional Products

> Ultima actualizacion: 2026-04-08 (macro-analyst refresh — tariff regime change post-SCOTUS, FOUR.L trading update, industry Q1 data)
> Status: NEUTRAL (deteriorating — tariff + recession headwinds intensifying)

**April 8 update:** (1) **Tariff regime restructured post-SCOTUS:** Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs Feb 20. IEEPA tariffs terminated Feb 24. However, Section 301 tariffs on China (7.5-25% depending on product) REMAIN in effect from 2018-era. Trump imposed new 10% global tariff under Section 122 (valid 150 days to Jul 24). New Section 301 investigations launched Mar 11 targeting China — could result in HIGHER tariffs on specific categories later in 2026. Net effect for promo products: tariff burden on China imports REDUCED vs peak 2025 (when IEEPA + reciprocal pushed effective rates to ~34-49%), but still elevated at ~17-35% depending on product category (Section 301 + Section 122 global 10%). NOT back to pre-2025 levels. (2) **FOUR.L at 3,538p** (vs entry target 3,400p — only 4% above entry). Jan-Feb 2026 trading "in line with Board expectations" — orders and revenue slightly down vs 2025. Tariff costs being phased in by suppliers, tariff policy "continues to evolve." Cash $133M at year-end. Analyst consensus Strong Buy (4 buy, 1 hold), avg PT ~4,376-4,908p. No Q1 formal trading update yet beyond the Jan-Feb commentary in the FY2025 results release. (3) **Industry stress deepening:** PPAI data shows 56.8% of suppliers cite tariffs as biggest margin challenge, 54.3% cite rising freight, 50.6% cite reduced client budgets. Only 53% of distributors expect profit in 2026. Nearly half of suppliers report margin declines. Industry in "holding pattern" — stable demand but shrinking profitability. Sourcing diversification (China+1) accelerating across consumer goods broadly but promo-specific alternatives still limited given China's dominance in hard goods customization. (4) **Recession risk elevated:** Corporate marketing budgets discretionary — cut early. SMB confidence under pressure from tariff uncertainty + higher input costs. This is the demand-side risk layered on top of the supply-side tariff cost risk.

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The promotional products industry encompasses branded merchandise (apparel, drinkware, bags, pens, tech accessories, etc.) customised with an organisation's logo or message for marketing purposes. It is a surprisingly large market ($26-28B in North America alone) that serves as a tangible complement to digital advertising. The industry value proposition is simple: physical branded items create lasting impressions and brand recall that digital ads cannot match.

The market is extremely fragmented with ~26,000 distributors in North America, of which fewer than 1,000 exceed $2.5M in revenue. This fragmentation creates a structural opportunity for scale players with direct-to-customer models. The industry is cyclical, tied to corporate marketing budgets and general economic confidence, particularly from SMBs (small and medium businesses).

Current headwinds include US tariff disruption (90-95% of hard goods sourced from China), new customer acquisition softness, and general macroeconomic uncertainty. However, the industry has been relatively stable at $25-27B over the past 5 years, growing at low single-digit rates.

### Tecnologicos

| AI personalisation of promo products | Low | Low-positive | 3-5y |

### Regulatorios
| Regulacion | Probabilidad | Impacto |
|------------|--------------|---------|
| Tariffs on China imports (Section 301 + S122) | ACTIVE | High - 90-95% of hard goods from China. Post-SCOTUS: IEEPA tariffs gone but S301 (7.5-25%) + S122 (10%) remain. New S301 investigations could ADD more. |
| Environmental/sustainability rules | Medium | Low-Medium - shift to eco products |

### Competitivos
- Nuevos entrantes: Low barrier to entry but hard to scale
- Sustitutos: Digital advertising is the main substitute, but promotional products offer tangible ROI that digital cannot replicate

### Supply Chain Diversification (China+1)
Broader consumer goods industry accelerating sourcing away from China (Vietnam +12% YoY, Thailand +23% YoY inspection demand growth). However, promotional hard goods customisation remains heavily China-dependent due to specialised manufacturing infrastructure. Diversification for promo products is slower than for apparel/electronics. This remains an overhang until alternatives mature (2-4 year timeline).
