# Sector: Auto Salvage, Remarketing & Vehicle Disposition

> Ultima actualizacion: 2026-02-25
> Status: **SOBREPONDERAR** — Duopoly market with secular tailwind, cyclical near-term headwind

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The US salvage vehicle auction market is a ~$20B fee-revenue market (vehicle values much higher) dominated by a duopoly: Copart (~40% share) and IAA/RB Global (~35-40%). Combined ~80% share. No other competitor has critical mass to challenge the incumbents due to land requirements (hundreds of yards), insurance carrier relationships (multi-year exclusive), and buyer network effects (1M+ registered buyers).

**Secular tailwind:** Total loss frequency (TLF) has risen from 15.6% (2015) to 24.2% (2025, all-time high). Drivers: vehicle complexity (ADAS sensors, aluminum bodies), rising repair costs, labor shortages in body shops. EVs compound this — battery damage = total loss more frequently than ICE. This is a 20+ year trend showing no signs of reversal.

**Cyclical headwind (2025-2026):** Softer insurance claims activity as consumers reduce coverage and raise deductibles. Catastrophe (CAT) comps from 2024 hurricanes (Helene/Milton) distort YoY comparisons. This is temporary — insurance cycles are mean-reverting.

**Adjacent expansion:** Non-insurance ("Blue Car") remarketing (banks, rental fleets, dealers) growing 27%+ YoY. Purple Wave acquisition adds heavy equipment TAM. International expansion (~18% of CPRT revenue) opens European and Middle East markets with lower penetration.

## Riesgos Sectoriales

1. **Insurance soft cycle (NEAR-TERM):** Fewer claims = fewer total losses = lower volume. Cyclical, not structural.
2. **Autonomous vehicles (LONG-TERM, 10+ years):** Could reduce accident frequency. BUT: lower accident frequency partially offset by higher repair cost per incident.
3. **EV total loss dynamics:** EVs are MORE likely to be totaled (battery damage), but the salvage VALUE of totaled EVs is lower (battery disposal costs). Net effect: more volume, potentially lower ASPs.
4. **Regulatory:** Environmental regulations on salvage yard operations could increase costs.
5. **IAA integration risk:** If RB Global successfully integrates IAA and improves its platform, competitive dynamics could shift.
