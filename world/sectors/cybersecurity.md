## Resumen Ejecutivo

Cybersecurity is one of the most structurally attractive sectors in technology. Global spending is projected to reach $240B in 2026 (up from $213B in 2025, per Gartner), growing at 12-14% CAGR through 2030, driven by an expanding threat landscape (AI-powered attacks, ransomware, nation-state actors), accelerating cloud migration, regulatory mandates (DORA, NIS2, SEC disclosure rules), and the proliferation of IoT/OT attack surfaces. Unlike most IT spending, cybersecurity budgets exhibit significant downside resilience -- enterprises cannot defer security investments without material risk. Even during budget tightening cycles, cybersecurity routinely grows 2-3x faster than overall IT spending.

**Geopolitical threat escalation (Mar 2026):** The Iran/Hormuz crisis (Operation Epic Fury, re-escalated Mar 6 2026) has materially elevated the state-sponsored cyber threat landscape. Palo Alto Unit 42 issued a March 2026 threat brief on Iranian cyberattacks. Iran's "Electronic Operations Room" (formed Feb 28) has coordinated disruptive operations targeting Western critical infrastructure. The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security, UK NCSC, and US intelligence agencies have all issued warnings about Iranian cyber retaliation targeting energy, water, financial, and government infrastructure. Iran has 10+ years of history attacking US critical infrastructure (including the thwarted Boston Children's Hospital attack). This geopolitical dimension creates a structural tailwind: cybersecurity budgets become even harder to cut when state-sponsored threats are actively escalating. Fortinet's OT/IoT security capabilities are particularly relevant given the focus on critical infrastructure protection.

The sector is undergoing a structural transformation via **platformization**: enterprises are consolidating from an average of 83 security tools across 29 vendors toward integrated platforms. This creates a winner-take-most dynamic favoring vendors with the broadest product suites (Palo Alto Networks, Fortinet, CrowdStrike). The $84B in M&A disclosed in 2025 alone -- including Palo Alto's $25B CyberArk acquisition (closed Feb 11 2026) and Google's $32B Wiz deal -- confirms the consolidation thesis. Point solution vendors face existential risk of being acquired or marginalized.

**Gartner 2026 Trends (Feb 5 2026):** Six forces reshaping cybersecurity: (1) Agentic AI demands oversight -- new attack surfaces from AI agents used by employees/developers; (2) Global regulatory volatility -- boards/executives increasingly liable for compliance failures; (3) Post-quantum cryptography moving into action plans -- asymmetric crypto unsafe by 2030; (4) IAM adapts to AI agents -- machine identity registration/governance; (5) AI-driven SOC solutions destabilize operational norms; (6) GenAI breaks traditional awareness tactics (57% of employees use personal GenAI accounts for work, 33% input sensitive data into unapproved tools).

However, valuations are the critical constraint for value investors. The sector trades at a significant premium to broader tech (30-80x+ P/E for most names). High stock-based compensation (SBC) is endemic, often 15-30% of revenue, creating real dilution that inflates apparent FCF margins. Only a handful of names -- notably **Fortinet** (P/E ~32x, our anchor position) and **Check Point** (P/E ~16x, legacy value play) -- trade at valuations where a margin of safety is achievable. The opportunity is to find quality compounders during temporary dislocations, not to buy the sector broadly.

## Tendencias Estructurales

### 1. Platformization (Consolidation of Point Solutions)
The defining trend. Enterprises averaging 83 tools from 29 vendors are consolidating toward 2-3 platform vendors. Platformized organizations detect incidents 72 days faster and contain them 84 days sooner. By 2028, 45% of organizations will use fewer than 15 tools (vs 13% in 2023). PANW completed CyberArk acquisition (Feb 2026) creating 4-pillar platform: Strata (Network), Prisma (Cloud), Cortex (AI-SOC), Identity (CyberArk). CrowdStrike post-outage has paradoxically benefited -- CIOs prefer "one throat to choke" over 50 tools. **Winners: PANW, FTNT, CRWD.** Losers: standalone point solution vendors.

### 2. AI in Security (Offense and Defense)
AI is simultaneously the biggest threat amplifier and the biggest market expander. Attackers use AI for polymorphic malware, deepfake social engineering, and autonomous penetration. Defenders need AI for real-time anomaly detection, automated triage, and threat hunting at scale. 90% of companies lack maturity to counter AI-enabled threats. This creates an **AI-driven spending supercycle** -- the threat landscape is expanding faster than defense budgets can keep pace. **NEW (Gartner 2026):** Agentic AI creates entirely new attack surface -- 57% of employees use personal GenAI for work, 33% input sensitive data into unapproved tools.

### 3. Zero Trust Architecture
Zero trust market growing at 17-19% CAGR, reaching ~$49B in 2025. 73% of Fortune 1000 planning implementations by end-2026. Drives demand for identity (PANW+CYBR now integrated), micro-segmentation, and SASE/SSE solutions. Benefits vendors with network + identity + cloud coverage.

### 4. OT/IoT Security Expansion
Manufacturing, energy, transportation adopting cybersecurity for operational technology. Rising IT/OT convergence creates new attack surface. Fortinet's OT security offering is a differentiator. Market growing 20-25% but still early-stage. **Iran/Hormuz crisis (Mar 2026) amplifies urgency** -- state-sponsored actors explicitly targeting energy and water infrastructure.

### 5. Regulatory Tailwinds
NIS2 (EU, Oct 2024), DORA (EU financial, Jan 2025), SEC cyber disclosure rules (US), CIRCIA (US critical infrastructure). Compliance spending is non-discretionary and recurring. Benefits established vendors with compliance certifications. **Gartner 2026:** Regulatory volatility increasing -- boards/executives facing personal liability for compliance failures, driving budget approval.

### 6. Geopolitical Cyber Escalation (NEW -- Mar 2026)
Iran/Hormuz crisis has introduced a sustained state-sponsored cyber threat environment. Iran, Russia, and affiliated hacktivist groups targeting Western critical infrastructure (energy, water, financial, government). CISA/NCSC/CCCS advisories issued. This is not a one-off event but a structural change in threat posture that makes cybersecurity spending even more non-discretionary. Asia-Pacific showing strongest budget increase response (22% of orgs expecting >10% budget increase vs 9% in North America).

- **Hyperscaler bundling:** AWS Security Hub, Azure Sentinel, Google Chronicle competing at infrastructure layer. Real threat for SIEM/basic monitoring. Less threatening for specialized endpoint/identity.
