## Resumen Ejecutivo

The semiconductor equipment and specialty chip sector contains some of the highest-quality businesses in the world -- oligopolistic market structures, extreme switching costs, and multi-decade secular growth driven by AI, automotive electrification, IoT, and advanced packaging. Equipment companies in particular are "picks-and-shovels" plays on chip manufacturing buildout, with asset-light models that generate exceptional returns on capital.

ASML plans to pass "lion's share" to US customers but risks slowing orders. China revenue expected "significantly lower in 2026 than 2024-2025." Stock at $1,293 (-8% from Feb).

This is NOT theoretical -- it is actively pressuring customer capex decisions and ASML's own margin guidance. Equipment stocks pulled back 5-14% since Feb highs partly on this.

**Previous (through Mar 7):**

1. **Earnings season confirmed AI capex boom is REAL and ACCELERATING.** AMAT Q1 FY2026 revenue $7.01B (record, +11% surge), KLAC Q2 FY2026 revenue $3.30B (+17% YoY), LRCX Q2 FY2026 revenue $5.34B (+22% YoY). CDNS Q4 FY2025 beat consensus by 6% with FY2026 guidance of $5.9-6.0B (+11-13%). All equipment makers beating estimates and guiding up. The "AI giga-cycle" narrative is backed by data.

This is nearly DOUBLE 2025 levels. ~75% ($450B) is AI-specific. Demand is consuming entire production runs with lead times >30 weeks. This is the most powerful demand driver the equipment sector has EVER seen.

3. **CRITICAL NEW RISK: Iran war / Hormuz closure threatens semiconductor supply chains.** South Korea (>50% global DRAM/NAND production) and Taiwan (~70% advanced logic) are highly dependent on Qatari LNG transiting Hormuz for fab electricity. South Korea's LNG reserves cover <2 months. If Hormuz remains closed through early April, fab power supply comes under pressure. Maritime insurers have cancelled war risk cover in Gulf. This is NOT a theoretical risk -- it is an ACTIVE crisis that could disrupt chip production within weeks.

However, equipment valuations remain elevated (P/E 35-50x for best names). The sector has pulled back 5-10% from February highs on general risk-off (Iran war, oil $92), but remains far above historical averages. Equipment makers have MINIMAL direct Iran/Hormuz exposure in their own operations, but their CUSTOMERS (fabs in Korea/Taiwan) face real energy supply risk.

Key sub-sectors: Lithography (ASML monopoly), Etch/Deposition (LRCX/AMAT/TEL oligopoly), Process Control/Inspection (KLAC/ONTO), EDA Tools (SNPS/CDNS duopoly), Analog/Specialty (TXN/ADI/MCHP/NXPI), Power Management (MPWR), Advanced Packaging (BESI/KLIC/ASMPT), Materials (ENTG/MKSI), Testing (COHU/ONTO).

**CRITICAL ASSESSMENT -- Hormuz impact on semiconductor sector:**
- **Direct impact on equipment makers: MINIMAL.** ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, CDNS all headquartered and manufacturing in US/Europe/Japan. No Gulf energy dependency for their own operations.
- **Indirect impact via customers: MATERIAL if crisis extends.** South Korea's LNG reserves cover <2 months. If Hormuz remains fully closed past early April, Samsung and SK Hynix fab operations face power supply risk. TSMC in Taiwan similarly dependent on Qatari LNG. Disrupted chip production would paradoxically REDUCE equipment demand in the near-term while creating pent-up demand for catch-up capex once resolved.
- **EDA software (CDNS/SNPS): INSULATED.** 100% software/subscription. No physical supply chain. Customer R&D continues regardless of fab power issues. Best positioned sub-sector for crisis resilience.
- **Net assessment:** Equipment stocks selling off on general risk-off, NOT on fundamental demand deterioration. If Hormuz resolves in 4-5 weeks, this is a buying opportunity. If crisis extends >3 months, fab disruption could defer $10-20B in equipment capex.

### Competitivos
- Nuevos entrantes: NAURA (China domestic equipment) -- growing threat for mature nodes, now backed by 50% domestic equipment mandate
- Chinese DUV multipatterning: Chinese chipmakers using older ASML DUV tools with multipatterning to produce smaller chips -- bypassing EUV restrictions for mature/mid-range nodes
- Chinese EUV prototype: Shenzhen lab assembled prototype EUV machine using ex-ASML engineers and secondary market components. Not production-ready but signals direction
- Sustitutos: None for advanced equipment. Chinese substitution expanding for mature nodes
- Key risk: Over-investment in AI leading to capex hangover (similar to fiber optics 2001). Hyperscaler capex at $660-690B (45-57% of revenue!) is historically unprecedented and potentially unsustainable
