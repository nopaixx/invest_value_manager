## Resumen Ejecutivo

The semiconductor equipment and specialty chip sector contains some of the highest-quality businesses in the world -- oligopolistic market structures, extreme switching costs, and multi-decade secular growth driven by AI, automotive electrification, IoT, and advanced packaging. Equipment companies in particular are "picks-and-shovels" plays on chip manufacturing buildout, with asset-light models that generate exceptional returns on capital.

**UPDATE Apr 7, 2026:** Three MAJOR policy developments since Mar 22:

**NEW (Mar 22 - Apr 7):**

6. **SECTION 232 TARIFFS ON SEMICONDUCTORS -- ENACTED.** 25% tariff on certain semiconductor imports effective Jan 15 via Presidential Proclamation. Phase 2 proposes 100% tariff on broader semiconductor imports, with exemptions for US-manufactured chips. Section 232 investigation report due to President by April 14. This is the most significant trade policy action on semiconductors since the original CHIPS Act. Equipment makers with US manufacturing (AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, TXN) benefit from exemption pathway. ASML (no US manufacturing) and European equipment firms face disadvantage. The policy is explicitly designed to force reshoring.

7. **MATCH ACT -- DUV BAN ON CHINA PROPOSED (Apr 2).** Bipartisan bill introduced in House (Senate version in parallel) to ban ALL DUV lithography sales to China (not just EUV) plus servicing of existing tools at named Chinese fabs (SMIC, Hua Hong, Huawei, CXMT, YMTC). If enacted, this would eliminate ASML's remaining ~20% China revenue (currently DUV-only after EUV ban). Allies given 150-day compliance window or US Commerce implements restrictions directly. ASML shares fell 4% on the news. This is the most aggressive export control proposal yet -- previous restrictions only covered EUV and some advanced DUV. If DUV is banned, ASML loses EUR 6-8B annual revenue from China.

8. **OIL AT $110 WTI (T1: macro_fragility.py) -- FAB OPERATIONS IMPACTED.** Up from $92 at last sector update. Hormuz constrained ~40 days. Korean and Taiwanese fabs have begun REDUCING CAPACITY UTILIZATION as precaution against power grid instability from LNG shortages. South Korea also facing bromine shortage (etching chemical) from Gulf disruption. Taiwan exploring joining a global LNG program. Two Qatar LNG tankers attempting Hormuz exit as of Apr 7 -- first movement since closure. If successful, could ease pressure slightly but structural risk remains. Asian LNG imports fell 8.6% YoY last month.
