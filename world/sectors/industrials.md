## Resumen Ejecutivo

The industrials sector is broad and heterogeneous. This view focuses on four subsectors directly relevant to our pipeline: **government IT/consulting** (BAH), **geotechnical/construction** (KLR.L), **specialty engineering** (GDWN.L), and the **defense spending boom** that intersects all three. The old lighting/Signify focus is archived -- LIGHT.AS was sold in Feb 2026 and we have no lighting exposure.

**The defining tension in industrials Q1-Q2 2026:** The Iran war + Ukraine + NATO 3%+ spending trajectory create a massive tailwind for defense-adjacent industrials, while DOGE creates a severe headwind for civilian government consulting. These forces are pulling in opposite directions WITHIN the same companies (BAH derives ~35% from civil, ~65% from defense/intel). Meanwhile, global infrastructure spending is robust -- German EUR 500B special fund (EUR 58B in 2026 alone), US Infrastructure Act funding (expires Oct 2026), and secular construction demand support geotechnical specialists like KLR.L. GDWN.L adds a new dimension: UK specialty engineering (valves for LNG, nuclear, defense) with Tier A quality but in freefall after contract losses and insider selling.

**Oil at $112 WTI (T1: macro_fragility.py Apr 7) is a REGIME CHANGE for all industrials.** Hormuz remains closed since Feb 28 -- ~11M bbl/day crude offline, the largest supply disruption in modern history. This drives: (a) materials/transport cost inflation across ALL industrials, (b) politically non-discretionary defense spending acceleration, (c) energy security infrastructure investment (LNG terminals, pipelines, nuclear -- all positive for GDWN.L valve business), (d) construction project margin compression but committed backlogs provide near-term protection. The Prices Paid sub-index of ISM surging to 78.3 (highest since Jun 2022) confirms this cost pressure is already flowing through manufacturing supply chains.

## Subsector Analysis

### A. Government IT / Consulting (BAH relevant)

**Structure:** Oligopoly. Top 5 (Booz Allen, Accenture Federal, Deloitte Federal, Leidos, SAIC) dominate ~60%+ of federal IT/consulting.

**DOGE IMPACT -- THE KEY RISK:**
- DOGE placed ~$65B in federal consulting contracts under review
- DoD cancelled $5.1B in "wasteful spending" -- $4B from BAH, Accenture, Deloitte combined
- BAH specific: 2,500 layoffs (~7% workforce), centered on CIVIL division (~35% of revenue)
- BAH Civil revenue expected to contract 10-12% in FY26 (fiscal year ending Mar 2026)
- Treasury terminated $21M in BAH contracts (Jan 2026) over data security failures
- Federal contractors identified $15B in "savings" under pressure
- BAH FY26 revenue guidance: $12.0B-$12.5B (flat to +4%), growth EXCLUSIVELY from defense/intel

**BUT -- Defense/Intel is SURGING:**
- BAH backlog hit $38B (record)
- Defense + Intelligence = ~65% of BAH revenue, GROWING
- Iran war + Ukraine + NATO 3%+ = structural demand for defense IT modernization, cyber, AI/ML
- CEO publicly "bullish on his biggest client" (DoD)
- The DOGE headwind is concentrated in CIVIL. Defense spending is politically untouchable in wartime
- **Oil crisis reinforces defense spending urgency -- no politician will cut military budgets during Hormuz closure**

**Net assessment for BAH:** DOGE creates a genuine 300-600bp headwind on total growth, but defense/intel demand is the strongest in decades. The question is whether defense growth can MORE than offset civil contraction. Current evidence: FY26 guidance suggests yes (flat to +4% total). QS 68 Tier B reflects this mixed picture.

### B. Geotechnical / Construction (KLR.L relevant)

**Structure:** Fragmented globally, KLR.L is #1 global geotechnical specialist.

**KLR.L FY2025 Results (Mar 3, 2026) -- RECORD:**
- Revenue GBP 3.08B (+3.4% YoY)
- Underlying operating profit GBP 218.2M (+2.6%)
- First net cash position in 25+ years (GBP 59.7M)
- Order book GBP 1.5B with "high tendering levels"
- Dividend increased 41%, GBP 100M buyback announced
- North America strong, UK HS2 winding down offset by other activity
- 2026 outlook: "further progress" despite macro uncertainty

**Construction demand drivers:**
- US: Infrastructure Act funding flowing (roads, bridges, water -- expires Oct 2026). Data center construction booming. 1% overall construction spending growth but infra-led
- Germany: **EUR 500B special fund now operational. EUR 58B allocated in 2026. EUR 93B earmarked for transport infrastructure. EUR 18B for digitalization, EUR 18B for hospitals.** Execution criticized but spending is flowing
- Global: Urbanization, climate adaptation, aging infrastructure replacement

**Headwinds:**
- **Oil at $112 = SIGNIFICANTLY higher materials/transport costs (steel, concrete, fuel) vs $92 in prior update**
- **US tariffs now 50% on steel/aluminum/copper (doubled from 25% on Apr 2 "Liberation Day" anniversary)**. EU-US 15% deal ratification stalled
- UK steel tariffs at 25% -- lowest globally, relative advantage for UK-based construction
- Labor shortages + immigration restrictions push wages higher
- Residential construction weak (rates still high)
- Post-Infrastructure Act cliff risk (Oct 2026 expiration)
- **ISM Prices Paid at 78.3 -- cost inflation is accelerating through supply chains**

**Net assessment for KLR.L:** Record results + net cash + buyback = strong fundamental position. Geotechnical is less discretionary than general construction (foundations/ground engineering required regardless). But oil at $112 + 50% steel tariffs materially worsen the cost environment vs the $92/25% tariff backdrop in the prior update. Contracted backlogs provide near-term margin protection. QS 76/79adj Tier A reflects high quality. **Oil regime change increases near-term cost risk but does NOT change the secular infrastructure demand story.**

### C. Defense Spending Boom (contextual -- no direct positions, but GDWN.L defense exposure)

**The structural rearmament story:**
- Global defense spending hit $2.72T in 2025, heading to $2.8T+ in 2026
- Germany: defense budget EUR 86B (2025) -> EUR 108.2B (2026), EUR 400B additional borrowing over 5 years
- EU "ReArm Europe Plan": EUR 860B ($860B), funded by EU bonds + relaxed fiscal rules
- NATO target raised from 2% to 3.5% GDP by 2035
- US defense budget heading to $1T+ in 2026
- **Iran war + Hormuz closure = spending urgency at MAXIMUM. Politically impossible to cut defense when global oil supply is disrupted**
- European defense stocks +65% in 2025 (STOXX Aerospace & Defense Index). Rheinmetall +90% YTD
- **UK: GBP 62.2B defence spending 2025/26, rising to GBP 73.5B by 2028/29. Labour committed 2.5% GDP from Apr 2027. AUKUS programme consuming 40%+ of equipment budget -- squeezing other MoD procurement**

**Valuation concern:** Defense stocks have repriced dramatically. Most trade at 30-50x earnings. Entry at these levels requires belief that the spending cycle is STRUCTURAL (multi-decade), not cyclical. The Iran war reinforces the structural view but much is already priced in.

### D. Specialty Engineering -- GDWN.L (NEW subsector)

**Structure:** GDWN.L is a UK-based specialty engineering group. Two divisions: Mechanical Engineering (valves, pumps for oil/gas, LNG, nuclear, defense) and Refractory Engineering (investment castings, jewellery equipment).

**GDWN.L CRISIS AND OPPORTUNITY:**
- Stock crashed 52% from 25,761p to 12,280p (current, T1 Apr 7)
- **Order book -21%: GBP 365M --> GBP 288M** (Feb 2026 trading update)
- Lost two significant tenders: Sellafield nuclear contract (>GBP 45M) and Easat radar contract for Estonia (~EUR 18M). Combined >GBP 60M pipeline evaporated
- Insiders sold GBP 25M+ at ~250p equivalent in Nov 2025 -- vindicated by subsequent collapse
- Refractory division weighed by elevated gold/silver prices depressing jewellery casting demand
- Trading update: "performance remains in line with October 2025 expectations" -- but October expectations were already reduced
- **POSITIVE: None of the LNG valve orders for Middle East/USA have been cancelled or placed on hold.** Oil crisis at $112 WTI makes LNG infrastructure MORE critical, not less
- **POSITIVE: Nuclear exposure (UK Sizewell C, global nuclear renaissance) is structural multi-decade**
- **POSITIVE: Defense valve applications benefit from rearmament spending**
- **R3 resolution: FV 11,000p, entry 8,500p, WATCHLIST. QS 80 Tier A.**
- At 12,280p, stock is 11.6% ABOVE FV (11,000p). Entry requires further -31% decline to 8,500p

**Net assessment for GDWN.L:** Classic quality-in-distress situation. The 52% crash reflects real fundamental deterioration (order book, contract losses, insider selling). But the core valve business for LNG/nuclear/defense is structurally positioned for the energy security decade. Oil at $112 and Hormuz closure INCREASE demand for non-Middle-East energy infrastructure (LNG terminals, nuclear plants) where GDWN.L's valves are specified. The question is whether the order book stabilizes and rebuilds. At current 12,280p, no margin of safety vs 11,000p FV. Need patience for 8,500p entry or positive order book catalyst.
