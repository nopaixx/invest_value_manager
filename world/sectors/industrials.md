## Resumen Ejecutivo

The industrials sector is broad and heterogeneous. This view focuses on three subsectors directly relevant to our pipeline: **government IT/consulting** (BAH), **geotechnical/construction** (KLR.L), and the **defense spending boom** that intersects both. The old lighting/Signify focus is archived — LIGHT.AS was sold in Feb 2026 and we have no lighting exposure.

**The defining tension in industrials Q1 2026:** The Iran war + Ukraine + NATO 3%+ spending trajectory create a massive tailwind for defense-adjacent industrials, while DOGE creates a severe headwind for civilian government consulting. These forces are pulling in opposite directions WITHIN the same companies (BAH derives ~35% from civil, ~65% from defense/intel). Meanwhile, global infrastructure spending is robust — German EUR 120B/yr fiscal stimulus, US Infrastructure Act funding (expires Oct 2026), and secular construction demand support geotechnical specialists like KLR.L.

**Oil at $92 WTI adds cost pressure across all industrials** — higher energy/transport costs, materials inflation, and potential demand destruction if sustained. However, defense spending is politically non-discretionary in current environment, and infrastructure funding is largely committed/contracted.

## Subsector Analysis

### A. Government IT / Consulting (BAH relevant)

**Structure:** Oligopoly. Top 5 (Booz Allen, Accenture Federal, Deloitte Federal, Leidos, SAIC) dominate ~60%+ of federal IT/consulting.

**DOGE IMPACT — THE KEY RISK:**
- DOGE placed ~$65B in federal consulting contracts under review
- DoD cancelled $5.1B in "wasteful spending" — $4B from BAH, Accenture, Deloitte combined
- BAH specific: 2,500 layoffs (~7% workforce), centered on CIVIL division (~35% of revenue)
- BAH Civil revenue expected to contract 10-12% in FY26 (fiscal year ending Mar 2026)
- Treasury terminated $21M in BAH contracts (Jan 2026) over data security failures
- Federal contractors identified $15B in "savings" under pressure
- BAH FY26 revenue guidance: $12.0B-$12.5B (flat to +4%), growth EXCLUSIVELY from defense/intel

**BUT — Defense/Intel is SURGING:**
- BAH backlog hit $38B (record)
- Defense + Intelligence = ~65% of BAH revenue, GROWING
- Iran war + Ukraine + NATO 3%+ = structural demand for defense IT modernization, cyber, AI/ML
- CEO publicly "bullish on his biggest client" (DoD)
- The DOGE headwind is concentrated in CIVIL. Defense spending is politically untouchable in wartime

**Net assessment for BAH:** DOGE creates a genuine 300-600bp headwind on total growth, but defense/intel demand is the strongest in decades. The question is whether defense growth can MORE than offset civil contraction. Current evidence: FY26 guidance suggests yes (flat to +4% total). QS 68 Tier B reflects this mixed picture.

### B. Geotechnical / Construction (KLR.L relevant)

**Structure:** Fragmented globally, KLR.L is #1 global geotechnical specialist.

**KLR.L FY2025 Results (Mar 3, 2026) — RECORD:**
- Revenue GBP 3.08B (+3.4% YoY)
- Underlying operating profit GBP 218.2M (+2.6%)
- First net cash position in 25+ years (GBP 59.7M)
- Order book GBP 1.5B with "high tendering levels"
- Dividend increased 41%, GBP 100M buyback announced
- North America strong, UK HS2 winding down offset by other activity
- 2026 outlook: "further progress" despite macro uncertainty

**Construction demand drivers:**
- US: Infrastructure Act funding flowing (roads, bridges, water — expires Oct 2026). Data center construction booming. 1% overall construction spending growth but infra-led
- Germany: EUR 120B investment in 2026, EUR 33.7B for transport infrastructure. EUR 500B special fund for infrastructure over coming years
- Global: Urbanization, climate adaptation, aging infrastructure replacement

**Headwinds:**
- Oil at $92 = higher materials/transport costs (steel, concrete, fuel)
- US tariffs ~20% on steel/aluminum/copper increase project costs
- Labor shortages + immigration restrictions push wages higher
- Residential construction weak (rates still high)
- Post-Infrastructure Act cliff risk (Oct 2026 expiration)

**Net assessment for KLR.L:** Record results + net cash + buyback = strong fundamental position. Geotechnical is less discretionary than general construction (foundations/ground engineering required regardless). But macro headwinds from oil/tariffs are real cost pressures. QS 76/79adj Tier A reflects high quality.

### C. Defense Spending Boom (contextual — no direct positions)

**The structural rearmament story:**
- Global defense spending hit $2.72T in 2025, heading to $2.8T+ in 2026
- Germany: defense budget EUR 86B (2025) -> EUR 108.2B (2026), EUR 400B additional borrowing over 5 years
- EU "ReArm Europe Plan": EUR 860B ($860B), funded by EU bonds + relaxed fiscal rules
- NATO target raised from 2% to 3.5% GDP by 2035
- US defense budget heading to $1T+ in 2026
- Iran war + Ukraine = spending urgency at maximum. Politically impossible to cut defense
- European defense stocks +65% in 2025 (STOXX Aerospace & Defense Index). Rheinmetall +90% YTD

**Valuation concern:** Defense stocks have repriced dramatically. Most trade at 30-50x earnings. Entry at these levels requires belief that the spending cycle is STRUCTURAL (multi-decade), not cyclical. The Iran war reinforces the structural view but much is already priced in.

### Oil $92 WTI impact on industrials
- BAH: Minimal direct impact (IT/consulting). Indirect: defense budgets more likely to INCREASE
