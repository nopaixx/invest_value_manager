## Resumen Ejecutivo

Water treatment and environmental services is a secular growth sector driven by water scarcity, tightening regulations, and industrial process optimization. The global water treatment chemicals market is valued at approximately $40B in 2026, growing at 4-6% CAGR. The sector is characterized by high recurring revenue (consumables model), strong switching costs (embedded equipment + proprietary chemistries), and regulatory moats. However, premium valuations reflect these qualities -- finding entry points requires patience.

Key trends: (1) Industrial digitalization driving service-based models, (2) AI/IoT integration for water monitoring, (3) PFAS regulation mixed -- federal rollback but state-level standards holding/tightening, (4) Growing semiconductor/data center water demand as a new growth vertical, (5) Oil at ~$97 WTI creating input cost pressure on chemical producers, (6) Tariff-driven supply chain reshoring.

## Oil Price Sensitivity (NEW)

Water treatment chemicals are partly derived from petrochemicals (ethylene oxide, propylene oxide derivatives). Oil at $97 WTI (up from ~$58 12mo ago, +58%) creates margin pressure:
- **ECL**: ~30% of COGS is raw materials, partially petrochemical-linked. Has pricing power to pass through but with 1-2 quarter lag.
- **VLTO**: Less exposed (water analytics is instrumentation, not chemicals). Product identification segment more insulated.
- **XYL**: Equipment/infrastructure focus = minimal direct oil sensitivity. Indirect via construction costs.
- **Net impact**: Sector demand is non-discretionary (regulatory/essential), so volume resilient. Margin compression 50-150bps for chemical-heavy players until pass-through catches up.

## Tariff Impact (NEW)

2025-2026 tariffs creating 15-25% production cost increases on certain chemical formulations. Key impacts:
- Specialty chemical precursors from China face elevated duties
- Equipment imports (pumps, membranes, sensors) 25-30% more expensive
- Domestic reshoring accelerating but takes 2-3 years to scale
- Municipal utilities passing costs to ratepayers (political friction)
- **Winners**: Domestically-sourced chemical producers, US-manufactured equipment
- **Losers**: Import-dependent distributors, municipal budgets

## Data Center / AI Water Demand (UPDATED)

AI data center water consumption is a material emerging demand driver:
- US data center water consumption projected to 2-4x by 2028 vs 2023 levels (150-280B liters/yr)
- Xylem+GWI estimate AI adds ~30 trillion liters annual water demand by 2050 (power gen 54%, semis 42%, DC direct 4%)
- Rack density growing from 36kW to 50kW by 2027, requiring more cooling water
- Shift from air to liquid cooling accelerating demand
- **Beneficiaries**: XYL (infrastructure), ECL (industrial water treatment), VLTO (water quality monitoring)

## PFAS Regulatory Update (UPDATED)

Federal PFAS regulation is being ROLLED BACK under current EPA:
- PFOA/PFOS MCLs maintained but compliance deadline extending from 2029 to 2031
- PFHxS, PFNA, GenX (HFPO-DA) MCLs being RESCINDED entirely
- Federal exemption framework being developed
- **BUT**: State-level standards remain strict (CA, NJ, MI, NY maintaining own limits)
- Net: reduces near-term urgency/capex for water utilities; long-term PFAS remediation demand intact but slower
- **Impact on sector**: Slightly negative near-term (less urgency), neutral long-term (contamination doesn't go away)

## Infrastructure Spending (UPDATED)
