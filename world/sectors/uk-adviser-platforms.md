# Sector: UK Adviser Investment Platforms

> Ultima actualizacion: 2026-04-07
> Status: SELECTIVO - Quality platform economics intact but AUM under short-term pressure from Iran war selloff + oil shock. Proprietary tech platforms preferred. Fee compression structural but slowing.

## Resumen Ejecutivo

UK adviser investment platforms provide technology infrastructure for financial advisers to manage client portfolios. Revenue is driven by assets under administration (basis points on FUA), creating natural leverage to rising markets -- and vulnerability to selloffs. The sector is in a consolidation phase: Hargreaves Lansdown taken private by CVC/Nordic Capital/ADIA (completed Mar 2025, GBP 5.4bn), leaving AJ Bell, IntegraFin (Transact), Quilter, and abrdn (interactive investor) as the listed/public players.

**CRISIS OVERLAY (Mar 2026, updated 2026-03-21):** FTSE 100 has recovered strongly -- crossed 10,000 and approaching 11,000, +14% YTD. Crisis AUM headwind from early Mar largely reversed. Platform AUM should be at or near record levels. Original concern (Iran war, oil shock, FTSE selloff) was correctly identified as TEMPORARY for all platforms. AUM-based fee revenue is mechanically linked to market levels -- a 10% market decline translates roughly to a 10% revenue decline (before new inflows partially offset). However, this is NOT structural damage to platform businesses. Platform switching costs are high (adviser relationships, client migration friction), and net inflows continue regardless of market direction. The key question is duration of the selloff, not its occurrence.

**April 7 update (incremental):** (1) FTSE 100 stabilised around 10,400 (closed 10,436 Apr 3, +4.7% weekly). Oil at $115 Brent keeping inflation fears alive but UK equity markets resilient -- platform AUA mechanically supported at current FTSE levels, though below Jan highs. (2) FCA targeted support regime went live April 6, 2026 -- new regulated activity allowing firms to provide group-based financial suggestions without full advice burden. Opens addressable market for platforms (23M underserved consumers). Quilter already announced plans; Transact well-positioned via proprietary tech. (3) IHP.L Q2 FY26 trading update scheduled April 21 -- key catalyst for H1 flow data and FUA update. Price at 317p (vs 390p FV, 19% discount). (4) 2026 platform ratings published: Transact retains FE fundinfo Platinum rating (top 10); Defaqto Gold Service Rating awarded. Quilter ranked #1 FE fundinfo for third consecutive year. Fundment (small challenger) topped Defaqto service rankings -- signals service quality differentiation intensifying. (5) Adviser consolidation accelerating: nearly two-thirds of advisers now use 2 or fewer platforms (up from 55% in 2018). Benefits scale platforms with broad proposition (Transact, Quilter, AJ Bell). (6) Altruist AI disruption: Feb 10 launch caused SJP -13%, AJB -7%, QLT -5% selloff but impact fading. Altruist CEO confirmed "here for UK market" -- longer-term disruption vector but proprietary tech platforms (Transact) better positioned to integrate AI than outsourced. (7) IHP.L position trimmed S155b: 327 to 272 shares at 302.5p. Now ~3.5% portfolio. Conviction HIGH but sized for starter growth path.

Revenue margin compression remains the dominant structural headwind: pricing pressure from competition and tiering effects as larger client portfolios enter lower charging bands. IntegraFin guides margin compression "slowing" in FY2026. Most platforms now at 20-23bps revenue margin vs 25-30bps five years ago. Volume growth (more FUA) has been outpacing margin compression -- the critical dynamic to monitor.

**Consolidation accelerating:** HL taken private removes the largest listed competitor. Quilter completed FNZ platform migration (GBP 62.5bn assets, 490,000 customers). Abrdn pivoting to interactive investor as core business (59% of group profit). Smaller platforms face scale disadvantage. Proprietary tech (Transact, AJ Bell) vs outsourced (FNZ-based: Quilter, abrdn) remains the key structural differentiator.

| Regulation | Status | Impact on Platforms | Timeline |
|------------|--------|-------------------|----------|
| **Consumer Duty** | Phase 2: Outcomes evidence | Platforms must PROVE good outcomes (fair value, suitable products). Benefits quality platforms with transparent pricing. Higher compliance costs disadvantage smaller players | Ongoing 2026. FCA expects continuous monitoring evidence |
| **Targeted Support** | **LIVE from April 6, 2026** | New regulated activity: firms can provide group-based suggestions (pensions, investments) without full advice burden. | **MPS Review** | FCA multi-firm review IN PROGRESS | FCA 2026 Regulatory Priorities confirmed MPS review progressing. Assessing Consumer Duty implementation by MPS firms. Distribution chain scrutiny + client categorisation consultation upcoming | Findings expected summer 2026 |
| **Pension reforms** | Ongoing | Pension freedoms continue driving SIPP usage. Any changes to ISA/SIPP rules affect platform flows | Budget-related speculation drives flow spikes |
| **Fee transparency** | Ongoing | FCA pushing for clearer fee disclosure. Benefits simple-fee platforms (Transact, AJ Bell) vs complex charging structures | Structural tailwind for transparent platforms |

**Net regulatory assessment:** FAVORABLE for quality platforms. FCA targeted support going live is a net POSITIVE -- expands addressable market for compliant platforms. Consumer Duty remains central pillar of FCA strategy 2025-2030. Distribution chain clarification consultation upcoming. Consumer Duty raises barriers to entry, increases compliance costs (disadvantages small players), and benefits platforms with transparent pricing and strong technology. IntegraFin/Transact well-positioned -- proprietary tech enables compliance automation and rapid targeted support adoption.

## Crisis Impact Assessment: Iran War + Oil Shock (Mar 2026)

**Transmission mechanism:** Oil $115 Brent (updated Apr 7, was $92 WTI at original assessment) -> inflation reaccelerating -> BoE holds/delays cuts -> equity markets volatile -> AUM pressure -> platform revenue pressure (mechanical, ~10% AUM decline = ~10% revenue decline).

**Mitigants:**
1. **Net inflows continue in crises.** UK pension contributions are structural (employer mandated). ISA season (Apr deadline) drives seasonal inflows regardless of market conditions.
2. **Platform switching costs very high.** Advisers do NOT move clients between platforms during crises. Churn actually DECREASES in volatility (advisers focus on client communication, not platform shopping).
3. **NII benefit from higher-for-longer rates.** Cash balances on platforms earn higher interest income when BoE delays cuts. Partially offsets AUM-based fee decline.
4. **Valuations compressed = opportunity.** IHP.L trading at 317p vs 390p FV (19% discount). FTSE at 10,400 -- below Jan highs but resilient.

**Duration sensitivity:**
| Market Selloff | AUM Impact | Revenue Impact | Recovery |
|----------------|-----------|----------------|----------|
| -5% (1-2 weeks, ceasefire) | -5% AUM | -5% quarterly revenue | V-shaped with market recovery |
| -10% (sustained 1-2 months) | -10% AUM | -10% quarterly, ~-5% annual | Recovers with market + inflows |
| -20% (recession scenario) | -20% AUM | -15 to -20% annual | Slow, 12-18 months. But inflows accelerate at lower prices |

**Assessment for IHP.L:** AUM dip is TEMPORARY and mechanical. Structural thesis (platform economics, switching costs, adviser relationships, proprietary tech, insider ownership) is 100% INTACT. This is a crisis-resistant business model -- revenue dips with markets but recovers. The business does not lose customers in selloffs.
