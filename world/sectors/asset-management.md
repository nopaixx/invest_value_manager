## Resumen Ejecutivo

Asset management is bifurcating into two distinct stories. **Traditional active management** faces secular decline: fee compression (~2% per annum decline in active equity fees), passive/ETF dominance, and margin erosion. This is a DECLINING business to AVOID. **Alternative asset management** and **platform/infrastructure businesses** are structurally growing segments with durable competitive advantages, pricing power, and high incremental margins. We are positioned in BOTH winning segments: Hamilton Lane (alternative AM, private markets specialist) and IntegraFin (UK independent adviser platform).

The alternative AM industry is at an inflection point. After a fundraising drought in 2023-2024 (PE fundraising fell 17% to $735B in 2025, lowest since 2020), the exit environment is recovering strongly (PE-backed exits surged +40% in 2025, IPO exit volume nearly doubled). More structurally, three mega-trends are reshaping the industry: (1) **Private wealth democratization** -- the $80 trillion retail investor pool is opening up via evergreen/semi-liquid funds, with fundraising into US semi-liquid PE vehicles more than doubling since 2023 to $204B in 2025; (2) **Evergreen fund adoption** -- now $493B+ in total net assets (Q3 2025), growing 30%+ annually, projected to reach $1.1T by 2029; (3) **Institutional allocation persistence** -- 70% of 300 global LPs surveyed in Jan 2026 plan to maintain or increase PE allocations. Private markets are expected to generate more than HALF of total asset management industry revenue by 2030, producing ~4x as much profit per $1B AUM as traditional managers.

Fee dynamics strongly favor alternatives: while traditional active equity fees compress ~2% annually, alternative managers retain pricing power due to alpha generation, illiquidity premium, and operational complexity. However, even in alternatives, GP management fee discounting is emerging (especially for large mandates), and the industry is maturing with structural shifts in deployment, returns, and value creation (McKinsey 2026). The winners will be those with (a) scale in evergreen/semi-liquid distribution, (b) strong track records, and (c) technology-enabled portfolio analytics.

**Key insight for our portfolio:** Hamilton Lane is positioned at the exact intersection of these secular trends -- #1 in private markets technology/data, $16B+ evergreen AUM growing 70%+ YoY, and a Guardian partnership delivering $500M/year in committed capital for 10+ years. IntegraFin sits on the platform infrastructure side, benefiting from the shift toward fee-based advice and platform consolidation in UK wealth management.

## Industry Structure: Two Worlds

### Declining: Traditional Active Management
- Fee compression from passive/ETF growth (~$1.5T/yr inflows to passive)
- Active equity weighted average fees down 7-8% over 5 years
- Margin erosion from rising technology, compliance, distribution costs
- Consolidation accelerating (Moody's 2026 outlook)
- **Verdict: AVOID for new positions. Structural headwinds.**

### Growing: Alternative Asset Management
- **Fee-earning AUM growing 10-20%+ annually** for top managers
- Management fees more defensible (1.5-2.0% on committed capital, vs 0.5-0.8% active equity)
- Performance/incentive fees provide optionality (carried interest, crystallization)
- Evergreen/semi-liquid funds creating PERPETUAL, non-redeemable AUM
- Private wealth channel ($80T addressable) just beginning to open
- **Verdict: SOBREPONDERAR. Structural secular tailwind. Select for quality.**

### Growing: Platform / Infrastructure
- Technology platforms (Transact/IHP.L, SEI/SEIC) benefit from both traditional AND alternative growth
- Sticky, recurring revenue from platform fees + switching costs
- Operating leverage as AUM grows on fixed technology base
- **Verdict: FAVORABLE. Asset-light, high-ROIC models.**

## Key Industry Themes for 2026

### 1. Private Wealth Democratization (SECULAR -- #1 THEME)
- $80T addressable market in individual investor wealth
- US Executive Order (Aug 2025) directing DOL to open 401(k) to alternatives
- 55% of surveyed managers expect HALF of fundraising from retail within 1-2 years
- Semi-liquid/evergreen vehicles are the delivery mechanism
- **HLNE is a direct beneficiary** -- 11 evergreen funds, $16B+ platform, technology enabling adviser access
- **IHP.L benefits indirectly** -- as alternatives flow through adviser platforms, Transact captures platform fees

### 2. Fundraising Recovery (CYCLICAL -- POSITIVE)
- PE fundraising troughed at $735B in 2025 (-17% YoY, lowest since 2020)
- BUT: 70% of LPs maintaining/increasing allocations. 75% of flagship funds re-raised at step-up.
- Exit environment recovering (+40% in 2025). IPO exits nearly doubled.
- Secondaries hitting records ($240B, +48%). Continuation vehicles at $115B.
- Recovery benefits fee-earning AUM growth for HLNE (new fund vintages)
- **Risk**: Iran oil shock could delay recovery if recession materializes

### 3. Evergreen Fund Structural Shift (SECULAR -- ACCELERATING)
- $493B AUM (Q3 2025), growing 30%+ annually
- Projected $1.1T by 2029
- The 5 largest listed PE firms now have $1.5T in perpetual capital (40% of combined AUM)
- HLNE has $16B+ evergreen with 70%+ growth rate -- INDUSTRY-LEADING relative to peers
- Direct lending evergreen tripled since 2022 to $209B
- This structural shift means AUM is PERMANENTLY higher quality (no redemption, perpetual fees)

### 4. Fee Dynamics: Diverging Paths
- Traditional active: -2% annual fee compression. Terminal for commodity active managers.
- Alternative management fees: Stable at 1.5-2.0% for buyout, slightly compressing for mega-funds
- GP management fee discounting emerging for large mandates (Penn Mutual research)
- Performance fees cyclical -- recovering with exit environment
- HLNE model: predominantly management + advisory fees (more stable), less performance fee dependency

### 5. Interest Rate Impact on Alternatives
- Higher-for-longer (Iran oil shock): Mixed for alternatives
  - NEGATIVE: PE valuations compressed, exit multiples lower, fundraising delayed
  - POSITIVE: Private credit benefits enormously (floating rate). Infrastructure with inflation-linked revenues benefits
  - NET: Slightly negative for PE-heavy managers, positive for diversified/credit-heavy
- Rate cuts (when they eventually come): POSITIVE for PE exits, valuations, fundraising
- HLNE exposure: Diversified across PE, credit, real assets, venture. Well-positioned for any rate environment.
