## Resumen Ejecutivo

Industrial Technology encompasses high-quality, asset-light industrial businesses that combine precision engineering, software, and recurring services. The sector includes serial acquirers of niche industrial businesses (Roper, Halma, Diploma, Judges Scientific, Lifco), precision instruments and test/measurement (Mettler-Toledo, AMETEK, Nordson), specialty industrial components and fluid handling (Graco, IDEX, Rotork), and environmental/water technology (Xylem, Veralto, Watts Water).

The best companies in this sector share several characteristics that make them exceptional compounders: high gross margins (50-70%), asset-light business models, mission-critical products embedded in customer workflows, regulatory-driven demand (safety, environmental), high switching costs, and significant recurring/repeat revenue. These businesses are effectively "toll booths" on industrial activity.

**Current opportunity:** Several quality compounders remain at significant drawdowns from 52-week highs. Roper Technologies (-39% from 52wH, $358.83) Q1 earnings Apr 23 -- key read on Deltek recovery and DAT freight cycle. Judges Scientific (-53% from 52wH, 4260p) FY2025 results showed 275.3p EPS but 2026 guide weak (200-250p, -18%). MEGP.L now our active position at 140p with record FY2025 results. Belimo (BEAN.SW) newly scored QS 75 Tier A at -35% from 52wH -- HVAC actuator monopolist added to pipeline. Meanwhile highest-quality names (Graco -7%, Rotork -6%, Halma -10%) offer modest pullbacks, insufficient MoS. Tariff headwind for physical-product names (Graco, Nordson) adds new complexity.

**UPDATE 2026-03-07 -- IRAN WAR / OIL $90+ IMPACT ON SECTOR:**
- **Input cost headwind (MINOR for this sector):** Most industrial technology companies are asset-light with high gross margins (50-70%). Oil/energy is a small fraction of COGS. Pricing power passes through cost increases. Impact: LOW.
- **Safety/defense spending tailwind:** Iran crisis + Ukraine reinforces government safety and defense spending. MSA Safety (safety equipment, PPE for military/first responders), Halma (safety systems), and defense-adjacent industrials benefit. Impact: MODERATE POSITIVE.
- **Capex cycle risk:** If oil >$90 persists 3+ months (NOW AT 6+ WEEKS), recession risk rises to 30-49%. Industrial capex under pressure from BOTH energy costs AND tariff uncertainty. Aftermarket/service revenue more resilient. Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH if sustained into H2.
- **Software-heavy names insulated:** ROP (80% software/recurring), Halma (regulation-driven), MEGP.L (consumer-facing self-service) have limited exposure to energy input costs.
- **Tariff impact (NEW Apr 2026):** Section 232 at 50% raw metals, 25% derivatives, 15% industrial equipment. Asset-light = LOW impact. Physical-product names (GGG, NDSN) = MODERATE impact (15-25% on imported components). Manufacturers accelerating automation to offset costs = SECULAR TAILWIND for automation suppliers.

**Tesis sectorial:** This is a sector where quality truly compounds -- ROIC consistently above WACC, stable/growing margins, and resilient demand. The best entry points come from temporary sector/company-specific headwinds, not secular problems. The key risk is valuation: these businesses are rarely "cheap" by traditional metrics (P/E 25-40x), so the MoS comes from understanding the durability of the compounding. The Iran/oil crisis + tariff uncertainty creates modest opportunity via risk-off pullbacks but does NOT fundamentally impair asset-light businesses. Physical-product names (Graco, Nordson) face more headwind from Section 232 tariffs.
