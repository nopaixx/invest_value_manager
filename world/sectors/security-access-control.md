The Security / Access Control sector encompasses physical security products: mechanical locks, electronic locks, smart locks, door closers, exit devices, access control systems, and related building security hardware. This is a structurally growing market driven by three secular forces: (1) the ongoing transition from mechanical to electronic access (~10% of commercial openings are electronic today, heading to 25-30% over a decade), (2) post-COVID institutional security upgrades (schools, hospitals, offices mandating stricter access), and (3) regulatory complexity growth (building codes, fire safety, ADA, cybersecurity for IoT devices) which raises barriers to entry and increases specifier dependence on incumbents.

**Macro headwinds update (Apr 2026):** Oil at $95 WTI (T1 verified, macro_fragility.py) continues to pressure input costs (steel, aluminum, zinc) and keeps rate cuts firmly off the table for 2026 -- Fed funds expectations now pushed to Q1 2027 at earliest. Steel/aluminum tariffs doubled to 50% Section 232 (effective Apr 6, 2026), with derivative products at 25%. This is a TRIPLE input cost headwind: (a) oil-driven energy costs, (b) tariff inflation on metals, (c) construction material price escalation (ENR Building Cost Index +4.2% YoY). Incumbents with pricing power (Allegion, ASSA ABLOY) can pass through but there is a LAG -- margins may compress near-term before pricing catches up. NET EFFECT: Non-residential demand supported by geopolitical security spending tailwind; residential demand weak (no rate cuts); input costs elevated but manageable for incumbents with pricing power.

The competitive landscape remains consolidated at the top: ASSA ABLOY (global #1, ~20-22% global market share), Allegion (#2 in Americas, ~11% global), and dormakaba (#3 globally) collectively dominate commercial access control. The top 5 players hold 70-80% of the total market. This is an oligopoly with high barriers to entry (brand reputation with specifiers, certification infrastructure, distribution relationships, and decades of regulatory expertise). Pricing power is strong for incumbents -- Allegion's Americas segment generates 27-29% adjusted operating margins, a level no peer matches in the region.

The sector is NOT cheap in aggregate. ASSA ABLOY trades at ~26x P/E, Allegion at ~19x (compressed from 21x), and the broader group averages 19-28x trailing earnings. **Barreras de entrada:** ALTAS
- **Specifier relationships:** Architects and building consultants specify products during design phase; relationships take decades to build (Allegion has 150+ architectural consultants averaging 15+ years experience)
- **Certifications:** UL Listed, ANSI/BHMA Grade 1, fire code compliance -- multi-year certification process
- **Building code complexity:** IBC, NFPA 101, ADA, local fire codes -- complexity is Allegion's friend
- **Brand trust:** In commercial, code-related liability means specifiers default to trusted brands (Von Duprin, Schlage)
- **Distribution:** Decades-long relationships with locksmiths, contractors, security integrators
- **Installed base:** 50% of Allegion's revenue is aftermarket/retrofit -- creates ecosystem lock-in
- **NEW -- Cyber resilience compliance:** EU Cyber Resilience Act (Sep 2026) and rising cybersecurity requirements for IoT devices raise the bar for connected lock/access manufacturers. Benefits established players with compliance resources

**Poder de pricing:** ALTO (incumbents) -- CONFIRMED by tariff response
- Allegion expanded gross margins from 42% to 44.2% DURING inflationary period (2021-2024)
- Pricing contributes 2-3% annual revenue growth above inflation
- **Allegion shifted from tariff surcharges to permanent list-price increases (13.5% on Schlage J Series, Feb 2026)** -- this EMBEDS pricing power permanently and removes surcharge uncertainty
- ASSA ABLOY pricing +2-3% organic contribution consistently
- Management guides $80M tariff impact as EPS neutral -- full pass-through expected

**Poder de proveedores:** ALTO (elevated further by 50% tariffs)
- Steel, aluminum, zinc are key inputs -- commodity exposed
- **Oil at $95 WTI (T1) + 50% Section 232 tariffs on steel/aluminum = INTENSIFIED input cost inflation.** ENR Building Cost Index +4.2% YoY. Nonresidential construction inputs +7.1% annualized (Jan 2026)
- Incumbents can pass through via pricing power, but margins may compress short-term before pricing catches up
- Lock hardware products classified as derivative articles (substantially made of steel/aluminum) face 25% tariff; products made almost entirely of steel face full 50%

**Poder de clientes:** BAJO-MEDIO
- Commercial: specifier-driven (low customer power)
- Residential: more price-sensitive, consumer choice matters more

## Mi Tesis Sectorial

The security / access control sector is a high-quality oligopoly with structural growth tailwinds. The transition from mechanical to electronic access control expands TAM, increases margins, and deepens switching costs -- benefiting incumbents disproportionately. Technology disruption from Amazon/Google/Chinese OEMs is real but limited to the residential consumer segment; commercial access control remains protected by specifier relationships, building codes, and certification barriers that are strengthening, not weakening.

**2026 update -- four accelerating trends:**
1. **AI integration** is making access control the "nerve centre" of smart buildings -- identity graphs, behavioral analytics, AI-driven just-in-time access governance. This INCREASES the value proposition and TAM for incumbent platform players.
2. **ACaaS** (Access Control as a Service) growing at 17.9% CAGR shifts the revenue model toward recurring subscriptions, increasing switching costs and predictability. NAPCO's MVP platform and Allegion's cloud offerings are leading indicators.
3. **Geopolitical urgency** (Iran crisis, critical infrastructure attacks, 75% YoY increase in utility cyberattacks) is elevating physical security from operational expense to strategic imperative. $247B market growing 7.2% CAGR. "Sovereign-ready" now standard procurement checkbox.
4. **Tariff-driven pricing power confirmation**: Allegion's ability to absorb 50% tariffs into permanent list price increases while guiding EPS neutral CONFIRMS the pricing power thesis. This is the opposite of commoditized -- it is pricing power in action during the most challenging input cost environment in years.

**Sector Status: NEUTRAL (unchanged overall, but with LIVE tactical opportunity in ALLE)**

The sector is NOT cheap in aggregate. Premium valuations (19-26x P/E) reflect the quality characteristics correctly. However, the combined selloff (tariff fears + construction cycle + risk-off) has pushed ALLE to $139.40 -- BELOW our R1 entry zone of $145-155 established in Feb 2026. MoS is now 27% vs FV $190. Forward P/E ~16x on guided $8.70-8.90 EPS. This is an attractive entry point for a Tier A compounder IF Q1 earnings (Apr 23) confirm the tariff pass-through thesis.

**What could change this to SOBREPONDERAR:**
- ALLE Q1 earnings confirming tariff pass-through + non-resi strength (Apr 23) -- would validate entry at $139
- Broad sector de-rating to 14-16x forward P/E (happening for ALLE specifically)
- Government physical security spending acceleration visible in H2 2026 data
- Iran resolution + oil decline triggering rate cut expectations and residential recovery

**What could change this to INFRAPONDERAR:**
- Sustained oil >$110 for 6+ months crushing residential AND non-residential construction
- ALLE Q1 earnings MISS + guidance cut (tariff pass-through not working)
- Smart lock commoditization spreading to commercial segment
- Major new entrant (Amazon, Google) successfully bypassing specifier relationships
- Feb nonresidential start collapse ($49.4B) proves to be trend, not noise
