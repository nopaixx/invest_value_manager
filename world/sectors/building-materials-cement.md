## Resumen Ejecutivo

The global cement industry is a mature, capital-intensive, cyclical business tied to construction activity, infrastructure spending, and housing cycles. The market is projected to grow at ~5.7% CAGR globally to $661B by 2033, with regional variance: Europe 5.4% CAGR through 2029 (driven by EU Green Deal, CBAM), US 6% annual growth through 2031 supported by IIJA/CHIPS Act/data centers.

The sector is fundamentally LOCAL due to cement's high weight-to-value ratio, making transportation expensive (~25% of cement price). This creates regional oligopolies with significant barriers to entry (permitting, capital intensity, environmental compliance). Top 5 US producers control 56% of shipments. Winners are determined by plant location, cost efficiency, and pricing discipline rather than brand or technology. The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), now in definitive phase since Jan 2026, raises barriers further by penalizing inefficient producers and imports.

**MARCH-APRIL 2026 UPDATE -- KEY CHANGES:**

1. **OIL SHOCK (Iran/Hormuz crisis -- PERSISTING)**: WTI at $102 (T1 verified Apr 8) -- down from $119 peak but still +65% YoY and +65% vs pre-crisis ~$62. Energy = 10-15% of cement production costs, plus significant transportation costs. At $102 WTI, the cost headwind adds ~7-10% to total production costs vs pre-crisis baseline. Diesel surged 20%+ in early weeks of crisis, directly hitting freight and plant costs. US nonresidential construction input costs surging at 12.6% annualized rate (Jan-Feb 2026). However, oil off peak ($119 to $102) suggests some stabilization. Still: this is a MATERIAL and SUSTAINED headwind for margins in H1 2026. Pricing pass-through lag remains 6-12 months.

2. **TARIFF RESOLUTION (Liberation Day)**: US Supreme Court ruled Liberation Day tariffs unconstitutional (Feb 2026, Learning Resources v. Trump). $166B refund process underway. This removes the 10% baseline + reciprocal tariffs on imported cement and building materials. HOWEVER: Section 232 tariffs on steel (50%) and aluminum (50%) REMAIN under separate authority. Net effect: cement imports de-tariffed, but construction costs still elevated from steel/rebar tariffs + oil.

3. **Italy construction mixed**: PMI recovered to 50.4 in Feb (from 47.7 Jan) -- marginal expansion. But Italy composite PMI fell to 49.2 in March (from 52.1), indicating broader economic weakness. PNRR deadline pressure intensifying. Residential still weak.

4. **US IIJA still only ~40% spent**: Data center construction additive. But US cement demand remains at "saddle point" -- neither declining nor improving meaningfully. 2026 potentially trough year. Housing starts surprisingly strong in Jan (1.487M SAAR, +7.2% MoM) driven by multi-family, but permits declining (-5.4% MoM) = forward weakness.

5. **CBAM definitive phase**: First certificate price published Apr 7, 2026. Q1 2026 price reflects quarterly average of EU ETS auctions (~EUR 65-70/mt, down from EUR 85 in Dec 2025 as economic slowdown depressed allowance demand). Lower carbon price slightly reduces CBAM barrier for importers but mechanism is structurally in place and prices expected to rise as economy recovers.

6. **M&A acceleration continues**: Heidelberg completed Burnco Edmonton acquisition Apr 1. CRH $40B deployment ongoing. Industry consolidation thesis intact.

**Key risk for any cement investment remains cyclicality**: P/E ratios appear low at peak earnings. Through-cycle analysis is MANDATORY. Current EBITDA margins for top producers (Buzzi ~27%, CRH 32%, Heidelberg 32% US) are near cyclical peaks. The structural improvement vs cyclical peak debate is the central analytical question.

**Oil + recession creates a DETERIORATING scenario vs March**: If oil stays >$90 for 3+ months (now at month 2+ above $90), construction activity weakens, cement volumes decline, and margins compress from energy costs before pricing catches up. Recession probability 30-49% adds volume risk on top of cost pressure. The sector is pricing in significant distress -- HEI.DE -30% from 52wH, BZU.MI -20% from 52wH.

- At 30-49% recession probability, expected volume impact = probability-weighted -4% to -7%

**Net assessment for cement stocks (Apr 2026):**

## EU Green Deal / Renovation Wave Impact

The EU renovation wave targets doubling the annual energy renovation rate of buildings by 2030, requiring massive quantities of building materials including cement-based products. Key drivers:

1. **CBAM (definitive from Jan 2026)**: Protects EU cement producers from cheaper, dirtier imports. Phase-in from 2.5% (2026) to 100% (2034). First certificate price published Apr 7 based on Q1 2026 EU ETS quarterly average (~EUR 65-70/mt, down from EUR 85 in Dec 2025). Lower carbon price temporarily reduces CBAM barrier but mechanism structurally in place.

2. **EU Recovery & Resilience Facility**: EUR 891.7B mobilized through 2030 for transport corridors, renewable energy plants, and building renovation -- all cement-intensive.

3. **Decarbonization investment**: EU cement makers investing in CCUS, alternative fuels, calcined clay. Heidelberg targeting 350K tons evoZero by end 2026, 1.35M tons by 2030. Transformation Accelerator Initiative targeting EUR 300M savings by end 2026.

4. **Clinker capacity reductions**: European producers planning 25-30% clinker capacity reductions. This tightens supply and supports pricing -- structurally bullish for pricing discipline.

**Net assessment**: EU Green Deal is a NET POSITIVE for incumbent EU cement producers. It raises barriers (CBAM), supports demand (renovation + infrastructure), and rewards low-carbon leaders. Short-term: EU ETS weakness reduces CBAM bite slightly. Medium-term: as recession fears recede, carbon prices will re-strengthen.
