# Sector: Building Materials / Cement

> Ultima actualizacion: 2026-03-18 (macro-analyst refresh — BZU.MI sold, oil $98 WTI sustained, FOMC today)
> Status: NEUTRAL-CAUTELA (cyclical at late-peak, oil shock adds cost pressure but infrastructure tailwinds persist)

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The global cement industry is a mature, capital-intensive, cyclical business tied to construction activity, infrastructure spending, and housing cycles. The market is projected to grow at ~5.7% CAGR globally to $661B by 2033, with regional variance: Europe 5.4% CAGR through 2029 (driven by EU Green Deal, CBAM), US 6% annual growth through 2031 supported by IIJA/CHIPS Act/data centers.

The sector is fundamentally LOCAL due to cement's high weight-to-value ratio, making transportation expensive (~25% of cement price). This creates regional oligopolies with significant barriers to entry (permitting, capital intensity, environmental compliance). Top 5 US producers control 56% of shipments. Winners are determined by plant location, cost efficiency, and pricing discipline rather than brand or technology. The EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), now in definitive phase since Jan 2026, raises barriers further by penalizing inefficient producers and imports.

**MARCH 2026 UPDATE -- KEY CHANGES:**

1. **OIL SHOCK (Iran/Hormuz crisis)**: WTI at $92+ (was ~$62 pre-crisis). Energy = 10-15% of cement production costs, plus significant transportation costs. This is a MATERIAL headwind for margins in H1 2026. However, cement producers have historically passed energy costs through with pricing, and the oligopolistic market structure supports this. The question is timing lag: 1-2 quarters of margin compression before price increases catch up.

2. **Italy construction contracting**: PMI at 47.7 (Jan 2026), third consecutive month in contraction. Residential expected -7.5% in 2026. However, PNRR infrastructure spending is the offset -- EUR 54B to complete by 2025-2026, civil engineering +5.6% in 2025. Net effect for Buzzi Italy: FLAT to slightly negative volumes, positive pricing.

3. **US IIJA entering final year**: Only 40% of $350B highway funding spent as of Sep 2025. Remaining 60% creates multi-year demand runway. Data center construction is additive NEW demand driver. BUT Sullivan Report projects 2026 as trough of current US cement demand cycle (~100M tons).

4. **CBAM definitive phase**: From Jan 2026, carbon now a visible cost for imported cement. Initially 2.5% of embedded CO2 in 2026, scaling to 100% by 2034. EU carbon allowances averaged EUR 85/mt in Dec 2025. This structurally benefits EU incumbents with lower carbon intensity.

5. **M&A acceleration**: Holcim deploying CHF 18-22B through 2030 (CHF 3-4B for bolt-on M&A). CRH plans $40B deployment over 5 years ($28B for growth capex + M&A). Heidelberg acquired Burnco (Dec 2025). Consolidation thesis intact -- sector continues concentrating.

**Key risk for any cement investment remains cyclicality**: P/E ratios appear low at peak earnings. Through-cycle analysis is MANDATORY. Current EBITDA margins for top producers (Buzzi ~27%, CRH 32%, Heidelberg 32% US) are near cyclical peaks. The structural improvement vs cyclical peak debate is the central analytical question.

**Oil shock creates a BINARY scenario**: If oil stays >$90 for 3+ months, construction activity weakens (especially residential/private), cement volumes decline, and margins compress from energy costs before pricing catches up. If oil normalizes on ceasefire ($70-80), the pre-crisis infrastructure tailwind resumes. Our sector status shifts from NEUTRAL to NEUTRAL-CAUTELA to reflect this uncertainty.

## Iran/Oil Shock Impact Assessment (March 2026)

**Direct cost impact on cement producers:**
- Energy = 10-15% of production costs. Cement kilns require massive heat (natural gas + electricity)
- Oil at $92 WTI vs $62 pre-crisis = +48%. If sustained, adds ~5-7% to total production costs
- Transport costs (heavy materials, high volume) also energy-intensive
- Asphalt/bitumen directly linked to oil price -- paving costs move instantly

**Pricing power response:**
- Cement industry has oligopolistic pricing structure -- cost pass-through historically 6-12 month lag
- EU producers expect 3-4% price increases in 2026 (pre-crisis estimate)
- If oil sustains >$90, expect accelerated price increases in H2 2026
- US pricing forecast +0.6% (IBISWorld) likely needs revision upward

**Volume impact:**
- Private construction (residential, commercial) MOST exposed to oil-driven economic slowdown
- Infrastructure (IIJA, PNRR) relatively insulated -- government-funded, less discretionary
- If recession materializes (40-55% probability per world view), volumes could decline 10-15%

**Net assessment for cement stocks:**
- SHORT-TERM (Q1-Q2 2026): NEGATIVE. Margin compression from energy costs before pricing catches up
- MEDIUM-TERM (H2 2026+): NEUTRAL. Pricing adjustment restores margins if volumes hold
- LONG-TERM: Infrastructure tailwinds intact regardless of oil

**Buzzi-specific:** 48% of revenue from US (IIJA-supported), 17% from Italy (mixed), 18% from Germany (fiscal stimulus). Net cash EUR 1.1B = no refinancing risk. Better positioned than leveraged competitors.

## EU Green Deal / Renovation Wave Impact

The EU renovation wave targets doubling the annual energy renovation rate of buildings by 2030, requiring massive quantities of building materials including cement-based products. Key drivers:

1. **CBAM (definitive from Jan 2026)**: Protects EU cement producers from cheaper, dirtier imports. Phase-in from 2.5% (2026) to 100% (2034). EU carbon allowances at EUR 85/mt create meaningful cost barrier for non-EU producers.

2. **EU Recovery & Resilience Facility**: EUR 891.7B mobilized through 2030 for transport corridors, renewable energy plants, and building renovation -- all cement-intensive.

3. **Decarbonization investment**: EU cement makers investing in CCUS, alternative fuels, calcined clay. Leaders who invest early will benefit from CBAM protection while laggards bear carbon costs.

4. **Clinker capacity reductions**: European producers planning 25-30% clinker capacity reductions. This tightens supply and supports pricing -- structurally bullish for pricing discipline.

**Net assessment**: EU Green Deal is a NET POSITIVE for incumbent EU cement producers. It raises barriers (CBAM), supports demand (renovation + infrastructure), and rewards low-carbon leaders.

## M&A Activity (2025-2026)

The building materials sector is in an active M&A phase driven by:

1. **Holcim**: CHF 18-22B deployment capacity through 2030. CHF 3-4B for bolt-on M&A plus CHF 4-6B for strategic M&A. Acquired 50% of Cementos Pacasmayo (Peru) for $550M. Spun off US business as Amrize (Jun 2025).

2. **CRH**: $40B deployment plan over 5 years ($28B for growth capex + M&A). Acquired Eco Materials Technologies for $2.1B (fly ash / decarbonization). Aggressively consolidating North American market.

3. **Heidelberg**: Acquired Burnco Rock Products (Dec 2025). Targeting 250bps margin improvement by 2030.

4. **Buzzi**: Acquired controlling stake in Gulf Cement (UAE operations). Sold cement plant to Austrian partners. NET CASH EUR 1.1B = M&A firepower without leverage.

**Implication**: Industry consolidating. Scale operators getting bigger. Smaller producers face choice: sell to majors or invest in CBAM compliance. Consolidation is STRUCTURALLY BULLISH for pricing discipline.
