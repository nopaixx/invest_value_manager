## Resumen Ejecutivo

The global shipbroking industry intermediates the chartering, sale & purchase, and newbuilding of vessels worldwide. Brokers earn commissions (typically 1-2% of transaction value) on shipping contracts without taking principal risk, making it asset-light with high margins. The industry is cyclical because commission revenue tracks freight rates, vessel values, and S&P transaction volumes, which correlate with global trade. However, top brokers with diversified segment coverage and recurring data/research businesses demonstrate meaningful through-cycle resilience.

The sector is fragmented but consolidating. Clarkson PLC dominates with ~25% market share and revenue 4.5x the #2 player (Braemar). The brokerage model is relationship-intensive and data-driven, creating barriers for new entrants but limited protection from shipping cycle downturns. Digitalization is a modest long-term threat (platform disintermediation), but the complexity of global shipping transactions, regulatory requirements, and relationship nature of brokerage work protects incumbents for the medium term.

**STATUS UPGRADE (Mar 7): NEUTRAL -> FAVORABLE.** The Hormuz crisis has TRANSFORMED the shipping landscape. Freight rates have spiked to all-time highs (VLCC daily rates $424K/day, 2x normal). Commission revenue for shipbrokers is directly tied to charter values, which are surging. Simultaneously, the crisis has massively increased demand for Clarksons Research data (SIN database, fleet tracking) as the entire industry scrambles for real-time intelligence on vessel positions, route diversions, and risk assessment. This is the BEST possible environment for the dominant shipbroker: higher commissions on higher transaction values + higher demand for data/advisory services. The crisis also accelerates the complexity moat -- navigating sanctions, war risk insurance, route optimization, and regulatory compliance requires exactly the expertise that top brokers provide.

Valuation remains reasonable for the leader. CKN.L pre-results (FY2025 due 9-Mar-2026) trading at P/E ~16.8x, EV/EBIT ~8.6x with net cash. H1 2025 showed revenue decline (-4%) and profit decline (-23%), reflecting softer pre-crisis dry bulk and tanker markets. However, the forward outlook has FUNDAMENTALLY CHANGED due to Hormuz. The forward order book ($231M) provides partial visibility but does NOT yet capture the crisis-driven rate environment.

**UPDATE Apr 8: Valuation has RE-RATED.** CKN.L now at 4706p, P/E 22.1x (vs 16.8x at sector view creation). The crisis tailwind is being priced in. At current price, CKN.L trades ABOVE its 5yr avg P/E of 22x — the discount is gone. This does NOT invalidate the thesis (H1 2026 earnings should be materially higher) but the entry calculus has changed dramatically.

## HORMUZ CRISIS IMPACT (March 2026) -- CRITICAL DEVELOPMENT

### Overview

The Strait of Hormuz has been at NEAR-TOTAL HALT since Feb 28, 2026, following Operation Epic Fury (US-Israel strikes on Iran). Iran's IRGC declared the strait closed on Mar 2. As of Mar 6, only 2 commercial transits in 24 hours (JMIC/Bloomberg). ~3,200 vessels idle in Gulf area (Clarksons data). ~280 bulk carriers trapped inside the Persian Gulf. ~200 compliant tankers stranded (Lloyd's List). This is the MOST SIGNIFICANT disruption to global shipping since the Suez Canal crisis, but LARGER in scale -- Hormuz handles 20% of global oil supply vs Suez at ~12% of global trade.

**UPDATE Apr 8 (War Day 39+):** The strait remains effectively closed to Western-aligned shipping but a SELECTIVE-ACCESS regime has evolved. Iran permits transits for vessels flagged to China, Russia, India, Iraq, Pakistan, Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, and Turkey. Only ~150 total vessels have transited since Mar 1. Recent weekly transits reached 21 ships (highest since war began) but this is still <5% of normal throughput (~60/day). IRGC is collecting passage fees in Chinese yuan. Omani, French, and Japanese vessels transited on Apr 3 in isolated cases. The situation is a de facto TWO-TIER SYSTEM: Iran-allied nations transit with fees, Western-aligned nations remain blocked. This creates a new complexity layer for shipbrokers -- navigating which flags/routes are viable, what insurance is available, how to optimize mixed fleets.

The crisis creates a windfall for tanker broker commissions even though VOLUME of Gulf-origin fixtures has collapsed, because (a) rates on alternative routes have also surged, (b) re-routing around Cape of Good Hope creates NEW fixture demand, and (c) S&P activity increases as owners reposition fleets.
