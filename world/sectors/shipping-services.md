# Sector: Shipping Services (Shipbroking & Maritime Advisory)

> Ultima actualizacion: 2026-03-18 (macro-analyst refresh — CKN.L FY2025 results released, container rates +30% since crisis)
> Status: FAVORABLE (crisis-driven activity surge + structural complexity increase)

## Resumen Ejecutivo

The global shipbroking industry intermediates the chartering, sale & purchase, and newbuilding of vessels worldwide. Brokers earn commissions (typically 1-2% of transaction value) on shipping contracts without taking principal risk, making it asset-light with high margins. The industry is cyclical because commission revenue tracks freight rates, vessel values, and S&P transaction volumes, which correlate with global trade. However, top brokers with diversified segment coverage and recurring data/research businesses demonstrate meaningful through-cycle resilience.

The sector is fragmented but consolidating. Clarkson PLC dominates with ~25% market share and revenue 4.5x the #2 player (Braemar). The brokerage model is relationship-intensive and data-driven, creating barriers for new entrants but limited protection from shipping cycle downturns. Digitalization is a modest long-term threat (platform disintermediation), but the complexity of global shipping transactions, regulatory requirements, and relationship nature of brokerage work protects incumbents for the medium term.

**STATUS UPGRADE (Mar 7): NEUTRAL -> FAVORABLE.** The Hormuz crisis has TRANSFORMED the shipping landscape. Freight rates have spiked to all-time highs (VLCC daily rates $424K/day, 2x normal). Commission revenue for shipbrokers is directly tied to charter values, which are surging. Simultaneously, the crisis has massively increased demand for Clarksons Research data (SIN database, fleet tracking) as the entire industry scrambles for real-time intelligence on vessel positions, route diversions, and risk assessment. This is the BEST possible environment for the dominant shipbroker: higher commissions on higher transaction values + higher demand for data/advisory services. The crisis also accelerates the complexity moat -- navigating sanctions, war risk insurance, route optimization, and regulatory compliance requires exactly the expertise that top brokers provide.

Valuation remains reasonable for the leader. CKN.L pre-results (FY2025 due 9-Mar-2026) trading at P/E ~16.8x, EV/EBIT ~8.6x with net cash. H1 2025 showed revenue decline (-4%) and profit decline (-23%), reflecting softer pre-crisis dry bulk and tanker markets. However, the forward outlook has FUNDAMENTALLY CHANGED due to Hormuz. The forward order book ($231M) provides partial visibility but does NOT yet capture the crisis-driven rate environment.

## HORMUZ CRISIS IMPACT (March 2026) -- CRITICAL DEVELOPMENT

### Overview

The Strait of Hormuz has been at NEAR-TOTAL HALT since Feb 28, 2026, following Operation Epic Fury (US-Israel strikes on Iran). Iran's IRGC declared the strait closed on Mar 2. As of Mar 6, only 2 commercial transits in 24 hours (JMIC/Bloomberg). ~3,200 vessels idle in Gulf area (Clarksons data). ~280 bulk carriers trapped inside the Persian Gulf. ~200 compliant tankers stranded (Lloyd's List). This is the MOST SIGNIFICANT disruption to global shipping since the Suez Canal crisis, but LARGER in scale -- Hormuz handles 20% of global oil supply vs Suez at ~12% of global trade.

### Impact on Tanker Rates -- ALL-TIME HIGHS

| Metric | Pre-Crisis | Current (Mar 5-6) | Change |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|--------|
| VLCC daily rate (ME->China) | ~$200K/day | **$424K/day** (all-time high) | +112% |
| 60-day VLCC charter | ~$13M | **$26M+** | +100% |
| Suezmax rates | Elevated | Record levels | Surging |
| MR tanker rates | Normal | Elevated | Rising |

**Implication for CKN.L:** Commission revenue is DIRECTLY tied to charter values. If a VLCC charter is worth $26M (vs $13M normal), the 1-2% broker commission is $260K-520K per fixture vs $130K-260K normally. This is PURE margin expansion -- no additional cost to broker a higher-value fixture. Tanker broking is CKN's largest revenue segment. The crisis creates a windfall for tanker broker commissions even though VOLUME of Gulf-origin fixtures has collapsed, because (a) rates on alternative routes have also surged, (b) re-routing around Cape of Good Hope creates NEW fixture demand, and (c) S&P activity increases as owners reposition fleets.

### Impact on Container Shipping -- MASSIVE SURCHARGES + REROUTING

| Action | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Maersk | Emergency freight increase. All ME-India to Med/US East Coast rerouted via Cape of Good Hope |
| CMA CGM | Emergency Conflict Surcharge (ECS): $2,000/TEU for 20-ft dry container |
| Hapag-Lloyd | War Risk Surcharge (WRS): $1,500/TEU on bookings from Mar 2 |
| MSC | Surcharges up to $1,800/container |

**Cape of Good Hope rerouting:** Adds ~10-14 days to ME-Europe routes, ~7-10 days to ME-US routes. This absorbs vessel capacity (longer voyages = fewer available ships), pushing rates up GLOBALLY, not just on Gulf routes. This is the same dynamic that boosted shipping during the Red Sea/Houthi crisis of 2024, but LARGER.

**Implication for CKN.L:** Container/liner broking is a smaller segment for Clarkson but the global rate impact (higher rates everywhere due to capacity absorption) benefits ALL broking segments. More complexity = more advisory demand.

### Impact on Dry Bulk -- MODERATE BUT GROWING

- S12 route (Arabian Gulf-India) jumped $2,250/day week-on-week to $18,083/day (highest since assessments began Dec 2025)
- BIMCO estimates disruptions currently impact ~4% of dry bulk cargo volumes
- ~280 bulk carriers trapped inside Persian Gulf
- Commodity freight rates for aluminium, bauxite, zinc, lead, lithium all elevated
- Impact LESS than tanker/container because dry bulk trade through Hormuz is proportionally smaller

**Implication for CKN.L:** Dry bulk was CKN's weakest H1 2025 segment. The crisis provides a modest boost but the primary driver remains tanker and container rate spikes.

### Shipping Insurance / War Risk Premiums -- TRANSFORMATIONAL

| Metric | Pre-Crisis | Current | Change |
|--------|-----------|---------|--------|
| War risk premium (% of vessel value) | 0.20-0.25% | **1.0-1.5%** (Marsh est.) | +400-650% |
| Tanker war risk (valued $200-300M) | ~$625K | **~$7.5M** | +1,100% |
| P&I club status | Normal | **CANCELLED** (Gard, Skuld, NorthStandard, London P&I, American Club) | Fundamental change |

**Key developments:**
- Major P&I clubs CANCELLED war risk cover effective Mar 5. Cover now only available on single-voyage basis
- War risk premiums surged by >1,000% in some cases (Insurance Journal)
- US government announced $20B maritime insurance plan via DFC to break shipping paralysis
- Reinsurance News reports US insurance proposal "may not be enough" to restart shipping
- Some marine insurers claim war cover IS available for Hormuz at elevated rates (Seatrade)

**Implication for CKN.L:** The insurance crisis INCREASES broker value-add dramatically. Navigating the insurance landscape -- finding cover, understanding exclusions, advising on risk -- is exactly the advisory capability that top brokers provide. This is NOT just commission revenue; it is Clarksons Financial Services (insurance broking division) earning directly from the crisis. CKN's insurance broking arm benefits from both higher premiums (commission on premium value) and higher transaction complexity (more advisory work per deal).

### Impact on CKN.L Specifically -- NET STRONGLY POSITIVE

**Revenue drivers analysis:**

| CKN Segment | Pre-Crisis | Crisis Impact | Net Effect |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|------------|
| Tanker broking | Weak (H1 2025 -23% PBT) | ALL-TIME HIGH rates, massive commission windfall | **STRONGLY POSITIVE** |
| Container/liner | Moderate | Cape rerouting absorbs capacity, global rates up | **POSITIVE** |
| Dry bulk | Weak | Moderate boost, 4% of volumes affected | **SLIGHTLY POSITIVE** |
| S&P (sale & purchase) | Moderate | Fleet repositioning, distressed sales, new demand | **POSITIVE** |
| Clarksons Research (SIN) | Stable, recurring | SURGE in demand for real-time shipping intelligence | **STRONGLY POSITIVE** |
| Financial services (insurance) | Stable | War risk premium surge = commission windfall | **STRONGLY POSITIVE** |
| Offshore | Growing | Oil supply disruption = offshore drilling urgency | **POSITIVE** |

**KEY NUANCE -- Volume vs Value:**
- Gulf-origin VOLUME has collapsed (near-zero Hormuz transits)
- But GLOBAL rates have surged (capacity absorption, rerouting)
- Net effect for commission-based broker: POSITIVE because commissions are % of VALUE, not volume
- A world with fewer but much more expensive charters still generates higher commissions
- PLUS: the complexity premium means brokers are more needed, not less

**RISKS to the CKN.L crisis thesis:**
1. **If ceasefire comes quickly (<2 weeks total):** Rates normalize fast. The windfall is short-lived. H2 2025 results (due Mar 9) will NOT yet reflect the crisis (crisis started Feb 28, H2 ends Dec 31)
2. **If crisis causes global recession:** Long-term trade volumes collapse, offsetting short-term rate spike. Net negative over 12-24 months
3. **If crisis is prolonged (>3 months):** Initial rate spike normalizes to "new normal" lower level as trade patterns adjust. BUT insurance/advisory demand remains elevated

**Assessment:** The Hormuz crisis is NET STRONGLY POSITIVE for CKN.L in the near-to-medium term (3-12 months). It validates the thesis that the dominant shipbroker benefits from COMPLEXITY and DISRUPTION, not just from high rates. The data/research moat (SIN database) becomes more valuable in crisis. The insurance broking arm earns windfall commissions. And tanker commission revenue surges even as Gulf volumes collapse, because global rates are at all-time highs.
